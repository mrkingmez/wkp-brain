## Bottom line

I can not show that any method beats what Zac did in Week 9. No free archive of past DraftKings or FanDuel salaries or ownership exists that I could find, so a true backtest is impossible for free: I have one real slate (Week 9, DraftKings) and tests on free nflverse data. What I can say honestly:

1. What is solid (measured in-house, 44 slates of nflverse 2024 to Week 9 2026): QB and his top WR are correlated at +0.44, QB and WR2 +0.38, QB and TE1 +0.30, QB and RB1 +0.11, QB and the opposing QB +0.17, QB and the opposing WR1 +0.12. The game environment matters: players on teams in the top third of implied team total (from the closing line) averaged 18.5 DraftKings points for the top three usage players per team-game against 13.7 in the bottom third. Per implied team point: QB +0.72 DK points, WR +0.40, RB +0.38, TE +0.21. Total explains only 1 to 9 percent of single-player variance (R squared 0.09 QB, 0.03 WR, 0.03 RB, 0.01 TE).
2. What is plausible but not shown: that stacking or a bring-back raises the chance of a very high score. In a proxy test (below) stacking did not raise the average score (+0.9, standard error 3.0) but raised the spread (standard deviation 25.6 vs 17.6) and the 90th percentile (134.8 vs 127.9). That is the shape you want for a tournament and the opposite of what you want for a cash game.
3. What is not supported: a $4,000 punt QB. No study isolates it. Week 9 lineup 1 used one (Drew Lock, 12.6 realized points against 14.7 projected) and the lineup missed its projection by 11 points before the DST.
4. Expected value: the widely repeated figure that 91 percent of profit goes to 1.3 percent of players comes from a 2015 MLB study (SECONDARY, primary not opened). Rake is roughly 8 to 15 percent (SECONDARY). A casual player should expect to lose a little. Treat entry fees as entertainment money, and set the monthly number before the season, not after a bad week.

## What the in-house tests found

A. Measured on free nflverse data (`dfs_study.py`), numbers at the top of this report. The closing line is used for implied team totals. The 2026 schedule has lines for 150 of 272 games (research brief); I did not confirm that nflverse spread_line and total_line are true closing lines.

B. Proxy stacking backtest, 35 slates (2024 to Week 9 2026), NOT a real DFS test. Salary is synthetic (3000 plus 260 times a trailing 4-game average plus noise), projection is the trailing average, DST not modeled.

| Build | Mean score | Std dev | 90th pct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain (best projection under the cap) | 104.7 | 17.6 | 127.9 |
| Stack (QB plus 1 WR/TE, same team) | 105.7 | 25.6 | 134.8 |
| Stack plus game-environment tilt | 114.7 | 26.0 | 151.6 |

The environment tilt (add points for a high implied team total) raised the average by 10 points (standard error 3.6) because the trailing-average projection knows nothing about the game. The simulator in dfs_optimize already draws from the market line, so this gain is NOT additive there. Do not read it as +10.

C. The v2 optimizer (`dfs_optimize_v2.py`, new file, v1 untouched): stack on by default, optional bring-back, punt-QB guard (QBs under $5,000 dropped unless allowed), objective is the chance of reaching a target score (GPP target = 95th percentile of the best-average lineup, cash target = a cash line, default 130), and lineups share at most 5 of 9 players. On the one real slate (Week 9, DraftKings, realized without DST):

| Lineup set | Mean realized | Best |
|---|---|---|
| v2 GPP, stack | 98.4 | 128.7 |
| v2 GPP, stack plus bring-back | 98.5 | 117.6 |
| v2 cash | 108.2 | 141.4 |
| v2 GPP, no stack, punt QB allowed | 97.5 | 129.2 |
| Zac's two as run (csv) | 121.4 and 143.2 | |

v2 did worse than Zac's two lineups on that slate. One slate says nothing about quality in either direction (two of Zac's players, Nacua and Stroud, had 39 points each), but it means I have no evidence to recommend switching.

## Recommendation, honestly sized

1. Use cash-style contests (double-ups, single entry) for most of the money; they reward average accuracy, which is the only thing the sims have shown. Keep GPPs to a small fixed budget.
2. In GPPs only: stack the QB with his WR1 or WR2, consider one bring-back, avoid a $4,000 QB, and do not copy a lineup (overlap 5 or fewer). This is a structural recommendation based on measured correlation, not a demonstrated profit edge.
3. Start the archive that would allow a real backtest next season: every week, save the DraftKings salary csv and, within 10 days of the contest ending, the contest "Export Lineups to CSV" with % Drafted; log your own entries and scores. Ten weeks gives a real slate set. I can build the saver.
4. Do not claim an edge until a dozen real slates support it. Tell me when the lunch notes (FF-2026-11-10-02) arrive and I will add the friend's method as a testable rule.
