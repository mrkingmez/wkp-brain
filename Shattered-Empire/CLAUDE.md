## Workflow — how edits happen
Zac downloads the current manuscript from Google Drive, attaches it to a Claude prompt, and edits chapter by chapter. Each round: download from Drive, attach, edit, then upload the changed file back to Drive, overwriting the working copy. Repeat through the full edit pass. When a full pass is complete, the final version is downloaded, renamed with a version number, and uploaded to the Claude project files as an archival snapshot.

This means: Google Drive holds the current live working manuscript. Claude project files hold versioned, dated snapshots of completed passes, not the live copy. When in doubt about which is current, Drive wins unless a specific version number is being requested.

NOTE: the manuscript file in this Drive folder is named "Copy of The Patient Dark - The Shattered Empire Book 1.docx" — an old working filename, not the real title. Worth renaming to match the confirmed title "The Patience of the Dark" next time it's touched, and confirming this is the actual live working file and not an orphaned duplicate.

## Editorial Style Rules (from Editorial_Style_Rules.pdf)
- No em dashes
- No colons in editorial suggestions
- "God stays" — do not suggest removing or softening religious references
- The author has final say on every call. Suggestions are suggestions.

## Hard Rules
- NEVER invent lines, scenes, dialogue, character details, place names, or lore that do not already exist in the manuscript or the canon documents. This is Zac's most important standing rule. If something is needed and does not exist, say so and ask.
- Check the Master Character Annex before using any character name or dialect. Name spellings are locked.
- Check the Aether Stone Magic System before writing or editing anything involving magic.
- Preserve Zac's voice. Do not sand prose into generic polish. The fiction-editing skill exists specifically for this and should be used for edit passes.
- Zac is not a heavy outliner. He works from "here is what needs to happen, now let us have fun getting there," and will change plans mid-write when a better story opportunity appears — see the Ribbon/Nessa example below. Do not push him toward rigid plot structure.
- Before starting any edit session, confirm which file/version is being worked from if there is any ambiguity — do not assume the most recently seen copy is current.

## Craft Note Worth Preserving
Concrete example of Zac's character-driven, non-outlined process: Ribbon was originally written to survive, but Zac realized killing him opened a much stronger arc for Nessa — turning her from "love interest / cool thief" into a character with her own real trajectory. This instinct for seizing a better story opportunity mid-write is a defining part of how Zac works and should be respected/supported, not corrected toward rigid outlining.

The core Brandon Sanderson audience — readers waiting for the next big reveal/twist, who also want more team-based military-adjacent stories (not lone-hero-focused) where every team member has a real arc, not just the protagonist.

## Book 2 — Plot, As Currently Planned
Three factions moving simultaneously, on a collision course:

1. **The team** — after the Aldoria capital takeover (where the last book left off), the team (minus Nessa and Alura) is sent on a new mission. Edric is funding them essentially as assassins/info-gatherers.
2. **Edric (Kane Ederic)** — trying to reforge the shattered empire under himself, the way his father tried and failed to do diplomatically. His current play: manufacture a secondary war between two Eastern Empire kingdoms that were never part of the core defensive pact, then step in as "mediator" to get both to bend the knee — folding them into his empire under the guise of peacemaking.
3. **Lucent's forces (the uncrowned king)** — a new lieutenant, a necromancer, is attacking the Eastern flank with an army of the dead. Zac has NOT yet decided if this necromancer is male or female — this is an open authorial decision, not something the in-story characters are debating.

Spotlight rotation for Book 2: Dane/Wedburn got heavy focus in Book 1; Aldrit gets more spotlight this book. Something specific and separate is planned for Alura, not yet detailed. Harmon and Wedburn's overarching thread continues.
