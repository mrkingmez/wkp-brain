The wind whispered like a warning across the ridge, dry and cold, carrying with it the acrid scent of ash from the half-dead fires burning below. From their perch atop the broken lip of the crater, the world dropped steeply into a jagged basin where the orc raiders had made camp. Crude fencing of scavenged wood and rusted spikes marked a rough perimeter, and beyond it, chaos had made a home.
Caged wagons sat at odd angles in the mud, doors hanging open, splintered and bent. Makeshift tents leaned against uneven rock. Worgs paced the outer ring, chains clinking with every restless prowl. Even from this distance, their breath steamed in the cold.
Luca crouched low, cloak dragging softly against dust-blown stone. His eyes tracked the camp in slow, methodical sweeps. Every movement below was logged, measured, and weighed. He exhaled once, deliberately.
“All right,” he murmured, voice barely louder than the wind. “We got the go-ahead.”
Aymir let out a soft huff of breath beside him, half grin, half anticipation. He nudged Taerel with the barrel of his rifle.
“You showed us your magic earlier,” Aymir said quietly. “Now we return the favor.”
They settled in behind a boulder, movements practiced and economical. Taerel popped the chamber and began loading cartridges, fingers steady despite the cold.
“Six beasts,” he said. “Four of us. No, five.”
“Five,” Aymir replied, peering down his scope. “I do not miss.”
“Neither do I,” Taerel said. “Or should we recall the winter fair four years ago?”
Luca lifted a hand. The brothers stilled instantly.
“Save it,” Luca said, not looking back. “Sound carries.”
No argument. No delay. "I go in first. If the prisoners can be cut loose before we raise an alarm, good. If we start firing before that, they die screaming. We do this right, or we do not do it at all." Luca said with command in his voice. 
Taerel muttered, “Your funeral,” but there was no mockery in it, just understanding.
Lysana shifted beside them, already crouched low, bow strung and resting across her lap. Her eyes traced the basin, angles, and routes mapping themselves behind her gaze. “Where do you want me?” She adjusted her grip on the bowstring, already shifting her weight.
Luca pointed toward a jagged rise across the crater’s far edge. “There. You’ll see everything coming in or out. Anything that moves toward me, orc, worg, or worse, you put it down before it makes a sound.”
She nodded once.
“When the prisoners are free,” Luca said, eyes narrowing on the distant shapes clustered near the tree, “stealth is no longer an issue.”
He turned slightly toward her. “Once that happens, leave your post and join me. Close and fast.”
Lysana moved to her post.
He looked back at the brothers. “You two stay up here. No matter what. Cover us. Don’t waste a shot. If it moves, you put it down. With this wind, it will be hard to relay information. Do the best you can.” The wind gusted again, tugging cloaks and stirring grit into the air. Taerel gave Aymir a knowing look as Luca walked away from them. Below, an orc barked something guttural and shoved a captive forward with the butt of a spear. The prisoner fell and did not rise.
Luca’s face hardened. He stood just enough to free his shoulders and reached beneath his collar. From a hidden recess, he drew free a small amulet, a black shard of polished onyx bound in silver wire. It pulsed faintly, slow and steady, like a second heart.
He closed his fist around it and breathed out. Shadows gathered close, clinging beneath the skin. “On my mark,” he said. “We end this fast.”
He was gone without another word.
Luca slipped forward like breath through smoke, dissolving into the shifting dark. His Shadow Walker gift did not make him invisible. It bent him, blurred him, turned him into a distortion where certainty failed. The moon ducked behind the clouds, and he took advantage, sliding low along the ridge until stone gave way beneath his palms.
He paused. Listened. A low murmur, rhythmic and wrong, barely audible beneath the crackle of fire and the hiss of wind through pine. Voices. Soft. Syncopated. Chanting in unison. The words dragged through the air like bone across stone, old and deliberate.
The sound was coming from the tree.
Luca eased forward, heart slowing despite the chill crawling up his spine. As the firelight sharpened, so did the shapes beneath it.
Nine.
Not five.
His breath caught. He slid over the ridge lip and dropped into a crouch, moving beneath the cover of a shattered cart wheel and a burned-out barrel. The chanting grew clearer now. Male and female voices, humming in broken synchrony. Not fervent. Not pleading.
Mechanical. Looping.
It wasn’t prayer.
It was binding.
The tree loomed ahead, its roots clawing up through churned mud and ash.
Nine prisoners were lashed to them with chains, wire, and blood-soaked hemp. Bodies slumped forward, heads ticking in time with the chant. At the edges, the dead had been fed to the roots. Ribs cracked and left like cages. Limbs taken. Flesh fused to bark.
Worg tracks ringed the mud. Claw marks. Viscera. A gnawed thigh bone lay near a bucket of old entrails.
These were not trophies. They were rations. Luca’s stomach hardened.
The living were barely that. Eyes wide but distant. Mouths agape, lips split and dry from heat and endless sound. One woman’s head twitched every few seconds, her voice slipping off the chant before snapping back in line. Another man convulsed every seventh beat, a reflex wired deep into his body.
This wasn’t devotion. This was possession.
Luca crouched behind a half-buried cauldron and drew his dagger. Its black edge caught no light. He brushed the chain at his throat once, feeling the shard’s pulse beneath his shirt.
Above, Aymir lay prone along a narrow granite outcrop, rifle settled perfectly into stone. He had checked it four times already. There was a rhythm to readiness.
He breathed out one word, barely sounding at all.
“Zaa’hil.”
The eye opens.
Beside him, Taerel shifted behind a notch of rock, careful not to silhouette. He flicked a tarnished copper coin across his knuckles, caught it without looking. The bite in his palm told him luck still held.
“Company,” he muttered.
A second patrol emerged from the trees. Five more orcs, larger than the first. One wore an iron pauldron shaped like a wolf’s head. Another dragged a length of chain fitted with hooks, letting it scrape for effect. Two more worgs paced beside them, leashed but barely restrained.
They were carrying another prisoner.
Lysana watched from her high perch, bow drawn, arrow nocked. She adjusted her stance without shifting weight. Hood up. Braid tucked. One slow breath through her nose. A flick of fingers across the fletching.
Make it count.
Taerel’s boot slipped. Not a fall, but close. He jammed his foot between two stones as pain flashed up his calf. He stilled, forced his breath slowly, then raised a hand.
Palm to chest. Circle.
Two points. I’m stuck. Still have a shot.
Aymir answered with a single nod.
Below, one of the returning orcs barked orders, gesturing toward the firepit, then the tree. The new prisoner was dropped near the flames, unconscious.
The two original guards turned and started toward the roots.
Luca flattened to the dirt. His hand slid to the first lock, fingers testing tension, silent.
Then the chanting stopped. All nine prisoners went still at once, as if a wire had been cut.
The woman’s head snapped up. Blind eyes blinked twice. Her lips moved again, but now they trembled. She wasn’t chanting.
She was whispering. Her head turned. Her gaze locked directly onto Luca. No eyes. No light. Just focus.
The gag slipped loose around her neck. Nothing. The woman did nothing. No cry. No gasp. Not even a moan.
Two orcs dragged her from the tree and hauled her toward the firepit, her feet leaving shallow lines in the snow. Her head lolled against the pull of the chain, mouth slack, eyes unfocused.
Luca watched, frozen. As they passed through the firelight, her face changed.
Not a flicker. Not a shift of skin. Something else pressed through, superimposed, wrong. In his next breath, he saw another face layered over hers, a face he remembered. Pale. Clean. Deep blue eyes. Hair the color of summer wheat, not the black matting her skull now wore. The woman’s lips did not move. The other face smiled.
Luca shook his head hard, once, as if he could dislodge the vision. His breath caught, sharp and painful. He had lived long enough to know the difference between a trick of light and something reaching. This had been reaching. He did not know from where. He did not know why that face. He folded it away with the precision of a man who has learned that some things only make sense after the blood has dried. 
One of the orcs leaned in, jaws opening, teeth bared as he reached for her throat.
Luca sprang into action. He crossed the firepit in three strides, blade whispering through the air. The first orc sagged forward, throat opened cleanly. The second barely had time to turn before Luca cut behind the knee, rose with the motion, and drove steel up through the palate. The body twitched once, then folded into the mud.
A rifle cracked from the ridge.
The runner at the tent mouth pinwheeled backward into the coals, fur smoking as it burned.
Aymir held his breath and his line. Ten orcs. Two worgs. Four still penned and slamming iron. He fired once, breaking a charge at the perimeter, then shifted without thought, tracking the next angle.
Snow sheared across his scope.
Lysana loosed.
The arrow hissed through falling flakes and vanished behind an orc’s ear. The body dropped as if the ground had given way beneath it.
Below her, the worgs broke.
Chains snapped against iron rings as the beasts surged free, splitting instinctively. One tore downslope toward the ridge. The other bounded uphill, straight for Lysana, fur matted with blood and foam.
Aymir already had the first in his sights. He fired. The round caught the beast’s shoulder and twisted it sideways, spraying snow and blood. It kept coming.
Taerel slid half a step behind cover, worked the bolt smooth and sure. He waited for the blur to clear the pine trunk and fired. The bullet took the worg through the eye. It tumbled end over end, stopping a stride from Aymir’s boots.
The second beast hit the ridge top with a roar that shook branches loose. Lysana barely saw it through the curtain of snow. She dropped from the rock, landed hard, rolled, dagger flashing free.
The worg skidded where she had stood, claws shrieking on stone, then turned and lunged. She cut sideways, shallow but true, drawing a red line along its flank. It whirled, jaws wide. She slid beneath the leap and drove her blade deep into its gut.
Hot blood sprayed across her arm, steaming.
The beast shrieked and twisted for one last strike…
A rifle cracked.
The worg collapsed mid-turn and slammed into the snow at her feet.
Silence fell thick as the storm.
None of them paused for confirmation. There was no time for it.
In the heart of the camp, the remaining orcs were fully roused now.
Four broke toward Luca near the firepit, axes raised, screaming. The rest peeled back toward the pens, hands on chains and crude locking mechanisms, working fast but carefully, opening just enough to prepare for release.
Inside the cages, the remaining worgs battered the bars, snarling and foaming. One smashed its skull into a beam hard enough to crack bone. Another chewed at the slats, spitting blood and splinters. The cages held. Barely.
Luca moved first.
A flick of his hand sent a silver dagger spinning end over end. It struck an orc clean through the eye socket. As the body fell, the blade shimmered and vanished, reappearing against Luca’s belt with a soft metallic click.
Another orc swung low. Luca danced aside, ducked a wide follow-up, and whispered something lost in the wind.
The shadows answered.
They surged from beneath his feet, oily and cold, coiling like living smoke. One orc screamed as the darkness seized its leg and dragged it down. Luca pivoted and plunged his blade through the exposed throat.
Blood steamed against the snow.
Aymir fired again, dropping an orc mid-run. Taerel’s follow-up severed a knee, sending the last attacker crashing face-first into the churned slush.
Only bodies remained near the firepit.
Luca swept his gaze wide, breath heavy but steady.
Too steady.
On the ridge, Aymir reloaded fast. Taerel mirrored him. Four rounds each. The pens were still chaotic.
The remaining caged worgs slammed themselves against the bars again and again, eyes wild, teeth snapping. Cracks spidered through the timber braces.
Taerel raised his rifle.
CRACK.
The largest beast took the round square in the chest. It flailed, smashed the bars once more, then collapsed in a heap of blood-slick straw.
CRACK.
Aymir dropped another mid-lunge, ribs and spine breaking under the impact. The third fell to Taerel’s shot through the jaw. The fourth to Aymir’s, cracking bone and ending the thrashing.
The pens went still.
Snow fell harder now, muting sound, swallowing heat.
Lysana was already moving, sliding down the incline, bow slung, second dagger drawn. She landed behind Luca, breath coming tight, eyes sharp.
Her look asked the question he was already thinking. “Is that all of them?”
Luca didn’t answer. He moved forward slowly, daggers ready, eyes sweeping the camp, counting bodies, counting shadows. His shadow stretched wide behind him like a hound straining at the leash.
Around them, embers popped. Tents flapped. The storm pressed close.
At the tree, Aymir and Taerel approached the prisoners cautiously.
“No, no… stay still,” Aymir said sharply as one man thrashed against his bonds. “Do not move.”
“We cannot cut them free yet,” Taerel said, planting himself between the tree and the clearing. “If there’s another wave…”
A groan cut him off. Two orcs stirred near the overturned crates. Not dead. Just dying.
They dragged themselves upright, blades scraping the snow, eyes locked on the prisoners.
Taerel turned first.
“Aymir!”
“I see them.”
Aymir met the first charge head-on. Steel rang. Sparks sprayed. He twisted, cut deep across the thigh, ducked a hammer swing by inches, then slashed the throat clean and stepped past the falling body.
Taerel moved more slowly, his injured foot dragging. Pain flared, but he raised his scimitar in time to catch a vertical chop. The impact nearly drove him to one knee.
The prisoners screamed behind him.
“We are orc-taken! We are orc-taken!”
The chant broke into ragged panic.
Taerel twisted, used the slope, shoved the orc off balance, and drove steel into its side. Not a killing blow, but enough. The orc stumbled, overextended, and Taerel finished it with a hard cut to the neck.
Two down. The clearing went still again.
Too still. Across the camp, beyond the firelight, something moved in the trees.
Not retreating. Waiting.
No one spoke.
The storm pressed close, swallowing sound and distance alike. Snow fell thicker now, muting the crackle of dying fires and the wet hiss of blood on heat.
Luca moved first, slow and deliberate. Not advancing so much as confirming. His eyes swept the camp in methodical arcs, tracking bodies, tracking gaps. Every corpse mattered. Every shadow mattered more.
Aymir circled the perimeter with rifle lowered but ready, boots crunching softly through churned slush. Taerel stayed nearer the prisoners, blade still out, body angled protectively despite the tremor in his injured leg. Lysana shadowed Luca, steps light, breathing controlled, her attention flicking between the treeline and the pens.
They were alive. That was the problem.
The prisoners remained lashed to the black-rooted tree, unmoving. No struggle. No relief. Just slack faces and distant eyes, some still murmuring under their breath.
Taerel frowned. “They should be breaking down. Crying. Running.”
“They’re not even looking at us,” Aymir said, lowering his voice. “Just past us. Like they’re waiting.”
Luca didn’t answer. He crouched near the firepit, the pressure in his ears deepening as he drew closer. The air felt wrong here. Not hot. Not cold. Focused.
“Something is still holding,” he said.
He brushed ash aside with his gloved hand.
The pattern underneath wasn’t random. Not scattered. The coals had been laid deliberately, curved lines spiraling inward, bone fragments placed at regular intervals like punctuation marks.
A ritual.
Luca cleared more ash.
Something pale glimmered beneath the soot.
“Hold,” he murmured.
Aymir froze mid-step. Taerel shifted his stance. Lysana’s head snapped toward the treeline.
Luca’s fingers closed around a length of gnarled wood half-buried in cinder and snow.
Carved.
Runes bit deep into the shaft, lines etched with silver that caught the firelight in dull pulses. Eye marks. Spirals. Binding chains worked so tightly into the grain they looked grown, not cut.
A staff. The pressure intensified, buzzing faintly against teeth and bone.
“That’s it,” Taerel said, fingers tightening on his scimitar hilt. “That is the anchor.”
Luca didn’t respond. His gaze lifted slowly, scanning the camp again. Too quiet. Snow shifted beyond the firelight. Not falling. Moving.
Aymir’s rifle rose a fraction. “Mugs are still warm in that tent.”
Lysana dropped to a knee. “So someone stayed behind.”
Luca released the staff and straightened.
“Or someone never left.” The sound came again. Snap. Crack. Snap.
Branches parting under deliberate weight. From beyond the outer ring, the treeline opened. He stepped into the firelight.
The war chief was immense, taller by a head than the others, grey-green skin stretched taut over scar-laced muscle. One eye was clouded white, the other black as coal. Jagged armor hung from him like pieces torn from corpses. Across his chest were trophies of bone and tooth, fingers and ears woven into cord and sinew.
In his hand, he carried a cleaver that looked more like a butcher’s tool than a blade, its edge nicked and dark with old blood.
He didn’t roar. He didn’t rush. He smiled.
Luca’s shadow rose behind him, stretching long and wrong across the snow.
Lysana stepped forward, daggers reversed in her grip.
Behind them, Aymir and Taerel didn’t hesitate. Taerel tore his injured foot free and slung his rifle. Aymir did the same. Steel flashed as scimitar and saber came free, both men charging downslope without a word.
The war chief lifted his cleaver and pointed it at Luca.
A challenge. Luca stepped to meet it. No words. Only movement.
The chief came forward in a sudden rush, cleaver sweeping low. Luca slid off the line, blades catching sparks as steel kissed steel. The impact drove through his arms and into his shoulder, nearly rocking him back a step.
He rolled with it.
Lysana cut in from the side, blades flashing for the gap beneath the arm. The chief crashed his elbow back, catching her hard enough to stagger her, then brought the cleaver down in a brutal arc meant to split Luca in half.
Luca folded under it, felt the wind of it skim his scalp, and answered with a cut across the chief’s forearm. Not deep. Deep enough to draw blood.
The war chief laughed.
A deep, wet sound.
He pressed forward, forcing Luca back step by step. Each swing carved furrows into the snow where Luca had been a heartbeat before. Luca let him overcommit, reading rhythm, waiting for the moment the weight carried too far.
Aymir hit the chief’s shoulder from the flank, saber biting shallow and skidding off armor. Taerel followed, scimitar flashing for the knee. The chief twisted, absorbed the blows, and backhanded Aymir hard enough to send him skidding through the snow.
Taerel met the cleaver head-on.
The impact drove him to one knee. Pain flared white-hot through his injured leg. He gritted his teeth and held.
Luca moved.
He stepped inside the arc, caught the cleaver wrist with his right hand, and drove both blades up under the ribs. The armor held. The breath did not.
The chief roared and wrenched free, staggering half a step.
Lysana struck again, both daggers punching beneath the collarplate. Luca surged with her, steel sliding home at the neck.
The war chief dropped to one knee, breath hissing, that terrible smile still fixed on his face.
Then he fell forward into the snow and did not rise.
Silence snapped tight again. Only the wind remained, and the hiss of dying fire.
Luca remained still for a long breath, blades loose in his hands, eyes locked on the fallen war chief. Snow settled across the corpse in thin sheets, softening edges, dulling color.
He waited for movement. None came.
Beside him, Lysana held still for the same three seconds he did, reading the same silence. She did not speak. Neither did he. Two people who had been trained to distrust quiet, checking it anyway, together, before they let themselves believe it. She straightened slowly, one hand pressed to her ribs where the chief’s elbow had caught her. She tested the breath, nodded once to herself, then turned toward the prisoners.
They were weeping now. Not loudly. Not all at once. Just small, broken sounds, like something long held finally cracking. A man slumped forward against his chains, shoulders shaking. One of the women sobbed openly, fingers digging into the bark behind her as if she needed to feel something solid to stay upright.
Taerel lowered his scimitar but didn’t sheath it. Not yet. “It’s done,” he said, voice rough. “The orcs are dead.” The words didn’t land the way they should have.
None of the prisoners ran. None rushed forward. They stayed where they were, eyes glassy, expressions slack, as if the fight had happened somewhere far away.
“They don’t look…” Lysana hesitated, searching for the word. “Present.”
Luca turned from the corpse and approached the tree slowly. The pressure in the air had eased since the chief fell, but it hadn’t vanished. It lingered, like the echo of a sound too loud to forget.
One of the prisoners looked up as he neared. A young man, hollow-eyed, grime caked into the lines of his face. For a moment, something sharp flashed behind his gaze.
Then he moved. He shoved past Taerel with a sudden burst of strength and lunged for a fallen orc’s axe half-buried in the snow. “You killed him!” the man screamed, voice tearing apart. “You murdered the blood-chief!” He swung wildly.
Luca stepped aside, calm and precise. As the axe cut empty air, Luca was already behind him. The hilt of his dagger cracked sharply against the base of the man’s skull.
The prisoner collapsed unconscious before he hit the ground. Silence snapped tight again.
Taerel swore under his breath. “What in the hells was that?”
“Influence,” Luca said. “Still there.” He turned back toward the firepit.
The staff lay where he had left it, half-buried in ash and snow, its runes pulsing faintly, stubbornly. The glow had dimmed since the chief’s death, but it was still there. Still reaching.
“That the staff caused all of this,” Aymir asked, knowing the answer. 
Luca crouched and studied the staff again. The pressure buzzed against his teeth, weaker now but persistent, like a headache that refused to fade.
Taerel struck it with his scimitar. The blade rang and skidded off clean, leaving no mark. Aymir packed snow around it until only the top showed, smothering the glow. The snow melted in seconds, not from heat but from something else entirely. Luca tried water from his pack. It beaded on the runes and ran off like oil from stone. Each attempt left the staff exactly as it had been, pulsing faintly, patient, as if it had been waiting for them to run out of ideas. 
Lysana paced, frustration on her face. 
“So what, then?” Taerel asked. “Spell for a spell, or something simpler.”
“Sometimes,” Luca said slowly, “it is the simple answer that works.”
He shrugged his pack off and rummaged through it, fingers closing around a thick burlap sack meant for travel rations. He hesitated for a heartbeat, then pulled it down over the staff, fully covering the runes.
The glow dimmed. Not extinguished. Muted.
The pressure eased enough to breathe again, but it didn’t vanish. They waited.
At first, nothing happened.
Then one of the women gasped, sharp and sudden. Another prisoner blinked hard, confusion flooding his face. A third sagged forward, retching into the snow as if waking from a nightmare that had lasted far too long.
A fourth didn’t wake at all.
He slumped sideways, eyes rolling back, breath hitching once before stopping altogether. Moments later, one of the older men stiffened, a thin sound tearing from his throat as his body seized and went still, snow already dusting his face before anyone could reach him.
Another followed moments later. One collapsed silently. The other clawed weakly at his chest, mouth working soundlessly, then sagged into the mud as whatever had been holding him together finally let go.
The spell wasn’t breaking. It was peeling. Slowly, unevenly.
“Where am I?” someone whispered.
“What happened?”
Luca stood, sack-wrapped staff in hand. “It’s weakening. Not done. But weakening.”
They moved carefully then. Lysana and Aymir cut chains one by one, slow and deliberate, watching for sudden turns. Most of the prisoners barely noticed their bonds falling away. Some collapsed outright. Others backed away, trembling, staring at their hands as if they didn’t recognize them.
When the last chain fell, Taerel lowered himself to one knee among them, keeping his voice low, steady.
“Tell us what you remember. Just the last clear thing.”
A woman spoke first. Her voice was cracked, distant. “We were near Fort Dran. Heading west to trade. There was talk of unrest, but nothing certain.” She swallowed hard. “The sky changed. Fire came from the cliffs. They hit both sides of the pass. Too fast.”
Aymir stiffened.
Another man nodded weakly. “We ran south through the canyon. Thought we’d made it. They caught us anyway.” His fingers brushed his throat. “Not with blades. With fear. With something else. We stopped thinking straight.”
“That’s enough,” Luca said gently, but firmly.
He knelt so his eyes were level with theirs. “You’re safe for now. But we need to move. Scavenge what you can. Food, blankets, anything useful. And take something small from the fallen. A ring. A token. Something to bring home.”
He rose and looked back across the ruined camp, the buried tracks, the snow already working to erase what had happened here.
“Grab what you can,” he said. “We need to put some distance from here now.”
Snow fell heavier now, soundless and steady, burying blood, bodies, and answers alike.
