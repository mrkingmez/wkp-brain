Brother Aldrek sat in a stone-walled chamber, dimly lit by a swaying lantern overhead. The desk between him and the officer was battered, stained with ink, and worn with age. An iron mace, his, lay beside a folded report.
The officer leaned forward, eyes bloodshot from lack of sleep. His voice was already thin with irritation.
Aldrek remained still, his chains quiet. “Not all truth comes from canon.” A pause. “Some was written by those too afraid to admit what they saw. I speak only what I’ve seen. What the Creator has guided me to speak.”
“You were preaching at the Stonewalk Promenade,” he said. “Talking about fire from the skies, stars turning to ash, the death of the world. Armed. In public.”
Aldrek inclined his head. “The people needed to hear it. Even in silence, the truth burns.”
The officer’s fist slammed the desk. “You were inciting a riot and preaching heresy! That filth you spout isn’t in the Book of the Creator. It’s outlawed—”
Then it happened.
A tremor ran through the floor like a held breath, followed by a dull boom that echoed through the walls. Dust trickled from a ceiling joint.
The officer stiffened, hand flying to his sword hilt. “Flames, what now?”
He glared back at Aldrek. “Stay here. Don’t move a muscle. You’re locked down well enough.”
He stormed out, muttering curses and orders as his boots vanished down the corridor.
The silence afterward was sacred.
Aldrek sat still a moment longer. He bowed his head, whispered something only he and the Creator would understand. Then the shimmer of light beneath his skin, White magic, came alive at his wrists.
He closed his eyes, focused on the tension in his shoulders. He exhaled as pain flared, bones snapping before the shackles finally gave way, leaving his wrists burning and numb. The chains clinked softly against the stone.
He took a moment and let the light from his shard wash over his hands, relieving the pain, before he reclaimed his mace with quiet reverence and stepped into the hall.
He passed through cells, empty and shadowed, while others were full of murmuring voices and rattling chains. He gave them no attention. He turned a corner and reached a wall that hadn’t seen attention in decades, a sealed stone arch with a rusted iron door half-sunken in the frame. Dust and cobwebs clung to the corners like old memories.
The hinges were fused. The bolts rusted through. And an old supply cart, splintered and overturned, had been shoved in front of it as if to forget it ever existed. Crates of spoiled grain slumped nearby, molded over with the stink of rot.
Aldrek exhaled through his nose.
He planted his mace into the ground and rolled his shoulder once. Then he grabbed the edge of the cart, muscles coiling, and hauled it aside with a growl. The old wood groaned but shifted. Crates toppled, and grain spilled in dry, stinking waves. A rat hissed and darted away into the gloom.
He knelt at the edge of the door, placed the hooked head of his mace into the rusted seam, and pushed. Metal groaned.
Again. A loud snap. A bolt cracked in half, ancient metal giving way.
He braced his foot against the wall, shifted his grip, and slammed the mace into the side once more, another pop. The door stuttered inward an inch.
Light spilled from the other side. Then the door gave, the gap no wider than it needed to be before voices echoed down the hall. And there stood Nyssa Quill, brows lifted in amused surprise, twin daggers in hand, and her silhouette outlined by torchlight behind her.
“Well, well,” she said, stepping lightly through the gap, “if it isn’t the heretic himself, tearing down prison doors like some holy hammer.”
Weslin followed, tall and composed, a sword resting across his shoulder. His smile was faint but genuine. “It’s… it’s good to see you, Brother.”
Aldrek stepped back, brushing rust from his hands. “You are late.”
Nyssa smirked. “You were early.”
Weslin turned his eyes toward the corridor. “We don’t have long. They’re distracted for now, but we’ll only get one shot at this. We need to find the keys.”
“First floor,” Aldrek nodded. “Office block. The records should be there, if they’ve kept any.”
“I saw it,” Nyssa said, already moving. “Main offices, off the east wing. We passed it during the fire run.”
Together, the three of them slipped back into the corridors of the first floor, shadows sliding through firelit halls, toward the offices where the hunt for Riven Duskwind would begin in earnest.
The Warden’s office sat at the far end of the east wing, wide double doors slightly ajar, brass handles tarnished and scraped. The hallway outside had been abandoned in the panic. Ash’s fire had driven most of the guards topside.
Aldrek shoved the door open with his shoulder, scanning the room with quick precision. The walls were lined with old maps, prison schematics, a row of ledgers on one shelf, and a desk piled with disorganized parchments. The Warden had fled in a hurry.
Nyssa was already moving, instantly starting to flip through scrolls and drawers. Weslin checked the armoire methodically, while Aldrek tried to force open a smaller lockbox behind the desk.
“Hey,” came a gravel-thick voice behind them. “You guys redecorating or just trashing the place for fun?”
Ash leaned in the doorway, breathing hard but still managing that crooked grin. His coat had burn holes in it, one shoulder scorched, and soot streaked across his cheek. A few sparks still danced off his fingertips like nervous ticks.
“We’re looking for a ledger,” Nyssa said, not looking up. “We need to find Riven.”
Ash raised an eyebrow. “Ledger, huh?”
“Keys,” Weslin muttered from across the room, searching a bookcase. “We’ll need keys to the lower levels.”
“Also keys,” Aldrek added, holding up a drawer and tossing it aside when it came up empty.
Ash snorted, running a hand through his damp hair. “Right. Because someone not on the books is in the book.” Ash pushed off the doorframe. "Keys are usually on a hook by the desk. Just saying." 
“Help,” Weslin said, calm but firm. “Find the keys. In and out, remember?”
Aldrek pried open a lower drawer near the back, the one untouched in the Warden’s rush to flee. He rummaged around and pulled out a ring of keys. Twenty thick iron ones, some labeled, most not. “I’ve got them,” he said as he shook them once. 
“Good,” Weslin said, already moving. “Let’s not waste the chaos.”
Nyssa smirked. “Bout time you embraced the chaos, pup.”
Ash laughed. “Took you long enough.”
They left the office behind and slid down the hall, keeping close to the walls. The stairwell leading down into the second level, where the real cells were, the ones carved into stone and shadow, waited like a mouth.
Nyssa glanced at the others as they reached the landing. “Alright, we hit level two. We don’t know which wing he’s in, but it’s probably isolation. Someone like Riven wouldn’t be tossed in with the general rats.”
“Agreed,” Aldrek said. “Too dangerous. They’d keep him away from voices.”
Ash’s face turned a little more serious, heat fading from his eyes. “Then we split. Weslin and I can clear the west wing,” Ash said it anyway, knowing none of them liked it.
“I’ll take east with Nyssa,” Aldrek nodded. “We move fast, quietly. First one to find him signals the others.”
“What about guards?” Weslin asked.
Ash cracked his knuckles. “Most of them are screaming upstairs. The rest? I’ve got a few tricks left.”
The stairwell was steep, narrow, and smelled of old stone, damp air, and fear. The four of them descended together, soldiers of a moment, shadows with a purpose.
Riven Duskwind was waiting below, untouched by the chaos above. Now the hunt had begun.
The corridor sloped downward, pressing in like a throat swallowing them into the dark. Weslin’s boots made barely a sound, but each step landed with too much weight, not from the stone beneath, but from the knot forming in his chest.
Ash moved beside him, casual as ever, fire-kissed eyes scanning shadows like this was just another walk in the woods. But Weslin? Weslin was trying not to breathe too loudly.
They’d split at the hallway fork, Nyssa and Brother Aldrek veering right, Weslin and Ash left. The second subterranean floor was lined with narrow cells, most sealed tightly. The air tasted like rust and stone dust. Old magic, faded fear. The prison stank.
Weslin stopped. His breath caught as his eyes snagged on the edge of the ceiling above.
There, half-obscured by soot and mineral drips, lay a delicate series of runes. They were meant to blend into the architecture. Defensive. Passive until provoked.
He swore under his breath, a whisper only the stones would hear.
“You see something?” Ash asked, pausing with a hand resting casually on the hilt at his hip.
Weslin hesitated. “Yeah. It’s a ward. I… I missed it.”
Ash looked up, frowning. “You sure?”
“That wasn’t there a moment ago,” Weslin said as he pointed, his voice tight. “It was active now, tripped,” Weslin said bitterly. “It was lit, see that faint shimmer?”
Ash squinted. “Yep. Damn. Can you untrip it?”
“No,” Weslin said quickly. “If I tried to unravel it now, I’d destabilize the layer underneath. Wards like this are stacked; if you break one out of order, the feedback could collapse the whole floor. Or worse.”
Ash gave a low whistle. “Lovely. Then let’s not trip anymore.”
“I should’ve caught it,” Weslin muttered, eyes still on the rune. “I’ve been training with Aurelia on this. She’s been helping me learn how to focus past the noise. Most Adepts don’t listen for the sound of the power itself anymore,” Weslin said, then blinked as he'd just heard himself. "Sorry. That's not, I'm doing the thing where I talk when I'm scared." But I’m still… still learning how to hear it. So it's fresh. Loud in my head.”
Ash tilted his head, listening now, not just with his ears. “You were listening too hard, maybe.”
“I was focused on her voice,” Weslin admitted. “We’ve been linking; she helps filter everything. I thought I had it.” His face tightened. “And now the link’s severed. That ward didn’t just maybe alert someone, but it muffled our net as well.”
Ash didn’t tease. Instead, he gave a short nod and reached up to pat Weslin on the shoulder. “You caught it late. You still caught it. And we’re still walking. You’re doing fine.”
They resumed their sweep, checking cells. Each held remnants of shattered lives. Bent bedframes, dried blood on old straw, scratches worn deep into the walls like tally marks from ghosts. Most cells were empty now, but the silence they left behind was a heavier kind of noise. Weslin tried not to look too long at the claw marks near one iron door, or the dried flowers someone had left near another.
“Nothing,” Ash muttered after a while. “Not even a whisper of Duskwind.”
“Nyssa and Aldrek will have had better luck,” Weslin replied, though he didn’t believe it.
Ash gave a half smile. “Well, at least we're getting more exercise than I expected.”
Weslin frowned but let it go. The fire-mage’s humor was always two steps off serious. Ash wasn’t cocky. He just refused to show when he was nervous.
Weslin envied that. They met the others at the stairwell. Nyssa shook her head. “Clean,” she said. “One junkie, two crazies, no con men.”
 Brother Aldrek crossed his arms. “Onward, then.” They descended.
The third level opened into silence. But not the kind that comforted. This was the hush of absence. The corridor stretched long and wide, arched high above with old stone ribbing like the inside of a cathedral. Iron sconces lined the walls, but no flames burned. Instead, small crystals pulsed from above, dull blue and rhythmic, like the slow heartbeat of the prison itself.
Aldrek and Nyssa took the left wing, branching off through the central corridor. Weslin and Ash swept the right.
These cells here were larger, maybe meant for long-term containment or dangerous prisoners. Each door was constructed from heavy black steel plated with ward-etched star iron, the kind of door that dared you to try. The viewing slits were narrow, fitted with a lock neither could figure out at a glance, and the hinges didn’t squeak. This floor was kept.
But most of the cells. Empty, but not all.
Aldrek paused at one, eyeing the scrapings across the walls in what might’ve once been blood. A small figure sat curled in the corner, head twitching to a rhythm only it could hear. Then it spoke.
“No air, no air, no air... but that’s all right. They said I didn’t need it where I’m going.”
He moved on.
Nyssa stepped past another, where a pair of eyes gleamed out from under a ragged blanket. Then the blanket shifted, and three Joblins hissed as they scurried backward into the dark. Small, hook-nosed eyes glowing like lantern ticks.
One gave a cheerful wave.
Nyssa blinked. “That’s a new one.”
Further down, one cell held something massive, a hulking beast covered in plated bone and scars, chained at four points. It breathed heavily, as if even sleep took effort. She couldn't tell if it was snoring or growling.
Aldrek glanced inside. “Orth.”
Nyssa looked over. “You’ve seen one before?”
“Fought one once. Preferred not to again.”
But none of the cells held what they were looking for.
Not yet.
The team regrouped near the center of the third floor, silent confirmation passing between them. Empty.
Ash looked toward the stairwell leading down again.
“Well,” he said with a small smirk, “time for the good stuff.”
They began to descend the stairwell to the fourth level, and halfway down—
BOOM. A pressure in the air followed, heavy and deliberate. The second explosion sounded overhead, deeper, throatier, more intentional. Dust rained from above, and the walls trembled slightly beneath their boots.
Weslin jumped. “That wasn’t a collapse, right?”
Ash casually dusted his coat. “That one was mine.”
They stared.
He shrugged. “I figured we might need a little more breathing room on the way out, so I rigged up a few more gifts for our friends above. Just enough to make‘em sweat.”
Nyssa arched a brow. “That wasn’t exactly a candle pop.”
Ash smiled. “I like big distractions. And I’ve still got one more surprise on the timer. Should go off any moment—”
A slow, heavy footstep echoed behind them.
They turned.
A man stepped from the corridor shadows, huge, wrapped in dark leather reinforced with polished bone plating. His arms were exposed and crisscrossed with glowing veins of red magic that pulsed just beneath the skin like lava in stone. His presence filled the corridor like heat.
“I was told someone would come,” he said calmly, voice like crackling coals. “You’re later than I thought.”
Ash’s smile vanished.
The brute continued. “Lord Merrick was clear. Riven Duskwind doesn’t leave this place. And neither do those who try to take him.” He raised a hand to the small glowing gem embedded in his ear. “They’re here.”
A low crack echoed from somewhere above. Then—
BOOM.
The walls rumbled. A second passed. Then came a roaring gust of heat, distant, but violent. The brute flinched slightly.
Screams echoed throughout the area. They all heard, “—we’re—cut off—fire’s spread—second floor is—”
Another voice screamed through a wall horn, “We can’t make it to the stairs! There’s fire everywhere…. we’re sealed in.”
Then... nothing. Silence.
He growled, smacking the gem. “Damn it.”
Ash stepped forward, flames licking across his knuckles. “Sounds like your backup’s going to be late.”
“You did this?” the brute asked.
Ash didn’t flinch. “Third surprise. I might’ve set fire to your artillery shed.”
“Clever,” the brute said with a cold grin. “Let’s see if your fire holds.”
He lifted both arms, and blue flame erupted from his palms.
Ash let out a little laugh. “Now this is interesting.” He stepped between his team and the threat, flexing his hands until both were wrapped in fire, his natural, crimson-orange blaze crackling around him like a living aura.
“You three, finish the mission,” Ash said, not looking back.
“Alone?” Weslin said.
Ash smiled faintly. “I need the practice.”
No more words.
The two men lunged, fire to fire, the air itself twisting between them as the hallway lit with violent color.
The others turned and ran.
The fourth floor felt like descending into a vault buried beneath time.
Everything here was tight. Precision-forged. These vault doors were seamless slabs of star-forged iron, reinforced with runes. There were no torches here either, only soft white glowstones set into the ceiling, casting sterile light.
They moved slowly now. One wrong door could trigger a dozen alarms. But as they passed, cell after cell revealed men and women too broken to speak, too dangerous to be allowed light. Down here, even the glowstones seemed reluctant, their light thin and flat, as if they had learned not to show too much of what they were illuminating. 
Then Aldrek stopped. “Here,” he said. 
Nyssa moved to the cell he pointed to, gaze narrowing. Three prisoners inside. One was tall and broad, arms like quarried stone, with the stillness of a man who had learned that moving first was usually a mistake. The second, delicate, smooth-faced, with an arched brow and a gaze that missed nothing. Possibly female. Possibly not. Definitely dangerous. And the third…
A smirk, even before he finished looking up. Dark hair. Clever eyes. Lean frame with the look of a man who hasn’t had a full meal in a while. “Well,” Riven Duskwind said. “Took you long enough.”
Nyssa smiled despite herself. “Had to make an entrance.”
Weslin blinked. “This is him?”
“That's him,” she said, still smiling. 
Behind them, a noise of creaking boots. Weslin looked up.“Uh… is that guard supposed to be there?”
At the far end of the hall stood a man in a prison guard’s uniform, turned away from them, shoulders hunched like he hadn’t heard. Aldrek stepped forward and charged, swift, silent.
He tackled the man in one swift motion, sending both to the ground in a clash of armor and curses. The guard rolled with it, groaning. “Bloody hell, get off me, you ogre.”
The hood slipped back. Nyssa sucked in a breath. “Dain?!”
