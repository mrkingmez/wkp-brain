The snow had crusted hard overnight, its surface gleaming like hammered glass under the late morning sun. Pines swayed in the wind above Luca’s team as they picked their way through the narrowing forest trail, hooves crunching frost, every breath steaming. The path curled like a blade toward the looming mountain pass, an ancient road barely visible beneath the press of winter’s slow reclaiming.

Luca led, his silhouette stark against the pale glow of the day, cloak trailing behind like the shadow of a noose. He said nothing. He rarely did. Behind him, Lysana adjusted her reins with unconscious precision, the tips of her fingers red but steady, her posture alert. Aymir and Taerel followed last, their horses moving in sync, silent but for the low squeaks of saddle leather and the wind’s thin whistle through trees stripped bare.

Taerel shifted in the saddle. Not restless. Just counting something the rest of them couldn't see. But today, his body twitched with the tension of something brewing under his skin. The chill of the mountain air stole the last warmth from their bones.

“I’ve been thinking,” he said aloud, tone conversational. “About something I can make.”

Aymir, riding just to his left, cocked a brow. “The enchanted band?”

Taerel nodded. “That bracelet Luca uses to talk to the others. I think I can replicate its function. Maybe not perfectly, but enough to keep us connected.”

“You’re going to have to tell them,” Aymir said, eyes forward. “About what you are.”

Taerel hesitated, then sat up straighter. “Luca opened up last night. Told us who he really was. Where he came from. That… means something. I think it’s time I do the same.”

Aymir gave a small nod. “Then do it right. I’ve got your back.”

Without another word, Taerel urged his horse up to Luca’s side. The wind tugged at his cloak, and his pale gloves twitched as he steeled himself.

Luca didn’t glance over. “Something wrong?”

“I need to examine your bracelet,” Taerel said. 

Luca hesitated, then unfastened the band and passed it over. “Don’t lose it.”

“I’m not Lysana,” Taerel said with a smile. 

“Careful with that," Lysana called from behind, the amusement clear in her voice. "You'd notice the quiet before long.” 

“Touché,” Taerel said with a grin.

“I need to understand its enchantments. Structure. Runes. Everything, so that I can copy them,” said Taerel.

Luca’s eyes flicked sideways. “You can read runes?”

“Better than most.” Taerel exhaled. “Just… let me show you something.”

Taerel scanned the ridge and the road below, listening. Only when nothing stirred did he pull his horse to a stop. The others reined theirs in. He reached up and unclasped something near his temple. His fingers hesitated just a moment, then he removed the mask. It didn’t slide off with ceremony. No glow, no ripple. One moment, Taerel wore the calm brown-eyed face they knew. The next, it was gone.

His skin darkened, shifting to a deep midnight black, the color of sky before a storm. His eyes turned bone-white, pupil-less, lit softly from somewhere that had nothing to do with the sun. His hair unfurled, long and ashen, tied back into a tight warrior’s tail that hung over one shoulder like spun silver rope.

Lysana drew a breath. “By the stars…”

“I’m Zhar’ai,” Taerel said quietly.

For a heartbeat, no one spoke, nobody moved. The wind hissed through the trees, tugging at cloaks and reins.

Lysana blinked once, eyes wide. “You could have warned a girl before you turned into a walking night sky,” she muttered, half under her breath.

Aymir’s jaw clenched, then eased. “Told you he was dramatic,” he said quietly, though his gaze never left his brother’s face.

Luca did not flinch. He studied Taerel as if weighing a tactical report, not a confession. “I didn’t raise it sooner,” Taerel said, not looking away. “I am cautious. Caution keeps people alive. Trust comes later. In our line of work, information is currency. Secrets are capital.”

Taerel paused, then gave a half-bow from the saddle. “One of the last. My tribe could read and write runes in ways no one else could, feel their meanings like music. But the Brotherhood hunted us for decades. Most of my people are dead. I didn’t know if I ever could show you… or anyone. I’ve spent most of my life running, not just from them. From being seen at all. You get hunted long enough, you stop knowing what trust even feels like. ”

He glanced toward Luca, his mask now tucked away, his true face open beneath the sky.

“Trust isn't something I was taught to give.” He let out a slow breath through his nose. “Not when everyone I’ve known paid in blood for what we are. I don’t know if this is the right time, or even if it’s safe… but I’m tired of hiding. And I think you two deserve the truth.”
“Then how are you guys brothers?” asked Lysana.

Aymir answered, “What gave it away?” He gave a short chuckle. “Short version.” He kept his eyes ahead. “When our father helped hide a few of his tribe, his mother was among them. We were being hunted, as well. When our father fell for his mother, it was double the pain. Things grew tense for our family. Yet, we all escaped. Taerel was raised in hiding, behind stories and shadow.”

“I show you all this because we need this,” Taerel replied. “You heard what happened to the other team. Our communications could fail at the most inopportune time. This isn’t just about trust anymore. It’s survival.”

“I thought you called each other brother because you have been together so long, but you are really brothers,” Lysana said.

Taerel smiled faintly. “Aymir got the looks. I got the ancient abilities and an unfortunate habit of second-guessing every room I walk into. ” He looked down to examine the bracelet. He turned it over with reverence. “Arcanite core. Aether-harmonic bindings. Clean work,” Taerel said. “Whoever made this understood resonance.”

He tapped a sigil near the base. “This here is the nexus rune. It links with the partner band, one-way mental projection. This is some good work.”

He handed it back. “I am going to need some material, but I think I could recreate it for us.”

Luca accepted it silently, slipping it back on. “I will keep my eye out.”

They rode on. Taerel checked the seal on his gauntlet again, though it had not shifted.

The sun rose higher, snow blindingly bright on the slope ahead. Around midday, the trees began to thin into wind-scarred pines twisted by years of storms. The ridge beckoned just ahead, its rocky crown giving way to a distant haze of smoke curling faintly into the sky.

Taerel and Aymir rode side by side, the muffled clop of hooves subdued beneath the soft hush of wind-swept snow. Lysana dropped back to their pace, twisting slightly in her saddle to glance at Taerel with an amused look.

“Were you planning on keeping your little secret to yourself the whole time?” she said casually, glancing ahead toward the thinning trees.

Taerel blinked. “You mean the whole… terrifying midnight-black, rune-carved soul-wielder look?”

She smirked. “Yeah, that one.”

Aymir chuckled. “He used to keep the mask on in his sleep. We thought he was shy. Turns out he’s just worried about making people nervous.”

“It’s more than that,” Taerel said, his tone sobering. “The Brotherhood hunted me, my entire life. You can see that sharing is not in my best interest. My people, the Zhar’ai, were hunted to the brink of extinction. All because they wanted to control our ability.”

“Have you always looked like a spirit from a midnight storybook?” Lysana asked.

“All my life,” Taerel replied with a smirk. The expression did not quite reach his eyes.

Ahead of them, Luca’s voice carried back through the cold. “Keep talking if you have to,” he said, not looking over, “but keep your ears open. We are close to the pass.”

Aymir snorted softly. “See? That is Luca’s way of saying he is glad you took the mask off.”

“And your mask?” asked Lysana.

“A gift, from one of the families we helped,” answered Aymir

She gave him a playful shove with her shoulder. “You’re weird, Ty. I like weird. Just don’t go glowing in the dark without warning.”

Taerel gave a small shrug. “Because fear spreads fast. And people who get nervous make mistakes. I’ve been safer behind a lie. Plus, I just met you guys. When you're hunted all your life, you're a little hesitant to share your secrets.”

Lysana leaned forward in the saddle, her expression thoughtful.

That’s when the smell hit them.

“Tar,” Aymir muttered. “Smoke. Not just a cook fire either, it's big.”

Taerel sniffed once. “Forge smoke as well. Heavy.”


They crested the final ridge as the smoke became more visible. Luca raised a hand and slowed. They crept to a low ridge, careful to keep their silhouettes below the rise. From there, the trees fell away into a snow-covered basin. From this height, they could see far across the wide valley basin.

And in the basin, nestled in smoke and frost, was another war camp.

A sea of movement lay below, crude orcish tents, timber palisades reinforced with scavenged iron, siege gear lashed to beasts of burden. Goblins darted between structures like rats, carrying bundles of scrap and weapons. Larger orcs, musclebound, brutal, stood watch on makeshift towers or hunched near bonfires, sharpening axes. Worgs, massive wolf-like beasts with curling fangs and intelligent eyes, paced in tight circles inside their pens.

At the camp’s center stood a totem, tall, made from a twisted spire of bone and rusted blades, bound together with sinew and marked with fresh blood. Lysana’s hand tightened on her reins at
the sight. “It smelled wrong, a tang of unnatural stillness as well,” Aymir said. 

Before it sat a hunched shaman in robes of stretched skin, muttering to himself as his staff pulsed with an eerie violet glow. Next to that totem, protruding from the earth like a black thorn, was a staff nearly identical to the one Luca’s other team had found in the previous orc encampment. This one was larger. Sharper. Meaner.

A chill slipped down Taerel’s spine. “That’s the same design.” Taerel's hand had gone still on his reins without him choosing to stop it. One had been bad. Two meant something had changed, or was changing, and nobody had told them yet. 

Luca went still. “They’re connected, maybe.”

Below, smoke wafted from the battered walls of the pass’s fortification. Black scorch marks marred the stone, and several towers were partially collapsed. Signal fires burned on the upper parapets. Not many. But enough to show someone was still alive inside.

Luca’s voice dropped. “We need to fall back before they spot us.”

They backed down quickly, following a ledge that dipped into thick pine cover. Once they were safely back from the ridge, Luca pulled the bracelet back onto his wrist and pressed two fingers against its inner side, eyes closing.

“Harmon,” he said in a low voice, “this is Luca. Confirm the visual of a major orc encampment outside the pass’s gates. Fort appears damaged but manned. Orcs number fifty-plus, with goblins, joblins, and at least one worg pack. Central totem matches a known hostile artifact. Will provide full report shortly.”

He removed his fingers from the band, exhaled slowly, and turned to the group.

“Alright,” Luca said. “Now we get every detail. Spread out. Count them. Watch their patrols. Map the damn place if you have to.”

Lysana gave a nod, already unpacking her spyglass. “I’ll check elevation, vantage, and watch lines. Maybe find a flank route.”

Aymir pulled his long scope from the saddle. “I’ll follow the supply flow. See how they’re managing rations and movement.”

Taerel pulled out a small writing board, glancing up at Luca. “I’ll look for weak points. Hidden paths. Open perimeters. And how their units move together.” He observed that their patrols held wide, unbroken arcs, as if nothing above them was worth fearing. 

They held to the tree line, using each snowbank to mask their silhouettes, moving in short, deliberate surges between cover. Lysana lay prone in the frost, elbow dug into a flat stone, taking in the camp. “North wall’s loose,” she whispered, talking to herself. “Gap near the cooking pits. Could run a team through there if they’re quick and quiet.”

Aymir’s voice came low, from the brush nearby. “From what I can tell, I see they have gotten three main patrol units rotating in an hourglass pattern. They’re trained. Not tribal chaos. And... I’m seeing at least two dozen joblins doing repairs.”

Taerel murmured, “There’s a break in their southern perimeter. Weak slope, covered in scree. Would take a climber with some guts to exploit it, but it’s there.”

Luca crouched beside a rock outcropping, watching a pair of orc sentries switch shifts. Their pattern was exact, military even. That chilled him more than their numbers.

“They’re too organized,” he muttered. “Orcs don’t do siege warfare. They don’t build palisades and wait. They charge. They break. They don’t starve people out.”

Lysana’s voice came back, flat and certain. “They’ve changed. Or someone’s changed them.”

Luca’s eyes narrowed at the swirling campfires below. The siege lines moved like a slow tide around the battered fort. He traced the formation with a gloved finger, lips tightening.

“We could break this,” he muttered, barely audible. “Slip down under the cover of the dark. Kill their leader. Scatter the worgs. If I get to the shaman, cut the head from the snake, it might be enough.”

“No.” Came from behind him, Lysana didn’t raise her voice.

Luca turned, brows raised.

She crouched beside him, chin forward, jaw tight. “That’s not our directive. We’re here to gather information, not start a war. If we engage now, without knowing what else is waiting in those hills, we risk everything, including the people inside that fort and the city.”

He studied her. Her tone wasn’t challenging; it was reminding. Grounding.

She added, “Give us time. A day. Maybe two. We’ll learn their routines. Their weaknesses. Then, when the order comes, we won’t be guessing.”

Luca stared back out at the torch-lit ring of tents. Then he nodded once, a sharp dip of his chin.

“You’re right,” he said quietly. “We don’t strike blind.” 

“You aren’t alone on this one. You have a team. Let's work as one, okay.” Lysana said with a smile, and a true smile in his direction.

Luca nodded once. He looked away first.

By nightfall, the group pulled back again, setting up a low-profile camp a short ride from the ridge, hidden among fallen logs and a dense copse of trees. Snow fell in slow flakes, catching in their lashes and furred cloaks.

They gathered close around a small ember fire, just enough to warm their hands.

“Here’s what we know,” Luca said, voice low. “Orcs, goblins, joblins, and trained worgs. One shaman. Siege tactics. More than we first counted. Seventy to eighty total.”

Taerel’s fingers brushed the runes along his rifle once, then fell still. Somewhere out in the dark, an orc barked a short command. The sound carried thin on the wind, then faded.

Luca waited until the silence settled again before continuing. “Multiple tents for storage, crates, tar, and ironworks. No spellcasters beyond the shaman, but I can’t confirm his capability.”

Taerel added, “Totem in the center. Another of those staves. Larger. Probably more powerful.”

Aymir chimed in, “Fort looks like hell, but it’s holding. Fires on the ramparts mean survivors. Probably trying to wait this out.”

Lysana blew warm breath into her cupped palms. “That means they’re not pushing until something changes. Until reinforcements arrive. Or until their food runs out. Either way, we have time. But not much.”

Luca nodded grimly. “We’ll stay the night. Two-person rotations. One is keeping an eye on the ridge. One is watching our camp. Rotate every two hours. That way, we get rest without losing our edge.”

Taerel stretched out the shift schedule, and they divvied up in silence.

“I’ll send the full report to Harmon,” Luca said. “Then we wait. See what he says.”

He stood, walked back to the edge of their campsite, and activated the bracelet.

“This is Luca. Full recon complete. Hostiles number seventy to eighty, more than our first count. The fort is under siege but holding. The city is largely in ruins. Orcs are unusually organized. Camp includes orcs, goblins, joblins, worgs, and a central shaman with a matching staff. An estimated three weak points for infiltration. Will hold position until further instruction. Expect a detailed map and cycles in follow-up.”

Luca sat quietly, one boot planted in the snow, fingers toying with the edge of his scarf. When the voice of young Weslin appeared in his head, Weslin relayed the message from Harmon. The message was sharp and to the point. ‘What can you tell me about the fort? Are the defenders alive? Anyone left in the city? Can you get word to them?’ The connection was narrow, strained, thin, and distant. Enough for words. Not enough for distinctness.

The message burned with urgency. It wasn’t a command, it was a plea wrapped in the voice of a man trying to keep a war from boiling over. Luca tensed. He turned and gathered the team without hesitation.

“Harmon wants a clearer picture of the fort,” he said once they were together. “Survivors. Defensive status. The city just outside, what’s left of it, if anyone is left in it. He wants us to send them a message, let them know we're out here. We need to find a way to do that, or create one.”

"Right, and if we blunder in announcing ourselves, we get them killed before they even know there's help coming," Aymir said, arms folded. “We have to get it right, we have one shot at this.”

Lysana’s gaze flicked toward the ridge. “We will not blunder,” she said. “We find the right way in, or we do not go at all.”

Luca’s jaw tightened, the lines at the corners of his eyes deepening. “Which is why we do this carefully,” he said. “No chances we do not have to take.”

"Smoke patterns, maybe," Lysana said, her eyes moving toward the ridge. "Light signals. Or we get a runner close enough to throw something over the wall without getting seen." 

“We’re not getting that close,” Luca said. “Not until we know every damn rock and ledge around that place.”

Lysana nodded. “We’ll scout for access routes. Learn the area around us, but we should wait for light to do this, making it a bit easier to find them.”

“Good,” Luca said, his voice low and focused. “We will maintain surveillance on the camp overnight still. Reporting any change, any shift, anything. While we still look for alternate entry points to the city and the fort. We need to know everything.”

Taerel adjusted his pack. “And if we find a way in?”

“Don’t go in,” Luca said sharply. “Not yet. Mark it. Sketch it. Bring it back. We get that intel to Harmon, and he’ll decide. Also, be mindful. We don’t know how deep their patrols run.”

“Got it,” Aymir said. 

Luca watched them break into motion, their boots scraping the snow, their cloaks drawing tight, their eyes already scanning the treelines and ridge contours.

He lingered for a moment as the wind howled across the rise, pulling at his cloak like a ghost’s hand, and then began to follow. It did take them a long time to return to the ridge. The four of them pressed low against the rocks just beneath the ridgeline, watching the siege camp through narrow slits in the frost-bitten trees. Below, the sprawling orc encampment still moved like a living beast, fires flickering, shadows trudging between tents, worgs pacing alongside iron-banded cages, and the guttural sounds of low, savage speech echoing off the mountainside.

“Do any of you see a good vantage point to get a better view?” Luca asked.

Taerel’s eyes narrowed as he scanned the ridgeline beyond their current perch. A fallen pine angled against a split boulder about thirty yards upslope caught his attention, not much, but just enough to conceal movement if they kept low.

“There,” he whispered, pointing with two fingers. “That ridge curves south. If we loop behind it, we might catch a sharper angle above the city, while still having an eye on the enemy’s camp.”

Luca gave a tight nod. “Let’s move.”

Lysana was already moving, hunched low, her boots crunching softly. “It’s steep, but doable,” she muttered. “We’ll have to belly-crawl the last stretch. Anything more upright, we’ll silhouette ourselves.”

The snow was knee-deep in places, crusted and noisy. Each step had to be deliberate, each movement measured.

Twice, Taerel reached out, steadying a branch before it could dump a sheet of snow across their backs. Lysana stayed ahead, eyes flitting between ridgeline breaks and where the smoke from the fort curled faintly upward.

As they climbed, the wind shifted. A distant orc horn called from the camp below, long, low, and guttural.

Everyone froze.

No movement came in response. No footfalls, no shouting. Just the wind again, and the distant crackle of frozen wood in some unseen fire. A warning call, maybe. Or just an echo of routine.

Aymir gave a low whistle once they were closer. “This spot’s tighter,” he said, crouching behind a jut of rock. “We’ll see what we need from here.”

They settled behind the new ridge, breath tight, eyes locked to the devastation ahead.

From this new angle, they could see farther than before, past the broken outer wall, down into the remnants of the city that hugged the base of the pass. Smoke still curled up from blackened structures, most of them gutted or collapsed from the initial attack. At a glance, Taerel guessed that at least seventy percent of the buildings were reduced to charred skeletons.

Luca felt something in his chest loosen and twist all at once. Through his looking glass, Luca could make out survivors huddled within the stone arches of the city’s old chapel, its roof miraculously untouched. Inside, flickering lantern light betrayed movement, small, slow, deliberate. He thought it had to be civilians who were injured and afraid. “They’re holed up in the chapel,” he murmured. “Still people alive down there.”

“I count about thirty people, for what I can make out,” Aymir said to Lysana, who was standing beside him. Her fingers were white-knuckling a small slate where she’d scratched notes with charcoal. 

Taerel, crouched nearby, added, “Saw a few inside the city wall, ten maybe. Forward observers, probably. Helping civilians. I saw a pair dragging someone into cover.”

“And the fort?” Luca asked.

“They’ve got some kind of auxiliary archer group on top of the wall,” Aymir said. “They might be able to hit targets in the kill zone if they had to, but they’re spread thin.”

Lysana gave a low grunt. “That city should’ve been wiped clean. But it wasn’t. And the orcs haven’t breached. Why? This doesn’t make any sense.”
“They’re being contained,” Taerel said. “Someone wants them alive, or they just don’t care. Look, you can see they shifted targets.”

“There has to be something else at play here,” Luca muttered.

Then his eyes fell back to the center of the orc camp. Looking for any kind of answer, but all their eyes were on the twisted black totem crowned with scavenged bones. “And that staff,” Luca said 

“A better version,” Taerel said. “No doubt. And it’s active.”

“We need to report,” Luca said. 

They backed away from the ridge, boots crunching softly in snow, breath steaming. Once they reached a safe distance, Luca lifted the bracelet to his lips and whispered the report, concise, tactical, and thorough.

Again, Luca pictured Weslin in his head, then he gave his report. "There are civilian survivors within the city. We have confirmed movement within the chapel. Defensive perimeter remains intact, fort not yet breached. Archer detachments observed on the wall and auxiliary units inside the perimeter. Damage to the city is estimated at 70% or more gone. A staff similar to the previous artifact is located in the center of the enemy camp, under shaman control.
Will begin phased surveillance now. Requesting guidance on potential communication with survivors and whether reinforcements are being prepared. Will report again soon."

“Do any of you see a way to get them a message of some kind?” Luca asked.

“Maybe a note on an arrow from here, then we move?” Lysana asked.

“The orc might see that, don’t you think?” Luca replied. 

“Not sure, but best I got, boss,” Lysana said. 

“All right, let's see if we can think of something else before we go with that,” Luca said. “Time to start the two-man teams rotation. One on overwatch at the ridge, one on camp guard. Two rest, two hours each rotation.”

“Who pairs with whom?” Lysana asked.

“You tell me,” Luca said. “I trust you.” He was already turning away.

“I’ll take Taerel with me for first watch,” she said without hesitation.

Aymir nodded. “Luca and me then.”

“Sounds good,” Luca replied. “Be sharp. I want eyes on everything,” Luca said. Aymir and Luca started to make their trip back to their camp, while Taerel and Lysana started setting up a more permanent observation position.

Above them, the mountains stood still. Below them, the siege dragged on. And in the space between shadow and silence, a cold wind howled like prophecy through the trees.
