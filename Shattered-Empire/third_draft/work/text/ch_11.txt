Harmon had paid generously, and it showed. Three suites, spacious by any measure, each held a heavy four-poster bed and thick woolen rugs that softened both step and sound, their intricate swirling knots and geometric patterns speaking of distant weavers long forgotten beneath countless passing feet.
The beds themselves were quite a surprise for a town this small. Coarse but clean linen sheets, thick woolen blankets, and a fur throw folded neatly at the foot promised warmth against the cold. Above them, heavy canopies of dark carved wood bore vines and gryphons worn smooth by age. A faint trace of lavender clung to the bedding, subtle but unmistakable, a small luxury amid an inn that would otherwise have smelled of ale and smoke.
Each suite opened into a private sitting area gathered around a modest hearth. The stone walls were thick enough to mute all but the loudest sounds below, and brass lanterns cast a steady golden light that played across old tapestries stitched with heroic hunts and legendary duels, their colors dim but their stories intact.
Harmon had claimed the central suite, its wide windows giving him a view of both alley and avenue, and set up a crude command post on the main table, maps unrolled, reports from contacts in the city pinned with brass daggers. Aurelia and Weslin occupied the adjoining suite, which was quieter and more isolated, the perfect place for what they needed. 
Aurelia sat cross-legged near the hearth in her and Weslin’s room, a small leather pouch open before her, its contents carefully arranged on a low stool: a sprig of dried holly, a shallow bowl of water, a simple brass mirror no bigger than her palm. None of it was necessary for her magic, not truly, but it helped beginners to focus.
Across from her, Weslin sat stiffly in a chair facing her. In his hands, he clutched the smooth, palm-sized shard Harmon had given him earlier, the faintest golden shimmer coiled inside it like smoke. His blue-gray tunic was freshly laundered but wrinkled from travel, and his boots still carried a stubborn dust from the road. His hair, dark and shaggy, fell into his eyes as he listened intently.
Neavin and his bonded spirit, shimmer barely visible, appeared like a distortion in the air, a ripple of presence, were in the same room with them. Not a separate thing. Not a summoned creature. It was Neavin, and Neavin was it, a perfect, indivisible union. The spirit hummed faintly at the edges of perception, like a low, comforting chord that filled the room without ever needing to be heard aloud. He was here to listen and see what he could learn as well.
"You need to understand something before we begin, Weslin," Aurelia said, her voice even but firm. "This isn't a game. This isn't a story you heard growing up. Every mistake you make could hurt someone, or worse, yourself.” She paused to take a drink of water.
“You’re behind where most students start,” Aurelia said evenly. “That doesn’t mean you can’t catch up. It does mean you’ll be pushed harder than you’re ready for. You will not be rushed. We go as fast as you progress. Understood?”
Weslin’s fingers fumbled the shard, nearly dropping it before he caught it with a muffled curse. His ears flushed red, but he puffed up his chest. “I get it, ma’am,” Weslin said quickly. “I won’t waste the chance. I’ll work harder. You’ll see.”
Aurelia paused, studying him. The eagerness in his face was almost disarming, bright, open, unscarred by failure or fear. She let a small, reluctant smile touch her lips. "It's not about the amount of energy, Weslin.” Her voice stays even. “Control is. Discipline. Focus mental strength.” She paused. “You'll need to be sharper with your mind than Harmon will make you with a sword." 
Weslin straightened his back, nodding fiercely. "Yes, Ma'am. I’ll... I'll be sharp. Like a blade. Like a real Adept." His boots tapped an erratic rhythm against the table leg.
Aurelia reached out, gently but firmly pressing a hand to his bouncing knee, stilling it. "First lesson," she said softly. "Stillness is your first weapon. Still mind, still body. You can't catch a river in a closed fist. You have to be the bank that guides it."
He nodded again, more carefully this time. Around them, the fire popped once, and beyond the thick stone walls, the low murmur of their companions moving about their rooms filtered in like the pulse of a heartbeat. He closed his eyes and focused on staying still, listening to the rhythms of the world around him. 
Neavin followed suit. He closed his eyes and stilled his breathing. He could feel his bond with Veyrith, like a cord connected to him, not knowing where, but also everywhere. He went deeper on the cord, feeling a slow, patient thrum of trust, then another cord, this one of loyalty, and it was tied firm around his soul. Hearing thoughts traveling through the cords, Veyrith's thoughts were not like a man’s; they were filled with scents and instincts, flashes of memory that brushed against his mind like a passing mist. 
“Cold Floor, Too Still. Humans chitter like mice. No threat. Safe here. Need to run soon. Hunt soon.”
Then the following thoughts changed, “Neavin is pack. Bound, not caged. Chose this. Chose him. I am free.” 
The spirits’ memories rippled once more to the past, Blurred images of running wild in his mind. A calm filled Neavin. Beneath the whirl of animal thoughts and the steady thrum of loyalty, Neavin’s mind sharpened with a quiet sense of gratitude. He was glad he had stayed. He had come into the room to listen and observe, and somewhere in Aurelia's quiet instruction to Weslin, he had found something that applied equally to him. The stillness was not emptiness. It was space. 
If mastering the art of mental discipline could strengthen the bridge between him and his bondmate, this could make their bond not just a tether, but a true unity. Then every word she spoke was a weapon he would gladly learn to wield. 
Aurelia raised a hand, graceful and slow, and spoke in her warm, patient tone.
"Now, to move to the training lesson, today is about resonance," she said, her voice like a balm. "Before you call power, before you direct it... You must first listen to it."
She placed the mirror flat on the stool and gestured. "Focus not on yourself, Weslin. Focus on the world around you. Let the mirror catch it. Let the water reflect it. Let the spirit between you and the world hum with it. Feel that pulse."
Neavin shifted slightly, a wave of warmth rolling through the room, not overpowering, but undeniably there.
Aurelia let the quiet work, the mirror catching candlelight, the water holding it. After a time, she asked, gentle but direct, "What do you think I asked you to do, and why?”
Weslin looked up, “To teach me how to focus?”
“No, what else do you think it could be?”
Weslin’s knee started to bounce, heat rising in his cheeks. “I, I do not know.” Aurelia stilled his leg with a hand. “You do. Try again. What did you take from both exercises, and how does it help you learn?”
He swallowed, then found the words. "To let the noise pass without taking me with it. To notice what is there, even when no one speaks.” His leg began to bounce faster.
Aurelia walked by, putting her hand on his knee to stop it from bouncing, “Calm yourself, remember lesson one. Deep breath. Think. What happened to you at the feast was this,” Aurelia said calmly. “You felt the world too close…” Another pause. “This teaches you to be still while it moves.”
Weslin closed his eyes and tried to clear his head like before. When he opened them again, he was in a new state of mind. He was still nervous, yet different. “I was overwhelmed by everything going on.”
“Good, so now take that and ask why I would ask you to do these simple exercises?”
Again, he took another deep breath, and instead of panic, he didn’t know the answer; he took his time and let his mind come to a thought. “Because there will always be a world around me, so I must be able to focus on the world out there? As well as hearing the connections beyond sound.”
“Very good. Yes, we are different from most other adepts because when we are surrounded by the very thing, we can manipulate it. Yes, it can and mostly is overwhelming. So we need a way to focus, keep doing this exercise until the next time we can meet. Understood?”
“Yes, ma’am.”
“Good, I will try to meet with you every other day, that way it gives you time to work on the exercise. Mission will dictate the rest.”
Weslin got to his feet, “Yes, Ma’am.”
Garr filled the doorframe without knocking. "Harmon needs you in the other room," he said. He did not wait for an answer. 
Aurelia inclined her head, and Garr vanished. Weslin glanced up. “You knew he would come,” Weslin didn’t ask. He knew.
“I keep a thread open, I heard the thoughts before he moved,” she said.
Neavin rose, Veyrith a weight of feathers on his shoulder. “If you do not mind, I would like to attend these lessons when I can. The stillness steadies the bond. It keeps me from slipping too far into her and losing my own edge.”
She smiled, “No, I do not mind, just know I am not doing this for you, but you are welcome to keep coming.”
He bows his head, “Thank you.” She just nods back, still with a smile on her face.
Weslin and Neavin stepped out together, and Aurelia gathered her things. “He is going to be good indeed,” she thought, then a quieter truth, “and good may not be enough tonight.” She turned toward Harmon’s suite.
When she arrived, she noticed the air inside Harmon’s suite was dense with the smell of wax and parchment. The lavender scent from the bedding was a strange counterpoint to the grim business at hand. Nyssa, Ash, Garr, and Brother Aldrek were already in there, sitting around the wide oak table. The brass lanterns above cast long, shifting shadows across their faces. Maps and loose reports lay spread out between mugs of untouched ale and a half-finished loaf of travel bread. Beyond the windows, the sky had been pressing lower all afternoon, the kind of grey that meant weather before morning. Harmon stood at the head of the table, one hand resting firmly on the maps, the other tucked behind his back, his jaw set in a hard, thoughtful line.
He swept his gaze across them, not hurried, but deliberate, as if weighing each of them on invisible scales before he spoke.
“All right,” Harmon said, he looked across them all once more. “We move at dusk. Scout first. Final preparations after. Then Dain makes his attempt.”
He tapped a dagger against the map, pointing to several rough circles he had drawn. Exits, alleys, and other points of interest. "First, Nyssa, Ash," he said, locking eyes with each of them in turn, "your job is simple, but critical. We need eyes on this city. I want a proper map in my hand before nightfall, not the butchered sketches we bought off the caravan we passed. Find the true entrances, the choke points, and patrol routes if you can. I want streets, guard posts, sewer accesses if they exist."
Ash gave a quick, sharp nod, already shifting on his boots like he was ready to run through the wall. Nyssa just grinned, twirling a dagger once between her fingers before letting it vanish into a hidden sheath on her belt.
"And the prison itself," Harmon said, voice low. “Shift changes, watch posts, guard count. In and out, fast. No heroics. If this turns loud, I want exits, not excuses." 
Nyssa’s grin flashed, Ash’s jaw set, and for a heartbeat, they shared a look that said neither would be the one to blink first.
“Try not to burn half the quarter just to make a point,” Nyssa muttered, rolling her shoulder. “I know about you.”
Ash’s smile was all teeth. "Try not to pick the wrong pocket and get us all killed. Know about you as well.”
The sharp edge lingered, and Harmon let it hang a moment before moving on. They would have to learn from each other the hard way.
Nyssa chuckled softly, but both of them straightened a little, ready.
Satisfied, Harmon turned his gaze to Brother Aldrek, whose bulk leaned against the far wall with arms folded over his tabard. His mace hung at his hip, quiet but heavy.
"Brother Aldrek," Harmon said, his voice softer but no less firm, "your task is shadow work. When Dain leaves, I want you trailing him. Blend with the crowd; your robes will help. Keep back far enough not to draw the guards' attention, but close enough that if something turns foul, you can reach him in time. Understood?"
Brother Aldrek gave a shallow bow of his head, a slow, deliberate thing that spoke of certainty rather than submission.
“Good."
Harmon moved around the table now, slowly, hands behind his back, his boots silent against the thick rugs. His next words were directed at Aurelia, who sat with Weslin beside her, the boy perched eagerly on a chair far too big for him, legs swinging just a little above the floor.
“Aurelia,” Harmon said, “link those on the prison recon and hold it as long as you can.”
“Understood,” she said, calm but steady.
“Weslin, you stay with her. Learn, keep her safe while her mind is elsewhere. Observation is your mission tonight." 
Weslin sat up straighter, practically vibrating with the need to prove himself. "Yes, sir!" he said, a little too loudly, then flushed red when a few smiles flickered around the room. Harmon didn’t chide him, just moved on, already calculating. 
Weslin kept his expression steady and his hands in his lap and told himself that observation was a real job. It was. Harmon had said so. He had not said it like it was a consolation, but Weslin had heard it that way and was doing his best not to keep hearing it that way. He looked at the table and breathed. 
"Neavin," he said finally, meeting the man's sharp eyes, "I’ll need your eyes airborne once we move. Give me the city from above, not just the prison, but the approaches. Watch for followers. Look for anything out of place. If Ash and Nyssa are followed, I want to know before they do."
Neavin gave a small, almost imperceptible nod, but there was a gleam of eager pride behind it, the pride of being trusted.
"Weslin, you are responsible for him, too," Harmon added, almost as an afterthought. This time, Weslin just nodded his head. 
Neavin touched two fingers to his brow toward Weslin. A promise. Harmon looked across them all. “Tonight decides whether we get him back at all." 
Aurelia's hand moved to her shard. "I cannot set the link without Dain," she said. Not a request. A constraint. "Where is he?" 
Harmon answered, "Suite across the hall.”
Aurelia drew a slow breath and closed her eyes, centering herself in the familiar pattern of her craft. Weslin, seated nearby, leaned forward unconsciously, his small hands clenched in the folds of his tunic as he watched her every movement.
Aurelia’s right hand lifted and hovered above the center of the table, palm down, fingers slightly spread. A faint golden shimmer began to gather around her wrist, subtle at first, like heat distortion over stone. Then it thickened, threads of luminous energy weaving outward in gentle spirals. The Shard at her throat began to glow as she drew power to fuel this connection.
Mind to mind, heart to heart, thread to thread, she murmured inwardly, invoking the discipline of connection the way she had been taught long ago. These words have no meaning to the magic itself. 
The first strand shot outward, unseen by mortal eyes but humming in Neavin’s mind like a faint pluck of a harp string. He blinked once, sharply aware, feeling the moment her consciousness brushed against his. It wasn't intrusive; more like a whisper beside his ear, offering him the chance to answer.
Neavin opened himself carefully, and the thread anchored, a warm presence sitting just at the edge of his mind.
Aurelia moved on without pausing, drawing another line of gold, this one seeking Ash and Nyssa as they lingered by the door. Nyssa flinched for just a heartbeat, her thief’s instincts rebelling against anything slipping past her guard, but Ash placed a reassuring hand on her shoulder, and together they allowed the tether to latch.
Brother Aldrek was next. His acceptance came heavier and grounded, the link settling without resistance. Aurelia’s brow furrowed slightly. Sweat beaded at her temple. This many threads took focus. Now comes... the more delicate part.
Without opening her eyes, she reached across the hallway, through the faint soul-echo she could sense from Dain. It took longer. The walls blurred in her mind’s eye like mist, but she pressed through it carefully, searching for the spark of his mind in the next suite.
“There,” she whispered.
Dain’s presence was sharp-edged, alert even while dressing, like a blade half-drawn from its sheath. She sent the thread, careful not to startle him, a brush of intent more than force. After a moment’s hesitation and a suspicious pause on Dain’s end, he accepted the link with a mental grunt of acknowledgment. It wasn't perfect, slightly thinner than the others, but it would hold for now.
Aurelia exhaled shakily.
The final connection was Harmon himself. She found him easily, his mind like the center of a wheel, firm and unyielding. It was as if he opened it just for her. When she tethered him, the whole web tightened, drawing taut until it hummed softly across their collective thoughts. 
One by one, the team would feel it settle, a faint sensation of awareness of each other, not true thoughts yet, but presences, emotional impressions, a readiness to speak or listen mentally when needed.
Aurelia opened her eyes. They glowed faintly gold for a heartbeat before dimming back to warm brown.
"It is done," she said softly, her voice a little hoarse.
Weslin, wide-eyed, leaned closer. “How do you hold it all at once?”
Aurelia offered him a faint, tired smile. “You do not hold it. You weave it. Like a tapestry. Each mind strengthens the others.”
Harmon nodded approvingly. He rolled his shoulders slightly, already feeling the link’s potential stir across the bond.
"Good work," Harmon said. His thought carried across the new web, steady as a drumbeat.
Aurelia sank into a chair, her shoulders bowed, a tremor running through her fingers as she brushed the shard at her throat. A sheen of sweat caught in the firelight, and her words came measured, deliberate. "It is simple in use,” she said quietly. “Think of the person you mean,” she said quietly. “Shape the words. They will hear you.”
Nyssa sent a dry flicker of a thought, Ash answered with a crisp "heard,” Aldrek’s presence settled like stone, and for a heartbeat the web quivered before holding firm.
“Feels like shackles," Dain’s voice slipped across the tether, dry and sharp.
“No,” Aldrek countered, his thought like a hammer-strike, “discipline. Unity.”
“Chains dressed in finer words,” Dain shot back, earning a roll of Nyssa’s eyes. Aurelia’s hand tightened on her shard, sweat beading at her brow.
“Thank you, Aurelia. How long do we have?”
“With my current shard, I would say about three hours.”
“Good. Everyone, let's get a move on.”
Nyssa gave a lazy mock salute and nudged Ash with her elbow, and the two of them slipped out the door like smoke.
Brother Aldrek moved back into the shadows of the room, melting into a watcher’s stance. Across the hall, they could hear faint movement, the sounds of Dain preparing, leather buckles clicking, and the heavy thud of boots being drawn on.
Dain stepped into the room with his usual swagger, road dust scrubbed away and beard trimmed to a courtly shadow. His coat was cut in deep navy velvet, lapels neat and fitted close at the ribs, flaring just slightly at the hips. Silver thread marked the cuffs and high collar, subtle flourishes that hinted at coin. A cream silk cravat, pinned with a modest sapphire brooch, lent a merchant’s polish, while pressed trousers and polished boots grounded the look. At his side, a sword belt with its ornate hilt finished the image, wealth dressed as purpose, arrogance tempered into poise.
“You clean up well,” Harmon said, folding his arms and motioning for Dain to come closer to the command table. “But remember, you’re not here to impress. You’re here to buy.”
Dain raised a brow. “Right. Lord Alric Rendsdale of House Emberlight, cousin to the Wraithmoors,” Dain recited. “Enough coin to move a clerk,” Dain said. “Enough temper to make him hesitate.”
“Cold, but invested,” Harmon said. “A cousin you once trained with is locked up. Your House would rather the records stay clean. You want the problem to go away, not a sermon about forms.”
Aldrek’s hood tipped, a rasp of disapproval that was half prayer, half warning. Dain only smiled.
“Riven’s your old friend. You don’t care what he did. You just want it handled quietly. Bribe first. Pressure second. Bluff last. Don’t make threats unless you’re prepared to make them stick.”
Dain nodded once. “Got it. And backup?”
Harmon gestured to Brother Aldrek, already in his robes and shadowed hood, leaning quietly by the wall. “He will follow once you are on the move. Stay a full block behind. He will blend in with the beggars and street priests. If things go sideways, he will step in. 
”What about the others?” Dain asked. 
“They are in motion,” Harmon said. “Routes, patrols, choke points. You felt the tether, use it.”
Dain nodded. 
Garr snorted. “The way you preen in that getup, I half expect you to trip over your own boots. One slip, and the rest of us pay the price.”
Dain’s smirk sharpened, his hand brushing the cuff of his coat. “Jealous, Garr?” Dain said lightly. “Some of us were made to stand in halls and talk to lords,” Dain said lightly. “Others were meant to be seen, not heard.”
The air bristled. Garr’s jaw worked, muscles tight as stone, and his hand twitched near the hilt at his belt.
“Enough,” Harmon cut in, voice iron. “Save it for the enemy. Pride won’t matter if you’re both dead before dawn.”
The room eased, but Garr’s glare lingered, promising this wasn’t over.
Harmon stepped toward the window and turned to the last man in the room. “Neavin. I need you airborne,” Harmon said. “Stay sharp and keep them safe.”
Neavin closed his eyes. “Understood.”
Veyrith, his bonded spirit, still a falcon on his shoulder, folded her wings and stretched in anticipation. The falcon was one of her favorite shapes. When Neavin released her fully, her form solidified, her talons clicking softly against the stone floor.
Neavin whispered a word in the Old Tongue, and Veyrith leapt soundlessly to the open window, wings flaring wide.
Then Neavin's breath slowed. His hands lowered to his knees. His vision shifted. Snapped.
The room around him faded, and the world opened through Veyrith’s eyes, a sharp, crystalline burst of color and clarity. Wind streamed across feathers. The rooftops below sprawled like a broken puzzle of slate and chimney smoke. His heartbeat stilled, matching hers. Every flick of movement, every flutter of cloth or glint of armor, pulled at his attention. He could feel the currents, the heights, the breath of air over his wings.
He was flying.
“Eyes are open,” Neavin whispered, voice half his own. “I’ll track him from the skies.”
“Good,” Harmon said, eyes sharp. “Then it begins.”
Dain turned and strode for the door with his sword bouncing gently at his hip.
Harmon watched him go, the quiet weight of planning coiling tight around his shoulders.
“Keep him safe,” he murmured to no one in particular.
Then, to the room at large, “We move on his signal.”
And the command center fell into measured silence, broken only by the hum of thought, the press of wind beyond the windows, and the gathering storm outside.
