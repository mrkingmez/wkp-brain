Adin sat and watched his friend gather the boy and leave the tavern. After he was gone, he took a long pull from his tankard, with his mind racing. Luca appeared beside him, standing next to his chair. "Is that him?" Luca's voice was low. 

Adin watched Harmon go a moment longer before answering. "Not how you remember him?" He smiled. "That is him. I would know him anywhere."  A brief smile appeared on his face before he continued. “We had some good times back in the day.”

“And why have you decided to help him? You are not on the same side of the king’s law anymore.”

“There is something you do not know,” Adin said quietly as if the memory was on his tongue. “Who we were.” He paused and stood a little taller. We answered to no king. Only to God himself. Because of that, and that alone." He paused, the memory surfacing and being pressed back down. "I know he will have my back. He knows I will have his. That has not changed in all the years between. There is a kind of trust that does not come from law or blood. It comes from what you have seen together, what you have done." He almost said brothers. He let it sit. "Brothers," he said finally, then shook his head once. "No. There is no word for it." A muscle near his temple shifted, as if it were painful to leave. 

Luca did not push. He had learned which silences Adin came back from quickly and which ones he did not, and this was the second kind. Whatever he had been to Harmon, whatever Harmon had been to him, it lived somewhere below any word either of them could reach for.

The two of them made their way to the kitchen area of the pub. Adin was moving with a purpose. He hadn’t made haste like this in a while, and it showed in his gait. The kitchen roared, fires burning in three hearths, staff moving with the practiced indifference of people paid not to notice things. The smell of smoke and rendered fat was thick enough to wear. Adin moved through it without slowing, and Luca followed close enough to catch the words when they came. The stairs at the back left corner dropped them into the cellar's cold, barrels stacked man-high, the air sharp with oak and spirits, the noise of the kitchen overhead becoming a murmur. 

Luca asked, “There’s a question that still isn't sitting right. Why the different paths?”

Adin paused at the top of the stairs, one hand braced on the railing as he caught his breath, then said, “The world did not change. We did. This led us down different paths. We both still have a lot of mutual respect. This idea of his is something we thought of years ago, but it resulted in nothing. But if he is coming to me now to help build it, then yes, I am in, and who I am now will help more than who I was then. And as for what he shared with me, it all lines up with some of the stories I have been hearing on my web. Meaning I can profit a bit from it as well.”

“So,” Luca asked carefully, “then you lied.”

“Not a lie,” Adin said, justifying himself. He had not told Harmon about the fragments because fragments meant nothing yet, and because a man like Harmon, told half a thing, would charge toward the rest of it. Adin had learned long ago that information given too soon was as dangerous as none at all. “I withheld, it's different, he said as he started down the stairs. It was a short trip down to a room about half the size of the kitchen. Large bags of flour were stacked on top of each other in the far corner. A rack of meat that was bought earlier that day. There were barrels stacked from the ground to the ceiling against the wall across from the stairs. This room had a pulley system attached to a rig on the ceiling to help move and stack the barrels or bags of flour. They made their way down the wall with the casks of ale and other spirits to the end, when Adin then paused as he started to look for something on his body.

Luca looked around the room, then turned fully towards Adin. “So you’re going to need me,” Luca said. He held Adin’s gaze deliberately. “You know the cost.”

Adin did the same. “I am well aware of the cost. Yes, I want you with him because I know you can get out of any trouble. I also want one of my own with him,” Adin said. “Someone I trust.”

“I understand. As long as you are willing, I will serve.”

“That is all I ask.”

Adin then found what he was looking for deep in a pouch inside his cloak. He pulled out a small rod-like item about the length of his pinky and narrow, one end flat, while the other was bent into a circle. He quickly inserted it into a tiny hole next to the last barrel, so small that if you didn’t look closely, you would have missed it. He fished around with the rod until it looked as if it locked into place, then he turned it counter-clockwise a good turn and a half until the circular part was flat. He pressed down on it, and a sound of air releasing came as part of the wall swung open. Inside appeared to be a small room with a desk, a chair, and a few shelves filled with books and numerous other things.

Luca entered first, followed by Adin. When Luca looked around the new L-shaped space, he realized it was deeper than it appeared from the doorway. The other hallway of this hidden room was lined with crates, chests, and other storage containers. Adin closed the door behind them as Luca made his way over to a wall directly across from the door and fiddled with something on a shelf. Light filled the room from what looked like an oil lamp on the wall, yet no oil was present. The light was clean and steady, nothing like the flicker of oil or tallow. Luca had grown used to it the way you grow used to anything in this life, by deciding not to question it. 

Luca had activated a dozen of these over the years and still did not entirely understand them. He did not ask. Some of Adin's resources were better accepted than examined. 

Adin made his way over to his desk on the far side of the room. On his way, he activated a few more of the same type of lamps. He walked around his desk so that it faced the door; some habits die hard, Adin thought, and how silly that is in a secure room like this. He sat quickly and began searching through the drawers and the papers on his desk.

Luca examined a painting that was on the floor, leaning up against the wall. “When are you going to hang this?” He paused as he took it in. “Wait, isn’t this a Selvian...”

 His voice dropped. He crouched to look at it properly, hands on his knees, the way you look at something you are not sure is real. "I stood in front of this once. Thirty years ago, perhaps more. Ropes were keeping the crowd back." He straightened slowly. "There were ropes." 

“Yes, it is, and yes, there were,” Adin said.

“Shouldn’t this be hanging in a gallery or something? I mean, this is the….” Luca trailed off in disbelief.

“The Last Light of Aether, considered one of his greatest works,” Adin finished. “It may have been on display before, but I didn’t like my painting being there. So I freed it and brought it home.”

"Most men would have had a copy made," Luca said.
"Most men would have," Adin agreed. He had already moved back to his search. "Not me. Regarding your question about when I’ll hang it, I’m not planning to set up a permanent shop here yet. When I decide on where that will be, I’ll hang it.”

“Not sure you want this to be your permanent setup? Why not here? We have worked out of here for ten years now. You like it here, I like it here. Are your nomadic ways popping back up again? You need to talk?”

“What are you now, my confessor?”

“No. You do have me playing bartender. Hearing people's sad stories is part of the job. A job you do not pay me enough for.”

“Not enough?”

“Yeah, a good crier makes more than I do, and comes with less listening.”

Adin was not laughing. Looking up from his search, he said, “I don’t hear anyone else complain about their pay. You could always find work elsewhere? I am sure there are plenty of others,” he said the word a bit heavier and paused before continuing, “who are looking for you.”

"Easy. It was a jest." Luca set the painting back where he found it. "You have always carried the gravity. I only balance it." 

Adin continued his search. Luca made his way behind Adin’s desk to the shelf, eyeing a ring that sat on display where it always did, untouched. The ring of black stone bound in iron, its surface gleaming coldly, beautiful, but with a weight that made the air itself feel wary. Luca always liked going through Adin’s treasures.

“Are you going with him?” Luca asked, looking at the ring.

“What? No.”

“If the stories are true. I thought with this here, you have the strength of twenty men and the endurance of a much younger man. In your case, a much, much younger man.”

Adin did not even look up this time. “You are full of jokes today. I should make you tomorrow’s entertainment.”

“Well, you could. But I don’t think you want my kind of jokes getting out,” replied Luca.

“True.” Adin paused, reaching for something that had fallen behind a drawer. He pulled out what looked like a book. “Found it.”

“What?”

“It is a ledger, one that I keep of names of people who can get stuff done, like you.”

“No one is like me; I am the last.”

Adin looked up from the ledger. He held Luca's gaze for a moment, long enough that the statement had somewhere to land. Then he looked back down. "Yes, I know. If anyone would understand it is me. Yet, they do all have skills.". People who have been overlooked or wrongly labeled. People who will be perfect for Harmon’s mission.”

Luca picked up one of the statues on the shelf and looked it over as he asked, “Who do you have in mind? Also, don’t think I didn’t notice that you didn’t answer my question about why you don’t use that ring. I can see he is.”

Adin completely ignored the second part of the statement and said, “Get over here, and I will show you.”

Luca set down the trinket he had been fiddling with and looked at the ring again. “Well, if you're not going to use it, I could.”

The speed at which Adin moved from his desk to shove Luca and stand between him and the ring gave Luca pause and put a flash of fear in his eyes. Luca stumbled back a few steps. “Easy! It was just a question.”

“We are not here to play around,” Adin growled, stepping squarely between Luca and the ring. “You know that you can’t. You’d grow sick,” Adin said coldly. “Go mad. Die within moments.” He steadied himself and waited for his pulse to level. “I have entrusted you with my secret, and if you were as smart as you claim, you would know why I am hiding and not using the ring.”

Luca raised both hands in surrender. “Fine, fine,” Luca said, palms raised. “I’ll stop jesting with you. I would love to see that man again.”

Adin slowly calmed down. “I wish I could. With great power comes even greater drawbacks,” Adin said quietly with a slight quiver to his words.

Luca lowered his hands slowly. The room was quiet, save for the faint sound of the tavern above them, boots on floorboards, a distant laugh. Neither man looked at the ring. Some things are loud enough without being touched. 

Both men let the matter drop there. Adin signaled for them to return to his desk.

“You’re going,” Adin said. “In case you were wondering,” his head was already in the ledger.  

Luca shrugged off the confirmation. He moved slowly back to the desk, asking, “May I go through the crates and find some extraordinary daggers for this venture?”

Adin turned to a large case and said, “Very well. You might find one in the chest better than the crates. There is a belt of four that will suit you. Take the belt, not the blades alone. When you throw one, whether it strikes or misses, it will return to its sheath. That should be sufficient. Now, the names.”

Luca held the belt without moving for a moment. In his line of work, the returning blade was not a luxury. It was the difference between a clean job and a loose end. He understood exactly what he was holding. “Yes. That will serve,” he murmured.

Adin opened the book to its back pages, then turned back three pages to a list of names. The two pages of this old ledger were filled with names accompanied by strange markings that Luca could not decipher. Adin picked up a pair of spectacles from around his neck, set them on his nose, then looked down at the page and began running his hand down the column of strange markings.

He read in silence, his finger tracking the column with the patience of a man used to waiting for the right answer. Then it stopped. "He doesn't want banners," Adin said, half to himself. "He wants a key." He looked up from the page. "I find myself wondering what door he hasn't told me about." 

“You believe he kept something from you?” Luca asked.

“Possibly. It doesn’t matter now, but I do like to know a few moves ahead if I can.” Adin sat back up and returned to his ledger.

“Now, let’s see,” Adin said as he continued scanning. “Gusterson?”

“Absolutely not. He is not fun to work with. He’s also a hammer, and you need someone with more finesse.”

"The brothers. Aymir and Taerel." He made the note without asking Luca's opinion. "They docked last night. I call that providence." 

“You do know they are not really brothers, right?”

“Of course. It is my job to know.”

“I would agree. They will fit right in.”

“That makes three. We need about seven more or so.” A pause before asking, “Who else can you think of?”

“I would want a big hitter. Renic was in the common room today. We could use his strength. He is a skilled sword-hand, not a bad thief either.”

“Isn’t he a part of the Oloho tribe? You think he will play well with others?”

“If the coin is right,” Luca said with a shrug.

Adin grunted in agreement and made a note. “Then he may serve well enough. But men like that do not come cheap, and we have no time to haggle.” He tapped the ledger with two fingers, his expression turning harder. “We cannot afford delays. What do you think of Senna? She ran the Copperveil district two years back."

"Retired," Luca said. "Or claims to. She will come out for the right ask."

Adin made a mark. "Orist. He owes me three favors. Put him down." His finger moved. "Fell. River routes. Knows every lock between here and the eastern crossing. Not cheap. But reliable, which is rarer." Another mark. "And a man called Cael. Former crew captain. Can move twelve people through a checkpoint with no paperwork and no bribes."

"He is not a fighter."

"I did not ask for another fighter. I asked for someone who can move." Adin circled the name. "And here. Pelkin. Not clever. But steady."
Luca leaned over. "Pelkin will not like Renic."

"Then Pelkin will manage his feelings. Both names go down." He sat back. "That is close to enough. Three more, perhaps four.”

Luca glanced at the growing stack of names, brow furrowing. “How are you going to notify everyone? What if they say no?”

Adin gave a short, humorless laugh. “I am not asking. When someone like me sends you a note, you do not decline, not if you intend to keep your place in this city. We will also cast a wide net, more names than we need, and see who answers.” He pushed the ledger toward Luca. “Start writing the summons. I will use my network to deliver them.”

Luca dipped a quill, already scribbling as Adin dictated names. The scratch of ink filled the room as the list grew. Some names were argued, others struck out. Finally, a small stack of sealed notes sat ready on the desk beside Luca’s new dagger belt.

Adin leaned back, rubbing at his eyes. “He wants to ride at the fourth bell. Before dawn.”

The words hung between them, heavy as stone.

“That’s tight.” Luca glanced at the shaft of light slanting through a narrow vent. “Just under half a day.”

Adin gathered his notes, stacking them with deliberate care, then pressed them into Luca’s hands. “Get these delivered. Use every alley rat, every cutpurse, and every errand boy you know. I will send runners to cover the rest. By the time the fourth bell sounds, they must be here, armed and ready.”

Luca slipped the satchel across his shoulder, the weight of parchment and steel settling against his side. He buckled the dagger belt tight and gave a nod.

“Once these summons go out,” Adin said, holding Luca’s gaze, “there is no turning back.”

Luca’s answering smile was thin, but steady. “Then we’d best not falter.”

They sealed the hidden door behind them and moved through the tavern’s bustle, one toward the city and its shadows, the other toward his network.

He thought about Harmon's face when they had spoken. The weight sitting in it, the years behind the eyes. He wondered if he wore the same weight and simply could not see it on himself. He decided it did not matter.

Outside, Opindale carried on as it always did, festival noise, vendors, the smell of food and horses in the heat. The sun was still high. The fourth bell did not care.
