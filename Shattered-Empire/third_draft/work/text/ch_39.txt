The wind held its breath as Luca crouched just below the ridgeline, his cloak wrapped tight, eyes narrowed on the Orc war camp nestled in the hollow below. The camp boiled with motion, guttural barks, clanging iron, and the methodical tramp of boots. Spiked tents rose in haphazard rows, thick with soot and blood. Black-smoked cookfires blazed from stone pits, and a massive cauldron already steamed near the center of the camp like a bloated toad, waiting to be fed.

Everything about the camp was wrong.

The Orcs moved too cleanly. Their timing was too sharp. Even the war thralls, half-broken Goblins in tattered bone necklaces, worked in silence, stacking crates and prepping meat like men who’d been drilled, not born into chaos. It was discipline wrapped in flesh, and Luca hated it.

He reached for the pouch at his hip. Four poison types. All handcrafted. Each one was built for a different kill, slow, fast, hidden, or loud. The camp churned beneath him while he worked, the contrast making his hands steadier than they had any right to be.

He had a black onyx shard around his neck, cracked, old, but still charged, pressed heavily against his skin. Not warm. Not alive. Just… waiting. If he overdrew, it would die. And so would he.

Behind him, Taerel exhaled and whispered, “Almost thirty of us. Over a hundred of them.”

Luca glanced back. The archers crouched along the ridge, dipping arrowheads into the poison vials he’d distributed. The other soldiers were checking straps and blades, each man deathly quiet.

“That’s not that bad of odds,” Aymir muttered, scanning the camp. “That’s a sermon waiting to be preached over a hundred graves.”

“Remind me to play Kingspit with you,” Lysana said from the next rock over, not looking at him. “You clearly think this is an easy hand.”

A few of the younger archers chuckled, thin laughter, cracked by fear.

Luca stood and, without a word, he dropped over the edge.

He descended the ridge like mist, slipping from shadow to shadow, boots silent on loose rock, yet still sure-footed, breath held between strides. The air was sharp with smoke and charred meat. He moved through it like a man dissolving into the night.

Two Orc warriors marched past him, less than five paces away. Their armor clinked, their gazes empty. One had red paint smeared across his lips, some war mark or ritual, maybe both. Neither noticed him.

He crept past tents, behind crates, past a stack of flayed bones still wet from the butcher. His path to the cauldron was blocked by a Red Thrall cook, crouched and tossing fat chunks of meat into the bubbling pot.

Luca crouched low. Waited.

The cook stepped away, muttering something, and he moved.

He reached the cauldron, pulled the stew orbs from his pouch. One, two, three. No splash. But the fourth slipped from his fingers mid-throw.

It landed on the rim. Wobbled.

A voice barked behind him.

He twisted fast, crouched behind the cauldron’s smoke, and froze. An Orc was yelling at one of the Goblin thralls for burning the meat. Not at him. Not yet.

Still crouched, Luca stretched his dagger out, hooked the wobbling poison orb, and flicked it clean into the stew.

As he slid back from the edge of the fire, a bellowing argument broke out between two Orcs over a ladle. One shoved the other, who swung wildly, nearly dumping the pot in retaliation. Neither killed the other, just a snarl, a punch, then back to line.

Luca narrowed his eyes. For all their strange discipline, it wasn’t perfect. There was still chaos beneath the surface, mistakes, tempers, and ego.

Good. He moved. There was still chaos in them. He could use that.

He stayed low, belly to the dirt, and moved.

He quickly crawled to the meat station. The slabs were hanging from a crossbar beside a prepping table. Orc blood soaked the ground below it, forming a sticky puddle. As he reached the first leg, footsteps closed in from behind.

He ducked, rolled under the prep table, and held his breath.

An Orc cook, a massive brute, slammed a cleaver into the board above him, humming a low tune. Luca could hear the slap of flesh on wood, the grind of bone under steel. He was close enough to smell the rot in the Orc’s breath.

Then, behind them, a flare of shouting, two Orcs in an argument over a ration crate. The cook grunted, spat, and stormed off to investigate.

Luca didn’t hesitate. He slashed a shallow line across three pieces of hanging meat, shoved the rub in fast, and slipped back into the alley between tents.

The ale barrels were stacked near the east wall. He was almost there when he froze. A patrol of three Orcs rounded the corner. He ducked back, no cover.

Nowhere to run.

They came closer.

Luca drew from the shard. He stepped into the shadow of the tent wall and vanished.

He reappeared on the far side, gasping, heart pounding in his chest like a war drum. His legs buckled. Too much pull. Too close.

But he was through.

He reached the barrel. Drew the final glass spheres. Popped the casing. Dropped them into the half-tapped cask. They fell without a sound.

He turned and found himself face to face with a Goblin thrall carrying a tray of stacked cups.

They stared at each other. The Goblin didn’t blink. He just sniffed. Luca didn’t move. Then the Goblin relieved himself mid-stare. Luca blinked once, then shoved the distraction out of his mind as the camp’s noise sharpened back into focus.

He melted back into the dark.

He crested the ridge fifteen minutes later, sweat matting his collar, legs aching, shard buzzing under his skin.

He stumbled into view.

Lysana was the first to reach him.

“You’re late,” she said, brushing hair from her eyes. Her voice was light, but her eyes searched his face.

“I got held up,” he rasped. “And pissed on. Not in that order.”

Taerel winced. “Details we did not need.”

Aymir just handed him a waterskin.

Luca took it. Drank deep. But Lysana didn’t let him walk away.

She stepped close, hand lightly on his wrist. “Luca.”

He looked up.

The noise of the ridge, the soldiers, the wind, it all fell away.

He saw the concern in her eyes. The way she looked at him was like he wasn’t just a killer. Like maybe she saw something left.

He opened his mouth. Closed it.

Then, finally, quietly, “It’s been a long time since I thought I’d come back from anything at all.”

Her hand tightened slightly.

“You will,” she said. “And when you do…”

He smiled. “Then we’ll talk.”

Lysana smiled back.

And for a second, the war vanished.

A distant clatter rose from the camp, metal on stone, snapping the moment back into the cold. She turned, fingers lingering a moment too long, and made her way to join the archers. Her bow was already strung. Her arrowheads dipped in poison.

Luca crouched beside Aymir and Taerel, staring down into the camp below.

Luca looked between them, voice low. “They’re not all gone.”

Both brothers turned. “Who?” In unison, like they'd practiced it. 

“The Orcs, their bloodlust. It’s still in there. Whatever’s guiding them hasn’t erased everything. I saw two nearly throw each other into the stew over a spoon.”

Aymir gave a half-smile. “Would’ve saved us a poison ball.”

“Means there’s still cracks in whatever’s steering them,” Taerel muttered. “Cracks we can use.”

The Orcs started gathering. A hundred or so strong. Lining up at the cauldron, which was steaming and ready. 

And those on top of the ridge, they waited.

The wind slipped cold across the ridge.

Nearly thirty souls watched the Orc camp below with the tense stillness of hunters holding their breath. Shadows stretched longer now, dusk turning the snow-lined stones a bruised violet. The smoke from the campfires drifted thick and low, stirred only by the occasional grunt or barked command.

Luca sat back on his heels, watching the movements with narrowed eyes. He ran the mission back in his head one more time and caught it. “I never used the fourth poison,” he muttered.

Taerel glanced over. “What?”

“The fourth one. Meant for the bread,” Luca said. “They stack it in crates by the far tent. I just... things were moving too fast. I didn’t want to risk it.”

“Let’s hope they’re not craving toast,” Aymir said.

Lysana added dryly, stringing her next arrow, “And pray the moldy bread wasn’t the main course. I hear Orcs like their loaves aged with a hint of battlefield mud.”

That got a few chuckles from the nearby archers, who crouched just inside the ridgeline brush.

They were good soldiers. Luca didn’t know all their stories, but names had been passed down like a roll call of grit. The waiting gnawed at them, so Luca let his attention drift across the faces he’d be trusting his life with.

Dorn was crouched nearby, the beard on his chest practically resting on his knees, quietly describing to Fenrick the correct way to brine wild boar. Fenrick, who looked barely seventeen, was only half listening. He was still fiddling with his arrowheads, turning them over like worry stones. Mira didn't look at either of them. 

She just sat with her shaved head down, adjusting a fletching with the focus of someone who preferred not to think too far ahead. Beside her, Halden was already doing his rounds, checking a man's wrapped ankle and murmuring something low and steady, the way a priest talks to someone who already knows the verdict. The fifth was Brant. Older, lean, carrying a limp like a souvenir. His eyes moved the way a marksman's do, always measuring distance.

They were joined by Sergeant Rockwen and his seventeen other soldiers, all armored and silent, veterans from the northern pass and defenders of the fort. They crouched in staggered lines along the slope, bows and blades ready.

Only a few civilians were with them now, two older men and a sharp-eyed woman acting as field medics. The rest had remained back in the cavern refuge, watching over the wounded and children and managing supplies.

Luca sat beside Lysana, his pulse still erratic from the earlier infiltration.

“You good?” she asked softly, handing him her waterskin again.

He took it and sipped. “Mostly. Still trying to believe I didn’t get my head lopped off.”

Lysana smiled. “It’s weird.” She paused, looking into his eyes. "A week ago, I didn't know you. Now losing you would mean something. That's the weird part. "

Luca glanced at her, caught off guard by the blunt honesty. “Yeah,” he said quietly. “Same.”

They both looked away, awkward for half a breath.

Then, without thinking, he reached for her hand, just a light brush of fingers across hers. She didn’t pull away. Instead, she turned her palm over and laced their fingers together, warm even in the chill wind.

“I was always better on my own,” Luca said. “But... this week.”

“Don’t ruin it with words,” she murmured. “Just hold on.”

Taerel coughed behind them, clearly watching.

Aymir grinned. “Well then. Shadow finally found himself a candle.”

“Careful,” Taerel added. “He’s getting soft. Next thing you know, he’ll write poetry.”

Luca didn’t let go of her hand. Instead, he raised the other and gave them a sharp two-finger salute, like an old court insult. “Eat snow.”

“Classic,” Aymir said, grinning.

"You know," he said, quieter, "for years I thought the talent was reading a room. Turns out I was just looking for exits."  

Taerel snorted. “That was before you kept company with people who bleed for a living.”

“Maybe,” Luca said. “But watching you three, the way you move together, cover for each other, throw insults like it’s strategy... It’s different. Feels like it works. Even if I’m not sure what the hell I’m doing half the time.”

Aymir grinned. “That’s the trick, Luca. No one ever does. We just bluff better than the next bastard.”

“Still,” Luca said, quieter now, “if this thing falls apart tonight, I want you to know, I’ve never trusted a crew like this one. Not since... not ever, really.”

Lysana bumped her shoulder lightly against his. “Then stop thinking about how it ends. Start thinking about how it begins.”

Dorn told a story quietly to Fenrick about his time guarding the eastern border. Mira sharpened a knife, humming under her breath.

Halden knelt beside an older civilian, checking his wrapped ankle and adjusting the cloth.

The tension was there, tight, coiled, but for now, it was laced with quiet purpose. Everyone moved like they belonged.

Still, every few minutes, all eyes would shift back to the camp below.

An hour passed. Nothing.

Then movement, one of the Orcs at the outer ring, wobbled and staggered. Halden straightened, arrow notched.

But the Orc simply bent over and vomited violently before righting himself with a growl and stalking away.

“Too early,” Luca said. “Could be something else. None of mine should cause vomiting.”

Another twenty minutes.

A Goblin fell off the back of a wagon, rolling into the mud. Two others laughed and pelted him with scraps.

“Also nothing,” Taerel muttered. “That one tripped on a rope.”

They waited.

A cold gust swept the ridge, carrying silence with it, then conversation slowly bubbled up again as nerves tried to fill the void. Dorn talked about spices with Mira. Sil Brant made a bet with Aymir over who’d notch the first Orc’s eye. Fenrick asked Luca if it was true that he once killed a man with a spoon.

“No,” Luca replied, deadpan. “It was a fork.”

“Seriously?”

Luca shrugged. "No. But I did throw a spoon into someone's eye once." 

“Wait, you’re serious?”

Luca just smiled.

Lysana leaned close, lips brushing his ear. “You’re awful.”

“I know. I love it," he whispered back, eyes still on the camp. 

Taerel, adjusting his rifle, winced. He had to brace it differently now, resting it against the support rocks rather than pulling it tight to his shoulder. The wound still ached. But he didn’t complain.

“See anything new?” Luca asked.

“Pattern’s still the same,” Taerel said. “Except... something’s off.”

“Off how?”

“It's like someone’s giving direction, without fully leashing them.”

“Guided chaos,” Aymir said.

“Exactly.”

“Then someone’s holding that leash,” Luca murmured.

Another silence fell.

A single Orc, near the stew line, buckled to one knee. Another behind him growled and pushed past him, not noticing.

Then a third Orc lurched sideways, hitting a post.

Taerel exhaled slowly. “Here we go.”

Everyone stood a little straighter.

“Poison’s taking,” Luca said. “Took long enough.”

They all readied weapons, quiet now. Arrows dipped. Blades loosened in sheaths. The brothers took their final positions, rifles steadied against rocks, scopes aligned.

Lysana stood beside Luca, her hand brushing his again, just once.

“You sure about this?” she asked.

He nodded once. “No. But I want it anyway.”

She smiled. “Let’s go kill some monsters.”

And the wind rose.
The order came with the wind.
“Archers, loose three volleys, center mass!” Luca’s voice cracked sharply over the ridge.
The command sliced through the dusk, and Mira was the first to move. Her bow sang once, then twice more, followed by the entire line, five archers rising in sync, loosing poisoned shafts that cut through the evening air. The hiss of strings and the twang of release filled the hillside like a violent chorus.
Below, the first volley struck with blunt inevitability. Orcs stumbled mid-step. Two collapsed before they could lift weapons. The second volley thudded in among those moving to react. The third punched through a tightly packed group near the main fire.
“Keep firing until you’re empty!” Luca shouted. “Taerel, Aymir, start picking targets!”
The rifles began barking moments later. One crisp shot, then another. Taerel grunted, adjusting the brace under his elbow, wincing through the pain. Aymir fired again, faster, more fluid. One Orc took a round to the throat and dropped. Another keeled over near the stew.
From above, it looked almost surgical.
But the poison was doing its own work too. Dozens had already dropped, some shaking, others simply slumping where they stood. It was working, gods, it was actually working.
They were going down.
Luca spotted three Orcs collapse before Mira’s next arrow was even nocked. “Save your arrows!” he called out, raising his hand. “They're already falling. No sense wasting steel.”
The archers lowered their bows one by one, Mira exhaling slowly as she reached for a cloth to wipe her fingers. Dorn clapped Fenrick on the shoulder, the young soldier looking stunned at the ease of the takedown. Even the veterans, hardened, weather-worn, allowed the tension to slip from their shoulders for a breath.
Luca turned, face set. “Brant, Halden, Dorn, you’re with me. Everyone with a blade, move. Aymir, Taerel, you stay and cover us. Lysana, with me.”
She was already beside him, sliding her bow across her back and drawing her twin daggers in a single, fluid motion.
“We do this clean,” Luca said, voice low as they descended the ridge. “Check the bodies. Finish what the poison started.”
They moved in a loose wave, boots crunching the frost-lined slope. As they reached the lower slope of the valley, the true scale of what they’d done began to register. There were bodies everywhere. Orcs, Goblins, even the monstrous cooks, all crumpled in heaps of slack limbs and spilled weapons. Steam rose from collapsed tents. The stew still boiled.
Luca crouched, checked a still-breathing Orc, and drove his dagger clean into its throat. No flinch. Just muscle memory.
A few of the others moved through the camp like ghosts. It wasn’t a fight. It was an execution.
Until the ground moved.
Silence dropped through the smoke first. Then the vibration began, faint but rising. He paused, frowning, then looked to Lysana. She was already glancing back toward the ridge.
“What was that?” she asked.
“I don’t—” he began, before the ground heaved hard enough to stagger them both.
The sound that followed was not like the ground moving. It was like it was opening.
One of the largest tents at the camp’s far edge tore open from below, its wooden supports splintering as the ground erupted with a spray of mud and frost. And from the gaping hole poured shapes, scores of them. Twisted, long-limbed, hunched things with wide mouths and bulging black eyes. The Joblins.
They came like a wave.
Hundreds, at least. Scampering and bounding over one another with shrieks and chatter and hoots like iron dragged across bone. They wore scraps of fur, bits of armor, jagged tools, and crude weapons. Many were unarmed, yet each one moved with purpose, as if flung by instinct.
Luca’s heart hitched.
“The tunnel...” he muttered. “They’ve been digging. This whole time.”
Lysana’s breath hitched. “The bombardment… it wasn't random. It was a cover. They were hiding this.”
Luca heard it land even through the noise. If that was true, the fort team had been fighting the wrong battle the entire time. He filed it. Dealt with it later. Right now, there was only the next thing trying to kill him. 
“Form up!” Luca shouted. “Everyone, form up! Hold your line!”
The archers scrambled to the edge of the camp, Mira shouting orders, Fenrick fumbling with his next arrow. Taerel and Aymir on the ridge opened fire again, shots cracking down into the charging swarm. Joblins fell, but the horde didn’t slow.
Lysana had her bow out again, arrows flying with terrifying rhythm. “One,” she muttered. “Two. Three.”
She didn’t miss.
Luca saw a Joblin coming at her from the side and lunged. His dagger struck the base of its neck, and the creature collapsed into a gurgle. Another was already in motion behind it. This time, his offhand blade sank into its gut.
The magic of his belt flared, and both blades vanished from his grip, reappearing at his side in twin sheaths moments later.
He didn’t pause, but the flare stung sharper this time, a reminder that the belt wasn’t endless. At his chest, the cracked shard pulsed once and went quiet. Not dead. But closer.
More Joblins swarmed from tents, from the pit, from under half-collapsed shelters.
The rest of the soldiers were now in the fight. Brant drew a heavy saber. Halden pulled civilians back while still notching arrows. Dorn carved a path through a knot of bodies with a war axe. 
The ridgeline thundered with more rifle fire.
And through it all, Luca caught sight of Lysana, her face set, eyes hard, cutting through the chaos with precision. He moved to her side again.
“Watch your flank!” she called.
“I’ve got it,” he said, spinning to drive a blade through a leaping Joblin’s throat.
They locked eyes in the chaos, panting, smeared with blood and frost. “Let’s survive this,” Luca said, voice low and certain. And then they turned and fought.
