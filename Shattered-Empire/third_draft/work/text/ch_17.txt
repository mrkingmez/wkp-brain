
Maps were strewn like fallen leaves across the broad oak desk, creases forming crooked valleys, inked roads glistening faintly in the low golden light of early morning. At the center of it all, bent over with a tension that had become part of his shape, was Harmon. Sleeplessness clung to him like an old coat. The dark smudges beneath his eyes told of hours spent in silent calculation, and his jaw sat clenched in the low light like a man trying to hold an army back with his teeth.
He hadn't slept. The hollowness in his face wasn't weariness alone. It was the particular strain of a man who knew exactly what was at risk and could do nothing about it until morning brought word.
The knowledge that more than three hundred miles away, Luca and the others were risking their lives. Every hour without word added weight to his chest, and by now, he wore a mountain.

He pushed himself up from the desk, hands bracing on the edge of the wood as if the act itself required negotiation. His limbs moved slowly, almost reluctantly, as he stood and stepped over maps, parchment rolls, a compass, and a half-drunk cup of cold tea. The air in the room was heavy with ink, sweat, and worry.

He walked the room slowly, boots whispering over the worn rug beneath his feet. With each step, the stiffness in his body protested. He hadn't noticed the ache in his knees until now, nor the knot just under his shoulder blade that had taken root there sometime around midnight. But it was the pull of the light, thin as a thread of salvation, that drew him.

The window, until now ignored in favor of the ever-turning gears in his mind, bled with the early fire of dawn. And as he reached it, drawing the curtain aside with the edge of a tired hand, the light poured across his face, painting him in gold and shadow. It was the first time he had truly looked out since they’d arrived.

Forgehallow stretched beyond the glass like a living machine. Chimneys coughed out morning smoke, rooftops glinted with soot-frosted dew, and the low hum of waking industry murmured through the cobbled veins of the city. Far beyond, the sharp hills of the north cut against the sky like broken teeth, and somewhere past them, hidden by time and terrain, Luca’s team was either resting in success or bleeding in silence.

A quiet breath left him. Not a sigh, more like the kind of exhale a man gives when he's remembering how to breathe again.

He stared at the rising sun, the first he’d truly watched in decades. 

“This is falling apart,” he murmured to himself, voice low and raw, like a confession to the morning. “I built this too wide. Too ambitious. Now it’s slipping.”

His gaze dropped, voice barely above a breath now. “I believed I could hold together too many threads. Fool’s work.”

He looked down at his hand, the right one, and thumbed the ring on his finger. The metal pulsed faintly against his skin, not with light but with weight. Magic pulsed from it, subtle and disciplined, like the low drumbeat of a heart, keeping him sharp. Keeping him standing. Without it, he knew, he would have collapsed hours ago. The cost of that ring’s power was something he had stopped counting.

A knock, too sharp to be polite, too familiar to be alarming, rattled the frame of the door before it creaked open. Boots on wood. Urgency in motion.

Weslin. He was halfway to Harmon before he could even turn from the window, breath tight and eyes lit with something unreadable. “Sir,” Weslin said, slightly out of breath.

Harmon turned fully now, drawing himself upright even as fatigue clung like iron weights to his limbs. He studied Weslin’s expression, the kind that only came when something had broken the silence across miles.

“We’ve got a message,” Weslin said, voice steady but edged. “Came through the twin bracelet.”

Harmon’s eyes sharpened like drawn steel. “From who?”

“Luca!” A long pause, not of confusion but of gathering. Then Harmon nodded, already moving past the scout, hand grazing the map-littered desk as he went, brushing a curled edge back into place out of unconscious habit.

“Come with me,” he said. “We’ll talk while we eat.”

Together, they descended the narrow tavern staircase, footsteps muffled on the worn steps, the scent of smoke and early fare rising to greet them like an old friend.

As Harmon reached the lower floor, the voices of his team drifted to him first. Ash’s low laugh, cracked through the room like fire through dry wood. Aldrek murmured something into his cup that sounded like a prayer. Nyssa was already poking at Neavin with some sharp remark while Aurelia rolled her eyes. Dain leaned back in his chair, half a grin tugging his lip, and Garr, silent as always, watched them all like a sentinel of stone.

They were already gathered around a long oak table near the fire, the kind of table that held secrets, plans, and stories in equal measure.

He paused for a heartbeat at the foot of the stairs, letting the sight root him. They were still together. Still breathing. Still his.

And now, Luca had reported.

They claimed a small, square table near the far corner of the inn’s common room, slightly away from the larger cluster of morning patrons but close enough to feel the warmth of the central hearth. A wide sunbeam slanted in through the high windows, catching the motes of dust swirling in the air like lazy dancers. The low din of the tavern ebbed and flowed, wooden plates clinking, a pipe player near the fireplace strumming a slow, almost mournful tune, and the occasional laughter of two traders discussing the price of good flax.

Weslin eased himself down across from Harmon. His brow was still furrowed, though now it bore more fatigue than tension.

Harmon kept his voice low, forcing the words to stay close to the table. The fire cracked loudly enough to swallow anything that slipped past his teeth. He leaned forward, resting his forearms on the table, fingers loosely interlaced. “All right, boy,” he said, voice gruff but even. “Out with it.”

Weslin nodded and quietly relayed the message from the bracelet. Luca’s voice still echoed faintly in the back of his memory. “They’re… back at their camp,” Weslin said, then pushed on. “Ten survivors pulled out. Rough shape. But alive.”

Harmon nodded slowly but said nothing, his face unreadable.

“There’s more,” Weslin continued. “Luca said… something strange happened at the site. He’s got an item. He wants someone here to look it over. Something about mind control.” The last words came with a subtle shift in his voice, uncertain, as though he didn’t quite believe them himself.

Before Harmon could respond, a voice slurred into their bubble of conversation like a lazy wave lapping at the shore.

“What’ll it be, gents?”

The waitress slouched at their table, apron streaked with flour and grease. “Break-fust, aye.”

“Two house plates,” Harmon said.

“Drunks?” asked the waitress.

“Apple juice,” Weslin said.

“Two,” Harmon added.

She nodded and shuffled back toward the kitchen, the floor creaking beneath her shoes.
Still staring at the table, Harmon exhaled through his nose. “They’re alive and saved civilians,” Harmon said, fingers tightening briefly on the bracelet. “That's all that counts. Tell them job well done, and to send a report in full when able.”

Weslin nodded and pulled his sleeve back slightly to reveal the twin bracelet, already glowing faintly in response. He tapped the bracelet as it had become a habit for him now, and whispered the words Harmon had given him.

Harmon leaned back in his chair then, and it was like watching a massive bellows release. The tension dropped from his shoulders; he closed his eyes and took in a long breath, his chest finally moving freely.

“Well,” he muttered. “That’s one thing. One out of ninety-nine.” A slow, dry smile tugged at the edge of his mouth. “Let’s enjoy breakfast before the next fire starts.”

The common room was beginning to buzz a little louder now. A merchant in a fur-collared coat was loudly complaining about the roads to a poor farmhand who clearly didn’t care. A pair of local hunters was playing a dice game near the window, their laughter peppered with crude jokes about a girl in the stables. The hearth crackled softly, and somewhere above them, boots thudded across the ceiling beams, likely Aurelia pacing again.

It didn’t take long for their food to arrive.

The house breakfast was simple but hearty. Thick-sliced fried ham with crisped edges, two golden eggs over toast, a scoop of skillet-fried potatoes tossed with onions and herbs, and a sweet-buttered biscuit on the side. A pint of apple juice in a frosted clay mug accompanied each plate, chilled from the root cellar.

Weslin wasted no time digging in, clearly famished.

Harmon, meanwhile, took a bite and grunted in approval. “Not bad,” he said through a mouthful of ham. “Could use some heat, but I’ll take flavor over spice this early.”

They were halfway through their plates when Garr approached the table, walking with that wide-legged confidence born of too many years on rough ground. He was still shrugging into his longcoat, his swordbelt hanging loosely at his side.

“Mornin’, old man,” he said, voice dry as kindling. “Mornin’, kid.” He nodded at Weslin. “Food smells better than the room upstairs, I’ll give it that.”

He stood beside the table, arms folded, waiting for either of them to acknowledge his presence beyond a look.

Harmon swallowed, then dabbed at the corner of his mouth with a napkin. “We’re just finishing. Sit down if you’re here to talk. Otherwise, get your own plate.”

Garr smiled but didn’t move. “Just wanted to check in before the whole crew assembles. Place has a feel to it this mornin'," Garr said. "Guesson you already know." 

Harmon gave him a knowing look. “They always are.”

The last of the eggs and bread disappeared from Harmon’s plate as he set his fork down with a sigh and leaned back in the creaky wooden chair. Weslin, cheeks stuffed with the last bite of ham, gave a small grunt of satisfaction. Around them, the common room of the tavern was still buzzing with the quiet hum of morning. Harmon stood, wiping his hands on a linen cloth, and motioned for Weslin to follow. Together, they crossed the worn planks toward the table where the others waited, Nyssa lounging with one boot on a bench, Neavin still eating, Aldrek watching the room like a sentry.

Dain leaned back with his arms stretched across the top of the bench, idly sharpening a slim dagger while muttering something that made Aurelia raise a disapproving brow. Ash was nursing a chipped mug of black tea, steam curling up beneath his chin. He looked up first as Harmon approached.

“How’s everyone doing?” Harmon asked, his voice steady but with a slight smile tucked into the edge of his tone.

Dain was first to reply, flipping his dagger in his palm. “I have been in considerably worse positions," Dain said, flipping the dagger again. "Upside down in bandit camps, for instance. Or explaining myself to angry husbands. This ranks somewhere in the middle." 

Nyssa smirked. “I’d say I’m peachy, but there’s not a peach within a hundred miles of this place.”

Neavin didn’t look up. "Still breathin'," he said, not looking up. "Reckon that's fine enough." 

Brother Aldrek simply nodded, his heavy brow knit in mild concern. “Fed. Focused.”

Aurelia gave Harmon a serene glance, her voice low and measured. “Prepared, as ever.”

Ash took a slow sip from his mug, then spoke in his usual gravelly voice. “Still alive. That’s my standard lately. Doesn’t take much to impress me.”

Weslin chuckled softly behind Harmon, still wiping grease from his chin.

Harmon pulled up a stool and leaned in, his tone shifting to something more clipped, urgent. “Luca’s team made it back to their camp. The raid was a success.”

The team let that settle in before Harmon continued. “For us, we need to have this done tonight. At dusk, we will breach the prison. One last look around this morning. Split into teams to cover more ground.”

He paused, letting the words settle over the group.

“Garr. Ash. You’re heading to the local records office. Maybe the library as well, if needed. I want those building drawings. You’re inspectors,” Harmon said. “Structural safety. Aging foundations. Say the city’s had complaints. You don’t ask for answers. You ask what’s already on the shelf. Sound official, look bored, and make them think it’s nothing. 

Ash gave a low grunt. “So, lie like it’s just another Jovis. Got it.”

Harmon shot back, “Today it's only Martis.”
Garr smirked. “I’ll handle the charm. You handle looking like you’re about to punch someone if they breathe wrong.”
Ash raised both hands, feigning offense. “Intimidating? Please. I’m adorable.”
A few smirks and giggles flickered around the table.

Harmon moved on. “Nyssa, Neavin, you’re covering the exterior. I want every inch of that prison mapped. Number of guards, pacing, and vantage points. Neavin, I want your little friend in there. If he can shift small enough to slip through the bars, do it. We need an inside layout, even if it’s rough.”

Neavin gave a small grin. “He’s eager,” he said, wiping his fingers on his trousers. “I’ll let him loose.”

Nyssa twirled a lock of her hair around one finger. “We’ll play nice with the guards. Or not. Depends how rude they decide to be.”

“Aurelia,” Harmon said, his tone softening slightly. “You’re going to do what you do best. Slip through the minds of the guards, patrols, and even the staff. Find out what they’re thinking, what they know. Names, routines, anything that feels off.”

She gave a graceful nod. “Understood.”

“Careful with it,” Harmon added. “Shallow touches only. We don’t need them noticing.”

“You’ll take Weslin with you. Show him and talk to him about what you are doing. He’s due for his second lesson anyway. Make sure he completes it before you head out.”

Weslin blinked. “Wait, lesson first? And then mind-reading patrol?”

Aurelia gave him a gentle, knowing smile. “Yes. Lesson first. Then the fun begins.”

Harmon’s eyes shifted to the last pair. “Aldrek. Dain. You’re our pressure team. Find a place with loose lips. Use the noble getup if you have to, drop a name or two. Anyone talking about the prison? The prisoners? Anything they’ve heard, any rumors they’re holding back. Also… keep your ear to the wind on Lord Merrick. I don’t trust that man based on the stories I have heard, and I want to know what he’s planning, and who he’s working with, if he’s positioning himself for something bigger.”

Dain leaned back and stretched. “So, drink, flirt, and pretend I care about local gossip? Sounds exhausting.”

Aldrek gave him a sidelong look. “Try not to flirt with anyone dangerous this time.”

“Where’s the fun in that?” Dain winked.

Harmon stood up straight, voice firm. “Alright. Any questions?”

Nyssa raised her hand lazily. “And what exactly will you be doing while we’re running around dodging guards and charming bureaucrats?”

Harmon didn’t miss a beat. “I’ll be here. Suffering. Doing commander stuff, like reading reports, drinking bad coffee, and pretending I don’t want to strangle half the people in this city.”

Dain grinned. “So the usual, then?”

“Exactly,” Harmon replied dryly.

A round of chuckles rippled through the table. Garr stood first, nodding toward Ash. Aurelia rose in one fluid movement, turning toward Weslin with that gentle but expectant teacher’s presence. Nyssa was already halfway to the door, with Neavin tucking food into his cloak and following silently. Dain groaned theatrically as he stood, and Aldrek simply followed, calm and steady.
The group broke apart like clockwork, each team sliding into motion, the air thick with purpose. Boots found cobblestone. Outside the tavern, the sky was beginning to brighten into the pale gold of a new day, one that they were already asking a lot from, and more before it was done.
