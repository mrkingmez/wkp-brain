The sound of breath was louder than their steps now. Every exhale misted out in a long plume, swallowed by the dense winter air as quickly as it formed. Their boots plunged into the thick snowpack, each step sinking nearly to the knee with a soft crunch and a sucking sound that clung like a whisper. The trail ahead was barely visible, just a faint ribbon between white-laden trees and windblown drifts. Where the storm had blown hard the day before, the snow had formed uneven ridges that forced the team to navigate more like mountaineers than soldiers.
Taerel cursed as his boot snagged on a buried root, forcing him to throw a hand out to balance. “Ghosts don't plant roots under the snow,” he muttered. “Right. That’s… right.” He nodded and continued talking to himself. “I mean, just checking.”
Aymer snorted, adjusting the rifle slung across his shoulder. “Maybe it's a snow wraith. Turns your guts to ice before you see it.”
Lysana gave a dry chuckle as she hauled her legs through another drift. “And here I thought it was the frost-fanged elk we had to worry about. The ones that wait in silence and charge when you're taking a piss.”
Taerel glanced at her with a grin. “Those? N. They only go for the handsome ones. You’re safe.”
Aymer rolled his eyes, breath pluming from his nose. “You remember that mountain guide from the tavern last night? The one rambling over stew like it was his last meal? “He swore he saw flesh-pale things crawling under the trees. Long arms. No legs. Dragging themselves forward like centipedes the size of dogs.”
Taerel scoffed. “Said they smelled like moldy bread and screamed like babies when they hunted.”
Lysana shook her head, amused. “And here I thought the wine was the worst part of dinner.”
Aymer chuckled. “He called them 'Gravepullers,' remember that? Claimed they’d dig their way up through snowbanks and tug animals down from underneath. Scraping first, then silence.”
Taerel made a mock shiver. “Sounds like a bard’s tale cooked up to keep drunks inside the tavern.”
Lysana’s smirk faded slightly. “Maybe. But the way he described the carcass he found… bones cracked clean, marrow sucked out... wasn’t the kind of detail you make up over a bowl of stew.”
Aymer shrugged. “Still better than thinking it's what we’re trained to expect, Worgs, raider hounds, or those wolf-bred trackers the Orcs breed.”
Just ahead, a faint ridge in the snow caught Luca’s eye. He gestured subtly, and the team shifted pace, instincts kicking in.
A few yards later, Lysana crouched low, brushing snow away from a patch of churned ice. Her hand paused as more details revealed themselves. She outlined deep claw marks, a wide-set, and they must be heavy. Not canine. Something broader. Almost... plated, she thought.
Taerel looked up from another spot just off the trail. “Got something else over here, too. Smaller tracks… narrow, but deep. Padded feet. Moving in a scattered line, like scouts circling something bigger.”
Lysana joined him and examined the prints. Her expression darkened. “Not the same creature. Different stride. Quieter.”
“More than one predator," Luca said. "Different species." He looked to the trees, the brush beyond, then back to the trail ahead. He looked at his team, “Eyes up. We’re not alone.”
Luca said nothing. He moved ahead, each step deliberate, his blade resting across his shoulder. The others followed without discussion. Behind the silence, his mind ran the same question it had been running since Rellisford, whether the trust trailing behind him was something he had earned, or simply something people defaulted to when they did not know what else to do with their eyes in the dark.
Lysana’s gaze lingered on his back for a moment, reading every shift of his shoulders like a language she hadn’t yet learned. There was steadiness there, and something colder beneath it.
The deeper they moved off the main path, the more the forest thickened, the trees gnarled and heavy with snow, their black limbs bowed and creaking. The moon filtered through a high veil of cloud, just enough to throw silver highlights across the untouched snow. Now and then, the wind stirred the canopy and sent a fresh flurry down like sifted ash, coating shoulders and hoods in silence.
Then Luca’s hand snapped up, a quick, cutting gesture, and the line stopped instantly.
Lysana moved to his side, crouching as her eyes scanned ahead. About fifty yards out, the moonlight caught something dark in the snow, not part of the natural terrain. Her lips pressed into a line as she stepped forward carefully, boots sinking, eyes flicking from patch to patch of broken whiteness.
Taerel and Aymer immediately fanned out. The brothers moved with practiced fluidity. Aymer veered left, rifle raised, scanning the tree line, while Taerel took the right, boots light despite the weight of snow. Neither said a word now.
At the center of the road, the shapes resolved into two mounds, black and twisted. As Lysana knelt beside the nearest one, her hand brushed through the snow to reveal part of a saddle, torn leather crusted with frozen blood. A horse's body, ribs exposed where something had feasted, its side ripped open with savage intent. Steam no longer rose from it. Whatever happened was hours ago.
Lysana sucked in a sharp breath and gestured for Luca to come closer. “Multiple tracks. Some human,” she whispered. “Most not.” 
She pointed toward the wilderness flanking the road. The snow there was trampled and torn, dragged through by creatures that didn't care to hide their trail.
Luca gave a short whistle, three sharp notes, and the brothers returned immediately. No words were needed. They read the scene in silence, their expressions shifting from curious to grim.
Lysana stepped closer, brushing snow from a clean print near the center. The claw marks were deep, wide, and clean through the ice.
“Wolves?” Aymer asked, voice low.
She looked up. “No. Worse,” she paused, taking a closer look. “Worgs.”
Taerel frowned. “And what are those? Pretend I’ve never been this far north.”
“They’re bred hunting beasts,” Lysana said, steady but grim. "Orc stock. Twisted through ritual." She brushed more snow aside. "Not born, made that way." 
Aymer leaned in, squinting. “So, wolf-like?”
“Not just wolves,” she corrected, tracing the splayed pad and talon grooves with a gloved finger. “Think bigger. Smarter. A worg’s built for war. Broad as a horse through the chest, jaws that crack bone like kindling. Their eyes shine like wet coals, aware, calculating.”
She brushed aside more snow. “They do not howl or circle. They stalk, drive prey where they want it. They don’t kill out of hunger, but because someone commanded them. And if a pack’s loose here without a handler…”
Her gaze lifted toward the black tree line. “Someone has let them off their chain.”
Luca’s voice came cold and certain. “How many?”
“At least four,” she answered. “Maybe more.”
He nodded once. “Spread out, and let’s keep going.”
The four slipped back into formation, vanishing into the white wilderness, the wind closing behind them like a breath withheld.
The snow clawed at their knees now, a heavy drag with every step. The drifts had thickened along this stretch of the forest road, pushed up in uneven mounds by wind and time. Saplings leaned low beneath the weight of white, their branches creaking with slow complaint. No one spoke for a while, their breaths fogging in the air, the only sounds the wet crunch of boots and the occasional snap of a distant branch.
Taerel broke the silence. “How far do you think they’ve gotten?”
Lysana bent low with a gloved hand just above the crust of snow. She did not answer, she didn’t move. Her eyes followed the twin gouges in the frost, signs of dragging, heavy, deliberate. When she finally spoke, her voice was quiet, but sharp as a blade through cloth.
“That depends on whether their quarry’s alive or dead.”
She looked back, her breath curling like smoke.
“If it’s alive, they’ll move slower,” Lysana said. “They keep the meat fresh that way. Not for eating. For control. If the order was delivery, not slaughter, they won’t rush it.”
Aymer’s face twisted. “Control?”
Lysana brushed snow aside, exposing a blood-soaked tuft of fabric snagged on a root. “They don’t always eat right away. Worgs like to break things first. Legs. Arms. Ribs.” Her voice stayed flat. “They drag it out. Keep it breathing. That’s how they make it behave.”
She paused, then added, “Sometimes they’re told to wait. Keep it alive so their masters can decide what to do with it. Or so they can all feed together.”
She straightened. “But if the order was kill… then they’re already gone. Whatever’s left would be scattered through the trees by now.”
They walked on now with dark images running in their minds, as the tracks guided them deeper into the brush line where the road had long since vanished under snow. It was colder here, the trees thick enough to block the wind and trap the chill like a held breath.
A few minutes later, Lysana raised a hand and crouched again. “They stopped here.”
The team clustered around. The snow was trampled flat in a wide crescent. Bits of leather and snapped belt straps were buried in the slush. One dark patch looked like bile. Another frozen blood.
“Feeding?” Taerel asked.
Lysana shook her head. “No bones. No deep blood pools. Looks like a pause. Maybe a break. Could’ve been checking wounds. Could’ve been resting.”
“Still dragging,” Luca said quietly from behind them, his voice a low growl that almost blended into the wind. Lysana looked at the space where he had been. Then she went back to the tracks. Some questions were kept for later, when there was time to sit with them. He was standing off the trail, crouched behind a tree. “Two bodies. Weight shift is consistent. The small one struggled more.”
He vanished back into the shadows before anyone could respond, already ten paces ahead by the time they registered he’d spoken.
Aymer nudged Taerel with his elbow and smirked. “Ten crowns,” Aymer murmured. “I take down one before you.”
Taerel snorted, adjusting his rifle as he kept walking. “You’ve the aim of a drunk ox in the dark. I’ll take that bet. Just don’t cry when I do, before you get your powder dry.”
“Please,” Aymer grinned. “You’ll be busy figuring out which end of the gun’s the dangerous one.”
Their laughter was soft, but real. A needed flame in the bitter cold.
Behind the laughter, Luca slipped between birch trunks like a shadow given flesh, his coat dissolving into the snow-splattered trees. The cold did not seem to touch him. Neither did the warmth behind him. His world had already narrowed to sign, scent, and what came next. 
As they pushed deeper into the woods, the wilderness grew heavier like a tightening fist. The trees clustered close, twisted in places where the wind had battered them for decades. Ancient trunks groaned under the weight of old snow, and new flakes drifted lazily through the air, settling in quiet piles. Their boots crunched softly in the otherwise undisturbed snow, leaving a narrow line of motion behind them, four figures threading through a land that hadn't seen company in months.
“Do they not have sled dogs for this sort of thing? Why didn’t we get some of those?” Aymer muttered, adjusting the rifle on his back. “Maybe a fur-lined throne while we're at it.”
“Could have stayed home,” Taerel replied dryly, stamping his feet as he walked. “Heard it’s warm next to the fire. Real cozy. Comes with soup.”
Aymer smirked. “And miss all this?” He gestured broadly at the endless woods. “These towering sticks and glorified dandruff?”
Taerel gave a short laugh and nudged his brother with an elbow. “Shut up and keep walking. You are scaring the snow.”
Lysana didn’t laugh. She was too focused, eyes locked forward as she dropped to a knee again, brushing aside the top layer of snow. The lantern’s dim, hooded light glinted off her brow as she studied the nearly invisible signs ahead. She didn't speak for a moment. Her gloved hand hovered over something unseen.
Luca slowed beside her, one hand on his sword hilt, scanning the dark between the trees. “Still have them?” Luca asked. 
She shook her head. “No. That's just it. They've faded. Completely. One moment, I could see where the worgs had run; their stride was long, chaotic, staggered. And now…”
She stood slowly, eyes narrowing into the black beyond. “It is like they vanished. No break in the pattern. No blood. Just… gone.”
A gust of wind carried through the pines, low and cold, stirring the lantern flame. Shadows danced across their faces.
Taerel scanned the dark woods around them, the trees clawing upward like black fingers against the night sky. He turned a slow circle and grunted. “Do… any of you actually remember how we are supposed to get back?”
Aymer raised an eyebrow. “Back where?”
Taerel gestured vaguely toward the way they'd come. “Back to the cove. Y’know. Our camp. The place with food. Heat. Not trees.”
“Oh, you mean civilization? No idea,” Aymer said cheerfully. “I assumed we were just going to keep walking until we found a dragon.”
“Great,” Taerel said, deadpan. “Glad we brought the smart one.”
“I thought you were the smart one,” Aymer replied with a grin. “Luca’s the serious one, I’m the charming one, and you’re, what? The decorative one?”
“Decorative?” Taerel scoffed. “I’m the one who kept us from falling through that frozen creek two hours ago.”
“And I’m the one who told you it was thin ice,” Aymer countered.
“You told me after I stepped on it!” Taerel said. 
Lysana let out a long breath through her nose, glaring over her shoulder. “Both of you shut it, or I will leave you here. The worgs wouldn’t even bother eating you, too much gristle.”
That quieted them. Aymer stepped forward, raising the lantern a bit higher. Lysana reached for the lantern without looking. “Give me that.”
“By all means, Lightbearer.” He handed it over with a slight bow.
She did not dignify it with a response. Instead, she held the lantern low and stepped ahead. “Stay here. I’ll scout it.”
Taerel frowned. “Alone?”
Lysana glanced once at the churned snow behind them, then at the untouched ground ahead. “Yes, alone,” she said. “One set of prints. Less noise. Less scent. If they’re watching, they will see a group. If I move, they’ll miss it. Plus, I am trained to do this.”
She didn’t wait for an answer. “Stay here. Watch the trees. Quiet.”
Without waiting for protest, she moved forward, boots silent on the snow. The others exchanged glances but did as told, crouching low among the thick roots of an ancient birch.
Lysana moved ahead, each step deliberate, her eyes on the ground, sorting through what she knew and what did not fit. Something did not fit. 
“Tracks don’t just disappear,” she whispered. “Not even in snow this deep…”
She swung the dim, hooded lantern in a slow arc, casting amber light across the forest floor. The snow glittered, untouched, pristine. Too pristine.
Then, the ground dropped.
She stopped at the edge of a great, shallow slope, wide as a river bend, stretching out in a long depression like something had scooped the earth out centuries ago and left the wound to fester. Snow coated its rim and clung to the thick branches that hung low over the edge, casting the entire area in dense shadow. But in the middle… her breath caught.
She stared.
Mouth slightly agape.
What she saw in that sunken hollow turned her blood to ice.
It took her a second to recover, then she dropped the lantern light low, shielding it with one hand, and turned back toward the others. She raised her arm high, made a sharp wave toward herself, then dropped low and extended her hand flat toward the snow.
‘Come. Quiet.’
They obeyed immediately.
One by one, the team crawled up beside her on their stomachs, pressing through pine needles and ice. Aymer flopped into place next to her, chin up like a lazy cat, and murmured, “So… what’s got you looking like you saw a ghost get eaten by another ghost?”
She didn’t answer. Not yet. Luca arrived last, easing into position beside her, his hand gripping the frozen ground close to hers, and then they all saw it together.
