The chamber was silent, save for the drip of unseen water and the faint rasp of cloth against stone. It might have been underground, or in the hollow belly of a fortress long abandoned. It didn't matter. The air was damp, heavy with a salt-bitter tang that clung to the walls, and the only light came from the thing in the center, a mirror framed in iron so black it drank the torchlight. Its surface did not reflect. It rippled faintly, as if its silver skin were water disturbed by a breath too shallow to hear.
Before it stood Zhaelor, his long fingers clasped before him. The ridges of his head gleamed with a dull sheen, his eyes entirely without surface, the kind of eyes that took everything in and gave nothing back. Even in stillness, he was not still. The thing that moved was not his hands or his posture but something behind his face, measuring.
"My lord," he said at last, his voice low, resonant, devotion held at arm's length from something older and colder underneath. The mirror's surface shivered, and with it came a voice, calm, distant, yet filling the chamber as though it seeped into the stone itself.
"Speak."
Zhaelor bowed his head. "The first foothold is secure. Stormhaven reels, its wards shattered. Sythrael Korr commands in our name. Your knight returned to you. The city's council stumbles like blind men, ripe for plucking. The populace is already under our hand."
A pause. The mirror's surface trembled with a flicker of heatless light.
"But," Zhaelor admitted, his mouth tightening, "there was a complication. The governor's family was not killed. They were recovered. A small fracture in the plan, nothing more. The governor yet breathes."
The voice in the mirror did not rise, but its stillness made the air itself heavier. "And the king?"
"King Wymark remains himself." Zhaelor's tentacled mouth pressed flat. "Drake broke before the final bell. When he moved, the King was already wearing steel beneath his coat. A whisper reached him ahead of the blade, not one of ours. The changeling was in the hall and could only watch."
For a long moment, the mirror gave nothing back. Then, at last, it spoke. "Do not gnaw on scraps, Zhaelor. The governor's kin were never more than a distraction. As for the king…" The voice grew smoother, almost indulgent. "I will dispatch another hand. A different servant will see to him. Do not trouble yourself with Wymark. His kingdom is already ash in the making."
Zhaelor's eyes narrowed. "Another hand? Another lieutenant?"
"Yes," said the voice. "Hidden from even you, as you were hidden from her. My reasons are my own."
Zhaelor's lips thinned, though he bowed once more. "As you command."
"And my sons?"
Zhaelor answered without pause. He had expected the question. "The first delivered what is left of the Brotherhood to your cause, and in doing so, destroyed what remained of their order from within. The traitor Arthur was slain in doing so, as you desired. The changeling nearly broke the king's line, but the window closed before he could move. Both still serve. Both are weapons loosed."
"Good." The mirror pulsed faintly, and something pleased moved under the calm. "Let them be storms without tether. The kingdoms cannot fight a thousand tempests at once. Spread them thin. Break them piece by piece, until even their stories are dust."
Zhaelor inclined his head. "The Morlin legions wait. They are ready to march at your word."
The mirror's surface flared once, a dull heartbeat of pale fire, before it stilled again. "Then march they shall. And you, Zhaelor…" The voice softened, and the softening was the threat. "Prince of my new kingdom. Steward of what I claim. Do not fail me again."
The iron frame groaned as though something pressed against it from the other side, and then the mirror fell still. The chamber darkened, and the cold settled back in.
Zhaelor stood alone, the torch guttering at his side. For a long time, he did not move. His long fingers flexed once, then curled back into his robes. In the silence, he spoke words not meant for any ear but his own.
"In the end, my lord… we shall see whose chains hold whom."
The mirror gave no answer. Only the steady, dripping silence remained.

The river town had no name worth remembering, a clutch of warehouses and weather-worn inns where barges stopped only long enough to trade, drink, and leave again. Locals called it Darrowmere, after the black stretch of water that bent around its edge. To Nyssa, it felt less like a town and more like a shadow someone had forgotten to sweep away.
The warehouse she sought leaned half-sunken into the mud near the riverbank, its roof sagging, its shutters nailed tight. Lantern light flickered faintly from cracks in the boards, though no footsteps stirred. The air inside stank of damp wood and rat musk.
Nyssa slipped through a side door, boots whispering over warped planks. The silence was thick, the kind that pressed against the ribs. She gripped the hilts of her daggers, though her fingers trembled.
A hand settled on her shoulder.
She spun, blade half-drawn, only to freeze.
Luca stood in the gloom as though carved from it. His storm-gray eyes glinted with a light that belonged to something older than him. His coat, torn and dark from battle, pulled the shadows toward him.
He led her deeper into the dark. The only light came in thin ribbons leaking through cracks in the boards. A small fire burned in one of the corners. 
"I heard your cry," he said. His voice was low, and it had weight to it, the kind that bypassed the ears and landed somewhere behind the sternum. "We will have our justice."
Something went still in her chest, then kicked hard. "Justice?" she whispered. "Or vengeance?"
He tilted his head, almost smiling. "Both. Always both."
Her grip on the dagger slackened. Her unease did not. "What do you have planned?"
"What I always do." Something underneath his voice moved when he said it, and the shadows in the room shifted with it.
She swallowed. "And what is that?"
He paused. "Did the brothers never tell you who I am? What I am?"
Nyssa shook her head. "No."
For a heartbeat, something flickered across his face. Surprise, regret, distance. His gaze dropped, and he exhaled through his teeth, muttering as though to himself. "I stayed too close. Too long." Then the distance closed back over him. "It doesn't matter. Not now."
He lifted his hand. Between his fingers gleamed a shard of black stone, polished smooth as glass. Power hummed faintly from it, a sound that felt more in the bones than in the ears.
"I've accepted your contract," he said. "As I hold this." He turned the shard once in the light. "This once was in Adin’s hand, as it has been through thousands of others. Now it is mine. It came to me at the pass. Now I decide where justice is owed. I hear the prayers of those whom others have failed." He closed his fist around it. "I am finally trusted and ready to hold it. For you, I offer a choice. I can see to this myself, or I can give you the means to take that justice with your own hands. I offer you a different stone. At a different price."
"If I walk this path with you," she asked, her voice small, "what does it mean?"
He stepped closer, the shadows coming with him the way shadows do around a man who has stopped casting one of his own. "It means severance. No ties. No connections. If you become what I am, you will no longer be Nyssa Quill. You will be a shadow, a whisper, a story mothers tell to keep their children in bed. A glance caught from the corner of the eye, and then nothing.” 
He moved around her as he said it, slow, unhurried, a man who had delivered terms that would not be offered again.
"And Dain?" The name slipped out before she could stop it.
Luca turned his back on her, speaking toward the flame. "I see your heart, and I will not pretend otherwise. If you want this power, he cannot be part of you. Not in love. Not in bond. Stand beside him if you must, fight beside him if fate wills it. But he cannot be yours. This is the price. My price."
Nyssa's hands shook. She looked down, and her gaze caught on the gloves she wore, the same ones from that night on the dock. She had never washed them, not truly. A faint stain lingered still, a smear of Dain's blood from when she had pressed her hands to his wounds, holding his life in while the river tried to take it. In her pocket, folded deep, she carried something else. A lock of his hair she had cut that night, when she thought he might not live. A piece of him, hidden.
Nyssa's fingers tightened unconsciously on the small token she'd carried with her from the docks. She shut her eyes, and for a heartbeat, the darkness gave her something softer than blood and grief. Dain's crooked grin when he'd teased her at the tailor's, the way his eyes had flicked nervously after the words left his mouth, as though he hadn't expected her to answer back. The brush of his shoulder against hers when they walked too close, neither giving ground. The night by the fire when she'd caught him watching her hands as she sharpened her daggers, and she'd asked if he wanted a lesson, half mocking, half invitation.
Flirtation had been their armor at first, a game they both knew the rules to. But somewhere between the danger and the silences, it had stopped being only a game. His voice had gentled when he spoke her name. She had found herself holding her breath, waiting for his laugh, his glance, his steadying presence. And when she thought she might lose him on that dock, when she saw him broken beneath those armored boots, it had torn something in her she hadn't been ready to name.
Her breath caught. She wanted to speak, to deny, to cling, but another memory came instead, sharp and unbidden. Riven's face. His grin, his lies, his loyalty. His sacrifice. The river swallowed him whole.
Her eyes burned. Her chest ached. She pressed her gloved hand over her mouth. Held it. Breathed.
When she lowered it, her eyes were dry. Her jaw set. She met Luca's gaze.
"I want it all."
Something shifted in his face. He reached inside his coat and drew out a pure black shard, larger and darker than the one he wore, an onyx the lantern refused. He had kept this one a long time. “It is a seed,” he said. 
He stepped in close and lifted his hand, pressing the shard of onyx against her chest. His other hand cupped the back of her head, steadying her as if she were prey about to be drowned.
He whispered words in a tongue she did not know. The sound was wrong, older than the river outside, older than the stone walls that held them. The shadows in the warehouse stirred, slithering across the floor toward them. They gathered, circling, tightening. They crawled up her boots, over her legs, winding up her arms, pouring into her mouth, her lungs, her veins.
Nyssa gasped. Her eyes flared blue, brighter, colder, unnatural. She staggered, then straightened, cloak now black as midnight, her old clothes burned away into something woven of shadow itself. Her hair blazed red in contrast, almost aflame in the gloom. Around her neck, where the shard had pressed, a necklace now hung, black onyx set in silver, pulsing faintly with power.
Everything she had worn, everything she was, burned away. It was all gone. She was remade into shadow. Everything but one thing. Deep in the dark of her new cloak, her fingers closed on the lock of hair, still folded, still his. The change had burned away the rest and left her that. She closed her hand around it and said nothing.
She looked at her hands, no longer trembling. She breathed, and the shadows breathed with her.
Her voice, when it came, was steady. "There's nowhere Merrick can hide now."
Luca smiled. "Good." He let his hand fall away, his shadow merging with hers until they were one. "Then let us begin."
The lanterns guttered out. Darkness took over the whole warehouse.
