Snow crunched beneath iron-shod hooves as Luca and his team rode north, the frozen trail winding away from the last fragile breath of civilization, Riverrun’s End.
Luca rode at the front, his dark cloak trailing behind him like the wing of a raven. Though he said little, the others followed his lead without question. Behind him, Lysana rode with reins held loose in one gloved hand, casting a lingering glance back at the town disappearing into the swirling gray. Taerel kept his rifle slung tight across his back, the faint scrape of metal on leather marking each steady trot. Aymer, wrapped in wool and waxed canvas, tugged his scarf higher against the rising wind.
Riverrun’s End had served its purpose, nothing more than a cold waypoint on the road north. Built where the Andralis River spilled from the frozen heights of Blackfrost Lake, the gray-stone port town clung to life like a barnacle on a dying whale. It was neutral ground by decree, a trading post and political buffer between three wary kingdoms that still bore the scars of war while diplomats toasted peace far away in Opindale.
The town rose in weathered tiers, sloping upward from the docks that could berth six merchant ships and two river barges. Timber-and-stone buildings leaned against one another like old soldiers, battered by snow and wind. At the north end of the wharf, a drydock scaffold stood like a ribcage over a disassembled two-mast cog, where the hammering of tools and the creaking of timbers never ceased.
Four squat stone barracks overlooked the harbor, their yards churned to slush by boots and training drills. A detachment of thirty Aldorian soldiers occupied them, sent as liaison to the new garrison forming near the Pass.
Beyond the barracks, narrow lanes wound between rows of cramped houses, their chimneys coughing gray smoke into the frozen air. Traps and drying racks hung from porches, heavy with hare, elk hide, and river fish. Further inland, a cluster of shops surrounded a small cobbled square where merchants sold smoked meat and rough spirits beneath sagging awnings.
By the time their packs were loaded and Riverrun’s End had vanished behind the veil of snow, the silence broke.
“Still think we should’ve stayed the night,” Taerel said, his voice carrying through the cold. “That inn had beds. Real beds. The kind where you wake up sore in the hips instead of the spine.”
“You saying you need softness, Ty? I thought your people slept on stone and bathed in ice water,” Lysana said, grinning.
Taerel replied, “We do. But that doesn’t mean we don’t appreciate the occasional mattress that doesn’t squeal like it’s being murdered.”
Aymer chimed in, “You were just mad because the innkeeper wanted to braid your hair.”
Taerel spoke through gritted teeth, sounding a bit frustrated. “He tried to oil it. Called me a ‘Western lion’. I am not… No. I am absolutely not built for that kind of attention.”
Lysana laughed, short, bright, and warm. Even Luca cracked a grin that didn’t quite reach his eyes. It was a strange dynamic, four people from different lives, strangers no more than fifty hours ago, each carrying old scars, riding toward either new ones or maybe something else.
But Luca said nothing more, already watching the road ahead. When Lysana fell behind in adjusting her saddle straps, Luca slowed his mount until they were side by side.
Luca said quietly, “You good?”
She looked up, surprised. “Fine. Just a strap.”
 He nodded. “Call out if you need anything, or if you fall too far behind.”
This small exchange lifted Lysana’s eyebrows, just a little.
They rode for a bit longer, and it was late morning when the snowfall intensified. The clouds dropped low, iron-heavy and choking the sun, casting the world in shades of blue and steel. Trees became silhouettes. The wind bit harder now, stinging exposed skin. It wasn’t just snow anymore. It was sleet mixed in, sharp as ground glass.
 They rounded a bend and reined in abruptly.
 A wooden cart stood sideways in the road, wheels half-buried in ice. Its driver, a lean figure in patchwork leather armor, stepped out slowly from behind it. He held a chipped longsword in one hand and in the other, a short club. No insignia, no colors. But every inch of him screamed desperate and dangerous.
"Well, well," the bandit leader said. "Look what the storm dragged in." He grinned widely, teeth yellow in the gray light. “You’ve wandered too far from your warm little town, rich ones. Those cloaks alone could buy me a new cart. The weapons…” His eyes lingered on Luca’s dagger, “Maybe we’ll take those as well, drop them slowly.”
"I don't think you know who you're dealing with," Taerel said. 
From the tree line, three more figures emerged, one of them leveling a repeating crossbow. Another man shouldered a rusted hatchet, and the third was a woman with two long knives, licking her lips like she was already counting coin.
"I don't think it matters now," the bandit said, snapping his fingers. 
The crossbowman called out. “Dibs on the rifles! Especially the long one with the iron-banded barrel!”
Lysana whispered to Luca, “They’re too relaxed,” her eyes now locked on the treeline. “They never planned to let us walk.”
So here's how this goes," the bandit leader said. "Leave the weapons, the coats, the packs. Maybe we'll let you walk." 
"That's not a deal, that's begging for an arrow," Lysana said. 
"No," Luca said low and flat. 
"What was that?" the bandit squinted. 
"Here's the only deal you're getting," Luca said. 
He stepped forward. His cloak blew back in the wind, revealing the dark obsidian-threaded armor beneath, dull and jagged like cooled volcanic glass.
“Leave everything and walk south. Do not look back.” He took a long, slow breath through his nose, “Or you can stay, and never see the light of day again.” Luca said with no emotion in his voice.
There was a beat of silence. In one instance, the bandit leader laughed a deep, hearty laugh. “That’s the stupidest threat I’ve—”
Luca did not smile. He did not speak. He simply looked at the bandit leader with the particular patience of someone for whom the outcome had already been settled, and waited for the man to make the last decision of his life. 
 A ripple passed through the ground.
The bandit leader’s expression turned to a snarl in a flash, and he snapped his fingers 
The crossbowman lifted his weapon. A jet of blackness exploded from behind him, wrapping around his arms and yanking him violently into the trees. There was a dull crack of bone. He didn’t scream.
One of the knife-wielders spun toward the noise. A dagger flashed from Luca’s hand, obsidian-edged and whisper-quick. It hit the woman in the throat with surgical precision. She fell to her knees, coughing blood, and collapsed without a sound.
The dagger was gone from her throat, back in Luca’s belt. Lysana saw the dagger leave his hand and return to his belt in the space of a single breath. She blinked once. Made note of it, asked nothing. There would be time for questions later, assuming the later arrived. 
The last two rushed forward, howling.
But Luca was already moving. He stepped sideways into the shadow of his horse and disappeared.
He reappeared behind the axe-wielder, grabbing him by the jaw and yanking him backward into the dark. There was a muffled groan, then nothing.
The bandit leader was the last one. He turned and ran.
But his shadows caught him before he made it ten steps. A lance-like object jetted out of the darkness of his own shadow and pierced his heart. He crumpled to the frozen ground.
 And then… silence.
Taerel stared at the empty road for a long moment. When he spoke, the aristocratic polish was still there, but it had been shaken loose from its setting. "I will need some time," he said carefully, "to decide how I feel about traveling with you." 
Aymer said with eyes never leaving the treeline, “I’m not sure whether to thank you… or decide I’ll be sleeping lightly from now on.”
Lysana smiled faintly, “Both.”
Luca said nothing. He stood in the center of the snow-slick road, breathing evenly, eyes scanning for more threats. Then he turned back to his horse.
“We move,” Luca said. 
They rode in silence. The storm howled louder.
Snow piled in thick drifts as the storm closed its fists around the road. The bandits had long since vanished into the whiteness. Their corpses were left for the cold to claim. Luca rode in silence at the front, his broad shoulders hunched, cloak rimmed in ice. His face was calm, distant, unreadable, his hands steady on the reins despite the storm.
The wind howled through the trees with an ancient, bitter voice. Snow fell in slanted waves, not flakes but claws, wet and heavy, biting into skin even through layers of wool and leather. Visibility narrowed to a hand’s span in front of their faces.
“Storm’s turning,” Taerel muttered, pulling his scarf higher over his mouth. “We need shelter, or we’ll freeze upright in the saddle.”
“No argument here,” Lysana said beside him, brushing snow from her brow. Her voice was clipped, focused. She scanned the treeline. “There’s a cliff bend not far from here. If it’s still the same as I remember, it should have a hollow just past the rise.”
It was Luca who nodded first, his gaze piercing through the snow as if he could see the shape of it already. A few minutes later, they reached it—tucked beneath the curve of a rocky overhang, half-buried by snow and ringed by sparse fir trees, the hollow loomed like a mouth left open in warning.
“Dismount, head for the cove,” Luca said, already swinging down. “Get those horses inside.”
They moved fast. Taerel and Aymer tethered the horses near the back, while Lysana led them under the shelter. Aymer, tall and powerfully built, pulled his rifle from the saddle scabbard with gloved fingers. Snow melted along its edges. As he moved, his shoulder brushed Taerel, shorter but no less capable, with tawny skin, piercing eyes, and a jagged braid that trailed from his temple like a whipcord.
“What are we going to do for a fire?” Lysana asked. “It is still freezing in here." 
“I don’t know, all I can find is wood that is all wet.” Replied Luca
“You want firewood,” Aymer said, tossing a glance toward his brother, “you’re the one with the hatchet.”
Taerel rolled his eyes but grinned. “At least I know how to use it.”
Taerel crouched by a half-rotted log at the edge of the hollow, outer bark soaked and useless. With practiced hands, he split it, peeling back to the dry heart. Aymer hovered too close, offering advice he didn’t need.
“You’re angling it wrong,” Aymer said.
“I’ve lit more fires than you’ve had meals,” Taerel shot back, not looking up.
“Aye and half of them smoked us out,” Aymer grinned.
“At least mine burns,” Taerel snapped. 
The spark caught then, flaring to life just to prove him right.
Lysana knelt, unpacking two small wax-wrapped tinder bundles from her satchel, southern kindling bought cheap in Tenlane, good for starting fires in damp weather. “Use these first,” she said, tossing them over.
Taerel caught one, tucked it under the dry splinters, and struck steel to flint. Sparks leapt like insects. Smoke hissed. Then, slowly, the fire caught. The golden glow spread. Shadows flickered across the hollow walls, jagged stone, and hanging icicles like frozen teeth. Their breath misted, drawn into the warmth like moths to flame. They laid out bedrolls in a rough crescent, kept the horses close, and built a small windbreak from stones and a weather-beaten tarp to shield the fire’s glow. 
Aymer went to find more wood to bring to Taerel to ensure they had enough for the night, quickly returning with a good armful. Taerel went to work. He stacked extra wood near the fire. “Don’t want to be stumbling around trying to find fuel in this storm, at night.”
Dinner was simple but warm. Dried meat soaked in broth, boiled roots, and crumbling dark bread. Aymer passed around a battered flask of honey-wine, sharp and sweet, and they drank in grateful silence.
“I’ve missed real food,” Taerel said, tearing off a piece of bread and dunking it into the pot. “Food in Riverrun’s End tastes like it was chewed once already.” He grimaced, recalling the taste.
“Dried feet with ambition,” Aymer added.
“Don’t knock ambition,” Lysana said, her mouth twitching at the corner. “Keeps you warm longer than soup.”
“You've been up here before, right?” Taerel asked, adjusting a boot near the fire. “You know these lands?”
Lysana nodded. “I trained under Harmon. Did a sweep through these hills for a patrol mission years back. This hollow was a shelter marker then as well. Used by long-range scouts and supply runners.”
“Ever see anything strange?” Aymer asked, raising a brow beneath his mask.
Lysana hesitated, her eyes glinting in the firelight. “Not see. But we heard things. Old wind stories. Hushed echoes through the trees, voices that didn’t match anyone in the patrol. Woke to claw marks in the snow once, deep ones, far too wide for any wolf.”
Taerel leaned in. “What kind of beast?”
“Didn’t stick around to ask,” Lysana said. “But the locals, few that there are, call them frost-haunts. Say the spirits out here never sleep. Say this land remembers blood.”
Silence fell for a beat, the fire cracking.
Taerel exhaled sharply. “Great. Just great.”
“Better than Luca’s ghost stories,” Aymer muttered.
At that, Luca chuckled, low and smooth, like rolling coals. He had been sitting quietly near the edge, head down, watching the flame curl against the stone. When he looked up, his eyes gleamed with the knowledge of too many winters.
“You want a real tale?” he asked. “I’ll give you one.”
“Creator help us,” Taerel said.
But Luca’s voice came quietly, steadily.
“Not far from here, past the frozen ridgelines, they speak of something older than the mountains. Something that hunts by warmth. Doesn’t need to see. Just... feels you.”
“What is it?” Aymer asked, tone more serious now.
“No name,” Luca said. “Not one it ever gave. The tale is. It mimics the voice of its prey.” He paused, letting his eyes move around the group. “Hides in the storm.” Lower, he said, “Screams like a dying child.”
No one laughed this time.
Aymer was first to speak, “So what really can we expect?” Hoping the story Luca just told was a campfire story and not true.
Lysana gave him a look of despair, as if to say, "You poor boy, you have no idea of the horrors up here." “Wolves. Elk. The usual." She said it without flinching and looked back at the fire, and if there was more to say, she had already decided this was not the night for it. 
Taerel looked at Lysana. “Are there really folk up here? Settlers? Homesteads?”
She nodded. “A few. Some hunters, off-grid types. People who wanted to disappear, build their own corners of the world where no one bothers them.”
“Or where no one finds them,” Aymer said quietly.
The fire popped. For a long moment, nothing moved but the smoke curling toward the icicle ceiling. The group finished up their meals and cleaned up a bit, and then repacked their stuff. Their bedrolls were laid out in a rough circle around the fire. Luca spoke up, clearing his throat. “We should stand watch,” Luca suggested, shifting position to a good vantage point. “I will take the first shift.”
Lysana said, “No need, I will take second then.”
Aymer said, “Since I know Taerel needs his beauty sleep, I will take third.”
"Fine. I will take the last one.” Taerel glanced at Aymir. “Thank you, brother.” 
They all gave a little chuckle and went to make their provisions for the rest of the evening. 
Aymir had been watching her since the hollow. Not in the way Luca watched her, which was its own thing entirely, but the way a soldier watches someone they are deciding whether to trust with their flank. When the others were busy with their packs, he said quietly, "You've done cold missions before. Properly cold." It was not flattery. It was reconnaissance. Lysana looked at him. "A few." He gave a short nod. "Good to know," he said, and went back to his kit. 
The fire burned low, crackling softly beneath the growing hush of the night. Shadows danced against the rocky walls of their makeshift camp, licking across bedrolls and gear in slow, flickering patterns. One by one, the others settled into their blankets, the silence broken only by the occasional shuffle of boots or the sigh of cold breath. Luca remained seated by the fire, shoulders squared as he took first watch, the hood of his cloak drawn up against the biting air.
Luca leaned forward, something that might have been a grin tugging at the edge of his mouth. “All right,” he said. “Might as well pass the time. Have you all ever heard about the frost-wasps up here?”
Taerel groaned. “Don’t start.”
“No, no,” Aymer said, grinning. “Let the man tell it.”
Luca’s voice lowered. The firelight cast shadows over his face, giving his eyes a glint that could have been amusement or warning.
“Way up north, closer to the Forgotten Lands, there are creatures that don’t belong on this side of the mountains. One of them’s the frost-wasp. Big thing, body like a bloated beetle, wings like torn silk. It flies low in the storm winds, seeps into your camp like smoke. You don’t hear it until it’s already too close.”
Lysana snorted. “That’s not real.”
“Ah, but here’s the thing,” Luca said, holding up a finger. “They don’t sting. Not like bees. No, they... burrow. Into warm flesh. Lay their eggs. You feel a tickle in your neck, and next thing you know, you’re the hive.”
Taerel made a show of pulling his scarf up over his neck. “Now I know you’re full of it.”
“Oh, maybe,” Luca said. “But some of the settlers up here swear by it. Had a trader in Greyharbor say he saw a man burst open right at his campfire, full of squirming larvae.”
Lysana rolled her eyes but pulled her cloak tighter around herself. “Stop playing shadow boy.”
They laughed, even as the fire popped. The conversation drifted from there, hunting stories, old friends, and strange foods from far-off lands. Luca remained still at the edge, his eyes reflecting firelight like twin coals, half-listening but not joining. It was long before the conversation died out and gave way to sleep. 
The wind, once relentless and needling, had faded not long after sunset, leaving behind only a gentle, steady fall of snow. It drifted in through the broken trees and scattered crags like fine silver ash, catching in the moonlight until the whole mountaintop looked like a world carved from pearl. The silence that came with it was almost holy. But Luca couldn’t feel its peace. His eyes scanned the pale horizon, but his thoughts were turned inward, chewing on doubt. He'd always worked alone, in the shadows, solving problems with clean exits. Now, he was responsible for three others. 
He shook his head clear and went to check on the horses. He removed their feeding bags. checking on their general well-being, making sure today’s long ride hadn’t injured them. Stay busy, and you won’t think about it, his inner dialogue told him. But no matter how busy he made himself, cleaning up the camp and watching the opening, his insecurities kept creeping back in. 
A while later, Lysana took his place. She offered him a brief nod, nothing more, and settled in at the edge of the camp, sword across her lap. While the others slept, she worked silently, pulling a cloth along the length of her blade with calm precision. Oil glistened along the steel, catching bits of moonlight in her careful movements. She wasn’t worrying about leadership, or the road ahead, or whatever strange things might be lurking beyond the veil of white. She was simply present, letting the night exist around her. The snowfall softened even more now, slow and rhythmic, like the sky had grown tired of fighting. It was the kind of quiet that made you forget the world was ever cruel. Just white air, silver light, and the slow ritual of steel being tended. Lysana didn’t need to say a word. The stillness was enough.
Then came Aymer’s watch. He sat with his back to a frost-slick boulder, cloak drawn tight, eyes tracing the dark horizon. His fingers absently rolled a carved wooden token between his hands, a small charm Taerel had given him long ago. He wasn’t looking for trouble, not exactly. Just listening and watching.
Then it came. A scream ripped through the night, human, ragged, and gone too quickly for wind. It left behind a silence that felt wrong.
Aymer was already on his feet. “That was no wind,” he snapped, rifle in hand. “Up. All of you.”
 Taerel blinked groggily. “What?”
 “Something’s out there. I heard a scream.”
 Lysana sat up, reaching for her weapon. Luca was already throwing on his cloak.
 Snow fell softly now, whispering against the earth. The night was still, but that scream had cut through it like a blade.
 “What direction?” Luca asked.
 “Northwest, I think,” Aymer said.
 Lysana looked to Luca. “Could be travelers. Or settlers.”
Luca frowned. Damn it. Cannot ignore it, filled his head, but it was not spoken. 
 “You said some folks live up this way,” Taerel added. “Hunting lodges, off-grid homesteads. People looking to get away from everything.”
 “Or run from something,” Aymer muttered.
 Luca moved quickly, “We leave the camp set up. Bring only what we need. Weapons. Cloaks. No torches, we don’t want to give our position away too soon. But grab that hooded lantern, that should work.”
 “Let’s move,” Luca said, stepping out into the snow.
 The night stretched wide before them, cold and silent again.
They moved with purpose, firelight falling behind them as they stepped into the snow. Cold whispered across the hollow.
The night ahead waited. Wide. White. Watching.
