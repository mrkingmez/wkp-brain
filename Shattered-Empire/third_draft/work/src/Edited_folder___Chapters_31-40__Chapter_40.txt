The Joblins were already on them.

A tide of snarling, gray-skinned bodies poured from the breach, dozens, then hundreds, spilling over one another in a mad, slathering rush. Their screeches split the night, some guttural, others sharp and yipping. Teeth bared. Eyes wild. Clawed hands grasped at blades and flesh. Behind them, a handful of Goblins wove through the swarm, more coordinated, less reckless, but no less bloodthirsty.

“Hold the line!” Luca shouted.

Steel rang out as the first wave hit.

Luca’s dagger buried itself in a Joblin’s neck. He spun, the other blade catching another in the gut. Blood sprayed, black and greasy, with a smell like burnt oil and decay. His belt pulsed with heat as the blades vanished and reappeared at his sides, already ready for the next kill.

To his left, Dorn bellowed as he swung his axe in wide, brutal arcs. Each blow cleaved a path through the swarming bodies. The man looked like a blood-drenched windmill.

Halden had his back against a crate, one hand keeping a civilian medic safe behind him while the other fired arrow after arrow into the horde, each shot clean and quick.

Sil Brant fought as a man possessed, his saber flashing in tight, vicious cuts. He’d already taken a slice across the thigh but hadn’t slowed down.

The air stank of blood, bile, and unwashed bodies. It filled Luca’s nostrils with every breath. The Joblins didn’t fight like soldiers. They didn’t parry. They didn’t flank. They just charged. Reckless. Feral. Biting. One leapt at Mira, scrambling up a corpse to get height, teeth snapping for her face. She drove her blade into its throat and shoved it away, gasping.

Luca ducked under another, slashing it open from hip to chin. His eyes burned from the smoke and stink. His arms ached. They were still coming. Everywhere, climbing over their own dead, clawing for anything warm, something to kill.

“Shields!” he barked, pushing one of the soldiers back as a Goblin with twin knives ducked low and struck high. Luca intercepted with a rising slash that snapped bone and dropped the thing cold.

A scream tore through the noise.

Fenrick.

Luca turned just in time to see the youngest archer go down, tackled by two Joblins who bit and clawed through his armor. He vanished beneath the writhing mass. Mira screamed, breaking formation, but Dorn caught her by the arm and yanked her back.

“There are too many!” someone shouted.

“Keep fighting!” Luca growled.

Above them, the ridge flared.

CRACK.

Aymir’s rifle boomed. One Goblin spun in midair as the bullet punched through its head.

CRACK.
CRACK.

Taerel fired next, slower, more measured, but no less deadly. His shoulder was trembling now. He’d switched to a supported firing position, resting the rifle on a stacked brace of rock. It slowed him, but it gave him steadiness.

"Too many," Taerel muttered. "They really couldn't coordinate a faster death, could they? This is dragging terribly."

Aymir reloaded fast, jaw set. “If we keep this up, we’ll run dry before they stop coming.”

Taerel looked down at the valley. “You thinking what I’m thinking?”

Aymir gave a grim nod. “Time to join the fun.”

A gust of freezing wind dragged more screams up the ridge, close enough to rattle their boots. The brothers stood, slinging rifles over their shoulders. Taerel winced, the strain pulling across his healing wound, but he said nothing. Before he turned, he reached into his bag and pulled free two black, fist-sized orbs. Then they descended fast, blades drawn, boots kicking up frost and blood-drenched soil.

Below, the formation was barely holding. Joblins were crawling over corpses, friend and foe alike, biting, ripping, shrieking. One had its jaw locked around a soldier’s thigh. Luca stabbed it twice before it let go.

He was covered in blood now, some was his, most not. Sweat stung his eyes. His breath came ragged. But still, he moved.

The Joblins didn’t care about numbers. They didn’t care about death. They just came.

One lunged for his face, too close to dodge.

Thunk.

An arrow drove through its temple.

Luca glanced to the left. Lysana was there, face grim, the scarf at her collar snapping in the wind, bow already drawn again.

He exhaled. “Good shot.”

“Get your damn head down,” she snapped, nocking another arrow.

Luca grinned despite himself and dove back into the fray.

The battle roared.

Steel met flesh. Teeth met bone. Soldiers yelled, stumbled, and shoved each other upright. The civilians were retreating into the far tents, dragging the wounded with them. Halden limped after them, blood trailing from his calf.

Dorn dropped to one knee beside Fenrick’s body. "Gone," he growled. "Little fool didn’t even scream long."

Luca felt the pang, deep and hard. The boy hadn’t belonged here. None of them did. But now it was too late for regret.

Another screech cut across the field.

A Goblin this time, lean, armored, and fast, ducked through the clash and drove a blade at Brant. Brant twisted, barely dodged, and caught the Goblin with a thrust under the chin.

But they were tiring.

All of them.

Luca’s vision pulsed at the edges, a dull throbbing haze every time he blinked. Even his hearing warped, screams stretching like they were underwater. He wasn’t the only one. Even the strongest of them, Dorn, was beginning to grunt with each movement.

The tide hadn’t stopped.

Then came the roar.

Not from the enemy. From Taerel.

“DOWN! BLACKFIRE OUT!”

Luca hit the ground, yelling the same command to those around him.

Everyone ducked.

Taerel, standing ten paces from the front line, hurled a metal orb the size of a clenched fist into the advancing swarm. It gleamed with runes, pitched black, threaded with red.

It landed.

BOOM.

The explosion was thunder. Fire. Shrapnel. Dirt. Blood.

A pulse of death.

Dozens of Joblins disappeared in the blast, torn apart mid-scream. The concussion knocked several back into their allies. Even the Goblins hesitated, but only for a breath.

They regrouped and came again.

The humans surged.

“We’ve got momentum! Push the advantage.” Aymir bellowed, slamming his blade into a stunned Goblin.

Luca pushed forward again, daggers flashing. This time, the tide felt different, but the numbers still pressed in.

Bodies fell faster now. Arrows sang overhead. Dorn rallied the line with a brutal roar. Even Mira, face streaked with tears, found her rhythm again, every shot fired with surgical rage.

Taerel hurled the second orb.

This one landed at the edge of the breach, the tunnel mouth the Joblins had poured out of.

BOOM, deeper this time, a rolling detonation that punched through the earth like a buried thunderclap. Stone collapsed. Tents buckled. A cascade of rubble buried the tunnel, sealing it with finality. Dust billowed outward.

Luca turned, panting, blood dripping from a dozen shallow cuts. His shoulders ached. His hands shook. But for the first time that night, they had the momentum.

“Great shot! Tell me you’ve got one left or we’re all screwed!”

“Last one,” answered Taerel.

“Then now is the time to push forward!" Luca bellowed.

The swarm was thinning, but not breaking. Goblins fought with savage precision, parrying and lunging. The Joblins never fled. They kept coming. Bruised. Bleeding. Dying and yet still coming.

And then… A groan. One of the Orcs went from motionless to beginning to rise.

Then another.

And another.

Twelve total were now standing.

Luca’s stomach turned to stone. The poison hadn’t just failed, it betrayed them. 

But these hadn’t just survived. They were moving. Slow. Twitching. But alive.

Massive. Hulking.

Still deadly.

Aymir stood beside him now, panting, sword bloodied. “That’s... not good.”

“No,” Luca said. “It’s not.”

Taerel chimed in, “If they want to die fighting, who are we to stop them? Let's give them what they want, death.”

The three of them nodded their heads and got back to work, just as the Orcs charged. Even poisoned and unbalanced, the twelve brutes that had risen from the wreckage held ground like stone towers in a river. Their poisoned minds twitched, their bodies buckled, but their violence hadn’t dulled. Not enough. They bought time. And blood.

Goblins and Joblins regrouped. The swarm surged again from every shadow, climbing the wreckage, crawling over each other, shrieking war cries in a hundred ragged voices. The humans’ momentum cracked, then buckled. Screams tore through the ranks. Blades flashed. Bones shattered. The snow turned black.

It should have been a rout.

But the tide turned again, and this time, toward death.

Luca ducked under a rusted cleaver, swept the legs out from a charging Goblin, and drove his short sword through the skull of a crawling Joblin trying to bite his boot. Blood hissed against the snow, steaming. His lungs were raw. His eyes burned. His muscles screamed for reprieve.

A soldier went down, a clean slice across the thigh, then a dozen Joblins on him in a blink. They tore and bit and slashed. The man didn’t scream for long.

“Hold the line!” Brant roared, trying to push the soldiers tighter.

Sound folded in on itself, metal grinding, boots skidding, someone choking on smoke. And still the tide poured in.

A massive Orc, one of the twelve, barreled straight for Luca’s exposed back, broad, snarling, axe raised high.

Taerel caught the movement and tried to raise his rifle. Pain lanced through his shoulder, white-hot. The stock wouldn't seat right. He fired anyway. Missed. Swore. Adjusted and tried again. 

Another miss, snow kicked up beside the Orc’s foot, but the beast never slowed. “Luca!” Taerel bellowed.

Too late. Luca turned just in time to see the axe begin its arc. Instinct took over.

He pivoted, parried high with his short sword, but the Orc’s weight overwhelmed him. He twisted, dragging the brute’s momentum to the side, and hurled the Orc forward into a tangle of Goblins. They collapsed in a mess of limbs and curses.

CRACK, CRACK rang out, and Taerel found his mark this time as the Orc lay still.

Yet, more were closing and closing fast.

Two shapes flanked him, a Joblin and a Goblin, blades drawn, mouths open with madness, one from the side, the other from behind.

Arrows whistled.

One struck the Joblin in the chest.

The other pierced the Goblin’s eye. It fell without a sound.

Luca turned, breath ragged, heart pounding.

Lysana.

Still standing. Still calm. Still with him.

Their eyes locked. She smiled, and for half a breath, that was enough. He saw there was something more behind it.

He mouthed ‘thank you’ for saving him again. Something caught his eye. A shadow above.

A Joblin dropped from a ledge, dagger raised.

Luca saw it. He reacted, daggers flung from his belt, whistling through the air with lethal purpose.

He watched as time slowed down around the whole battle. He knew. He knew, even in the frozen stretch of that heartbeat, the dagger would be too late.

The creature landed on her shoulders, driving his blade into the side of her neck.

Lysana gasped. Blood blossomed, bright against her leathers. Her knees buckled.

The Joblin never got another breath. Luca’s blades found it instantly, one through the back, one through the throat. It twitched. Then died.

But she was down.

Luca didn’t scream. He moved. Spinning. Cutting. Breaking. His body was on fire. His blade was death. He didn’t pause. Didn’t falter.

He left a trail of death behind him. A Goblin rushed, he ducked, rolled, came up behind it, and opened its spine with one slice. A Joblin leapt, he impaled it in mid-air, yanked the blade free, and let it drop twitching.

Fifteen paces. 

Ten.

Another Orc reared up, weapon high, and Luca’s dagger slammed into its eye, dropping it like a tower.

Five paces.

He was there. Lysana’s body lay in the snow, eyes still open. He dropped beside her. Cradled her. Blood on his hands, on his neck, his chest.

The battlefield fell away. She blinked.

Once. Tried to speak. Couldn’t.

But she smiled, a final gift. Her fingers slipped from his, a moment later. She was gone. Her hand went limp in his.

Something inside him stopped. Not broke, stopped. Like a spring gone still.

Blood pooled beneath her. He took her hand anyway.

All sound dulled except the wheezing in his chest. The pulse in his ears. The weight of her hand in his.

He whispered her name. Once. Twice.

Aymir reached them first, sliding on his knees, hands shaking. “No—no no no—”

He gathered her up, sobbing openly. Her scarf bunched in his fists.

“She was right here. She was right…”

Taerel came slower, favoring his arm. He didn’t cry. Didn’t breathe.

Just stared. He had never seen death up close. Not like this. Not to someone who’d laughed beside him. It was the first time someone he knew had died, and died in front of him. 

Luca was still.

Then he rose. A new look on his face. Lysana's body lay back in the snow.

He looked at the battlefield, the writhing masses, the beasts that had taken her, the nightmare not yet over.

He reached for the shard and pulled. Every ounce. All of it.

And the world went dark.

At first, there was only confusion, a collective gasp as sight snapped away. The Darkness fell over everyone and everything. It wasn’t the absence of light. It was the presence that filled this grove. It filled the lungs. It pressed behind the eyes. It smothered everything like a wet cloth over a flame.

Sight vanished. 

Sound dulled.

Complete deprivation of the sense.

Then the screams began.

Not human.

Not normal.

Screams like metal folding into itself. Like something clawing its way out of a stone throat. Screams from things that didn’t speak, just fed.

There were gurgles. Snaps. Wet, sucking gasps.

Cracks like bone being chewed.

A hiss, long and low, that slithered into the eardrums and coiled there like smoke.

Someone tried to shout a warning, but only a strangled rasp came out. A soldier whispered, “Gods…”

Something brushed Mira’s face.

She screamed.

Dorn drew his axe and swung blindly, hitting nothing.

One of the younger Archers collapsed, sobbing, hands over his ears.

Halden chanted prayers, faster and faster, the words blurring.

Somewhere, something let out a bubbling howl that rose until it fractured into three distinct voices, all shrieking together. The screams would curdle fresh milk. It was nothing any of them had heard before. Aymir thought the scream in the inferno plate would sound like laughter beside these gut-wrenching shrieks.

And still the darkness held. Long enough that time stopped meaning anything. No one moved. The snow stopped falling. Even the wind forgot how to breathe. The battlefield was a sealed tomb of screams and slaughter.

Then—

A single sound.

Crack.

The sound of a goblet hitting a stone floor. Loud enough to sound like something breaking open the sky.

As fast as the darkness engulfed them, the darkness vanished.

Light returned. The moon shone. The snow returned, and most importantly, they all could see, but what was around them scared them more than the darkness.

Around them was nothing but death.

Joblins looked gnawed apart. Goblins lay folded like snapped traps. Orcs slumped where they stood, as if the life had been poured out of them.

Three human soldiers who had fallen during the battle were now visible. There were two more on the ground, barely breathing, blood painting the frost.

The rest stood silent. Frozen.

At the center of it all, Luca knelt beside Lysana again.

The onyx shard was gone.

Only black dust remained.

Taerel limped forward.

Aymir followed.

They stopped beside him, staring at the ruin.

“Luca,” Aymir whispered. “What… happened?”

No answer.

Taerel swallowed. "Luca." One word. Nothing more.

Luca released her hand, looking to the heavens, and slowly rose. The wind tugged at his cloak. He turned, gaze empty.

Aymir stepped forward. "Luca." He took another step closer. "Please. Say something." Aymir tried to speak again, but the words collapsed in his throat. His voice, dry as dust, cracked.

“I did.”
