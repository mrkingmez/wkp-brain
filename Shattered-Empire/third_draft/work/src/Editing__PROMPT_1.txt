Evaluate:
• Are sentence lengths varied?
• Are short lines used strategically?

• Does the scene rely too heavily on balanced sentences?

STEP 3 — VOICE CONVERGENCE DETECTION
Without looking at tags, determine:
• Do characters respond in structurally similar ways?
• Do they use similar phrasing patterns?
• Do they share the same emotional expression style?
If yes, label as:
“Voice Drift — Interchangeability Risk”
STEP 4 — SAFE LANGUAGE CLUSTER DETECTION
Scan for clusters of:
• like
• just
• kind of
• sort of
• maybe
• probably
• I think
• It was like
• As if
If more than 3 appear within close proximity, flag:
“Weak Language Density Spike”
STEP 5 — PRESSURE ESCALATION CHECK
If the scene includes conflict, fear, confrontation, or ideological tension:
Check:
• Does speech compress?
• Does silence increase?
• Does humor drop?
• Does cadence sharpen?
If not, flag:
“Stress Response Underdeveloped”
STEP 6 — SUBTEXT VS EXPOSITION BALANCE
Identify lines where:
• Emotion is stated directly
• Motivation is explained instead of implied
• Conflict is clarified too quickly
Flag as:
“Overt Explanation — Subtext Opportunity”
STEP 7 — SILENCE USAGE ANALYSIS
Determine:
• Are pauses used strategically?
• Are powerful lines followed by space?
• Does dialogue ever stop abruptly for weight?
If not, flag:
“Silence Underutilized”
STEP 8 — IDEOLOGICAL DISTINCTION CHECK
If multiple factions are present:
Evaluate:
• Do their belief systems affect cadence?
• Do their sentences reflect ideology?
• Does moral framing differ?
If ideological speech patterns blur, flag:
“Ideological Flattening”
STEP 9 — AUDIBLE PRESENCE SCAN
Evaluate whether distinct characters possess audible identity at the scene level.
Ask:
• Do culturally or class-distinct characters have at least one clear sound-direction cue in their introduction?
• Is accent or speech grain introduced once and then trusted?
• Under stress, does audible texture intensify?
• Does narration describe how words land, or only what they mean?

Flag if:
• A culturally distinct character reads tonally neutral.
• Accent is implied structurally but never heard.
• Stress does not affect sound texture.
• Dialogue feels emotionally charged but acoustically flat.
Label issues as:
“Accent Presence Underdeveloped”
or
“Audible Grain Flattening”
