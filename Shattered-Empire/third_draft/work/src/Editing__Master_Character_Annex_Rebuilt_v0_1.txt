Global Editing and Voice Rules
Do not flatten character speech into clean modern prose.
Do not remove dialect merely because it is not grammatically standard.
Do not remove phonetic spellings when they are intentional, readable, and character appropriate.
Do not make every character sound educated, calm, and polished.
Do not cut environmental updates when the environment has changed. Wind, water, light, smoke, crowd movement, heat, cold, smell, pressure, and terrain can evolve inside a scene.
Do not cut repeated contextual grounding when it gives the reader orientation, tension, or immersion.
Do not prioritize speed over voice, world texture, tactical logic, or emotional truth.
Do not let two major characters share the same speech profile unless the overlap is explained and differentiated.
Every named character should have at least a short voice note, even if they only appear once.
Editing should make the manuscript sound more like Zac wrote it on purpose, not more like neutral AI-polished fantasy.
Accent and Dialect Rule
Accent should be built in layers. The default order is word choice, sentence rhythm, grammar pressure, slang or shortened words, then selective phonetic spelling. Phonetics are spice, not soup. Use enough to make the voice audible, not enough to make the line unreadable.
Allowed tools: ain't, reckon, ye, yer, gonna, ’em, nothin’, coulda, shoulda, figger, dropped g endings, regional idioms, literal translation, clipped grammar, and class specific vocabulary.
Control rule: Use heavier dialect when the character is tired, angry, frightened, drunk, rushed, among their own people, or emotionally unguarded. Use lighter dialect in formal scenes if the character is consciously controlling their speech.
Regional Speech Systems
Aldoria
Aldorian speech carries a Germanic influence. This applies broadly across the culture, though education, class, and personal control affect how visible it is.
Formal even when casual. Contractions are used less often.
Context often comes before the point.
Action can feel slightly delayed in the sentence.
Blunt directness can sound harsh to outsiders.
Metaphors may feel literal or slightly translated.
Precision is favored over clever wordplay.
Bad news is not softened much.

Sample line: "This man you trust, why?"
Sample line: "The door, it was open when I arrived."
Sample line: "If what you say is true, and I do not yet believe it, then we have a problem."
Sample line: "Under normal circumstance, I would agree. These are not normal."
Sample line: "That opportunity is gone, yes."
Sample line: "I do not think so."
Sample line: "Your plan will fail."
Sample line: "That is not good enough."
Sample line: "You are wrong about this."
Altisflumen
Altisflumen speech should feel more proper, with an English influenced class structure. Because the culture uses firearms and practical shard powered tools, its vocabulary can be more technical, precise, and professionally divided by class.
Educated classes sound polished and restrained.
Military speakers use precise technical terms and controlled command language.
Aristocrats and high officers carry formal diction, social restraint, and sharper class markers.
Commoners may still sound cleaner than rural regions because cultural identity prizes order, education, and craft.
Specialized terms include rifle, cartridge, powder charge, range marker, supported position, sightline, and volley.
Opindale
Opindale remains the most neutral Common speaking region. It is useful as the reader baseline. Court figures can be polished, commoners can be practical, but the region should not be overloaded with accent unless class, neighborhood, or profession demands it.
Moradaer
Moradaer is a confirmed Accord nation. Its detailed speech system still needs fuller worldbuilding, but current use should lean disciplined, direct, and shaped by military scouting culture when tied to Lysana / Lysara Vaelor. Avoid making it sound identical to Opindale.
Rural Hill Country and Mountain Regions
This is the strongest dialect zone. Neavin is the anchor example. Speech should carry land, labor, weather, mining, stone, rivers, family manners, and practical survival.
Dropped endings and contractions are common.
Phonetic spelling may be used selectively.
Metaphors come from stone, timber, weather, rivers, mines, animals, tools, hunger, and distance.
Manners can exist beneath rough phrasing.
Do not confuse dialect with stupidity.
Sample line: "Wind's gone mean. River knows it too."
Sample line: "Ain't no clean road through that."
Sample line: "Don't go puttin' court words on mountain trouble."
Unnamed Eastern Countries
Garr is currently tied to an unnamed eastern country with a Texan like flavor. This region needs future naming. Current speech should be plainspoken, warm when safe, blunt when pressured, and grounded in duty, roads, weather, horses, work, and practical leadership.
Class and Profession Voice Systems
Royalty
Royal characters control rooms through implication, restraint, timing, and status. They do not need to shout. They speak with the expectation that consequences will follow.
Formal but not always stiff.
Trained to hide emotional reaction.
Threats often arrive as courtesy.
Love and politics can exist in the same sentence.
High Nobles
High nobles weaponize manners. They speak in implication, reputation, history, obligation, bloodline, and insult wrapped in silk.
Minor Lords
Minor lords are more insecure and more likely to overperform refinement. They posture, sharpen small insults, and watch the reactions of higher nobles.
Guild Masters
Guild masters speak with practical authority. Their language is shaped by craft, money, reputation, logistics, apprentices, deadlines, and risk.
Middle Class
Middle class speech should be more polished than poor/common speech but less socially weaponized than noble speech. They code switch around rank and authority.
Poor / Common Folk
Common speech should reflect region and work. More idioms, contractions, direct survival concerns, suspicion of officials, and practical metaphors.
Soldiers
Soldiers use short commands, gallows humor, nicknames, repetition under stress, and practical warnings. Veterans should sound different from fresh recruits.
Guards
Guards speak procedurally. They care about permissions, orders, gates, locks, badges, curfews, and not getting blamed.
Priests / Clergy
Priests speak with moral caution, ritual awareness, and measured restraint. Former warriors turned priests carry command beneath calm.
Children
Children must sound their age, class, education, fear, and upbringing. Noble children may imitate adult formality. Poor children may be sharp and observational. Traumatized children may speak too little or too plainly. Do not make children tiny adult scholars unless the story justifies it.

Faction and Group Voice Rules
Brotherhood
The Brotherhood was created shortly after the Second Magic War to police magic, regulate magical items, and secure dangerous artifacts. Voice should carry discipline, training, old institutional authority, and in corrupted members, moral rot beneath formal duty.
Ringbearers
Ringbearers should feel older than the current political moment. Their speech may be modernized through long adaptation, but their perspective is ancient. Harmon and Adin should not sound like identical old men.
Adept Knights
Historical order tied to Lucent and the Pearl of Magic. If shown in flashback or lore, their speech should feel elevated, ideological, and dangerous with certainty.
Breachborn Religion
Religious movement around bonding through breaches. Followers may speak with awe, fear, devotion, and misunderstood spiritual vocabulary. Avoid making all adherents sound identical.
Moreland Agents
Moreland influence should sound strategic, deniable, and politically cold. Public distance, private funding, and indirect destabilization shape their language.
Voice Collision Audit
These pairs must be checked in every future voice pass.
Luca vs Aymir vs Taerel: Luca adapts to fit the room. Aymir is military disciplined and risk-taking. Taerel is aristocratic, cautious, and odds-minded.

Harmon vs Brother Aldrek: Harmon is command and strategy with grandfather warmth. Aldrek is priest-warrior redemption with moral gravity.
Dain vs King Edric: Dain is fallen noble sarcasm and anger. Edric is royal authority and political intimacy.
Neavin vs Garr: Neavin is mountain miner dialect and stone/weather practicality. Garr is eastern/Texan-like plainspoken leadership insecurity.
Ash vs generic soldiers: Ash has working-class dockworker/blacksmith bite. Soldiers use rank, role, gallows humor, and battlefield economy.
