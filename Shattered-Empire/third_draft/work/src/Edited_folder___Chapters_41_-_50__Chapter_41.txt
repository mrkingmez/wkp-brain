The climb was cold, steep, and miserable, but Riven liked it that way. Misery, at least, was predictable. The wind screamed across the face of the mountain, pulling at his coat and whistling past his ears like some banshee laughing at the climb. Below them, Stormhaven lay in darkness, save for flickers of torchlight and the thin silhouettes of patrolling guards. The city was full of monsters and liars. Up here, the only lie was the next handhold.

Nyssa was already ahead of him, her body moving with the same sharp efficiency he remembered from years of near-death misadventures, her boots bit into the stone, her braid swinging like a metronome, unbothered by the cold.

He'd chased her like this for a decade.

"Tell me again why we don’t take the road like normal people?" she called back over her shoulder, breath fogging in the air.

"Because normal people aren’t trying to save a governor’s family from being butchered in the next sixteen hours," he muttered. Then louder: "You said you wanted to beat the sunrise. I’m just giving you the chance."

Nyssa snorted. "You always give me just enough rope to hang myself."

"And you never take it. That’s the worst part."

They crested the first rise, crawling up onto a narrow ledge. Riven leaned against the rock wall, flexing his fingers. They ached from the cold and the climb.

Nyssa was already scanning the city below, wind brushing strands of hair across her face. He watched her a moment longer than he meant to.

"You’ve been different," he said.

She blinked. "What do you mean?"

"I mean you, you’re more focused. Is it the new crew? Is it Dain?" he asked with a slightly childish tone in his voice.

Her shoulders stiffened. "You’re not going to let that go, are you? What is your problem with him?"

Riven shrugged, still catching his breath. "Why would I? And nothing."

She gave him a sideways look. "It was harmless flirting at first. We all do it. You do it."

"Sure," he said. "But now you actually like him. Admit it."

She looked away.

Riven pressed on. "I knew it. I’ve watched you roll your eyes every time someone mentioned Dain back in Dunlow. You couldn’t stand him."

"I didn’t know him then."

"Exactly."

She hesitated, then sat down beside him on the ledge. The wind pushed her coat against her frame. "He surprised me. He’s not what I thought. Not some pampered relic of a crumbling house. He listens. He fights smart. He’s good with Weslin."

Riven nodded slowly. "He is. Doesn’t mean it doesn’t sting."

She flinched. "Riv..."

"Just once," Riven said, turning to face her, eyes sharp. "Just once, I want to know why it’s never me. Why is it always some brooding fool with a blade and a history he won't shut up about?”

Her jaw worked, but no words came.

He wasn’t done. “I’ve been falling for you since we were barely old enough to swing a dagger. Every stupid, dangerous year after that just made it worse.”

"I know," she whispered.

"Then why not me?"

She looked up at him, eyes glassy with unshed tears. "Because I can’t lose you."

That hit like a hammer.

"If we tried," she said, voice tight, "and it broke... if it didn’t work... I wouldn’t just lose a lover. I’d lose the only constant I’ve ever had. You’re my anchor, Riven. You’re the part of me that doesn’t shake, no matter what hell we’re in."

Riven stared at her.

She kept going. "All those other guys? They were warm bodies. Distractions. Even Dain. At first. But something’s changed. Not just with him. With this whole damn team. I didn’t know what to expect when we began this mission, but I enjoy being part of it. I like fighting for something bigger."

She glanced at him, tentative. "Both Harmon and Dain talked about family. A new home, after. Maybe a life, a future, a real future of not stealing for my next meal. And for once, that didn’t sound awful."

Riven exhaled slowly. The ache in his chest was worse than the one in his fingers.

"Dain’s not a bad man," he said at last. "He fights like hell. Cares more than he lets on. But I don’t envy him. Not if he ends up with you."

Her eyes flared. "Excuse me?"

"You’re a lot, Nyssa. All fire and knives and secrets. You tear through people like they’re cobwebs. You don’t mean to, but you do."

"I never wanted to hurt anyone."

"I know. That’s the problem. You hurt people without even realizing it. I can't blame you for it." Riven paused, held her gaze. "I think he'd be good for you. If you let him." 

“That almost sounds like approval.”

“No,” Riven said. “But I don’t disapprove either. And if he’s serious, really serious, then maybe for once, you should give him a chance. I mean a real chance. Let yourself be happy.”

She looked down at her hands, eyes glassy but dry. “And what about you?”

“I’ll survive,” he said. “Always have.”

She was quiet for a long time.

Then she said, "Do you hate me?"

He barked a bitter laugh. "I could never. Even when I should. Even when it hurts. Gods, Nyssa, I want to hate you sometimes, just so it’d be easier."

Her hand found his. Cold fingers laced together.

"I do love you, Riven," she said. "Maybe not the way you want. But I do. And maybe one day I’ll be brave enough to risk it. But until then, I need you. I need us."

He looked down at their hands. Then out over the ledge.

"Then don’t run," he said. "Not from me. Not from him. Not from yourself."

She gave him a look. "You trying to be wise now?"

“Trying,” he said, “but wisdom keeps dodging.”

They both laughed.

Then Nyssa said, "Remember when we stole that baron’s carriage and sold it for gambling chips in Ashvale?" 

"You mean the one with the stuffed bear head inside?"

"Still creeps me out."

"I still have the poker set."

They sat together for a while longer, the wind carrying the silence between them.

Then Riven stood. "Come on. We’ve got a statue to rob."

Nyssa stood too, brushing frost from her legs. "With traps. And a possible ambush."

"Wouldn’t be us if it wasn’t."

And side by side, they climbed into the night.

The climb leveled out into a narrow plateau nestled among the trees after a while. Snow dusted the ground in a soft layer. The world was so quiet it felt like they were the only two souls in it. Pines swayed gently above, and in the clearing stood the statue.

She wasn’t grand, not in the way kings built statues to themselves. No crown, no pedestal etched in gold. Just a woman in weathered armor, hands on a broadsword, the edges of her face softened by time and frost.

"Lady Veyra," Riven muttered, breathing hard from the climb. "Builder of the old bastion. First architect of the northern defenses. Died before the city was ever finished. They say she mapped every stone of this mountain in her head before she laid the first brick."

Nyssa blinked snow from her lashes. "Romantic."

"She was the only one who ever built something here that lasted. Until now."

They walked toward it slowly, their boots crunching over frozen soil. Riven crouched at the base first, brushing snow away from the hidden latch.

Only it wasn't hidden anymore.

The small recessed compartment beneath the statue's base had been forced open, splintered at the seams. A broken hinge hung loose. Bits of brass and steel lay in the snow like shattered teeth.

Nyssa crouched beside him. "Someone beat us to it."

"Looks fresh. Within the day, maybe."

She looked around, instantly alert. But the forest was still. No tracks, no waiting blades. Whoever had come and gone hadn’t stayed.

Riven sighed and leaned back on his heels. "So much for easy."

"Since when have we done easy?"

“Easy would’ve been suspicious anyway.”

She offered him a hand and pulled him to his feet. They stood there for a beat, silence spinning between them again. They scanned the area.

Nyssa broke it first. "We should look around to see if we can find where they went.”

“Agreed.”

“Riven, we haven’t had the time to talk like we normally do. I am sorry for that.”

“I understand, we have been in a go-go-go pace. Don’t apologize. We’ve barely had room to breathe,” replied Riven.

“Details, details. Never let them get in the way before, why start now?” Nyssa paused as something cleared in her mind. “I have something I want to ask you. I haven’t really stopped thinking about it since you said it.”

“Get it out, doesn't need such a build-up,” Riven said. 

“Fine. Back when we were climbing, you said something back there... about me giving this a real chance. What did you mean?"

Riven nodded. "I did. And I mean you should give him a real chance, and I meant it. If this situation with Dain is different, as you said, then treat it accordingly. Don’t use the same excuses. Don’t keep one foot out the door while pretending you’re ready to open it."

Nyssa folded her arms. Her gaze drifted past him, out over the edge of the ridge to the city below, lights flickering beneath a veil of early snow.

"I didn’t even know him," she said. "Not really. Before this mission, that is. Just stories. Rumors about the loud-mouthed noble with a sword bigger than his sense of subtlety. We were always up for the same contracts. He stole two of mine without even realizing it. So I made sure to even the score."

Riven smiled faintly. "Naturally."

"Then we both answered Harmon’s call. And I met him for real. Not the job-stealer. Not the myth. Just this... weird, brash, grieving bastard who acts like he has all the answers."

She shrugged. "I hated him all over again." Riven laughed.

She kept going. "But I kept watching. And underneath the bluster, the cocky grin, the 'I-always-have-a-plan' routine... he was just this guy. Hurting. Lost. The worst part is that he is unsure of himself and uses anger as a means to mask his insecurities. Sounds like anyone you know? Like when you and I split. I saw myself in him. Or who I used to be."

Riven tilted his head, voice softer now. "Mask his insecurities? Those are some big concepts coming from you.” A beat. “Maybe he deserves better than being your next distraction."

She looked at him sharply. But not angry. “Maybe we both deserve better than the messes we used to choose."

Riven exhaled slowly. His breath fogged the air between them.

"If you think it’s real," he said, "then give it a real shot. Don’t treat him like the others. We know how it ends when you do. One of you leaves first, the other pretends they weren’t surprised."

Nyssa winced. "You make it sound so simple."

"It’s not. But it is a choice."

She was quiet for a moment.

"What about you?" she asked. "What’s next for Riven, master of the backup plan?"

He grinned, "Hmm… A warm bed. Decent food. A city where I'm just some man with a pint and no history. Teach a few orphans the trade. Pretend I've never heard of a heist. Acting all respectable."

She rolled her eyes. "Be serious."

"I am. The next Mr. Hogan, remember him?"

"Yeah, that could work for you, but I know you are not serious. Maybe you should stay as well."

He tilted his head, his smile fading a bit. "Maybe I can find a place like Dain wants to build. Quiet. Honest work. If you stay, maybe I will stay too."

Nyssa looked at him, eyes softening.

"You still mean that? After everything?"

Riven nodded. “Always did. But we will see.”

She walked over to him and hugged him. "You’re coming with me."

He just shrugged.

They turned together, moving past the broken statue and toward the narrow path beyond it. Trees closed in overhead, the scent of pine and damp moss rich in the air. Their boots crunched softly on packed snow.

After a moment, Nyssa spoke again.

"Harmon ever say what happens after this?"

Riven shook his head. "But I think he knows this is more than a mission. For him, as well as the other. The fresh start is nice as well."

Nyssa nodded. "Then let’s not waste it."

Riven looked at her the way he always had when the words ran out. "Let’s not.”

“Well,” Nyssa muttered, still looking around for any sign of where the keys might have gone. “They’re definitely not here now.”

Riven walked up beside her, “So what do you think, someone on our trail, or did we just get outpaced by some unlucky bastard on a scav hunt?”

“Wouldn’t be the first time this city handed us a coincidence with a knife behind its back.”

He frowned, tapping the empty compartment. “This was done fast. Sloppy. Whoever took them didn’t care about hiding their tracks.”

“Or didn’t have time.”

“Or both,” he added, sitting back on his heels. “Still. We know where the map room is. Can’t exactly go back and tell Harmon we failed the mission because the keys were gone.”

Nyssa sighed, standing and stretching her arms overhead. “So what? You gonna pick the lock?”

“Not exactly. I’ve got something better,” he said, brushing his palms together. “But I’ll need my tool kit.”

She raised a brow. “We didn’t exactly bring a lot when we busted out of that prison. And unless you’ve been keeping a satchel in your teeth, I don’t remember you carrying anything.”

Riven smirked. “I’ve got a stash.”

Nyssa cocked a brow. “A stash?”

“Yeah. A little bolt-hole. Hideaway. Cache of goodies.”

“A ‘stashy-stash’?”

“Don’t mock the sacred.”

She smirked. “How come I don’t know about this?”

Riven shrugged. “We were apart for over a year. And a boy’s gotta eat.”

Nyssa rolled her eyes. “So you took some jobs.”

“A few.”

“Anything exciting?”

“You know the kind. Dangerous, poorly paid, morally questionable.”

“Ah. So your usual.”

He gave her a wink and dusted his gloves. “C’mon. It’s not far. We rappel down, hook around the cliff face. I’ll take you there.”

She blinked. “Wait. Your little secret hideout is right near this cliff?”

“Hey, you never know when you’ll need a quick exit.”

Nyssa chuckled as they unhooked the climbing rope. “This feels like old times.”

Riven grinned and clipped his harness. “Race you to the bottom?”

“You’re gonna regret that.”

They dropped over the edge in near-unison, rappelling face-first down the stone face like ghosts sprinting down a wall. The cold wind bit at their cheeks as they moved, boots kicking snow from narrow ledges, ropes humming with strain.

Riven touched down first, barely, and raised both arms in triumph. “Still got it.”

Nyssa landed beside him, unclipping with a huff. “You cheated.”

“Define cheating.”

“You started before me.”

“There are those details again.”

They moved quickly through the broken shale, around the narrow cliff base where the snow drifted thick and undisturbed. Riven crouched behind a moss-covered outcrop and felt for a flat stone.

Nyssa tilted her head. “Don’t tell me this is another one of your trick walls.”

Riven didn’t reply. He twisted the stone hard, and the rock groaned inward. A gap opened beneath the brush, revealing a dark crevice that descended into the stone.

Nyssa peered in. “How long has this been here?”

“Three years. Maybe four. Found it on a job running messages through the old tunnel routes.”

She followed him inside, ducking under low arches of stone until they emerged into a chamber carved from the natural rock. It wasn’t large, maybe the size of a small cottage, but it was dry and layered with soft sand and old furs. A lantern hung from an iron hook on the wall, still functional, still full.

Nyssa lifted it and lit it with a match. Warm light filled the room.

The space wasn’t luxurious, but it was homey in a haphazard way. Two battered lockers stood against the far wall, both sealed with old Dwarven latches. A cot. A dented kettle. A pile of black and gray gear neatly folded beside a small field desk.

“Not bad,” she said, arms crossed. “A little less than impressive, though. Where’s the butler?”

Riven walked past her to the lockers. “He quit. Something about my charming personality.”

He popped one open. Inside: weapons, rolls of tools, and a black leather satchel lined with iron picks and magical thread.

Nyssa poked at the cot. “So you’ve been playing house while I’ve been doing actual jobs?”

“Someone had to live comfortably.”

“Comfortable? Riven, this place looks like a goblin’s pantry.”

He ignored her and grabbed a folded tunic from the bottom shelf, then the gloves, belt, and cloak. In minutes, he’d stripped out of his travel-stained garb and stepped out in sleek gray and black. The new gear hugged his form like a second skin, tight leather, reinforced shoulders, and a hood he pulled low.

He turned. “Well?”

She blinked. “Damn.”

“Told you I cleaned up,” Riven said. 

“You had this while we were walking around in rags?”

“No, only in the last two years. I still travel light. Rags work.”

She threw a glove at him. “You’re the worst.”

He caught it midair and tossed it back.

The teasing faded after a breath, replaced by quiet.

Nyssa sat on the cot, staring at the flame in the lantern. “You know, this team... It’s different. I didn’t expect to like them.”

Riven said nothing.

She looked up. “I figured they’d be another crew we’d forget by next season. But... Dain’s not who I thought. And Harmon... he’s like a war priest without the prayers. Ash’s hilarious. Weslin’s, well, he’s a kid, but he’s trying.”

Riven leaned against the stone arch, listening.

“I think,” she said, softer, “If this is something more, I might want to stay. If Harmon would have me. This team, I mean. Maybe we could find something that actually... lasts.”

She looked at him. “Not just you and me running the next scam or stealing from the next noble. A place. A life.”

His voice was low. “You really do want me to stay, too?”

“I don’t want to build a life without you in it. Look, I’m sorry for the hard sell,” she said suddenly.

Riven looked over, brows lifting. “Didn’t know you were selling.”

“Please.” She huffed. “That whole ‘maybe we stay after this job’ bit earlier on the mountain, then this little part? That was me playing at subtlety.”

“Subtle as a brick through glass.”

“I know.” Her smile didn’t quite reach her eyes. “Time’s catching up. Feels like the world’s moving on without me. And I’ll blink, and we’ll still be running, still surviving, nothing more.”

He nodded slowly, saying nothing.

She glanced at him, then looked away. “And I want you in it. Whatever comes next, I want you there. So... sorry for the pitch. I just…” She stopped, inhaled. “Harmon asked me to say something to you. I think he wants you to stay. He thought maybe you’d listen to me.”

Riven leaned against the stone wall, arms crossed. “You’re the only one I do listen to.”

“Then listen now,” she said, turning toward him. “I don’t know what happens when this mission’s done. But I want it to be something more than just another night we survive.”

Her voice lowered. “I’m tired of counting victories by the number of wounds we didn’t take.”

He studied her, quiet, unreadable.

Then finally, he said, “That’s not a hard sell, Nyss. That’s just you saying you’re ready.”

She gave him a tired smile. “Maybe I am.”

For a moment, nothing passed between them but the sound of the flame flickering.

Riven held the moment, then let it go. "All right." He shook his head. "You still owe me seventy silver from Ironvale." 

"I’ll put it on your tab," Nyssa said. 

They smiled at each other, faint and genuine. “Then let’s go steal a map.”

They reemerged into the cold, locking the hideout behind them, and started the trek back toward the hidden entrance to the Sixth Tier.

Whatever waited in the vault beneath Stormhaven, it wasn’t guarded by just a broken lock.

And they were running out of time.

The city was a sleeping giant by the time Nyssa and Riven moved again.

Midnight had long passed, but the streets still twitched with life in narrow alleys and forgotten courtyards. Lamplight flickered on the fog-slick stone. A bottle broke in the distance, followed by a man’s slurred curse. The only people out this late were the desperate, the drunk, or the dangerous.

They stuck to the shadows.

Ducking low behind broken carts and slipping between awnings, they moved in silence, fluid, practiced. This was the kind of night they'd trained for. Cold air carried the scent of piss and rain-soaked ash. A cat hissed and vanished into a cellar door.

Riven tapped Nyssa's shoulder and nodded up ahead. A squat building hugged the base of a crumbled watchtower, more decorative than defensible now, one of the old city’s leftover bones. “That’s it.”

“A king’s vault without a king,” she murmured.

“Fitting, right?”

It didn’t look like much. A cracked dome, an ivy-choked lintel, and a faded sigil long since worn to memory. But this was the site, beneath it, the Builder’s Cache. A six-tiered archive, sealed and forgotten, where the original blueprints and fail-safes of the city had been buried under royal order.

And guarded.

Two figures paced the outer courtyard.

“Who are they?” Riven whispered. “They look like the same ones we saw back in the alley near the north gates.”

Nyssa crouched beside him. “Why here?”

“They’re looking for something. Maybe the same thing we are.”

“Two isn’t bad.” She loosened her daggers. “Want to see who’s rustier?”

Riven smirked. “Loser buys the next round.”

She nodded. “Hope you brought coin. I’m thirsty.”

They split.

Riven ghosted through the overgrown hedges, low to the ground, blade drawn. Nyssa moved to the left, dancing over loose bricks and crunching moss. The first merc heard something, turned, and caught a knee to the throat. Nyssa spun him around, jabbed her dagger into his side, then caught the falling crossbow before it hit the ground.

Riven went wide and fast, launching a stone past his target’s head. The man flinched, just long enough for Riven to close the distance and smash his elbow into the back of his skull. The merc crumpled.

Nyssa looked over, raising a brow.

“Still got it,” Riven mouthed.

They dragged the bodies into the vines, then circled toward the main door.

A handprint panel, old tech. Riven pressed his palm flat. Nothing.

“I thought you said you had something.”

“I said I might.”

Nyssa rolled her eyes. “You’re such a liar.”

He pulled a flat, rune-etched key from inside his boot. “Let’s call it flexible truth.”

The door groaned open. He looked at her with a big, toothy cheese-eating grin. She smirked, and they walked in together. 

Inside, the Builder’s Cache was a tomb of forgotten knowledge. Rows of stone shelves, built deep into the mountain’s edge, stretched into shadow. Dust coated everything. Lanterns sparked into life as they entered, reacting to movement.

“Looks like no one’s been here in decades,” Nyssa said.

“Except for the four armored assholes heading toward us,” Riven said, pointing at the advancing danger.

Two had already crossed the second tier, weapons raised. The others flanked low, moving with trained discipline. Crossbows first.

Riven ducked behind a stone lectern as a bolt cracked overhead.

Nyssa dove into a roll, kicking over a rusted support beam. It collapsed with a clang, drawing fire. She came up slicing, catching one merc’s leg and twisting him down into the stairwell.

Riven charged the flanker, trading steel. His blade scraped off the half-plate, but he feinted left, then swept the man’s legs and drove his dagger between the plates under the arm.

The last two regrouped.

“Trade?” Nyssa shouted.

“Switch!”

They crossed paths. Nyssa tackled the one coming at Riven, grappling with him between the shelves. She jammed his helmet sideways, blinded him, then cracked him over the head with a steel-capped scroll tube repeatedly until he stopped fighting.

Riven faked a stumble, baiting the final merc close, then yanked the merc forward by his collar and smashed his head into the railing.

Silence fell again.

Nyssa wiped blood from her lip. “Four beats two. You're buying.”

“We’ll call it a draw,” Riven said, breathing hard.

They moved quickly, checking corners. No more enemies.

Now came the real work.

They tore through drawers, map cases, and scroll racks. Dust choked the air, ancient vellum cracking under careful fingers. Riven found city plans, hundreds of them. Sewer systems, postern gates, maintenance tunnels, old codebooks, and ledgers.

It took time. The archive wasn’t organized by any clear system, just tiers and categories in an old script, some crumbling with age, others sealed in old iron boxes. They split up, calling out anything relevant. Lanterns flickered over their shoulders. A soft whir echoed as another torch came to life.

“There!” Nyssa pointed to a silver-sealed case tucked behind a false panel. They pried it open.

Blueprints. Diagrams of the tower. Not just the structure, but the protections. Wards. Locks. Triggers.

“Jackpot.”

Riven tucked the bundle into his bag. “Time to go.”

But as they exited the room, Nyssa froze.

“Five more. Coming fast.”

Five men wearing the same half plate flashed beneath the lanterns, crossbows leveled, footsteps hammering stone.

“We’ve got seconds,” Nyssa hissed, eyes cutting toward the corridor’s far end. “Options?”

Riven scanned the hall, no cover, no retreat. “No window. Just a one-way drop to the city’s bones.”

Nyssa grinned. “That’s why we're not taking the hall.”

She grabbed his arm and pulled hard left, toward a narrow recess in the wall half-hidden behind a stack of sealed crates. An old brass hinge barely glinted in the dim lanternlight.

Riven blinked. “Wait, is that—?”

“Builder supply shaft,” she said, already kicking the latch. “Used to drop gear down to the bottom level, below ground. We have done this before, remember?”

“I thought I was drunk that night.”

“You were. Still a good tip.”

The hatch groaned open, revealing a tight vertical chute just wide enough for one. Rusted rails lined the inside. Riven didn’t hesitate. He threw himself feet-first, boots skidding against the metal guide. The sharp scent of oil and mildew hit him halfway down.

Above, Nyssa grabbed the rope, flipped, and launched herself after him, headfirst. Wind whipped her cloak as she angled her descent, twisting at the last second to avoid colliding.

They dropped into darkness, boots slamming onto damp stone as they landed side by side in the old reliquary shaft, narrow, slick, and dripping with age.

This part of the secret archive was in worse condition than the rest of the place. 

Nyssa staggered upright first, shaking damp from her hands. “We’re not out yet.”

“Not even close.” Riven spun, listening. Above, muffled voices echoed down the shaft, angry, urgent. A crossbow bolt clattered off the stone lip and sparked beside them.

“Lovely. They’ve got angles.”

Nyssa snatched a lantern off the wall and shattered it on the floor behind them. The oil hissed across the stone. Riven lit it with a striker. Flames leapt up, snarling a wall between them and the shaft.

“Won’t stop them for long,” he said, turning.

“Doesn’t have to.”

They sprinted down the corridor, books and crates stacked haphazardly along the walls, ancient maps left to curl and rot. Water dripped from the ceiling in fat, steady drops. A heavy gate loomed ahead.

Riven cursed. “Storage lock. External.”

He dropped to one knee, pulled a blade from his belt, not for fighting, but for prying open the casing on the wall. “Keep an eye back,” he muttered, hands working fast. “This is a Builder lock. Needs a pattern pulse, but I can brute-force the timing pins.”

Nyssa took cover beside a stone column, eyes flicking between the fire behind and the hallway ahead. Shadows danced on the walls, in the firelight. Bootsteps pounded closer, sharp, angry, too many to count. The men were descending.

“I’d rather not get skewered while you do math.”

Riven didn’t look up. “Then buy me ten seconds.”

A bolt struck the archway overhead, shattering stone. Another zipped past her shoulder and pinged against metal. She ducked and threw one of her knives blind, heard a surprised grunt, then Riven’s voice: “Got it!”

The gate hissed open.

They ducked through and slammed it behind them. Riven jammed the locking bar into place and twisted until it groaned. The mechanism clicked shut.

Nyssa let out a breath. “Now what?”

Riven scanned the final chamber. It was small, no more than ten by twenty feet, lined with shelves of scrolls and dusty crates, and across from them: a stone wall slick with water and moss. He pressed his palm to the far corner.

“The runoff drains to the outer service trench,” he said. “There should be a fault seam.”

He shoved. Stone shifted, barely. Just enough to show a hairline crack.

“Help me.”

They both leaned into it, gritting teeth, groaning in effort. Metal buckled under their combined weight, and footsteps hammered the gate behind them. The seam gave way, opening a hidden vent barely wide enough for a person to crawl through.

“Builder engineering. Always a tight fit,” Riven muttered.

“Ladies first?” Nyssa asked with a grin, breath ragged.

“You’re taller.”

She slipped through, boots scraping metal. Riven followed, dragging the cover back into place behind them just as the sound of metal slamming against the last gate echoed down the corridor.

They crawled for a hundred feet through slime and pitch-black air thick with stone mold and dust. 

A grate gave way, and they tumbled out behind a drainage canal near the western barracks wall, hidden beneath the old smeltery that hadn’t burned coal in a decade.
They lay there a moment, chests heaving. The cold air had never tasted better.
Nyssa finally sat up. “Let’s not do that again.”

Riven grinned, bruised and grimed. “We made it.”

“Barely.”

“But we’ve got what we came for.”

He patted the satchel beneath his cloak, the stolen blueprints wrapped in waxed cloth.

Footsteps echoed in the alley ahead. They both froze. But it was just a dog… emaciated, sniffing a pile of old bones, uninterested.

Riven stood. “Come on. Time to disappear.”
