A thin veil of morning mist clung to the road as Luca’s team rode east-northeast, toward the mountains that loomed like sleeping giants beyond the tree line. The sun, still low in the sky, scattered pale gold through skeletal branches and left long shadows across the frost-hardened dirt path. Birds were silent. Only the sound of hooves crunching the frosted earth and the soft creak of saddle leather accompanied them.
Behind them lay the cave where they'd sheltered the night before with the rescued refugees. Luca had insisted they rest, even at the cost of time, and given up most of their remaining rations to the battered travelers. Taerel had seen to their wounds. Lysana, despite her quiet nature, helped as well. When dawn came, they pointed the refugees south, toward the port city, giving them directions, warnings, and hope, and then watched them vanish into the trees.
Now, riding with half-empty stomachs and tight supplies, the weight of duty pressed heavier than their gear.
Taerel rode in the rear, beside Aymer, their horses trailing just behind Lysana and Luca. The brothers spoke in silence, hands moving in fluid, precise gestures just above their saddles.
Taerel's fingers moved again, pausing just long enough for Aymer to catch him signing a message. ‘We could’ve moved quickly last night if we had a way to speak.’
Aymer nodded subtly, signing back, ‘We weren’t ready, too many unknowns. I know what you are getting at, we held that back, deliberately, remember?” Aymer added. “The cost we chose not to pay.’
Another flick of Taerel’s hand. ‘I could make something. Something simple. A pair of bands, maybe. Enchanted to carry sound or sight.’
Aymer’s jaw tensed. He glanced toward Lysana, who was riding a few lengths quietly behind them. ‘You want to show them?’ he signed.
‘We need to talk about telling them,’ Taerel sighed, eyes flicking ahead to make sure Lysana wasn’t watching. ‘About what I am.’
Aymer kept his expression neutral, only the smallest twitch of his brow betraying his thoughts. ‘Not yet. We don’t know them well enough to drop something that heavy.’
Taerel grimaced. He wanted to share. He wanted to help. It felt wrong hiding who he was, hiding the truth about the magic bound into his blood, a lineage that could script enchantments with just the right tools and time. His mask, a masterwork of his own design, projected the image of warm brown eyes and black hair, concealing his true self. It hides his ivory white hair, eyes like bleached stone. He could barely remember what he actually looked like.
“I could make something for us,” he signed. “A way to communicate. Better than hand signs or yelling. A talisman, maybe. Or etched stones.”
“Maybe later,” Aymer countered. “If this ride goes well, if we see something in them worth trusting. But don’t rush it. This team... it could work, it may not. Don’t ruin it by springing your soul on them too early.”
“But you trust them, don’t you?” Taerel asked.
“I want to. That’s the…” He paused, speaking not softer, but heavier, “difference.”
A shadow flicked beside them.
Lysana had drifted back.
“You two playing some kind of game back here?” Her reins tightened once in her fist as she asked, arching a brow. Her long braid of golden-blonde hair swayed as her horse pulled alongside. She wore a faint smile, but there was sharpness in her gaze. She’d been watching.
Taerel straightened, flashing an easy grin. Aymer, the older and more composed, answered first.
“Just passing the time. Counting pines. Ty’s ahead by five.”
“Six,” Taerel added, lifting a brow.
Lysana looked between them, unconvinced. “Counting trees, huh? Sounded more like code.”
“Game's got rules,” Aymer said, deadpan. “Can't share.”
Lysana snorted. “Fine. Just don’t be plotting to ditch us. I’d have to hunt you down.”
Taerel held up his hands. “Perish the thought entirely.”
As she rode ahead again, Taerel leaned toward Aymer, “She suspects,” he murmured, too quickly, like he wished it weren’t true.
From up ahead, Luca caught the sound of her laugh, low, quick, real. It shouldn’t have mattered, but something in it cut through the cold that had lived behind his ribs since the orc camp. For the first time, he wondered if distance was just another kind of cowardice.
"Of course she does," Aymer said. "She's smarter than both of us." 
Up ahead, Luca rode alone, silent and upright in the saddle. His cloak billowed in the breeze, eyes fixed ahead but distant. Ever since the fight at the orc camp, he'd said little. He moved like someone shouldering too much weight but refusing to shift it to others. Aymer watched him with a thoughtful frown.
“You think he knows we know?” Taerel asked quietly.
Aymer hesitated. “About him being a Shadow Walker? Yeah. No one takes out four orcs that fast unless they’re trained for something darker than war.”
“Should we ask?”
Aymer was about to answer when Luca abruptly raised his closed fist, then gave a short circular gesture, his signal for the others to close in. 
One by one, the horses picked up pace, crunching through the snow until the group gathered around him.  
Aymer was the first to speak. "What do you need?"
Luca glanced at them all, eyes steady. "We got a late start today, helping the refugees and resting up from last night. It was the right call, but we’re behind. We need to make up ground.”
A brief pause. “For the next few hours, we’re pushing.”
He shifted in the saddle, the wind tugging strands of hair loose from beneath his hood. "I want to stop before sunset. Still need time to hunt and resupply. “If we’re lucky,” Luca added. “If not, we make do. We gave up more than we could afford. I wanted some light to improve our chances. I don’t want us out alone in the dark."
There were nods around the circle, Aymer gave a small thumbs-up, Lysana tapped her reins, and Taerel gave a lazy salute.
But then Taerel leaned forward, his horse stepping slightly ahead. He cocked his head toward Luca, his grin both playful and curious. “One question,” Taerel said lightly, too lightly. “Forgive the bluntness, but are you a Shadow Walker?”  Lysana looked dumbfounded by the question, and Aymer just slapped his head.
Luca’s head snapped toward him. For a long time, he said nothing. His stare, sharp as a drawn blade, held Taerel in place.
Luca turned back without answering, his face fixed. “Let’s move.”
He spurred his horse, and the others followed, hooves tearing through frost-covered earth.
Above them, hawks circled. The crunch of ice under hooves mixed with the creak of leather and the breath of beasts working hard. Despite the cold, sweat gathered under furs and armor. Fingers tingled, muscles ached, but no one complained.
There was purpose in their movement, urgency beneath the cold.
As they crested a small ridge, Taerel leaned toward Aymer and muttered, "You think he heard?"
Aymer chuckled. “I think he heard you,” Aymer said dryly, “and he’s likely deciding how to make you disappear into those woods.”
"I should think I'd haunt rather well," Taerel replied, then sat straighter."  "Hey, Lysana, if I vanish, make sure someone tells a dramatic tale. Preferably involving fire."
Lysana rode up alongside, smirking. "Oh, I’ll tell them you screamed like a goat before you went."  She looked ahead to where Luca rode, the distance between them measured not in strides but in the silence he carried. Whatever he was, she realized, she wanted to understand it.
The laughter broke the tension as they pressed on, snow falling lightly now from the cloud-thick sky.
Ahead, the trail bent through a shadowed forest, and beyond that, the rising spine of the mountains loomed.
The gallop carried them far.
Hooves thundered over snow-slick foothills, the horses’ breath steaming in the sharp mountain air, time bleeding away with every mile. Frosted trees blurred past in streaks of white and brown as the narrow trail turned east-northeast, the sun dragging long shadows across the terrain. Wind stung their cheeks, threading under collars and stifling cloaks. Packs bounced at their sides, and the cold clung to their fingers despite riding gloves.
Snow hissed under hooves where it had drifted, the rhythm of their ride a pulsing, driven tempo, each beat an answer to Luca’s push for time.
But after a stretch of hard riding, Luca lifted his hand and eased back on the reins. His stallion let out a soft, damp snort as it slowed to a walk. The others mirrored the motion, reining in until the column bunched close again, horses stamping lightly, breath heavy in the air.
Luca angled his mount off the road and nodded toward a shallow depression nestled just beyond the tree line.
“Right there,” he said, his voice lower now, laced with calm command. “Little dip just off the trail. Trees thicken just enough for cover, and it looks dry underfoot. Let’s make camp. Still enough light left, so we’ll hunt, eat, and sleep properly for once.”
Taerel twisted in his saddle to glance back at the sky. “Didn’t realize we were stopping this early,” he said, already loosening his reins.
Luca gave a small shake of his head. “Rather stop now and eat warm than keep pushing into cold silence. We’ve got ground to make tomorrow. Better to rest right. As well as rest the horses.”
No one argued.
They pulled off the road together, winding between bark-dark pines and snow-laced branches. Frost crackled beneath hooves, and the occasional branch creaked overhead. The clearing, though modest, offered shelter, a few downed logs, and a windbreak from the trees.
As soon as they dismounted, the silence shifted from trail noise to purposeful motion. Taerel immediately led his gelding to a low-hanging branch, tying the reins with practiced ease as the camp took shape around them.
Nearby, Lysana loosened her saddle’s straps and whispered gently to her mare, brushing down the coat with swift, efficient strokes. Her fingers were red from the cold, but she moved with the grace of someone used to rough country nights. She clicked her tongue, adjusting the feed bag around the horse’s muzzle. “There you go, eat up. You did well today.”
Aymer dragged a gloved hand down the neck of his mount, then checked the sight on the long rifle slung at his back. He thumbed open the chamber, sliding in a smaller cartridge with quiet precision.
“I’ll see if I can bring back something with legs,” he said, shouldering the rifle.
“You mean like a deer?” Taerel called over, grinning as he wiped down his saddle.
“No, like a cursed elk with antlers made of knives,” Aymer fired back, deadpan. “Yes, a deer.”
Lysana chuckled as she pulled her bow free from the pack strapped to her mare. “I’ll go forage. I’ve been in this forest before, might even find us something weird and glowing.” She winked and added, with a theatrical bow, “Time to put my super-awesome ranger skills to the test.”
Taerel raised his arms. “Finally! I’ve been waiting to see what a real ranger looks like.”
Luca stepped forward before the banter could spiral. The last threads of sunlight caught in his hood as he looked at each of them in turn, his voice even but edged with finality.
“Don’t be long,” he said. “There’s something we need to talk about tonight. Something that can’t wait any longer.”
The words settled heavily.
Aymer paused mid-check of his rifle. Lysana’s smile faded just a breath. Even Taerel, still crouched by the fire pit, glanced up with narrowed eyes.
“Got it,” Aymer said.
“Be back soon,” Lysana added, tightening the bowstring.
They stepped off into the trees, the snow crunching softly underfoot, the evening chill chasing behind them. A raven called somewhere deeper in the woods. The scent of pine and smoke slowly rose as Taerel sparked flint to kindling.
The camp slowly took shape, humble, temporary, but alive.
And in the stillness that followed, the unsaid began to build, like frost gathering along the edge of a blade.
They were gone for only a few hours. Long enough to feel the cost of it. Lysana was the first to return, hood drawn back and cheeks pink from the cold. Her boots were damp from snowmelt, and in one hand she carried a bundled satchel of greens and roots, the other holding a limp rabbit by the hind legs. Her bow was slung behind her shoulder, the string freshly waxed.
“I bring gifts,” she said with a grin, shaking the satchel toward Taerel, who had just coaxed the fire to life.
She knelt beside him and unwrapped her haul. “Wild onions, burdock root, a handful of dried nettle leaves I found under a fallen log, and one unfortunate rabbit who wasn’t fast enough.”
Taerel leaned over the roots and gave a low whistle. “That’ll do just fine.”
Not long after, Aymer emerged from between the trees, his coat dusted with frost and a small blood trail marking his return. Over his shoulders, he hauled a young musk deer, its legs bound tight with cord. It wasn’t huge, but it was enough.
“Wasn’t far,” he muttered, easing the carcass down onto a flat stone near the fire. “Took a clean shot just below the ridge. Lungs. Didn’t run far.”
As the sun began to dip below the treetops, the group got to work.
Lysana and Taerel handled the rabbit, swiftly skinning it with practiced cuts. The hide peeled back in one fluid motion, and Lysana set it aside on a flat rock to cure later. Taerel broke the rabbit down into sections, legs, backstrap, and organ meat, then began simmering a pot of water drawn from a melted snow boil.
Luca and Aymer handled the deer. Aymer worked with quiet efficiency, skinning the animal with long, steady slices of his belt knife. Steam lifted from the exposed muscle as the cold air met fresh warmth. Luca cut the usable meat free, haunches, ribs, tenderloin, and passed each piece back to Taerel to rinse and add to the stew pot. Blood mixed with snow in red streaks across the ground.
Bones were cracked to expose marrow, dropped into the simmering pot for added richness. Lysana ground the burdock root into coarse shavings and added it alongside the chopped onions and nettle. The scent that rose was earthy and deep, tinged with woodsmoke and wild herbs.
By the time stars started to blink into the sky, a thick stew bubbled over the fire. Each of them sat close, steam rising into their faces as they sipped from carved wooden bowls. No one spoke much, just the quiet rhythm of spoons and firewood crackling, a shared warmth that pressed back against the mountain’s chill.
When they’d eaten their fill, they turned to preserving the rest.
Taerel pulled out a packet of coarse rock salt wrapped in waxed cloth, handing portions around. Aymer sectioned the remaining venison into thin strips, laying them on a flat stone to be rubbed thoroughly with salt and a pinch of crushed juniper from Lysana’s pouch, antiseptic and flavorful. These strips were then wrapped in thin sheets of oiled cloth, bound in twine, and tucked into their waxed leather satchels, meant to be hung away from camp overnight.
Lysana took the liver and heart, slicing and lightly salting them to cook in the morning. The rabbit hide was stretched and pinned between two forked branches to dry, its fur still intact.
The camp settled into a rhythm. Bellies full, packs heavier with preserved meat, and the fire burning low, just enough to keep the wolves guessing.
They moved with quiet efficiency, scraping out the pot, rinsing their bowls with boiled water, wiping them dry, tossing bones into the dark beyond camp. Taerel took the time to bury the offal near the base of a frost-slicked pine, patting the disturbed snow flat again with the heel of his boot.
Luca watched them move, efficient, wordless, together. A month ago, he would’ve eaten alone, half-awake with his back to a wall. Now, their quiet chatter felt like armor of a different kind.
As the last of the tasks wound down, Taerel gave a crooked grin and reached into his pack. "Figured tonight deserved a little something extra," he said, pulling out a tightly-wrapped bundle of oiled cloth. Inside were thick slices of sweet bread, dense and golden, speckled with crushed nuts and dried berries. He hadn’t meant to keep it this long.
He passed them around, and for a few quiet moments, they sat in a loose ring around the fire, savoring the unexpected treat, warmth on their tongues, sugar on their breath, and silence settling like a blanket.
That’s when Luca leaned forward, his gaze flicking from one face to the next, and said, “All right. It’s time we talked.” He paused, taking in all of them before he went on, "I owe you all an explanation," he began, eyes fixed on the fire.  "About who I am and why I do what I do." 
The group fell silent, the crackling fire the only sound.  They listened, the weight of unspoken truths hanging heavy in the air. 
Finally, his voice broke through the hush. Low. Rough. The kind of voice that had carried secrets too long.
“Before I tell you this story…there are a few things you need to understand.”
He lifted his gaze then, and for the first time, they caught the eyes, crystal blue, not just bright, but unnatural. Cold fire behind ice. No one else in the world had eyes like that. Not anymore. They were the mark of something old. Something broke open. The kind of eyes that saw through lies and straight into the heart of a man.
“First,” he said, “no one knows the real story. Not the ballads. Not the Brotherhood. Not even the bastards who were there. You’re gonna hear it now. Once. Second…” His mouth pulled into something between a smirk and a wince. “I’m not a Shadow Walker. I’m the Shadow Walker. The first. The one the stories got wrong." 
He leaned forward, elbows on knees, fingers curling into each other like he might wring the truth out of his bones. “I’m not proud of the things I’ve done. I’ve carried a lot of blood in silence. The only reason I’m telling you now is… I guess I’m tired. And maybe, just maybe, I trust you fools more than I should.”
He exhaled long through his nose. The flames danced in his eyes, turning them into something sharp and ancient.
"So if I go quiet, or I make a call you don't understand, let it stand. I'm not asking for pity. I'm asking for trust." 
Then he sat back, spine straightening like a man about to carry a weight he’d dropped too long ago.
“This story ain’t small. And it doesn’t have a happy ending. But it’s mine.” He let the last word hang for a moment, just long enough to taste the fire again. “Here it goes.”
He looked into the fire for a long moment. When he spoke again, his voice had dropped to something quieter and more careful, the voice of a man picking up something he had put down a long time ago. "My name is Luca now. It was not always so. I was born Luk’on, a child of war, not the Second Magic War you’ve heard whispered about, but the First, when the skies cracked, and the Pearl shattered, spilling power and madness into the world.
“My mother and father were both killed in that war. I do not remember their faces, only the smell of smoke and the weight of silence that fell after their deaths. My sister, Eryel, and I were taken in by our uncle, his name was Velion, and his wife, Saela. Our grandmother lived with us as well, a quiet seer with cloudy eyes who often spoke to things we could not see. We lived in a hidden village called Tafeld, long forgotten now. It existed before the sealing of the ethereal plane, before the walls between realms were nailed shut with divine fury.
“Back then, I was taught there were four planes: the Material, the Heavenly, the Infernal, and the Ethereal. Between them lingered the Fae, not a realm, but a veil, a wild space where the forgotten and formless drifted. After the First Magic War, the gods feared what might still crawl between those worlds, so they sealed the Ethereal Plane, using it as a prison for the beast from the Material Plane. Due to this, it left the Infernal unbarred... and from that darkness came Uzhiel, a demon-lord who wrapped himself in silence and shadow.  He was not the only one; a few escaped with him.
“When the Ethereal was sealed, the Creator intended to seal the Heavenly as well, to keep even angels from tampering with mortal fate. But one exception was made: a Watcher, one who would serve as both judge and ferryman. His name was Ariel.
“Ariel was unlike the other angels. He walked among men, not with pride, but wonder. He was drawn not to their temples, but to their flaws, to their pain, and most of all… to beauty.
And he found that beauty in my sister, Eryel.
“She was the only light I ever truly knew. After a head injury in childhood, I was not… whole. I drifted, lived in fragments. She was my anchor. She called me “Luk’on” and I called her ‘Sissy’, always, until the day it changed.
“Ariel came often to our village in disguise, but not to deceive, to dwell. He watched Eryel in the fields, in the rain, in prayer. He came to love her.
“And she loved him.
“To impress her, he did something no angel should: he reached into me and healed my broken mind. I remember the moment, like waking from a dream I’d lived my whole life. My first words to her as my full self weren’t ‘Sissy’… but ‘Eryel.’ Her name. My first true word.
It was then that Ariel revealed himself. No wings, just presence. We saw the truth of him, and I loved him like a brother.
“But love between angel and mortal was forbidden.
“And the demons were watching.
“Uzhiel, hungry for passage between realms, saw a crack in the Creator’s plans. If an angel could fall for a mortal… then perhaps heaven could be breached, just as the infernal already had.
“They set a trap.  
“They came in human skin, demons possessing villagers, and manipulated others, hiding in flesh and soul. To lure Ariel in,  the group, made of mortals and demons alike,  killed Eryel in front of him.” 
Luca stopped. The word sat there in the firelight, plain and final, the way true things are plain and final when they have finally been said out loud after years of living only in the dark. He did not look at any of them. He was looking somewhere else entirely. He was enraged and took his sorrow out on them.  Ariel suspected one, a man he believed corrupted. In battle, he struck him down… but the man was not fully possessed, or so the Creator claimed; Ariel had broken the balance. He was cast down.  
“His wings torn from him, he became something in between, no longer angel, not quite mortal. He retained fragments of his grace: he could still see the divine judgments etched into the air around souls, and could send champions to fulfill them. But his form darkened. His hair paled like ash, yet his eyes turned to crystal blue, because he saw good as well as evil. He called himself the Shadow Watcher.
“He buried Eryel with his own hands after the attack. The demons had murdered her and burned Tafeld to cinders. I returned too late; the smoke was still rising.
“I found my uncle Velion’s body beside the well. I found Saela… what was left of her. And I found Ariel, kneeling at Eryel’s side, weeping smoke, his fingers still glowing with failing grace.
“He looked at me and said, ‘You are not ready for this path. Do not walk it. There is no mercy in shadows.”
“I looked into the eyes of my sister, gone. My anchor. My light.
“I said, ‘Then teach me to walk without mercy.’” He stopped again. Longer this time. The kind of pause that is not about finding words but about deciding whether the next ones are still survivable. The fire burned. Nobody filled the silence. 
“He drew a short sword from his side and cast it into the dirt. ‘Then let the shadows claim you,’ Ariel proclaimed.”
“I stepped forward. As my hand closed around the hilt, a cloak of darkness wrapped around me. An onyx medallion appeared at my throat, etched with runes I did not know but could feel pulsing inside me. My eyes changed like Ariel’s. In that moment, Luk’on died. I became Luca, the Shadow Walker.
“We hunted. Ariel could no longer interfere directly. But he could still see the contracts, glimpses of fate’s judgment upon the wicked. He became a silent god of vengeance, receiving tribute from those who begged for his justice or revenge. He never accepted gold. Only intent. He judged their words, and I carried out the sentence.
“It was just Ariel and me for decades, quiet work,” Luca said. “Never long enough to leave a mark. We found that names don’t last when no one stays to speak them. Over all that time, somehow, their number never lessened. They were always more, so the number of contracts grew.
“We created an order. A small circle of assassins chosen by Ariel and trained by me, each one capable of bearing a shard of the onyx and resisting its hunger. We called ourselves:
‘Usiku Wavuli,’ The Night Veil.
“Drawn from an old tongue, the name meant more than just shadow. It meant concealment with purpose, darkness wielded for light.
“We were assassins of evil. We struck down demon lords, cult leaders, and corrupted kings. We carved justice into stone.
“We fought in the Second Magic War, tracking and eliminating Lucent’s lieutenants, beasts made from their magic and madness. I fought beside Jasper, a ring bearing you might know. Though he does not always admit it.
“But Ariel… he was changing.
“Angels are not meant to stay in the material plane. Even as a fallen one, he was bound by a higher law. Over time, his body began to turn to stone, his soul fossilizing beneath the weight of divine punishment. Before he became fully inert, he crafted a Stone, the Judgment Stone. It holds a sliver of his will, enough for a bearer to send me on contracts when Ariel no longer can.
“But the Stone is dangerous. It cannot be held longer than thirty judgments at most before the bearer begins to fracture, drawn into Ariel’s crumbling consciousness.
“Adin has the Stone now. He does not know the cost. Or maybe he does.”
The silence that followed was tight, not calm.
“I serve because I must. Choice left me a long time ago. I’m still going because justice does not rest, and vengeance is still owed.
“So when you ask who I am… I am the weapon born of a fallen angel’s love. I am the promise made beside a sister’s grave. I am Luca, last of the Night Veil, bound to the Stone, haunted by the name “Sissy” I will never say again.
“And I have not yet judged my last soul.”
When Luca finished speaking, the silence returned, this time heavy, not hollow. The fire crackled softly, trying to fill the space he’d left behind, but even the flames seemed to burn lower, as if they, too, understood the depth of what had been shared. No one rushed to speak. No one dared.
Aymer stared into the coals, his jaw clenched just slightly. He didn’t look at Luca, not out of disrespect, but because some pain deserves its own space. Taerel shifted where he sat, arms wrapped around his knees, face drawn tight in thought. There was no joke, no sarcastic jab from him this time. Just a quiet nod, respect, earned and unspoken. Lysana did not speak. She reached forward, almost without meaning to, and her hand brushed the edge of his glove. Stopped there. The contact was nothing, barely a touch, but she felt the warmth of it even through the leather, and something in her chest tightened with a recognition she couldn't name. She had survived her own fire. She knew what it looked like in someone else. The firelight caught the sheen in her eyes, which she refused to let fall. She drew her hand back slowly. The cold moved in between them, quiet and immediate. He looked away first, and she let him. 
Snow drifted down from the branches overhead, soft flakes catching in their hair and on their shoulders. The forest around them had gone still, like the trees themselves were listening. Luca let out a slow breath, then pushed himself to his feet. He glanced at the others, then at the darkness beyond the circle of firelight.
“I’ll take first watch,” he said it the way he said most things, like the decision had already been made some time ago and he was only now letting it be heard. 
None of them argued.
One by one, they rose and moved to their bedrolls, the soft crunch of snow underfoot the only reply. No goodnights. Just the rustle of cloaks, the settling of bodies, the quiet creak of leather and armor. Sleep would come eventually, fitful, maybe, but enough to carry them forward.
Luca stood with his back to the fire, eyes scanning the trees, shadow and snow melting together. He caught the faintest sound behind him, a soft exhale, maybe her turning in her bedroll. The sound shouldn’t have meant anything, but it eased the silence more than the fire ever could. For the first time in years, he didn’t feel entirely alone in the dark. The story was out now, no longer gnawing at his ribs. And though the cold still bit, it didn’t feel quite as sharp.
Not tonight, but it would.
