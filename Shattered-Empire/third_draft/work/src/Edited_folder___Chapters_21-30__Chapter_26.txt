The acrid stench hit first, thick, burnt oil and scorched pitch. It clawed in Aymir’s nose, dragged through his lungs, and jolted him upright before he fully understood why. He blinked hard, his heart punching against his ribs. The canvas above his head shuddered from the wind, but it wasn’t that; it was the smell. A smell he hadn’t known in years. A memory that had seared itself into his past like a brand: battlefield smoke, burning cities, and screams buried under mortar fire.

Aymir jerked his cloak aside and sat up, sweat already breaking across his brow despite the morning cold.

“Do you smell that?” he asked as Luca emerged from the frost-creased lean-to beside him, already slipping on his bracers and pulling his cloak over his shoulders.

Luca gave one slow nod as he buckled his sword belt. “Burning oil. Tar and pitch. They’ve started something.”

Without another word, the two jogged through the snow toward the ridge above their camp, boots crunched over the hard-crusted drifts. Wind screamed low across the valley, carrying the scent of smoke, fresh, foul, and purposeful.

Taerel was already prone at the edge of the ridge, scope braced against a rock. He didn’t look up.

“They’re hammering the fort. Not just arrows. Fire pots. Burning oil. Catapult loads soaked in pitch. They’re trying to cook the defenders alive.”

Lysana crouched nearby, her hand shading her eyes as she scanned the horizon. Her jaw tightened as a fresh burst of flame licked up from the city below, lighting the low clouds a sickly orange.

Luca crouched beside her, eyes locked on the distant chaos. The scent of war crept up the slope, mingling with screams and the low rumble of impact.

He didn’t hesitate. “Today’s priority changes. We find a way into that city. Now. Lysana, you’re with me.”

He turned, and this time he didn't wait to see if they were ready. "Aymir. Taerel. Eyes on the camp. Take down what you see. Movements, shifts, anything that doesn’t fit.”

Aymir gave a grim nod, already wiping the lens of his looking glass.

As Luca and Lysana melted into the tree line toward the alternate ridge, Taerel sat down beside his brother.

“We’ve seen a dozen battle drills,” Taerel said. “Why do I feel like this one’s different?”

Aymir didn’t answer right away. His gaze was distant. Fixed. Not on the orc camp, but on something far older.

His voice, when it came, was hollow. “Because it is.” A fresh burst of flame rolled up from the city below, a low roar that crawled through the wind and rattled the frozen branches overhead.

Taerel watched him a moment longer. “You went back there again, didn’t you?”

Aymir’s eyes didn’t move. “When the oil hit, I was back in the trenches. Smoke everywhere. Fire climbing bodies.” His eyes watered. “I don’t even remember how I got out.”

Taerel didn’t push. Instead, he reached over and lightly knocked his knuckles against Aymir’s arm, grounding him as another wave of smoke tore across the ridge. “Hey. We’re not there anymore. We’re here. I’ve got your back, always.”

It took a moment, but Aymir blinked. Shook his head once. The haze began to lift. His gaze dropped back to the camp. Worgs circled something below, snarling over the kill. Aymir watched them for a moment. "Five coppers on the mangy one with the half-tail gets it first.”

Taerel chuckled. “You’re on.”

Luca and Lysana’s first attempt came on the lower ridge. A narrow rock shelf wound behind a frost-cracked boulder, offering a potential path closer to the city’s eastern flank. But halfway down, the trail gave out, collapsing beneath a recent rockfall; jagged stone and icy debris choked the ledge.

“No good,” Lysana muttered, eyes scanning the drop. “We’d break our necks before we ever reached the bottom.”

They circled wide. The second route looped through an old hunting trail, its narrow cut choked by avalanche debris and stone, sealing it as thoroughly as the first. Near the bottom, they found the remains of a collapse. “Looks like an avalanche a while back,” Luca said, looking at ten feet of compacted snow and boulders.

"Again?" Luca hissed. "How is every path sealed?"
Lysana looked at the chokepoints, then at him. Her cheeks were wind-raw, her breath coming in sharp white puffs. "It's a fort," she said. "Keeping people out is its purpose." No complaint in it. Just a fact. She let it sit for a moment. "Either winter did this, or someone worked these ridges before we got here."
Luca went still. A distant scream rolled down the mountain and faded into the wind. His hands stayed where they were. So did he.
Lysana didn't move either. She stood at his shoulder, watching the same stretch of ruined wall, the same wall that kept giving them nothing. The wind cut between them, and neither of them said anything about it.
Below, the orcs loosed another volley. She watched the arrows climb and fall, black lines against the pale sky, swallowed by the noise and ruin below.
Her eyes moved to her own quiver.
"Charcoal marker," she said. "Do you have one?"
He pulled it from his kit without asking why. She was already moving, spreading a strip of oiled map-cloth flat against her knee, the waxed kind Rangers used for field notes. She wrote four short lines, her hand steady despite the cold, rolled the cloth tight, and tied it to a shaft with a cord stripped from her bootlace.
"We can't reach them on foot. We can't call out. We can't light a signal." She checked the weight of the arrow and balanced it once in her palm. "But they're already watching the sky. One more shaft won't stand out."
She nocked it and waited for the next orc volley. She fired it. Her arrow flew true and struck the church’s doorframe. “Now we wait,” she said, exhaling.
They didn’t move. Didn’t speak. Snow drifted down like ash.
Lysana crouched low beneath the skeletal shadow of a pine, her breath curling in the air like smoke. Above her, the wind had picked up, biting, constant, whispering across the ridge and through the mountain trees with the chill of old bones. She and Luca had taken shelter in their second vantage point, a narrow cut of rock and pine above the battered city, waiting for a reply to the arrow she’d loosed earlier.
Snow crept into the folds of their armor and boots, numbing fingers and stiffening joints. They hadn’t moved much since launching the arrow into the church’s doorframe. Every motion risked detection. Luca shifted now, flexing his cramping legs and rotating his shoulder.
The smell of ash and oil still clung to the air, sharper now, carried by the wind. And below, the three massive bonfires in the orc siege camp lit the entire valley with flickering orange and red, casting leaping shadows along the half-ruined walls of the city. From this angle, the light made it look as if the very earth were burning.
They had been waiting for hours. Long enough for the light to thin and the cold to settle deep.
A shadow passed across one of the far parapets before vanishing again. “They’re moving more tonight,” Lysana muttered, eyes narrowed.
Luca didn’t respond immediately. He had that stillness about him again—his breath quiet, his jaw set. She knew that look. He was thinking, calculating, trying to find the thread in the chaos.
“You think they got it?” he murmured.
Lysana nodded slightly, her hood tugged low against the wind. “They got it. They just don’t know how to answer yet.”
A long moment passed before she added, “We’re not exactly a normal cavalry rescue.”
Then the movement caught her eye. Then she froze. “Look,” she whispered.
Out of the church’s doors two figures stepped into view, wrapped in what looked like dyed bedding tied to old poles. The makeshift signal flags waved slowly, deliberately—right to left, then a short diagonal, then up, pause, then down. The flags were mismatched and frayed, but the message was unmistakable.
Lysana read the signal, breath catching in her throat. “They’re short on everything—medicine, food, clean water. The wounded can’t be moved. They need help fast.”
Luca exhaled hard. “All right. That’s our open line.”
They scrambled back down the slope toward the ridge line, and the two brothers were posted up. Their boots crunched through crusted snow. At the bottom, the brothers were already sitting against a log back from the ledge, Aymir sipping from a battered waterskin, Taerel hunched over the maps and noteboards by the dying glow of a covered lantern.
Aymir adjusted the charcoal slate he'd laid across a canvas-covered plank. “Orcs rotated guard lines,” he said, more to himself. “North flank just took in replacements. Standard pattern, no surprises.”

Taerel peered through the scope again. “Check the northwest pen. That worg pup from earlier is gone.”

Aymir marked it. “Might be out hunting.”

“Or scouting,” Taerel muttered. He adjusted the scope a fraction. “Movement feels tighter today.”

Taerel gestured toward the camp. “At this rate, I’m tempted to wager on which orc spills the stew next.”

“Left-handed tusker. Guaranteed,” Aymir said. “He’s been fumbling that ladle all morning.”
Both Luca and Lysana made their way to the brothers and their position. Aymir noticed them first as they returned. “How’d it go?” Aymir asked, brushing a light sheen of frost from his eyebrows.
Luca crouched near the boards. “We made contact. They have a high wounded count. They are held up in the church. We were also right about their supplies almost being gone, and we still don’t have a way in.”
“Seems the gods are unkind today,” Taerel muttered, glancing toward the orange sky as another fire pot burst in the distance.
Luca glanced at the brothers. “We’re stretched thin. No reliable means to pass messages to each other. Limited eyes. Low supplies.” His eyes stayed on the siege camp a moment longer, as if giving himself exactly enough time to let the weight of it settle. “Taerel, I need you to ride and find a village or anything at all. Get supplies and bring them back. Anything, anything useful that you can find, and medical supplies for those inside. They are going to need that, I think. Get what you need to start those bracelets, as well. We need better ways to pass messages."
Taerel’s gaze went to the dark timberline. “If the roads aren’t choked and the patrols don’t turn, I can be back within, let’s say, two days.” He swung into the saddle. “If they do, I'll come back when I can. Better late with provisions than swift and dead.”
“Take the eastern slope,” Luca added. “They’re more likely to have more patrols westward.”
Taerel gave a short nod and turned toward the camp.
Luca turned to Aymir. “Break down camp. We’re moving tonight. Lysana, take point and pick a new location. Somewhere east of the ridge. We’ve been there too long already.”
She nodded. “If we’re going to be ghosts, we can’t sleep in the same bed twice.”
Aymir gave a dry chuckle. “Truth in that.”
As Aymir stalked off toward the canvas shelters, Luca and Lysana lingered, glancing over the notched records the brothers had carved into the wood. Patrol rotations, forge output, and the number of orcs moving between tents. The system was crude but effective.
Each record was timestamped using the sun’s position and measured in fingers from the horizon, a technique old Rangers called the Fennar method.
They took turns scanning the camp again, confirming tallies, adding new ones. The siege didn’t sleep. Worgs prowled. Orcs rotated in disciplined patterns. Goblins argued around fires. And in the far corner near the totem, a group of humans still knelt in chains. Lysana had gone very still when she spotted them. She didn't say anything. She didn't have to. 
At the edge of camp, Taerel and Aymir stood by their mounts. “If it flips to tails,” Taerel whispered, holding up his worn coin, “tell Dad I tried.”
Aymir grunted. “Don’t be stupid. You’re too annoying to die.”
Taerel flipped the coin once more, caught it, and pocketed it without looking. "I've played worse hands." They grinned the way men do when the next breath isn't guaranteed, and they've stopped pretending otherwise.
“Blood before thunder,” Aymir said.
“Thunder before silence,” Taerel replied.
Taerel embraced him briefly but fiercely. They tapped foreheads.
“What do we always say?” Taerel asked.
“Not goodbye. Just later.” A cold gust swept through the trees and rattled the saddle straps, as if urging him on. Taerel mounted and turned east, vanishing between the trees. The camp faded behind him. And behind him, the old fires still burned, and The Pass burned with them.
At the new campsite, in a shallow depression between two clustered firs, half-hidden by drifted snow, Luca and Lysana laid out their gear again. No fire. No noise. Just lean-tos and silent breath, and the distant sound of the siege carrying up the mountain like something that didn't know they were there. 
Aymir was cleaning his rifle by lamplight, his hands steady despite the tension in his jaw.
Lysana finally asked, “Could you explain how that thing works? I would like to know.”
He glanced up, eyes tired but focused. “Science and gears. Not magic. Chambered rounds with chemical triggers in this revolving chamber, copper bands, and etched spirals to guide the propulsion. I know how to keep it breathing, and it speaks when I ask.”
She watched him polish the barrel, her gaze flicking toward the distant fires. The wind carried a rising edge, a warning that something was shifting.
Luca stood near the edge of the ridge, cloak fluttering in the wind. “I’ll take first watch. You two get some sleep. We rotate every four hours. Tomorrow... we start again.”
They nodded, settling into their blankets, pulling hoods low. Just as the forest began to settle, Lysana jerked upright.
“Flags,” she whispered. “They’re signaling again.”
Luca was at her side instantly. The movements were slower now, more hesitant. The color of the bedding flags had changed, but the pattern was unmistakable.
“They found something,” Lysana said, brow furrowed. “A tunnel. Its condition’s unclear. If it’s good, it should run behind the town, through the undercroft. They’re marking coordinates for us to find it.”
She turned to Luca. “Give me the map.”
He handed it over, and she traced a gloved finger along the edge of the valley.
“Here,” she said softly. “We start here tomorrow.”
Luca exhaled slowly. “Let’s hope.”
Aymir closed his eyes for a moment. "Thank you, God the Creator." 
The wind howled once more. Behind them, the camp sat in darkness. Ahead, the siege lit the world in flame.
And somewhere out there, Taerel rode into the unknown.
