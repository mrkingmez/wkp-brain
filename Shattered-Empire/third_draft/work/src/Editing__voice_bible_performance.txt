THE VOICE BIBLE
Performance and Cadence Reference

GLOBAL CANON RULES
No two major characters may:
· Respond to the same stimulus using the same sentence structure
· Share identical rhythm signatures
· Express agreement in identical phrasing
· Default to the same emotional register
If three characters could swap lines without detection, the dialogue fails the Voice Test.
VOICE ENHANCEMENT RULES:
- Dialogue meaning, intent, and narrative function must never change
- Dialogue may not be rewritten wholesale
- Enhancements are limited to micro word-choice refinement, cadence shaping, minimal delivery
beats, and structural dialect
- Phonetic spelling is forbidden
- If a line already reflects character voice, it must be marked STRONG and left untouched
- The goal is recognizability, not cleverness

INTELLIGENCE & COMPETENCE RULE:
- Accent, class, or profession never implies lack of intelligence
- Plain speech reflects efficiency or habit, not ignorance
- Soldiers and laborers are assumed tactically and situationally aware
- Stupidity must be explicitly intended by the author
- Flag accidental incompetence rather than enforcing it

MINOR CHARACTER RULE:
- Characters without named entries use Neutral World Voice
- No unique quirks or humor for minor characters
- Dialogue may only be polished for clarity, era consistency, and scene function
- Promotion flagged after 3 scenes or 10 lines

NEUTRAL WORLD VOICE:
- Plain, functional, era-appropriate speech
- Minimal emotional color
- No humor unless scene-required

WORLD & REGIONAL VOICES

These are speech cultures, not caricatures. They shape how people form sentences and how
emotion bends their voice.

ALTISFLUMEN

Inspiration: Victorian England

Cadence: Measured, formal, deliberate. Balanced clauses.
Consonants: Clean, articulated. Not harsh, but precise.
Vowels: Rounded and controlled.
Tempo: Moderate, slightly slowed under formality.
Emotional Leakage: Minimal. Emotion filtered through restraint.
Sentence Shape: Layered clauses. Subordinate phrasing common.
Under Stress: Contractions drop. Tone sharpens but does not shout.

Audible Grain Markers (Narrative Use Sparingly):
· Words shaped carefully
· Consonants placed deliberately
· Breath held between clauses
· Precision without haste
OPINDALE

Inspiration: Modern American neutral

Cadence: Direct, conversational, efficient.
Consonants: Neutral. No excessive clipping.
Vowels: Open, relaxed.
Tempo: Moderate to quick.
Emotional Leakage: Moderate. Emotion visible in tone.
Sentence Shape: Straightforward. Subject-verb clarity.
Under Stress: Speech speeds slightly. Humor may persist.

Audible Grain Markers:
· Easy breath
· Words come quickly
· Tone lifts at ends of questions
· Energy forward

ALDORIA

Inspiration: Heavy Germanic structure

Cadence: Firm, structured, weight-forward.
Consonants: Hard, struck cleanly.
Vowels: Tightened. Less airy.
Tempo: Controlled. Slightly heavy.
Emotional Leakage: Suppressed.
Sentence Shape: Declarative. Logical sequencing.
Under Stress: Contractions vanish. Sentences shorten.
Voice becomes iron-edged.

Audible Grain Markers:
· Consonants land hard
· Jaw set when speaking
· Words pressed forward
· Breath measured through teeth

TRIBAL LANDS

Inspiration: Native American rhythm influence
Cadence: Thoughtful. Slight pauses before key words.
Consonants: Clean but unforced.
Vowels: Open, grounded.
Tempo: Unhurried.
Emotional Leakage: Low. Emotion carried in stillness.
Sentence Shape: Imagistic. Metaphor from land and nature.
Under Stress: Speech becomes quieter, not louder.

Audible Grain Markers:
· Silence used intentionally
· Words spaced apart
· Breath steady
· Tone grounded

MOUNTAINEERS

Inspiration: West Virginia / Appalachian rhythm

Cadence: Rolling. Slight sway in rhythm.
Consonants: Softer edges.
Vowels: Broader, drawn slightly longer.
Tempo: Moderate but elastic.
Emotional Leakage: High warmth or blunt frustration.
Sentence Shape: Narrative tendency. Slight storytelling tilt.
Under Stress: Words compress, vowels tighten, warmth drops.

Audible Grain Markers:
· Words stretch slightly
· Breath carries through phrases
· Tone rises and falls naturally

MORELAND

Inspiration: Russian tonal weight

Cadence: Controlled, deliberate, heavy emphasis on nouns.
Consonants: Strong, pressed.
Vowels: Flattened slightly.
Tempo: Slow and purposeful.
Emotional Leakage: Minimal, but intensity sits beneath.
Sentence Shape: Statement > justification.
Under Stress: Tone becomes colder, not louder.
Audible Grain Markers:
· Words delivered flatly
· Pauses before decisive nouns
· Breath minimal

MORADAER

Inspiration: Swedish / Norwegian tonal lift

Cadence: Even, slightly lilting upward inflection.
Consonants: Light but clear.
Vowels: Rounded, clean.
Tempo: Calm.
Emotional Leakage: Subtle warmth.
Sentence Shape: Balanced but not ornate.
Under Stress: Tone tightens, musical lift disappears.

Audible Grain Markers:
· Slight upward finish
· Breath steady
· Tone smooth

KARETH

Inspiration: Southern / Texas rhythm

Cadence: Relaxed but confident.
Consonants: Softened edges.
Vowels: Broadened.
Tempo: Unhurried unless angered.
Emotional Leakage: Open. Emotion visible.
Sentence Shape: Direct but personable.
Under Stress: Warmth drops; vowels tighten sharply.

Audible Grain Markers:
· Words linger slightly
· Tone carries warmth
· Breath relaxed

MERRANT ISLAND / VELMOR'S ISLAND

Inspiration: West Indies linguistic mix
Cadence: Musical, varied rhythm.
Consonants: Fluid.
Vowels: Rounded and layered.
Tempo: Variable, expressive.
Emotional Leakage: High expressiveness.
Sentence Shape: Colorful phrasing, occasional idiomatic layering.
Under Stress: Musicality sharpens; rhythm becomes quick and pointed.

Audible Grain Markers:

· Breath animated

MAJOR CHARACTER VOICES
These are performance profiles. They describe how the voice sits in the mouth, how the past leaks

HARMON
Harmon's voice carries centuries of command. His cadence is military at its core, shaped by long
leadership and survival. His words land with weight, as if every sentence expects to be obeyed.
His phrasing contains blended rhythms picked up across his long life, giving him an old-world
sound that does not quite belong to the present age.

When he tries to be lighthearted, the humor is dry and slightly off. He jokes like a soldier who
learned humor late. It is sincere, but rough-edged.

When he is tired or angry, the ancient command voice resurfaces. When he is relaxed, a rare
warmth slips in.

Example:

"I am not angry. I am disappointed, which is far more dangerous."

"Try not to die. I have buried enough heroes already."

· Rarely uses rhetorical questions
· Uses declarative structure under stress
· Humor is dry, but never fast
· Silence is a tool -- he allows space after heavy lines

Pressure Pattern:
Under anger or fatigue, contractions drop and sentences shorten.

Forbidden:
· Rapid-fire speech
· Street phrasing
· Casual filler words

Texture:
· Words land like they were tested before release
· Uses silence after heavy lines
· Breath is controlled even in anger

Audible Grain:
He sounds like someone who expects obedience without raising volume.

Example:
Not: "We need to move."
But: "We move."

Under stress:
"I will not lose another." (shortened, contraction dropped)
ASH

Ash speaks fast, with a rhythm shaped by streets, docks, and danger. His words tumble forward

Especially when he is afraid.

His voice belongs to someone who grew up having to talk his way out of trouble. When truly
wounded, the humor drops suddenly, and the silence becomes louder than any joke.

Example:

"Oh sure, cursed tunnels again. Why not?"

"If this goes bad, at least it'll be memorable."

· Speaks faster than most characters
· Uses contractions heavily
· Often interrupts tension with short deflections
· Rarely finishes a full strategic explanation

Pressure Pattern:
When fear spikes, humor thins and lines become blunt.

Forbidden:
· Formal cadence
· Long metaphor chains
· Noble diction

Texture:
· Slight forward momentum in speech
· Humor rides close to fear
· Contractions frequent

Audible Grain:
He sounds like someone already halfway to the door.

Example:
"Well. That's ugly."
"Yeah, that's not ideal."

Under fear:
Jokes shorten.
"That's bad."

WESLIN

Weslin's voice is young and uncertain. Weslin is thirteen. He is not a seasoned thinker. He is still
a boy.

He has barely left Opindale. Much of the world is new to him.
Mountains feel too large. Cities feel too loud. Stone halls echo more than he expects.

He reacts before he refines.

He notices everything.

He points things out.

Sometimes too loudly.

He is still learning how to stand in a room full of adults without feeling small.

Wonder must be visible in him early in the book.
Curiosity is not subtle in him yet.

He asks questions he should not.

He says observations out loud that older characters would keep private.

He is intelligent -- but not seasoned.

He processes the world in real time.

He does not yet control how that processing sounds.

He hesitates and over-explains. Occasionally, older cadences slip through, inherited from his
father without his understanding.

Weslin's voice carries intellectual earnestness layered over emotional vulnerability. His cadence
is thoughtful and occasionally hesitant, as if ideas form in real time. He does not command
rooms. He works through them.

"When he is calm, he sounds analytical and searching.
When he is afraid, he becomes more precise.
When he is overwhelmed, he stumbles briefly -- then forces clarity."

Example:
"I think-- no, wait."
"There has to be a reason."
"That doesn't fit."

· Uses qualifying phrases naturally
· Occasionally self-corrects when refining logic
· Rarely speaks in absolutes unless cornered
· Prefers reasoning over authority

Weslin must sound like:

· A boy eager to prove himself
· Slightly unsure of how he is perceived
· Curious about everything unfamiliar
· Prone to speaking before fully processing
· Brave in bursts
· Self-conscious after

He should occasionally:

· Point at something
· Ask a question mid-stride
· Speak too loudly without realizing
· Correct himself awkwardly
· Apologize when he senses he overstepped
He must not sound like:

· A young scholar delivering structured arguments
· A tactical thinker giving clean battlefield insight
· A political analyst
· A composed adult

Confidence must be earned over time.

Pressure Pattern:

Forbidden:

· Sarcastic dominance
