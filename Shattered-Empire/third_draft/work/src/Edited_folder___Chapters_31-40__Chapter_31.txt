
The sun was low by the time Luca and Lysana crested the ridge. The wind had changed. Softer now, with less smoke in the air. But the sky still burned orange over the ruined horizon, stained by two days of fire and iron.

They rode in silence, both hunched slightly in their saddles, weariness bleeding into their posture. Below them, nestled behind the bluffs and bramble-tucked ledges, their camp remained just as they’d left it. Small. Quiet. Hidden.

As they approached, a shadow stirred by the fire.

Luca slowed. “Someone’s back.”

Lysana narrowed her eyes. “Two horses.”

They slid down from the saddle as they reached the perimeter, boots crunching the brittle shale underfoot. A figure stood and turned, his left arm wrapped tight in bandages from elbow to wrist, blood seeping faintly through the linen. His hair was damp with sweat, and his jaw clenched against pain.

“Taerel,” Lysana breathed.

The wounded man gave a crooked smile. “Surprise.”

Luca stepped forward, gaze flicking from the bandage to the tired slump in his shoulders. Beside Taerel knelt another man, hair darker but eyes unmistakably similar. Aymir, binding the last loop of a fresh dressing.

“He’s lucky I found him when I did,” Aymir said evenly. “He was half-slumped over the saddle, bleeding into the horse’s flank. No trail. Just a mark.”

“I told you I was well enough,” Taerel muttered, adjusting the bandage.

“Sure,” Aymir said dryly. “You looked great.”

Luca crouched down to inspect the bandage. “Crossbow?”

Taerel nodded. “Clean pass. Grazed deep. Could’ve been worse.” He flexed his fingers once, testing the numbness, and stopped before it spread.

“What happened?” Luca asked.

He didn't answer immediately. His jaw set once, then released. “I spotted a group of bandits moving through the high trail,” he said finally. “They had two packhorses loaded like a merchant train. Guessing they hit a caravan not long before I found them.”

Luca nodded once. He’d seen that kind of kill-the-wagon behavior before, strip everything of value and burn what was left.

“I shadowed them for a while. Waited until two of them wandered from the fire. Took them quietly, grabbed what I could.” Aymir’s eyes flicked over, as if checking whether Taerel was minimizing something.

He exhaled through his nose, the shame finally surfacing.

“I stayed too long. Should’ve pulled out early. Got greedy. A crossbow bolt took me in the arm before I made it to the tree line. Rode hard until everything blurred. Next thing I knew, Aymir was dragging me back.”

"Came back breathing and loaded," Lysana said. "That's not nothing." 

“Not good enough,” Taerel said. “I should’ve found a town. A route we could use. Something we could count on. This, what I brought back, won’t last.”

“You’re alive,” Luca said. “What you brought might keep them alive. That matters.”

Taerel didn’t look convinced, but he gave a nod all the same.

The wind shifted again. Behind them, the last red strands of sunlight spilled over the ridge, cutting across their tired faces.

“We’ve got a better place,” Lysana said. “Cavern system tucked into the base of the slope, back through the old passage. Dry, quiet, shielded. We can move the camp tonight.”

Luca gave a sharp nod. “Mount up. No reason to wait.”

They rode single file, winding down the slope through the narrow cut of shale that hid their path from any wandering eyes. The sky dimmed to purple, then black. No moon tonight, only scattered stars and the glow of dying fire across the western cliffs.

The passage was tight, but they moved with practiced quiet. Horses stepped carefully across the stone, hooves hushed on the rock. When the opening came into view, dark and swallowed by roots, it looked like nothing, just another wound in the hill. 

But inside… 
Inside was breath. Luca lit the first lantern and stepped forward into the vast, natural chamber. The others followed, the walls blooming with flickering gold as the light spread.

A single drop of water echoed from somewhere deep inside, a reminder that the cavern lived even when the world above tried to die. Aymir let out a slow whistle. “Well. You weren’t exaggerating.”

Taerel dismounted and winced as his boots hit the stone. “This is... good.”

“Better than good,” Lysana said. 

No one disagreed. They set to it quickly, hitching the horses near the dry corner, laying out their gear. Taerel moved more slowly but stubbornly refused help. Aymir let him.

Watch rotations were assigned: one at the ridge, one near the tunnel entrance. If anything came this way, they’d know. As Luca stoked a small fire in the center of the chamber, they all paused, looking around the space again. A brief silence fell.

Then they noticed it, the absence. The bombardment had stopped. No thunder. No shrieking wood. No screams carried on the smoke, just silence.

Aymir stood up slowly, looking toward the ridge. “It’s been a while since the last volley.”

A subtle tension passed through all three of them. Nobody moved. Luca nodded. Lysana opened her mouth to speak, but stopped. She looked at him for a moment, the way he moved toward the rifle without deliberating, and decided not to argue. He was already strapping his rifle across his back. "I'll be quick," he said, already moving. The stone throat of the ridge swallowed him. 

The others sat in a loose triangle around the fire, their shadows flickering on the walls. Taerel leaned back against a saddlebag. “I really hope this place doesn’t collapse on us.”

Lysana smirked. “Then try not to breathe too hard. You might finish what time has already started.”

The tension didn’t leave, but it settled. For the first time in days, they weren’t under direct threat or exposed to the elements.

The fire crackled low in the center of the stone chamber, its light dancing across the rough walls and casting long shadows. Outside the circle of warmth, the cavern stretched into darkened alcoves and narrow mouths, a web of stone veins lost in time.

Luca crouched near the packs Taerel had salvaged, pulling the leather lashings apart with the same economy he gave everything, no wasted motion. One by one, he laid out the contents, cataloging them in his head. Most of the supplies were crammed into satchels stitched from worn hide, probably looted, like the horses, from a traveling merchant or courier.

First came dried rations, hardtack, spiced jerky, and old root vegetables wrapped in burlap. Then a few medical rolls: aged linen, half-empty jars of salve, brittle herb bundles. Taerel tilted his head. “That jerky is definitely older than your sense of humor.” 

Luca didn't answer. He kept going, cooking tins. Tarnished silverware. A cracked flintbox. A handful of throwing knives tucked in a belt pouch. An empty flask with an etched crest long since scratched away. 

At the bottom, something more useful. Luca turned it over in his hands. A tightly bound roll of treated leather, thick and water-resistant, still supple despite its age. “This,” he murmured, “might actually be worth something.”

Taerel, resting against a nearby saddle, arched an eyebrow. “What, my random haul impressed the shadow assassin?”

Luca didn’t answer. Just gave the faintest nod of approval.

Aymir would’ve liked that moment, but he wasn’t there to see it, already halfway up the ridge by now. That silence still sat on them like a weight, every moment of it unnatural. The silence that made them uneasy was the same silence giving the people in that chapel the only rest they'd had in days. 

Lysana walked over with a strip of dried meat in her hand and sat beside Luca. She studied the items as he repacked what was worth carrying. “Taerel’s got an eye for loot.”

“I have an eye for desperation,” Taerel muttered. “That’s all this is.”

“Still,” she said, elbowing him lightly, “you came back with more than most would.”

The smile came. But his eyes were somewhere else for a moment, counting something that hadn't added up yet. 

Luca selected a small canvas satchel and began filling it with the essentials: dried food, clean linen, a few jars of ointment, one of the pans, and a wrapped salve stone. No frills. Just what was needed for a second trip to the chapel. His shoulders were tense, not from weight, but from thought.

He stood and secured the flap. “I’m going back as soon as I catch my breath. Yesterday would've been better. Tonight will have to do.” 

“I’ll come,” Lysana said, already standing.

Luca shook his head. “No. Two shadows draw twice the eyes. If I’m stopped, you stay hidden and hold this place. We can’t both vanish at once.”

She raised an eyebrow. “I’m fine.”

“You’re not irreplaceable,” he said. A beat passed. "But you come close," he said quietly, something close to a smile on his face. The words sat there a moment, unclaimed by either of them. 

She blinked, a flicker of something passing through her eyes. Then she grinned. “Fine. Go be a ghost.”

Taerel stretched his legs, wincing slightly. “What about me? I’m fresh and ready.”

“You’re bleeding through your bandage,” Lysana said flatly.

Taerel frowned. “That is what one calls contributing.”

“Contribute from over there,” she said, pointing to a sleeping roll.

He leaned back again with a dramatic sigh. “Tyrants. I’m surrounded.”

Luca allowed the banter to drift around him, but his mind remained sharp, chewing through variables, numbers, and distances. The supplies might last another day, two at best, even stretched thin. After that... they’d be boiling leather straps and praying for a miracle.

He stood, shouldered the satchel, and went. 

Time stretched thin as the two remained in camp. A single drip, somewhere deep in the dark, tapped out its count. They listened without meaning to. Lysana had the food going before Taerel had found a comfortable way to sit with his arm. He watched her work for a moment, then pulled himself upright and started on the bedrolls with one hand. Lysana flipped the meat, stood, and looked toward the tunnel. "Where's Aymir?" 

“On the ridge still,” Taerel said. “He’ll come back when he knows more. The smell of the food will make that happen faster, I bet.”

They waited, silent now. The fire crackled faintly, the only sound in the chamber. Outside, the world was still. Not calm, just… waiting. The kind of waiting that made men feel like prey. Taerel shifted where he sat, wincing as his arm tugged at the bandage. “I don’t like it. It’s been too long.”

Lysana stared toward the tunnel. “He’ll come back.”

As the dusk grew into full night, the fire had settled into a soft rhythm, glowing amber across the stone walls of the cavern. The warmth it gave off was meager but steady, and in its circle of light, Lysana and Taerel sat with plates in their laps, voices lowered to a quiet lull.

“Where are you from, exactly?” Lysana asked, breaking a piece of flatbread in half.

"South of Rindhall, in Altisflumen," Taerel replied, chewing slowly. “A city called Varneth. Not on most maps. Built tight into the river bends. Real quiet, mostly.”

She raised a brow. “So more farmland or stone and smoke?”

“Both,” he said. “We’ve got steam mills, waterwheels that churn all day. Plumbing in most homes. And steamships docked on the river that ran as far as the sea.” 

“Not bad,” she said.

Taerel gave a soft chuckle. “Not magic, though. That’s the key. We don’t really have Adepts out there. Or if we do, no one’s looking for them. Shards aren’t used for spells; they’re fuel or machinery, nothing more. Cracked shards power the forge motors, even a few of the street lamps in the square. But people don’t channel them. No schools. No tests.”

Lysana frowned. “So what happens to people who could’ve been tested?”

“They get overlooked,” he said simply. “And if they adapt anyway, they go feral. Or clever. Or disappear.”

“Hunter-types,” she murmured.

“Exactly,” Taerel said. “You learn to survive quietly. Fast. If you spark a light, someone’s going to notice. And out there… people don’t like what they don’t understand.”

She was quiet for a moment, then offered him a small smile. “So, of course, you left. Makes sense now.”

He shrugged. “I like breathing places. Places that don’t feel like they were built to smother.”

Lysana chuckled. “Yeah. You’re definitely not a city boy.”

He raised his flatbread like a toast. “Rooted in tradition.”

That was when they heard the soft scuff of boots at the tunnel’s mouth.

Luca returned, half-shadowed by the stone wall as he stepped inside. The weight in his stride said more than his face, which was expressionless and pale. His satchel was lighter, emptied, but his shoulders sagged from exhaustion, not burden.

Lysana rose without a word, pulled together a warm portion of what remained, and set it beside him on the nearest flat stone. He lowered himself into place without argument, unslinging the satchel from his shoulder and letting it fall to the floor with a dull thump.

She handed him a waterskin. “You made it.”

Luca drank deeply, letting the cold water settle the tremor in his hands before he reached for the food.

“They’re holding,” he said between bites. “Barely. The wounded are stable. What we brought helped. They’ve turned the chapel into a bunker, but even the strongest walls crack when they’re that brittle.”

He glanced up. “I’ve got new numbers. Forty-four civilians inside. Eighteen wounded. One critical. Eighteen guards, tired but still standing. Doing their best.”

Then he looked at Taerel. “What’s the final count from the siege camp?”

Taerel answered with a grim sigh. “Best I could estimate, we’re looking at anywhere from eighty to a hundred total. That includes beast handlers, archers, and whatever’s pulling siege rotation. They’re running three to seven patrols a day. There are twenty-three worgs.”

Luca gave a short nod. “All right. I need to finalize the report. Harmon’s waiting for it. I want to include more than just numbers. I want to give him a plan.”

No one spoke. Without warning, thunder. The first boom wasn’t distant. It was deep, echoing through the stone like a pulse in the spine. The second came moments later, then the third, as the siege resumed in full force. The cavern rumbled slightly from the force of the impacts. A faint cascade of dust trickled from the ceiling overhead. Luca didn’t react. He just chewed, eyes narrowed, listening.

“Guess they're back at it,” Taerel muttered. He quickly turned when he heard footsteps pounding from the tunnel mouth. A blur of motion, Aymir, cloak flaring, boots sliding on stone as he nearly lost footing on the incline. He hit the ground sideways, skidded, then popped back up without missing a beat.

Taerel arched a brow. “There will come a day when you arrive somewhere without the accompanying spectacle. I look forward to it.”

Aymir gave him a grin. “And deny the rocks their entertainment?”

He walked to the fire like the entrance hadn't happened and dropped down beside them, accepting the plate Lysana offered with a nod of thanks.

Luca studied him. “You were gone a while.”

“Had to be,” Aymir said, mouth full. “Wasn’t about just what I saw. It was what I didn’t see that mattered.”

Lysana leaned forward, brow furrowed. “Go on.”

Aymir set his plate down, now more serious. “It was strange. The bombardment stopped. Not staggered, not misfired, stopped. Everything froze. And for a few minutes, I thought maybe they were repositioning. But then I saw the cart come back.”

“What cart?” Luca asked.

“Hunting party,” Aymir said. “Five orcs, I had them logged leaving at first light. Came back dragging enough meat to feed a fortress. Wild drakes, boar, even a plucked carrion buzzard slung over the cart frame. They took it straight into the mess.” Lysana went still at that image, but she kept quiet. This was Aymir's report. He'd get there. 

“And then?” Taerel asked.

“Then the line broke,” Aymir said. “And not like panic. Organized. Calm. The siege crews walked off their lines. The handlers herded the beasts into their pens. Everyone moved like they had somewhere to be.”

He paused, eyes catching the firelight.

“And then they sat down. Not in shifts, all of them. Every orc, every wrangler, every crewman. Full formation. It was like a military exercise. Or a ritual.”

Lysana sat up straighter. “All at once?”

“They prepped in silence. Big pot fires, ladles, and even table setups. It was like watching a damn festival. And then they served.”

“They did all this in one go?” Luca asked, voice low.

“Every night, is what I am guessing. Last night there was a lull,” Aymir said. “Shorter. I dismissed it. Tonight it matched the same rhythm. Same stillness before the fires rose. I don’t believe in coincidence that precise.”

“And you’re thinking…?” Taerel asked, already suspicious of the tone in his brother’s voice.

Aymir just gave a slow, casual shrug. “I’m just saying... if someone were to slip something unpleasant into that meal—”

Luca blinked once. "Poison? It's ugly work." 

Aymir grinned. “So is a starving city. You do know poisons?”

Luca narrowed his eyes."Yes, it is part of the trade." 

Aymir smiled and exhaled slowly. “Yes. It is.”

Lysana was already getting up. “I’ve got roots. Oils in my pack. If we start steeping it now, it’ll be ready by dawn.”

Luca turned his head. “You know poisons?”

She said it with a playful smile. “There is much you do not know about me, shadow.”

Luca smiled back, holding her gaze a heartbeat longer than necessary.

Taerel grinned. “I hate how much I like this plan.”

Aymir sat forward, firelight flickering in his eyes. “We get one shot. One big, nasty pot of something awful slipped in just before dusk. If it doesn’t kill them outright, it’ll drop them hard enough we can either strike... or get those people out.”

Taerel had been listening to the poison plan with one eye on the tunnel. "The people," he said. "We still have to get them out."
Luca nodded in agreement. "We do it in shifts," he said. "Weakest first. Guards rotate as cover. We move when the patrol and bombardment pattern gives us a window. We will have to move slowly. Some of them are seriously injured, and those caverns leading here are not that simple to pass through.” He went quiet for a moment, and when he spoke again, it was almost to himself. "Good thing I held the message." 
He pointed toward the fire. “Let’s break it all down. I want to ensure I pass on the right information.”

They quickly went over the plan: the orcs' synchronized eating schedule, the hunting party's return window, the central food prep area, and how they could infiltrate the mess with enough time to deliver a poison payload before dusk. They estimated travel time, best-case doses, and fallback routes.

When it was done, Luca stood and tapped the thin, rune-etched bracelet on his wrist. The silver threads glowed faintly as the connection opened. 

He turned, voice low and precise. “Weslin, this is Luca. Let me tell you what’s been going on…”

As the bracelet dimmed, he looked to Lysana. “Go start your distilling. Let’s see how potent we can get.”

She nodded and walked off toward her packs.

Then he turned to Aymir. “Do everything you can. Tomorrow we go hunting for more ingredients. We’re going to need a poison so foul it’ll punch through even an orc stomach.”

Aymir gave a slow, wicked grin. “That’s a mission worth taking.”
