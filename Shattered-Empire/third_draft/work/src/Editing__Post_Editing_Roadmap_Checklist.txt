Post-Editing Roadmap Checklist (Jan – June)
Use this checklist once your editing phase ends (Dec 31). It guides you through the next 6 months toward beta reads, polish, and publishing prep.
Stage 1: Cooling & Fresh Read (Jan 1 – Jan 15)
☐ Put the manuscript away for 2 weeks. No rereading, no editing.
☐ Mid-Jan: Read the book straight through as a reader (not an editor).
☐ Make quick margin notes on pacing, emotional flow, and anything that feels ‘off.’
Stage 2: Beta Readers & Feedback (Jan 16 – Feb 28)
☐ Select 2–4 trusted beta readers (ideally fantasy fans).
☐ Create a 1-page feedback form (3–5 key questions: where did you skim? best moment? least favorite character?).
☐ Send manuscript + feedback form with a clear deadline (4–6 weeks).
☐ Collect and review feedback objectively—look for patterns, not single opinions.
Stage 3: Final Proof & Master Draft (Mar 1 – Mar 31)
☐ Incorporate beta reader feedback.
☐ Run a full proofread: grammar, typos, formatting.
☐ Double-check names, lore, geography, and travel times for consistency.
☐ Merge into one final Master Document.
Stage 4: Publishing Path Decision (Apr 1 – Apr 30)
☐ Decide on Traditional Publishing vs Self-Publishing.
If Traditional:
   ☐ Draft query letter, synopsis, and polish first 3 chapters.
   ☐ Research and build list of 10–20 agents for fantasy submissions.
If Self-Publishing:
   ☐ Hire professional line editor/proofreader for polish.
   ☐ Commission fantasy cover art.
   ☐ Format manuscript for Kindle/ePub + Print (POD).
Stage 5: Marketing & Launch Prep (May 1 – May 31)
☐ Begin sharing teasers (maps, character voice snippets, Harmon’s journal entries).
☐ Build community (email list, Discord/FB group, ‘Wolfpack’ style fan base).
☐ Consider ARC (Advance Reader Copies) for early reviews.
☐ Draft back cover copy + finalize metadata (title, ISBN, series notes).
Stage 6: Transition to Book 2 (June 1 onward)
☐ Outline Book 2 while Book 1 is in query or launch phase.
☐ Write a synopsis for Book 2 to ensure seeds from Book 1 connect.
☐ Begin drafting early chapters to maintain momentum.
