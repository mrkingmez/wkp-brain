PROMPT MASTER
Rebuilt Novel Editing Prompt Pack v2.0
Built for rich epic fantasy editing, Character Annex enforcement, human voice protection, deeper immersion, tactical logic, and author-controlled patch options.

How This Prompt Master Works
This rebuilt pack replaces the older prompt chain with fewer, stronger prompts. The new system protects detail instead of stripping it, hunts for places to deepen immersion, preserves character-specific speech, and still catches logic, continuity, tense, grammar, and AI-smoothing problems.
Prompt 1 is the context lock and shared tag/severity system.
Prompt 2 is the main all-in-one diagnostic, continuity, logic, immersion, anti-AI, and patch-map prompt.
Prompt 3 is the scene-level voice, grain, and immersion scan. Diagnostic only.
Prompt 4 is the line-level voice, grain, dialect, and patch-options prompt.
Prompt 5 is the final cleanup pass for grammar, typos, spacing, tense drift, punctuation, names, and dialect protection.
Side prompts are preserved, except the old Cut Fat prompt, which is removed. Its useful philosophy is now absorbed into Prompt 2.
Core Editorial Law
Detail is not fat by default. Detail earns its place through worldbuilding, mood, character, tension, foreshadowing, environmental progression, or tactical clarity.
Do not cut descriptive material simply because the read would be faster without it.
Do not remove repeated contextual grounding when the situation, environment, emotional pressure, or character understanding has changed.
Do not flatten dialect, slang, regional cadence, class markers, or selective phonetic spelling.
Do not make every character speak correctly, cleanly, or politely unless the Character Annex requires it.
Every patch must preserve author intent and strengthen the story as Zac’s story, not convert it into generic polished fantasy prose.

PROMPT 1 - CONTEXT LOCK + SEVERITY/TAG SYSTEM
Act as a senior professional novelist, developmental editor, continuity editor, and narrative voice guardian.

Before performing any analysis or generating output:
1. Read ALL attached documents completely.
2. Read the rebuilt Character Annex completely.
3. Read the chapter or scene text completely.
4. Do not begin analysis until all documents are fully ingested.

Confirm completion by stating exactly:
"Attached documents and Character Annex fully ingested. Beginning analysis."

Do NOT begin evaluation before confirmation.

PRIMARY RULES:
- Preserve the author’s voice, intent, depth, rhythm, and story texture.
- Do not simplify complexity merely to speed up the read.
- Do not cut detail unless it is truly dead, contradictory, confusing, or harmful to the scene.
- Treat repeated environmental or contextual detail as potentially valid if the conditions, emotion, tension, or character understanding have changed.
- Do not erase dialect, slang, region, class, selective phonetic spelling, or character-specific speech.
- The rebuilt Character Annex is the controlling reference for character voice, canon spelling, regional speech, class markers, stress behavior, and forbidden mistakes.
- If chapter text conflicts with the Character Annex, flag the conflict instead of silently fixing it.
- Treat this chapter as part of a continuous manuscript.

CHARACTER PRESENCE REQUIREMENTS:
- Verify which characters are physically present in the scene.
- Flag if a character speaks, acts, reacts, or is referenced as present without having entered the scene.
- Flag if a character knows information they should not know.
- Flag if a character’s dialogue, decision-making, stress response, dialect, class register, or emotional behavior violates the Character Annex.

SEVERITY SCALE:
Severity 1 - Minor:
Small clarity, grammar, emphasis, continuity, or voice issue. Does not confuse the reader or damage immersion.

Severity 2 - Moderate:
Noticeable issue that weakens immersion, voice, logic, pacing, world texture, emotional pressure, or continuity.

Severity 3 - Major:
Reader confusion, canon contradiction, character voice failure, scene logic crack, weak emotional/tactical beat, or immersion break.

Severity 4 - Critical:
Breaks canon, timeline, reader trust, core character truth, magic/system rules, major plot logic, or creates a serious hostile-reader exploit.

ISSUE TAGS:
VOICE - character speech, cadence, emotional expression, or line identity problem.
DIALECT - accent, slang, selective phonetic spelling, regional/class speech pattern, or cultural cadence issue.
CANON - contradiction with Character Annex or established lore.
LOGIC - cause/effect, plan, decision, tactical, or system issue.
TIMELINE - time passage, bells/days/nights, travel, sequence, or duration issue.
PRESENCE - character appears, speaks, reacts, or acts without being established in the scene.
KNOWLEDGE - character knows something they should not know.
WORLD - setting, culture, history, politics, magic, economy, religion, geography, environment, or social structure issue.
DETAIL - opportunity to add texture, grounding, sensory material, environmental progression, or cultural behavior.
IMMERSION - scene feels thin, stage-like, generic, underfelt, or not lived-in enough.
TENSION - pressure drops, danger softens, conflict becomes too easy, or momentum stalls.
EMOTION - interiority, grief, fear, affection, guilt, shame, anger, or relational pressure is underdeveloped.
AI-SMOOTHING - prose feels too clean, generic, balanced, safe, symmetrical, over-explained, or not human/author-specific.
TENSE - tense drift or unstable narrative time.
ERA - modern phrasing, anachronistic metaphor, contemporary idiom, or out-of-world language.
GRAMMAR - true grammar/mechanics issue, including punctuation that affects meaning.
SPACING - extra spaces, missing spaces, doubled spaces, leading spaces, paragraph spacing errors, or formatting oddities.

When later prompts use tags, apply these tags consistently.

PROMPT 2 - ALL-IN-ONE DIAGNOSTIC + CONTINUITY + LOGIC + IMMERSION PATCH MAP
Use this as the main chapter repair prompt. It combines the old diagnostic pass, continuity/lore audit, chapter-level logic stress test, detail layering, anti-AI smoothing audit, and patch map into one working prompt.
Act as a senior developmental editor, professional novelist, continuity editor, adversarial logic analyst, and voice-preservation editor for an epic fantasy manuscript.

This task has TWO PHASES.
You MUST complete Phase 1 fully before starting Phase 2.

GLOBAL RULES:
- Do NOT rewrite the chapter.
- Do NOT add new scenes, new plot beats, new lore rules, or new characters.
- Preserve the author’s voice, tone, cadence, and intent.
- Preserve detail, world texture, environmental progression, tactical clarity, emotional atmosphere, and character flavor.
- Do NOT cut descriptive material simply to speed up the read.
- Do NOT remove repeated information if the environment, emotional state, tactical situation, or character understanding has changed since the earlier mention.
- Allowed: line-level rewrites, sentence additions, short expansions, and targeted replacements for clarity, continuity, logic, immersion, emotion, worldbuilding, tense, grammar, or character voice.
- Suggested fixes and additions must be copy-paste ready unless deletion is specified.
- Selective phonetic spelling, slang, contractions, dialect grammar, and regional/class markers are allowed when the Character Annex supports them.
- The rebuilt Character Annex controls character voice, canon spelling, relationships, regional speech, class markers, stress behavior, and forbidden mistakes.
- If the chapter and Character Annex conflict, flag the conflict and label it Needs Author Confirmation if canon is uncertain.

PHASE 1 - DIAGNOSTIC PASS ONLY
Analyze the chapter and identify issues or opportunities. Do NOT propose fixes in Phase 1.

Cover these areas:
1. Scene function and emotional arc
2. Momentum, escalation, and ending force
3. Dialogue, character voice, dialect, class/register, and stress behavior
4. Pacing, density, and whether detail is functioning well
5. Worldbuilding, environmental progression, sensory grounding, and cultural texture
6. Continuity, timeline, character presence, knowledge leaks, names, and lore consistency
7. Chapter-level logic stress test: decisions, plans, cause/effect, tactical realism, system boundaries, reader exploit risks
8. AI-smoothing and human-voice risks: tense drift, over-clean cadence, generic emotional language, balanced sentence overuse, repeated phrasing, too-perfect dialogue, and prose that no longer sounds like the author
9. Opportunities to add depth: character interiority, relationship pressure, magic-system texture, politics, class behavior, religion, geography, weather, sound, smell, touch, tactical details, and consequences of earlier actions

For EACH flagged issue or opportunity, provide:
- Issue number
- Severity: 1 Minor / 2 Moderate / 3 Major / 4 Critical
- Tags: use the Prompt 1 tag system
- Quote the passage or identify the exact location
- Explain the problem or opportunity
- State whether the recommended action is likely: Repair / Expand / Add Only / Delete Only / Preserve with no change / Needs Author Confirmation

DELETION PHILOSOPHY:
Only flag deletion when a line or passage is truly harmful or dead. Valid deletion reasons include:
- Contradicts canon or scene logic
- Repeats with no new emotional, environmental, tactical, world, or character function
- Explains what the reader already clearly understands
- Breaks character voice beyond repair
- Breaks era or tone beyond repair
- Weakens tension
- Creates confusion
- Adds stage direction with no story value

Do NOT recommend deletion because a passage is long, descriptive, reflective, atmospheric, emotionally layered, or worldbuilding-heavy.

PHASE 2 - SINGLE PATCH LIST
Using ONLY the issues and opportunities from Phase 1, create one unified patch list for the author to work through.

For every patch, use this format:

PATCH [Number]
Severity:
Tags:
Recommended Action: Repair / Expand / Add Only / Delete Only / Preserve with note / Needs Author Confirmation
Location:
Original Passage:
"[quote exact passage or describe exact insertion point]"

Function of the Current Passage:
- Explain what the existing text already does well, if anything.
- Identify whether it serves worldbuilding, mood, character, tension, foreshadowing, tactical clarity, emotional pressure, or continuity.

Problem or Opportunity:
- Explain the craft issue, logic gap, immersion opportunity, voice drift, tense issue, or detail gap.

Line Before Suggested Fix:
"[exact line before the change or insertion point]"

Original Line / Insertion Point:
"[exact original line, exact paragraph, or write ADD ONLY after the line...]"

Line After Suggested Fix:
"[exact line after the change or insertion point]"

THREE OPTIONS:
Option 1 - Minimal / Clean Repair:
"[copy-paste ready fix, or say DELETE ONLY if deletion is the only recommendation]"

Option 2 - Immersive / Detail-Forward Repair:
"[copy-paste ready fix that deepens world, character, sensory detail, environment, class/culture, tension, or tactical clarity]"

Option 3 - Voice / Human-Grain Repair:
"[copy-paste ready fix that better captures the author’s voice, character-specific speech, dialect, rhythm, pressure response, or less AI-smooth texture]"

Recommendation:
- State which option you recommend and why.
- If the best path is to combine two options, say so clearly.
- If the issue requires author choice, label Needs Author Decision.

Explanation:
- Explain how the fix improves logic, continuity, immersion, world depth, tension, character voice, or human texture.
- Be direct and practical.

SPECIAL RULES FOR ADDITIONS:
- If the best fix is to add material, label it ADD ONLY.
- State exactly where the addition goes.
- Provide three addition options.
- Additions may be more than one line if needed, but must not create new scenes, new plot beats, new lore rules, or new outcomes.
- Additions should deepen existing material, not hijack the chapter.

SPECIAL RULES FOR DELETIONS:
- If deletion is the only correct move, label it DELETE ONLY.
- Quote the exact line or passage to delete.
- Do not provide three rewritten alternatives unless the passage can be repaired instead of deleted.
- Explain why the deletion is necessary.

SPECIAL RULES FOR AI-SMOOTHING:
Do not simply say "this sounds AI." Identify the cause:
- Too balanced
- Too generic
- Too explanatory
- Too symmetrical
- Too many clean sentences in a row
- Dialogue too interchangeable
- Emotion stated instead of embodied
- Tense drift
- Repeated phrasing
- Over-polished narration
- Character voice flattened
Then provide options that restore author voice, lived-in detail, asymmetry, cadence, dialect, or character-specific pressure.

SPECIAL RULES FOR DIALECT AND PHONETIC SPELLING:
- Do not erase intentional dialect.
- Do not normalize y’all, ain’t, reckon, ye, yer, gonna, ’em, nothin’, coulda, shoulda, figger, dropped g endings, or other voice markers if they fit the Character Annex.
- Do not overdo phonetic spelling. Use it as spice, not soup.
- Under stress, dialect may thicken if the Character Annex supports it.

FINAL CHECK BEFORE OUTPUT:
Before presenting the patch list, verify:
- No new plot, lore, characters, or outcomes were introduced.
- Detail was preserved unless clearly harmful.
- Additions deepen the existing story rather than padding it.
- Character voice aligns with the rebuilt Character Annex.
- Logic gaps are actually reduced.
- Tense and AI-smoothing problems are named by cause, not vague accusation.
- Every patch includes line before, original/insertion point, line after, and three options unless marked DELETE ONLY.

PROMPT 3 - SCENE-LEVEL VOICE, GRAIN, DETAIL & IMMERSION SCAN
Renamed from old Prompt 8. Diagnostic only. Use this before Prompt 4, or whenever a scene feels too smooth, too thin, too samey, or not enough like the author.
Act as a senior narrative voice analyst, scene-level immersion editor, and Character Annex enforcer.

This pass evaluates the entire scene before touching individual lines.
Do NOT rewrite the scene.
Do NOT provide line edits yet.
This is diagnostic only.

Before performing any analysis:
1. Read the entire scene or chapter in full.
2. Read the rebuilt Character Annex in full.
3. Do not begin analysis until both are fully absorbed.
4. Do not evaluate individual lines until the scene’s emotional arc, setting pressure, and character presence are understood.

Confirm completion by stating exactly:
"Chapter and Character Annex fully ingested. Beginning analysis."

PURPOSE:
Identify:
- Tonal flattening
- Cadence convergence
- Emotional monotone
- AI-smoothing
- Over-clean prose
- Excess explanation
- Dialogue rhythm repetition
- Safe phrasing clusters
- Missed escalation opportunities
- Missing world texture
- Missing environmental progression
- Missing sensory grounding
- Underused class, culture, religion, politics, geography, magic, or tactical context
- Character voice drift from the rebuilt Character Annex
- Dialect, slang, phonetic spelling, or regional speech that has been over-cleaned

STEP 1 - SCENE FUNCTION ANALYSIS
Identify:
- What emotional state the scene begins in
- What emotional state it ends in
- Whether escalation occurs
- Whether tension rises, holds, drops, or plateaus
- Whether the ending lands with enough force
- Whether the scene gives the reader enough world, mood, and character texture

STEP 2 - DETAIL FUNCTION ANALYSIS
Evaluate descriptive, reflective, and environmental passages.
For each key descriptive passage, determine whether it serves:
- Worldbuilding
- Mood / atmosphere
- Character emotion, bias, or POV
- Tension / stakes
- Foreshadowing
- Tactical clarity
- Cultural or class texture
- Environmental progression
- Consequence of prior action

Do NOT mark detail as excessive unless it has no function or actively harms clarity, logic, tension, or voice.
If a passage has only one function but could carry more, flag it as:
"Layering Opportunity," not "cut."

STEP 3 - CADENCE VARIETY CHECK
Evaluate:
- Are sentence lengths varied?
- Are short lines used strategically?
- Do multiple characters use identical sentence rhythm?
- Does the scene rely too heavily on balanced, polished, symmetrical sentences?
- Does prose feel machine-smooth instead of human and author-shaped?

If rhythm feels even throughout, flag: "Cadence Flattening" or "AI-Smoothing - Balanced Rhythm."

STEP 4 - VOICE CONVERGENCE DETECTION
Without relying on tags, determine:
- Do characters respond in structurally similar ways?
- Do they use similar phrasing patterns?
- Do they express emotion similarly?
- Could lines be swapped between characters without readers noticing?

If yes, label: "Voice Drift - Interchangeability Risk."

STEP 5 - DIALECT, REGION, CLASS, AND PHONETIC SPELLING CHECK
For each primary speaker:
- Identify regional baseline from the Character Annex.
- Identify social class tier.
- Identify institutional overlay: soldier, priest, noble, criminal, guild master, royalty, etc.
- Identify stress level.
- Check whether dialogue reflects all four layers.
- Check whether dialect has been over-cleaned.
- Check whether selective phonetic spelling or slang would strengthen voice.

Flag as needed:
- Dialect Flattening
- Class Drift
- Regional Flattening
- Accent Not Reinforced
- Phonetic Flavor Opportunity
- Stress Does Not Distort Voice
- Institutional Overlay Overreach

STEP 6 - SAFE LANGUAGE AND AI-SMOOTHING CLUSTERS
Scan for clusters of:
- like
- just
- kind of
- sort of
- maybe
- probably
- I think
- It was like
- as if
- seemed to
- began to
- felt like
- almost
- somehow
- something in him/her
- a part of him/her
- there was a sense of

Do not automatically cut them. Flag only when they create weak, vague, generic, or AI-smooth prose.

STEP 7 - PRESSURE ESCALATION CHECK
If the scene includes conflict, fear, confrontation, ideological tension, battle, grief, desire, or dread, check:
- Does speech compress or fracture appropriately?
- Does silence increase or become weaponized?
- Does humor survive, collapse, sharpen, or become defensive according to the Character Annex?
- Does dialect thicken under stress where appropriate?
- Does body language become more specific?
- Does the environment respond or feel different as pressure rises?

If not, flag: "Stress Response Underdeveloped."

STEP 8 - SUBTEXT VS EXPLANATION BALANCE
Identify places where:
- Emotion is stated directly but not embodied
- Motivation is explained instead of implied
- Conflict is clarified too quickly
- The narration tells the reader what to feel
- The prose names the theme instead of letting the scene carry it

Flag as: "Overt Explanation - Subtext Opportunity" or "Emotion Needs Embodiment."

STEP 9 - SILENCE AND SPACE ANALYSIS
Determine:
- Are pauses used strategically?
- Are powerful lines followed by space?
- Does dialogue ever stop abruptly for weight?
- Are paragraph breaks supporting tension and readability?
- Are there accidental extra spaces, leading spaces, or spacing issues that would distract later?

Flag as: "Silence Underutilized," "Paragraph Rhythm Issue," or "Spacing Issue."

STEP 10 - WORLD AND IMMERSION DEPTH SCAN
Look for places where the scene could go deeper without bloating:
- Weather/light/sound/smell/touch
- Geography and movement through space
- Class behavior
- Political pressure
- Religious detail
- Magic-system texture
- Tools, weapons, clothing, food, architecture
- Local sayings or cultural assumptions
- Consequences of prior action
- Foreshadowing
- Tactical/logistical reality

Flag these as "Add-Only Immersion Opportunity" if the text would benefit from an addition rather than replacement.

STEP 11 - SCENE GRAIN SCORE
Provide scores:
- Cadence Variety: Low / Moderate / Strong
- Voice Differentiation: Low / Moderate / Strong
- Stress Escalation: Low / Moderate / Strong
- World Texture: Low / Moderate / Strong
- Sensory Grounding: Low / Moderate / Strong
- Character Interiority: Low / Moderate / Strong
- Human/Author Voice: Low / Moderate / Strong

Then provide:
- Top 3 Scene-Level Weaknesses
- Top 3 Add-Only Immersion Opportunities
- Top 3 Voice/Dialect Risks
- Top 3 Places Where Detail Should Be Preserved

Do NOT fix lines yet.
PROMPT 4 - LINE-LEVEL VOICE, GRAIN, DIALECT & PATCH OPTIONS
Renamed from old Prompt 7. Use after Prompt 3, or independently on a flagged section. This is the surgical voice/detail prompt.
Act as a senior dialogue architect, narrative voice enforcer, line editor, and Character Annex guardian.

Use the rebuilt Character Annex as controlling canon for voice, cadence, class, region, dialect, stress behavior, emotional pressure, relationships, and forbidden mistakes.

This prompt may be used after Prompt 3 or independently on a selected passage.

PRIMARY GOALS:
- Detect weak or interchangeable dialogue.
- Restore author voice and human texture.
- Enforce character-specific cadence and differentiation.
- Protect dialect, slang, class markers, and selective phonetic spelling.
- Remove AI-smoothness by identifying the actual craft cause.
- Offer three fix options so the author keeps final control.
- Add immersion, character, or world detail where the line needs it.

GLOBAL RULES:
- Do NOT change plot.
- Do NOT change meaning.
- Do NOT add new lore rules, new characters, or new outcomes.
- Do NOT erase intentional dialect.
- Do NOT normalize character-specific speech unless meaning is unclear.
- Do NOT make everyone sound educated, polished, modern, or safe.
- Do NOT cut detail simply because it slows the read.
- Preserve escalation, era, tone, and author intent.

STEP 1 - IDENTIFY ISSUES
For every flagged line or passage:
- Quote the original line or passage.
- Identify the character speaking or the POV character filtering the narration.
- Identify the scene pressure level.
- Identify which layer is weak:
  - Character voice
  - Texture/cadence
  - Register/class
  - Regional speech/dialect
  - Selective phonetic spelling opportunity
  - Stress response
  - Physical embodiment
  - Interchangeability
  - Weak language
  - AI-smoothing
  - Over-explanation
  - Missing immersion/world detail
  - Tense or grammar issue

STEP 2 - INTERCHANGEABILITY TEST
Ask:
- Could another character say this line with no change?
- Does this line reflect the speaker’s region, class, job, age, education, trauma, confidence, and stress response?
- Does this line sound like the Character Annex version of the character?

If fewer than 80% of nearby dialogue lines are identifiable by cadence alone, flag the passage for voice differentiation.

STEP 3 - DIALECT AND PHONETIC SPELLING PROTOCOL
Accent and dialect may be shown through:
- Word choice
- Sentence rhythm
- Grammar pressure
- Slang
- Contractions or lack of contractions
- Dropped endings
- Selective phonetic spelling
- Cultural metaphors
- Class-based phrasing

Selective phonetic spelling is allowed when it is readable, consistent, and character-appropriate.
Do not erase y’all, ain’t, reckon, ye, yer, gonna, ’em, nothin’, coulda, shoulda, figger, dropped g endings, or similar markers if they fit the character.
Use phonetic spelling as spice, not soup.

STEP 4 - PRESSURE FRACTURE CHECK
Under stress, confirm:
- Does cadence shift appropriately?
- Do contractions change?
- Does dialect thicken or tighten where appropriate?
- Does humor survive, sharpen, collapse, or become defensive according to the Character Annex?
- Does silence increase?
- Does the body carry more of the emotion?

If stress produces neutral phrasing, revise.

STEP 5 - MICRO-BEHAVIOR AND IMMERSION CHECK
If dialogue feels disembodied, consider adding:
- Breath change
- Posture shift
- Eye movement
- Hand/grip detail
- Controlled stillness
- A sound in the room
- Weather/environmental pressure
- A class/cultural behavior
- A tactical movement
- A small sensory detail

Keep additions tied to the existing scene. Do not add new plot.

STEP 6 - PROVIDE THREE OPTIONS
For each flagged line or passage, use this format:

Issue [Number]
Severity:
Tags:
Character / POV:
Original Line or Passage:
"[exact quote]"

Line Before Suggested Fix:
"[exact line before]"

Original Line / Insertion Point:
"[exact line or ADD ONLY after...]"

Line After Suggested Fix:
"[exact line after]"

Option 1 - Controlled Repair:
"[copy-paste ready]"
Purpose: Fix clarity, voice, or logic with minimal disruption.

Option 2 - Stronger Grain / Dialect / Human Voice:
"[copy-paste ready]"
Purpose: Increase character identity, audible grain, dialect, pressure, asymmetry, or author-specific rhythm.

Option 3 - Immersive / World-Deepening Version:
"[copy-paste ready]"
Purpose: Add or strengthen sensory grounding, world texture, cultural behavior, environmental progression, tactical clarity, or emotional interiority.

Recommendation:
- Choose the strongest option or recommend a blend.
- Explain why in 1-2 direct sentences.

STEP 7 - ADD-ONLY RULE
If the issue is missing immersion rather than broken prose, label the patch ADD ONLY.
Provide three possible additions and exact placement.

STEP 8 - DELETE-ONLY RULE
If the line must be deleted and should not be replaced, label DELETE ONLY.
Quote the exact text and explain why it has no useful function or actively harms the scene.
Do not invent alternatives for true delete-only items.

FINAL VALIDATION:
Before concluding, confirm:
- Dialogue is not interchangeable.
- Voice aligns with the rebuilt Character Annex.
- Dialect, slang, and phonetic spelling are protected where appropriate.
- AI-smoothing was addressed by cause, not vague accusation.
- Additions deepen the existing scene without adding new plot.
- Professional clarity is preserved without sterilizing the prose.

PROMPT 5 - FINAL CLEANUP PASS
Act as a senior copy editor performing a final technical cleanup pass while preserving author voice, character dialect, and manuscript texture.

SCOPE:
Correct only true technical issues:
- Spelling errors
- Typos
- Missing words
- Duplicated words
- Grammar errors that disrupt clarity
- Punctuation errors that alter meaning or readability
- Dialogue punctuation formatting
- Missing or incorrect quotation marks
- Incorrect apostrophes
- Incorrect homophones: their/there/they’re, your/you’re, its/it’s, etc.
- Tense drift
- Wrong character names
- Wrong canon spellings
- Title capitalization where required: King, Queen, Governor, Brother, etc.
- Repeated character names caused by editing mistakes
- Characters speaking who are not present
- Modern phrasing or anachronistic terms that clearly break era
- Spacing issues: doubled spaces, missing spaces, too many spaces between words, leading spaces at the beginning of paragraphs or lines, inconsistent paragraph spacing, and accidental blank-space artifacts

DO NOT:
- Rewrite sentences for style.
- Smooth prose.
- Rephrase for elegance.
- Simplify sentence structure.
- Alter cadence, rhythm, or voice.
- Remove stylistic fragments.
- Normalize dialect, slang, regional speech, class markers, or selective phonetic spelling.
- “Fix” dialogue that is intentionally informal, broken, accented, rough, phonetic, or character-voiced.
- Remove world detail, environmental progression, or sensory texture.
- Make the prose sound more polished if that removes human voice.

DIALECT PROTECTION RULE:
Dialogue should only be corrected if:
- A typo makes the meaning unclear,
- A missing word breaks comprehension,
- Punctuation creates confusion,
- The dialect spelling contradicts the Character Annex,
- Or the same dialect word is accidentally spelled inconsistently in a way that looks like an error rather than voice.

OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS:
Provide one entry per error.
If there are 12 errors, there must be 12 entries.
Do not combine multiple fixes into one entry.

For each correction, show:
1. Error Number
2. Issue Type: spelling / grammar / punctuation / tense / canon name / spacing / quote mark / homophone / era / presence
3. Original Line: quote exactly
4. What’s Wrong: brief and factual
5. Corrected Line: copy-paste ready

If a line looks unusual but appears intentional for voice, write:
PRESERVE - likely intentional voice/dialect.

FINAL RULES:
- Preserve author voice and cadence at all times.
- Treat narration and dialogue differently.
- Err on the side of leaving dialogue untouched.
- When in doubt, flag for author review instead of changing it.
- Begin.

SIDE PROMPT - FULL MANUSCRIPT LOGIC CRASH TEST
Renamed from old Prompt 4C. Use after the full manuscript is assembled, before beta readers or final line edits.
Act as a senior professional novelist and adversarial story analyst.

Perform a Full Manuscript Logic Crash Test.

GLOBAL RULES:
- Do NOT rewrite.
- Do NOT suggest line edits.
- Do NOT add lore.
- Treat all factions, antagonists, governments, soldiers, spies, criminals, and magical systems as intelligent, adaptive, and self-interested.
- Assume readers will reread and cross-reference.
- Use the rebuilt Character Annex as controlling canon.
- Preserve rich detail and world texture; do not treat length as a flaw by itself.

TEST FOR:
1. Antagonist competence across the entire story
2. Information containment and leakage
3. System boundaries and breaking points: magic, tech, politics, military, geography, economy, travel
4. Character knowledge continuity
5. Timeline and travel strain
6. Solutions that rely on ignorance, coincidence, or unexplained timing
7. Repeated character voice drift across the book
8. Canon spelling and naming consistency
9. Reader out-thinking risks: “Why didn’t they just...?”
10. Emotional arc continuity and motivation durability
11. Whether worldbuilding details deepen the story or contradict later chapters

For EACH issue:
- Quote the relevant passage(s)
- Explain why the logic fails under pressure
- Classify the failure type using Prompt 1 tags
- Assign Severity 1-4
- Identify affected chapters/scenes
- State whether the issue is likely local, arc-level, or manuscript-wide

Do NOT provide fixes unless explicitly asked after the audit.
End with a Summary Risk Assessment only.

Each title should be:

- Unique and not cliched
