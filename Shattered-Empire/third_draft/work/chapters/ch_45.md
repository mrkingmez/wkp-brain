#TITLE Chapter 45
#FIRST IMPRESSION
This is the chapter where the tower talks. The fight with the Adept and his six Red Cloaks is the book's best set piece for pure teamwork. Ash holds the fire shield to the point of breaking, Aldrek's white wall backs him, Dain and Nyssa wait for the drop, and the moment the shield shatters they go in together. Then the book slows down on purpose. The looting (ball bearings, firestones, a water flask passed round) is small and human, and Ash taking the Adept's shard from a body he does not yet know is Talron's brother is a quiet piece of staging. The interrogation is the real prize. Talron's "My name's Talron. It always has been" and Nyssa's "Why the second part?" turn a captured soldier into a person in four lines. His account of the Garrison commander opening the gates, the men in strange armor, the black box for the tower's crown, the gate below Unity Square and the voice behind the thoughts hands the reader the biggest block of plot since the Pass, and it is mostly well paced. Aldrek's attempt to loosen the hold on Talron's mind ("There are cracks") is a lovely, unfussy beat. The weak points are small. The narration names Talron before he names himself, Dain loses his voice and then speaks, some of Talron's directions contradict each other (the stair "at the lowest level" turns up in the Grand Floor kitchen, bindings that never come off, a threat he never made), and a handful of stock lines sit on strong beats. The ending, where Talron walks off into the dark with a smile and a reminder, is a good unsettling cliffhanger. I name no one as Korr and say nothing about what he is.
