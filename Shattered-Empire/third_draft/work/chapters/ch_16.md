#TITLE Chapter 16
#FIRST IMPRESSION
This is the book's first full set piece for the Pass team, and most of it is excellent. The setup is clean (a shot count, a tree, a chant), the strike has a real shape (Luca goes in, the rifles answer, the worgs break, the pens go quiet), and the best beats are the quiet ones. Luca's gift is described as a bend, not an invisibility, which is exactly right. The face laid over the woman's face is the best moment in the chapter. "Or someone never left" turns a won fight into a second one. Lysana and Luca checking the silence together is a small, sharp piece of partnership, and "Present" is the right word for the prisoners. The burlap sack over the staff is a good, plain answer, and the cost of it (the prisoners dying as the spell peels) is earned.

Three things hold it back. First, the chapter has a nervous habit of defining things by what they are not ("It wasn't prayer. It was binding." "This wasn't devotion. This was possession." "The spell wasn't breaking. It was peeling.") and it reads as a tic by the third use. Second, there is a stale beat at the end of the duel (paragraph 163) that sends Aymir and Taerel charging downslope after they have already been at the tree for fifty paragraphs. Third, the counts and the positions slip in several places (nine prisoners against six, a runner that is never a creature, a worg that runs downslope toward a ridge, a locked spelling, Fort Dran). The prisoners' deaths are written as a run of "moments later." The chapter's close is strong, but the weather has been announced five times by then. The Zac questions (the avoided-thing exchange and who says "That is the anchor") are logged as questions, not decided.

@GRAIN Cadence Variety Strong (a still setup, a fast fight, a long search, a duel, a slow aftermath). Voice Differentiation Moderate to Strong (Taerel dry and formal, Aymir structured, Lysana plain, Luca compact), though Taerel's “Your funeral” leans casual. Stress Escalation Strong (the chant, the face, the second wave, the war chief, the dying prisoners). World Texture Strong (the tree, the ritual pattern, the staff, the testimony from Fort Dreknor). Sensory Grounding Strong, with weather repeated. Character Interiority Moderate to Strong for Luca (the face, the two quiet people), thin for the brothers. Human and Author Voice Strong in the fight and the aftermath, Moderate in the negation-heavy tree passages.
@COMPARE Against Chapters 13 to 15. The previous three were a campfire, a track and a debrief, and this is the payoff for all three. The fight is tighter than most of the book's action, because each kill has a different geometry and the rifles answer from the ridge on the beat. The aftermath is where the chapter earns its keep, since the win turns into a problem the team cannot cut through, and it lands on a plain, practical fix. The chapter ends on the weather, which is the right close, but it repeats a figure used four times earlier.
@POP Luca yes, strongly (the bend, the face, the staff). Lysana yes (the ribs, "Present," the shared quiet). Aymir yes in the fight and the "stiffened" beat. Taerel yes in the banter and the foot, less in the second half. All four have a distinct beat.
@AI Moderate. The tells are the repeated negation turn (five uses), stock openers and closers (whispered like a warning, sprang into action, chill crawling), aphorism closers (the rhythm of readiness, the blood has dried), and two repeated phrases (“Silence snapped tight again” and the snow-mute-swallow figure). The fight itself is clean.
