#TITLE Chapter 38
#FIRST IMPRESSION
This is the Drake chapter, one long ninth night in Opindale, and it is the book's best look at a good man being squeezed. The opening is strong. The feast "burns" below the window, the dagger sits in its violet box, and Cotter's calm answer to "Does it kill?" is "It does not kill, Governor. It replaces." That is a line a reader will carry into the next chapters. The balcony beat ("He stood there until he was empty and then until he was decided") and the stray line "A stage for dancers who didn't know the play was ending" show Zac's voice at its best. The weak spots are the count of days, which disagrees with itself three ways, and a lot of repeated mood. Drake feels like a ghost five times, a funeral twice, alone three times, and the threat is stated at P13, P74 and P97. The roof watcher is a good device, but one line of narration steps out of Drake's head to count his victims. Several details also collide with Chapter 36, including how many children Drake has and whether Cotter's name is spelled right. The chapter ends on a held breath, a tic the book has used too often.
