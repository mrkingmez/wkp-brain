#TITLE Chapter 44
#FIRST IMPRESSION
This is the descent chapter, and it is the creepiest one in the book. The team climbs through the tower's lower levels, the dwarven gauntlet keeps turning on them, and the mindless civilians are everywhere, each stuck in one small task. The butcher sharpening a cleaver "back and forth", the child mending a net with fingers that never slow, the blacksmith whose hammer "never strikes true" and the woman who folds the same cloths again and again do more than any monster. Ash's growing headache, the humming rods in the stone and Aldrek's line "The stone is speaking, but not to us" give the climb a clear spine. Aldrek's reading of the civilians as a sentence rather than obedience is the most interesting idea in the chapter. The empty prison with its one clean silver plate is a great image, the dockworker's scream is the right kind of shock, and the voice in the skull gives the fight an extra layer of dread. "That was new" in paragraph 97 is the best line in the book for what it says about this team. The chapter's problems are small and mostly about details. Ash's headache is described as unspoken after he has spoken of it twice, the gate to the prison is barred and then slammed shut, the cells are open and then not, and the tower's level count does not add up. The prose leans on a few repeated phrases ("not just cold", "sentence", "puppet") and a stock villain voice. The loudspeaker simile is a modern image in a world without them.
