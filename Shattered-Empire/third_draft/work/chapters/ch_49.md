#TITLE Chapter 49
#FIRST IMPRESSION
This is the chapter where the book changes rooms, and it earns the change. The seven-clank countdown (one memory, one clank, then the blade) is the cleanest piece of structure in the back half. It mirrors the bell sequence that closes Chapter 48, so the two chapters rhyme without saying so. Drake's failed attack is tight, and Edric's "Don't trust your closest friends" note is a very good hook. The Mirelle balcony scene is the strongest writing in the chapter. Her control over the assassin is real menace, and "Men are so easy to manipulate" followed by a kiss with her husband sets up a marriage that is both love and strategy. The feast vignettes (Sola Drey, Factor Jeld, Lord Colven) are lively and plant the north. The old man and the changeling closing the chapter are the right kind of creepy, and the countess's "the king rarely wanders far without his crown" plus Edric's "some eggs must break" memory is a very good turn on a character the reader likes. There are three real continuity problems. Chapter 48 ends with Harmon deciding "not tonight" on sending word, and here his message arrives within minutes and says the family is "with me now" when the family rides in Garr's cart. The bell is called "the first bell" in a chapter whose anchor is the seventh. Drake is sent to the room where the Steward is hiding. The Mirelle scene has a run of subtext-explaining lines that tell what the dialogue already shows, and the old man's thoughts repeat Chapter 48's habit of vague "last time" gestures. Zac's four stored simile cuts are applied as written. The ledger's open question on the seventh-bell anchor is answered, and the connecting line is on the page. I say nothing about who the old man or the changeling is.
