#TITLE Chapter 46
#FIRST IMPRESSION
This is Harmon's chapter, and it is the one the book has been building toward since the prison. A student he trained comes back with sabers and a reason, and the fight in the loft is the best one-on-one in the story. It is mean and uneven, it goes through the ring armor and the broken helm, and it works because both men are right and wrong at once. Torn's grief is real, Harmon's account of the fallback order is plausible, and neither version is allowed to win. Then Weslin's kill lands. The illusion, the dagger, the ringing behind his eyes, "I didn't know it would feel like that", and the squire's oath that comes out of him unplanned are the emotional peak of the book so far. Harmon's "The weight you're feeling right now is what separates you from what we just put in the ground" is the right line for the right moment. The second half is a handoff chapter with a sting. The cart ride, the name on the page, the missing children, Garr's retelling of Aurelia at the tower and Harmon's cane and his "let me speak to it first" are all good plants, and the violet bolt closes it on a hook. The weak points are mostly seams. The opening dagger beat is garbled, the narrator switches between "Torn" and "Teralon" mid-scene, Torn's confession names setbacks the reader never saw, and Garr's retelling of the tower voice does not match Chapter 43. Garr's place also finally settles, since he is outside here and the earlier chapters disagree. Four of Zac's simile cuts fall in this chapter and are applied as written. The same template ("the way a man...") shows up in other lines Zac did not flag, and I note them as options. Neavin's dialect in paragraph 166 is intentional and stays.
