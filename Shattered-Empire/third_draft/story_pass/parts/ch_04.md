### STORY NOTES
IMPRESSION
This is the emotional heart of the first act and it works. The turn is good, Mjakazi says no, Harmon reveals the quiet help, and the yes lands because it is earned. The weak points are repeats. Both men ask and answer the same two questions twice, and the opening description takes a full page to say the city is dressed for the pact. The chapter also hands Harmon a hunch ("A spark") without saying what he is testing for, so the reader sees a stake with no mechanism yet.
REVEALS in this chapter
- Harmon arranged Mjakazi's tavern, dockyard job and bakery. Planted lightly in Ch2 by "Not that baker's son?", payoff is right here.
- Mjakazi and Zeriah are linked by Nyahani, Zeriah's mother. New and warm, fair.
- Weslin has not been tested, and Mjakazi fears the result. New, and a fair setup for Ch6.
- Mjakazi had a violent past with a sword. Planted by the hands and the white eyes. Sentinel status itself stays off the page, which fits the rule.
MISSING
- The hidden sword. Mjakazi's sword is never mentioned as hidden or kept close (F-11), and the reader does not learn it exists after the line about his past. The line "wielded a sword that could carve through men like paper" is the only hook.
- Harmon never says why the baker's son in particular, beyond a spark. The reader does not know the rules of magic or tests yet, so the spark has no weight.
- Zeriah has no line of her own before the goodbye, so her grief is told rather than shown.
NOTHING CHANGES
- Scene 1 is a long walk and description with one new fact, the pact is once a decade.
- The second half of scene 4, after the reveal, is Mjakazi telling how he met Zeriah, which is warm and does not move the plot.
PITCH SEEDS

- Add one beat that shows Mjakazi's sword is somewhere close, as an object, not a speech. Anchor "wielded a sword that could carve through men like paper".
