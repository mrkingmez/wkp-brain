# Honest first impression

This is a big, warm, ensemble adventure with real heart. The Harmon and Weslin pair carries the book, the Prologue hooks hard, the veil scene in Ch 7 is a true set piece, the prison break (Ch 21 to 23) is the tightest sequence, Ch 40 is the one chapter that hits its peak and means it, and the Torn kill and the Nyssa pier choice (Ch 46 and Ch 52) are the right emotional bets. The cast is large and mostly distinct.

What drags is that the book retells itself. Information arrives in a briefing, then again in a relay, then again in a debrief. Whole events are told twice in different chapters, and several chapters repeat their own beats inside themselves. Act one takes eight chapters to leave Opindale. The Stormhaven middle spends about 48,000 words in puzzles, loft debriefs and corridors at a tension that mostly sits between 3 and 3.9 with repeated peak 5 scenes close together. Average chapter tension runs 2 to 3.5 across the book with one real spike, Ch 40 at 4.9, so the peaks that matter do not stand out.

The three biggest story problems

1. The book repeats itself. The mechanical scan found few pasted sentences (one in Ch 1, a few phrase echoes), so the repeats are retellings in new words. The 12 duplicates in section 4 and pitches 1, 3, 5, 7, 12, 13, 19 and 26 are the cure.
2. The two halves of the company barely touch and the Pass track is stranded. The Pass team is off the page for nine chapters after Lysana dies, the Pass aftermath comes after the Stormhaven finale, and Weslin never carries the poison plan to Harmon. Pitches 2, 15 and 16.
3. Several late payoffs are thin. Torn is planted in Ch 23 and goes silent until Ch 46, Talron is dropped, Luca leaving is never shown, Garr ignoring Luca messages has no consequence, and Rouric and the contract price are not set up. Pitches 4, 6, 15, 18 and 24.

# Length note and structural cuts

The manuscript is 235,177 words. For a debut epic fantasy that is roughly double the range usually quoted for first novels, which I recall as about 100,000 to 120,000 words, with exceptions at the top. I did not run a web search for this note, so treat the range as unverified. A long debut is not a rejection by itself but it is a hard sell and every repeat in this report is a cheaper way to lose length than cutting a character.

Biggest structural cuts, with estimated savings that are NOT counted and come from chapter word counts. Merge Ch 34 and Ch 35 (pitch 1), up to about 3,000 to 4,000 words. Cut one or two Bastion puzzles (pitch 3), about 2,500. Trim the Ch 2 to 8 repeats and the Ch 3 ledger (pitch 5), about 3,000 to 4,000. Trim Ch 52 and Ch 53 retellings (pitches 18 and 19), about 2,000 to 3,000. Ch 20 camp chores, Ch 41 and Ch 19 merges, about 3,000 together. That is about 14,000 to 18,000 words in total, which is real but does not change the book category. A larger cut would need a decision to remove a whole strand, and I am not pitching that.
