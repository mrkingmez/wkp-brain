## Description (by my estimate this is over 1,200 characters; trim once you confirm the real limit)

A podcast episode that only exists as audio is hard to search, hard to quote and hard to clip. I turn each episode into three things you can use the same day.

What you get:
- A transcript with a label on every speaker (Host, Guest, or the names you give me) and a time stamp on every turn.
- A YouTube chapter list in the exact format YouTube accepts: it starts at 00:00:00, runs in order, and names each chapter after the real subject being discussed, not "Segment 2". Chapters are built from the transcript, so each one matches what is actually said.
- Short show notes: a summary of the episode and the topics covered, ready to paste into your episode page. Your claims stay yours; I do not add facts that are not in the recording.

How I work: speech-recognition software, running on my own computer, makes the first draft with speaker separation. Then I proofread against the audio, fix names and terms, and check that each chapter matches the talk.

Good fit: interview shows, two-host conversation shows, review and commentary podcasts, roundtables of up to 4 voices.

Not a fit: heavy crosstalk, music-bed-loud audio, more than 4 speakers (ask first).

## FAQ

**What do the chapters look like?** Lines like `00:00:00 - Start`, then `00:07:42 - The trade deadline`. Minimum three chapters, each at least 10 seconds, ascending, which is what YouTube needs to show them.

**Are show notes written by software?** I write them from the transcript, and everything in them comes from what was said.

**Do you do video editing or audio cleanup?** No. Transcript, chapters and notes only.

**Do you use software?** Yes, a speech-recognition tool for the first draft, then I proofread against the audio.
