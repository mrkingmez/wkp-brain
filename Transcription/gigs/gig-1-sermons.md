## Description (by my estimate this is over 1,200 characters; trim once you confirm the real limit)

Your pastor preaches once. The sermon can be a transcript for the church website, an outline for small groups, and a clean list of every verse quoted.

What you get with each order:
- A verbatim transcript of the sermon. I keep the preacher's words as spoken. I do not tidy theology, reword a point, or fix a quote to what I think he meant.
- A timestamped outline (main points with the minute each one starts), so your media team can cut clips or write chapters.
- A scripture reference list: every passage the preacher cites or reads, with book, chapter and verse, checked against the text. Where the preacher's spoken wording differs from the passage, I flag it and leave his words alone.

How I work: speech-recognition software makes the first draft. Then I read the whole transcript against the audio and correct it, including names, places and Bible terms.

Good fit: Sunday sermons, midweek studies, funeral and wedding messages, guest speakers, one to two speakers (preacher plus a scripture reader or a Q and A).

Not a fit: music-only services, heavy crosstalk, recordings where the sound system buzzes over the voice. Send a sample and I will tell you before you pay.

## FAQ
**Do you change what the pastor said?** No. The transcript is verbatim. If something sounds like a slip, I leave it and mark it [unclear] or [sic]. His meaning is his.

**What does "checked against the text" mean?** For each passage cited, I look it up in the translation you name and confirm the reference exists and matches what was read. If he quotes a verse loosely, I note it beside the entry. I do not correct his sermon.

**Do you use software?** Yes, a speech-recognition tool makes the first draft, and then I proofread the full transcript against the audio.
