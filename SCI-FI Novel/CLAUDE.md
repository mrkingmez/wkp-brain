# Military Sci-Fi Novel (untitled)

## What This Is
Next book after Shattered Empire. Standalone military sci-fi, Top Gun-influenced. Zac is still torn between first-person and third-person POV; leaning toward first-person for a single-protagonist academy story, having previously worked in ensemble POV with Shattered Empire.

## Premise, Locked as of 7/24 Interview
A high school senior wants to fly. His only realistic path is a military flight academy — a special upper-tier post-school program he qualifies for on grades, but not what he actually wants (his father pushed him toward it; a separate high-achieving track). His older brother, after a "revenge tour," is military and tells him this academy path is what he should've pursued instead, even though the brother's own shipping business made real money for the family.

Protagonist enters the academy convinced he's a strong pilot. He is not as good as he thinks. Simulators don't match flying the real aircraft — his nondominant eye becomes a factor in tracking/targeting on his aircraft platform (reference case Zac cited: Nicholas Hayes / "Firebirds," an Apache pilot with the same real-world limitation). He fails to qualify for his preferred airframe (fighters) in the competitive airframe-assignment process.

Core emotional beat, stated directly by Zac: **this is a motivational story about failing at the thing you wanted and still finding a way to matter.** He doesn't win the competition. His perseverance and character are what get him recruited into special operations at the end of the book — not despite the failure, because of how he responded to it.

## Structure
- Full Book 1 = the academy arc (Top Gun-style: rivalry, airframe competition with real career stakes, personal stakes, growth).
- Ends with special ops recruitment — this is the Book 1 cliffhanger / Book 2 setup. The actual special ops mission Zac already has in his head is reserved for Book 2, not Book 1.
- Protagonist is not isolated from the wider war — his brother sends him letters with real information about what's happening outside the academy bubble, so escalating stakes are visible throughout even though the POV mostly stays inside the academy.

## Relationships
- A rival at the academy (competition/tension, likely mutual respect over time — not yet detailed).
- A close friend inside the academy who is a social outcast because of who his father is (a Lord) — this friendship is a way into the larger political/military world beyond the academy's walls.

## Target Audience
Same core readership Zac is chasing with Shattered Empire: Brandon Sanderson's core audience — readers who want strong worldbuilding and stakes, team/ensemble dynamics over lone-hero stories, and an appetite for military-flavored fiction that's under-served by current "military sci-fi" (most of which skews toward straightforward combat rather than character-driven team stories).

## Why Zac Stalled Before
Wrote 1-2 chapters previously and it felt "stale" — self-diagnosed as needing a stronger hook. The Top Gun framing (competition + personal stakes + failure-and-perseverance arc) is the fix identified in this interview session. Confirm this resolves the stale feeling once new chapters are drafted.

## Writing Process
Not a heavy outliner — same as Shattered Empire. Works from character-driven scenes within a loose "rail" toward the overarching theme, willing to change plans mid-write when a better story opportunity appears (see Shattered Empire's Ribbon/Nessa precedent in that book's CLAUDE.md).
