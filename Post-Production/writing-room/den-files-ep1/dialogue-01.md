## BLUF

The dialogue does not hold up yet for a read on camera. The single worst offender is verdict leakage: the script promises "Segments 1 through 6 are reporting only. Nobody grades anything" (P15) and then grades the movie in the very first host line of Segment 1 (P34), before the promise is even made on air (P37), and keeps editorializing through Segments 4 and 5. Close behind: the scripted read is roughly 20 minutes against about 50 stated minutes; Segment 2 and Segment 5 Piece 1 tell the same October 20th story twice almost word for word; and with the verdicts moved out, Winter Wolf and KingZ collapse into one narrator.

## MUST FIX

### 1. Verdict leakage ahead of Segment 7 (PROVEN, script's own rule P15 and P37)

The audience is told at P37 "We are keeping our opinions out of it until the end." These lines break that.

- P34, Winter Wolf: "For a movie this old it holds up. The first two thirds is a genuinely tense small town drama." This is a review grade, in Segment 1, before P37 sets the rule. Reaching for: Winter Wolf's verdict-first voice and a reason to care about the film. Shape of fix: the movie's standing can be stated as a reported fact (box office, reputation, what the Den remembers) rather than the host's grade, or the grade waits for Segment 7.
- P36, Winter Wolf: "It is what Paramount decided would sell." Assigns studio motive as the host's conclusion. It is sourced later (P72), so it lands here as opinion before the evidence. Shape: hold the motive until the source is on the table, or attribute it on the spot.
- P73, KingZ: "because the real one did not test scary enough." Goes past the quote it follows. P72 says Torme was told the account was "too fuzzy and too similar to other close encounters." "Did not test scary enough" implies test screenings and a scare metric nobody cited. Reaching for: the punch on the quote. Shape: the button should stay inside what the quote actually says.
- P76, KingZ: "if the countable facts get trimmed for convenience, that tells you what else was on the table." This is an argument, an inference from a small change to a larger charge. It is also Winter Wolf's job by the P10 spec, landing in KingZ's mouth.
- P78, KingZ: "the fake version of his own story." Loaded adjective, a judgment.
- P80, Winter Wolf: "They did not just dramatize it. They inverted it." Verdict line.
- P83, Winter Wolf: "It is the center that got replaced." Verdict line, and it closes the segment on a ruling.
- P87, Winter Wolf: "And the wildest part" is a reaction, which P15 says nobody does.
- P97, Winter Wolf: "The contract angle is the stronger of the two." The hosts weighing evidence is the Segment 7 job.
- P102, Winter Wolf: "Which is a fair objection." Then "the people with money riding on the story are the ones who kept it quiet." The first is a ruling, the second an insinuation.
- P110, Winter Wolf: "Supposed abduction. From the man who was in the truck. Who called the sheriff that night. Who had the contract on the line." Prosecutor cadence. The third beat ties the withdrawal to the motive from Piece 2, which is a conclusion.
- P111, KingZ: "On its face it looks like two men ... falling out, not a confession." Interpretation.
- P114, Winter Wolf: "That is the biggest thing anyone has said about this case in fifty years." A grade.
- Softer signals of weight, worth a look: P44 "Here is the part that matters" sits right before P47 "We are not going to tell you what it means." Saying a fact matters is telling the audience what it means. Same with P59 "That is the piece people forget" and P62 "here is what almost nobody gets right."

A second kind of leak, Segment 3 (PROPOSED, linked to P15): P51 to P57 narrate the sighting in the hosts' own voice as fact. P54 "Then the beam hits him." P57 "These men just watched their friend get hit by a beam of light." No attribution to the crew's account until P63 "He says." For a show that promises both sides, the hosts are on the record as asserting the beam. Reaching for: tension and a vivid retelling. Shape of fix: one clear frame early that this is the crew's account, or attribution woven through, so the vividness stays without the hosts vouching for it.

Balance note (PROPOSED): the leaks are not one sided. Segment 3 and P107 lean toward Walton, P76, P102 and P110 lean toward hoax. Each lean is still a lean.

### 4. One voice, two mouths (PROVEN, WRITING-ROOM "two characters who sound identical")

Name-cover test, run on Segments 2, 3 and 5: fails. Covered, I could not tell P53 (KingZ) from P54 (Winter Wolf), P89 (KingZ) from P90 (Winter Wolf), or P104 (KingZ) from P105 (Winter Wolf). Both deliver facts in the same clipped fragment style, both use the same "here is the part" opener (P44 and P62 Winter Wolf, P78 and P106 KingZ), and both drop buttons.

Why it happened (PROPOSED): P10 defines Winter Wolf as the verdict voice. v3 moved every verdict to Segment 7 (P15). With his job removed, Winter Wolf becomes a second narrator for six segments. Then the leaks in finding 1 are where his voice tries to come back.

Where they do separate: KingZ has the Den and community anchors (P35 "people in the Den tell me", P143), the "to be straight with you" warmth (P82), and the human math (P96). Winter Wolf has the short hard close (P38, P107, P114 asterisks). Seg 1 and the outro pass the test. Where roles cross the other way: KingZ makes Winter Wolf's sharp inference at P76 and the prosecutor's math at P96 to P97 is split oddly (KingZ sets it up, Winter Wolf rules on it).

Shape of fix (PROPOSED): give each host a non-verdict job in Segments 1 to 6 that only he does. For example, one consistently carries the documents and dates and the other carries the people and the town, or one always states the claim and the other always states the source. Then the voice split survives without opinions.

### 5. No contractions anywhere (PROPOSED, WRITING-ROOM "nobody talks like that")

The script never contracts. P39 "So let us go back", P56 "Of course they do not", P142 "We have got", P144 "I am Winter Wolf. He is KingZ." Read as written, two friends hanging out (P11) will sound like a deposition. If the hosts contract naturally on the day, the read and the page drift and timing changes. Shape of fix: decide whether contractions are allowed on the read, and mark it on the read-through copy so both hosts do the same thing.

### 6. Hard to say out loud (PROVEN, dialogue-doctor spec "the unsayable")

- P109, KingZ, the Rogers Facebook post read verbatim: "Michael H. Rogers, being of sound and rational mind, do hereby give notice that I am no longer to be considered a witness to Travis C. Walton's supposed abduction of November 5th, 1975." One 32 word legal sentence with two middle initials and a full date. Reaching for: the weight of his exact words. Shape: the operative phrase spoken, the full text on screen, or the quote cut to the clause that matters.
- P73, KingZ: "a movie stamped based on a true story." Needs quotation marks the voice cannot perform; read aloud it sounds like a typo.
- P94, KingZ: "A federal contract can be voided for an act of God without the contractor eating the penalties, and the argument is that a crew member being taken by a UFO would qualify." 33 words, two stacked clauses, no breath point.
- P105, Winter Wolf (32 words), P107 Winter Wolf (32 words, "someone last seen flat on his back on a logging road"), P64 Winter Wolf (30 words, comma chain), P115 KingZ (31 words, three nested items). Same problem: one breath nobody has.
- P119 and P120: eight and four item lists, with "October 20th, 1975", "November 5th", "November 12th" in one run. Number lists read flat and fast. Shape: split between hosts in smaller pieces, or let the dates live on screen.
- P121 and P141: "between November 5th and November 12th, 1975" and "November 5th, 1975" again. Full dates read aloud each time.
- P34, Winter Wolf: the cast and director roll call reads like an IMDb card in the verdict host's mouth.
- P104 and P113: "Researcher Charlie Wiser", "Researcher Ryan Gordon." Newspaper title construction; nobody says this to a friend on camera.
- P95: "proof of extraterrestrial visitation" and P115 "a verified formal confession": print vocabulary. Fine if quoting a document, but it is not marked as a quote.

### 7. Lines that exist only to hand the audience a fact, no anchor for the host saying it (PROVEN, WRITING-ROOM "a line that exists only to tell the audience a fact")

In a documentary most lines carry facts, so this is only the ones where nothing tells us why this host says it or why now.
- P34 Winter Wolf, credits list. Reaching for: establishing the film. No angle for the verdict host.
- P43 Winter Wolf, Walton's age and Rogers' acreage. "so, future family" is the one human touch and it works.
- P45 KingZ, "James Earl Jones." A cast name dropped as its own sentence with no reason given.

- P75 Winter Wolf, "The movie shrinks the crew from seven men to six." A bare fact with KingZ supplying the meaning (P76). The roles look swapped.
- P95 Winter Wolf, Enquirer offer amounts.
Shape of fix: each fact goes to the host whose angle it serves (finding 4), or picks up a hook to something the audience already cares about.

### 8. Slop tells and too clean rhythm (PROVEN, dialogue-doctor spec "slop tells", WRITING-ROOM "flag anything too clean")

- The "Not X. Y." construction: P51 "Not headlights, not a reflection. A glow.", P62 "Not a softer version. A different event entirely.", P80, P107 "It is not a finding.", P140 "Not Paramount's version. His." Once is a choice, five times is a tic.
- Trailer and aphorism lines: P24, P38 "Facts first. Verdict last.", P71 "Same event, opposite meaning.", P107 "Never searched is a gap in the search. It is not a finding.", P136 "That is the whole reason we are doing this show" (sums up the theme out loud).
- Rhythmic three-item lists: P23 "Real people. A real place. Something that actually happened.", P60 "Dehydrated, lighter, wrecked.", P66 "Fifty years, hundreds of interviews, multiple polygraphs", P110 the triple "From ... Who ... Who."
- Turn taking: every paragraph is a finished statement and the other host waits. Nobody cuts in, nobody trails off. For read copy that is partly the format, but the echo handoffs at P83, P101 and P113 are the old scripted banter wearing a disguise (v3 says banter is gone, P14).
