## MUST FIX

### M1. The scripted landing fights the verdict it follows. P206, P207, with P201 and MB2. PROVEN (script text), PROPOSED (fix shape)

- P206, Winter Wolf: "Either the biggest real encounter on record got turned into a monster movie with the facts scrubbed out, or seven men ran one of the cleanest hoaxes in UFO history for half a century and only the crew chief ever wobbled."
- P207, KingZ: "Both of those are a better story than the one Paramount sold you. That is the whole reason we are doing this show."
- Nobody would say that here. P10 makes Winter Wolf the host who makes the call. MB2 (correctly) adds a beat where each host says where he lands. Then the verdict host reads a line that lands nowhere. Out of six to eight minutes of real argument, a polished, balanced, read sentence will sound like a switch from talking to reading, and it undoes the position he just took.
- The unsayable: P206 is about 43 words, one sentence, one breath, with "on record", "facts scrubbed out" and "half a century" stacked. Nobody has that breath at the end of a heated block.
- Too clean and repeated: P201 already hands KingZ "the movie hands you a monster to point at" and Winter Wolf "the real story does not give you one". P206 and P207 restate it. P207's second sentence restates P33 ("That is what we are here for"). The theme is said out loud four times across the episode.
- Reaching for: a firm exit ramp that respects both readings and sends the viewer out on the show's thesis.
- Shape of fix: the exit has to survive whatever the two hosts just decided, so it either carries no verdict and no restated thesis (a plain exit into the outro), or it is left as a beat, not a line, and the pivot in P201 is the only place the thesis is said. Also see MB3 in the suggestions: the "seven men" frame in P206 argues a theory Segment 5 never put on the table. Zac's words.
