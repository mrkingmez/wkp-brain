## 0. MUST-FIX LIST (WRONG or stale items)

1. **P107 and P187, reappearance date November 12 is WRONG.** Nothing I checked supports Nov 12. Walton came to on the evening of Nov 10 and called about 12:05 a.m., early Nov 11 (thescarechamber.com, SOURCED). Press reported him found on Nov 11 (Wikipedia, SOURCED). A deputy reached the family ranch at 2:00 a.m. (Wikipedia). Fix: "November 10th into 11th" or "just after midnight, November 11th." Fix the date stamp in the asset list (P253 lists Nov 12 1975).
2. **P106 "five days and six hours" is arithmetically consistent, not independently sourced.** Last seen about 6 p.m. Nov 5, call about 12:05 a.m. Nov 11 gives 5 days 6 hours. A search summary also gave "five days and six hours." Keep, but P107 must change.
3. **P69 "Population approx. 4,000. 1975" is WRONG.** Snowflake census count was 1,977 in 1970 and 3,510 in 1980 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake,_Arizona raw table, SOURCED). A 1975 figure near 4,000 is not supported. 4,112 is Taylor's 2010 number (search result), likely the source of the mix-up. Fix: "a few thousand" or use the 1970 census figure.
4. **P72 "Rogers ... engaged to Travis's sister" looks REVERSED.** Sources say Walton dated Rogers' sister Dana and later married her, which made Rogers a "future brother-in-law" (Wikipedia calls Rogers "future brother-in-law"; the Dana detail is from a blog summary and a blog comment gave the name as Dora, so the first name is UNVERIFIED). In the film, Dana Rogers is Mike's sister and Travis's girlfriend. No source I found says Rogers was engaged to Walton's sister. Fix: "Rogers, whose sister Travis was seeing" or drop it.
5. **P91 and P213 "all seven saw it" and "every one of them told the same story for forty-six years" are WRONG.** The crew was seven including Walton. Six crew members said they saw the object and Walton was the one hit (Wikipedia). Also, crew member Steve Pierce expressed suspicion of a hoax as early as 1978 (Wikipedia, citing Pierce). In 2026 Pierce said he is "100%" sure it was a hoax (Sheaffer blog, SOURCED, see section 3).
6. **P47 "stood up and said it never happened" overstates Rogers.** His March 19, 2021 post said he was no longer a witness to Walton's "supposed abduction." Wikipedia says he later reconciled with Walton and retracted his confession. Fix: "withdrew as a witness."
7. **P162 and P164 "did not surface publicly for years" is WRONG as to timing.** The failed test (McCarthy, Nov 15, 1975) was kept out of the Enquirer by agreement, but Klass told NICAP about it roughly three months later (search summary of Skepticblog/NICAP material, February 1976). Fix: "kept quiet, and it came out months later." Also Walton's "first polygraph" claim is accurate in that he skipped the police test on Nov 14, then took the McCarthy test Nov 15.
8. **P102 and P103 timing.** Script says "five days into it" and "before anybody knew whether Travis was alive or dead." Wikipedia dates Gilson's crew polygraph Nov 11, the same day press reported Walton found. The "before anybody knew" line is not supported. Order within Nov 11 relative to his return is UNVERIFIED. Fix: say "the day after the five days" or "November 11th" and drop "before anybody knew."
9. **P115 "multiple polygraphs" Walton passed.** Sourced passes are one publicized test by George J. Pfeifer, announced Feb 7, 1976 (search summary of Skepticblog, SOURCED), and the 1993 film's closing title says a 1993 retest was passed. Also on record: McCarthy fail (Nov 15, 1975) and a 2008 private examiner on Fox's The Moment of Truth found deception (Wikipedia). If the script claims he never failed, that is wrong.
10. **P167 "half of Navajo County searches" is overstated.** Wikipedia: Nov 7 search of nearly 50 people, Nov 8 second search with a helicopter, ended Nov 9 at his mother's request.
11. **P121 "tar like substance" is not the film.** The plot summary says a gelatinous substance forced into his mouth and a tube inserted down his throat. Use "gelatinous" or "slime." P57 "corridor" is not in the film plot summary (zero-gravity chamber, platform). "Table" is a platform. Soft, but fix if it goes on screen.
12. **P168 "The Gentry tower was never searched. Not once."** Sources say "reportedly never searched." Keep "reportedly."
13. **P179 "no retraction of the 1975 account from anyone else on that crew" is STALE.** Steve Pierce, a crew member, told producer Ryan Gordon in August 2026 the Gentry tower was the "UFO" and the others were fooled (Sheaffer blog, 2026-08-16, SOURCED). Mike Rogers is reported dead (Feb 6, 2026), with his daughter claiming a deathbed confession (unverified, secondhand). P212 "Walton is still around" is still true. P206 "only the crew chief ever wobbled" and P191 "one man who walked his back in 2021" need a decision from Zac.
14. **P124 and P125 Tormé "too fuzzy" quote, hold it off screen.** See check 4 below.
15. **P78 and P147 "Travis and Rogers watched it together" is UNVERIFIED.** Sources only say the NBC film aired the same night Rogers wrote the letter. See claim table.

## 2. Five open fact checks (P244 to P248)

1. **P244 polygraph date, examiner, split.** RESOLVED. Nov 11, 1975. Arizona Department of Public Safety examiner C. E. Gilson. Rogers and five others tested. Five judged truthful, Allen Dalis "inconclusive." Purpose: to determine whether they murdered Walton. Wikipedia incident article, SOURCED. Note the order-of-events issue in must-fix 8. Dalis is spelled Allen Dalis in the sourced article and Allan Dallis for the film character.
2. **P245 Turkey Springs contract after Nov 5.** NOT RESOLVED, UNVERIFIED. What is known: extension to Nov 10; Oct 16 inspector said the job could not finish; penalty up to $2,500 plus bid disqualification (Wikipedia). I found no record of what happened after Nov 5. Do not state it on camera. A FOIA request is the route the script already proposes (P77). PROPOSED.
3. **P246 April 30, 2021 Gordon recording.** PARTLY RESOLVED. Public: audio posted by Gordon on July 4, 2021 (Wikipedia). The quoted words are in Wikipedia and match the script's "reportedly." Not verified: I did not hear the audio and I did not retrieve the audio URL (NOT AVAILABLE). Not verified: "on his watch." Keep "reportedly."
4. **P247 Tormé quote.** NOT RESOLVED. Wikipedia's incident article attributes "too fuzzy and too similar to other televised close encounters" and "flashier, more provocative" to Paramount's decision, citing Ryan Murphy, "Reworking 'Fire in the Sky'," Entertainment Weekly, March 19, 1993 (https://ew.com/article/1993/03/19/reworking-fire-sky/). I could not read the EW text (blocked). So whether Tormé said those exact words, or EW wrote them, is UNVERIFIED. Wikipedia's film article instead says Tormé said executives found it "boring." Do not put the quote marks on screen and do not say "he has said" until someone reads the EW piece. Tormé's name is spelled Tormé with the accent. He died Jan 4, 2024.
5. **P248 Enquirer figures.** RESOLVED. $100,000 standing offer by 1975 (it began at $50,000 in spring 1972). $5,000 "best UFO case of the year" prize to Walton and his co-workers. Wikipedia incident article, SOURCED. Division of the $5,000 not found.
