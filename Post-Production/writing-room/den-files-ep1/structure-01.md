## BLUF

1. The script reads at roughly 21 to 25 minutes of spoken host lines against a stated segment sum of about 55 minutes. With clips and a 6 to 8 minute verdict, the finished episode comes out around 30 to 35 minutes. That is a runtime decision, not something Segment 7 should be padded to cover. (PROPOSED, hand count, see Section 6)
2. The hook at P47 hands over a verdict the episode later walks back: "the one witness who could confirm the whole thing later stood up and said it never happened" against Segment 5's own reading at P179 (withdrew as a witness, recording disputed, no verified confession, Rogers denied the full hoax claim). That breaks the teasers-not-verdicts gate and the script's own accuracy. Must fix before recording. (PROVEN)
3. The dates don't add up: "five days and six hours after he disappears" (P106) can't land on "November 12th" (P107, P186, the P253 lower third) when he vanished November 5th. Must fix before recording. (PROVEN internal conflict. The correct date goes to fact-research.)
4. The reused series cold open says Hollywood "changes the names" (P32). Episode 1 shows the opposite: the film kept Travis Walton and Mike Rogers (P56). The line is reused in every episode, so it is worth settling tonight. (PROVEN)
5. Structure sags from P117. Segment 4 mostly re-runs Segment 1 and Segment 3, and it sits between the best cliff in the episode (P115, "until 2021") and its payoff in Segment 5. The October 20 beat is told in full twice (P75 to P83, P147 to P151) and a third time in the ledger (P186). (PROVEN repetition, PROPOSED fix shape)
6. The Segment 7 beat sheet never asks either host to answer the question the hook asks ("So which is it", P50). Its Reality Score measures something Segment 4 already settled. The believability beat (P199) argues against a seven-man hoax that no theory in Segment 5 actually claims. (PROVEN)
