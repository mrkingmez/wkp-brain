## Pass 1: Premise

Generic logline: two hosts take a movie sold as based on a true story and lay out what really happened. Works that fit: Insider's "How Real Is It" series, Dan LeFebvre's "Based on a True Story" podcast, History vs Hollywood, WatchMojo and Den of Geek "movies that lied" lists, AARP's film fact checks (which score accuracy on a 10-point "Truth-o-Meter"), JoBlo's "WTF Really Happened." Narrowed to Walton: Skeptoid 94, Expedition Files, Richard Dolan's UFO Chronicles, Paranormal Witness, Chris Ramsay's Debriefed, Up and Vanished's Unexplained, plus the Rogan and Larry King appearances the script itself pulls. The list is long. What gets the script off that list: (a) the ledger segment (P182 to P192), documented vs not documented, read flat before anyone gives an opinion, which none of the formats above does as a named segment; (b) two hosts scoring the movie separately and arguing the gap (P198). The name "Reality Score" was not found anywhere in my searches.

## Pass 2: Structure

Hook, then movie, background, real story, movie vs reality, the skeptic case, verdict. That is the shared skeleton of the format, and Segment 4 (P117 to P140) with numbered split-screen points and a "what the movie gets right" closer is the Insider / History vs Hollywood template beat for beat. Acceptable, because it is the genre's grammar, not one show's twist. Borrowed structure worth naming: Segment 5's "theory, then the counter" (P146 to P180) is the Buzzfeed Unsolved and Why Files theories block, but strongest-case-then-counter for each piece is stricter than either. The October 20 coincidence placed twice (P82, P147) and in two graphics (P83, P151) is the script's own staging, but the second pass mostly restates the first. PROPOSED.

## Pass 3: Character

Winter Wolf (verdict-first) and KingZ (warm connector) could read as the Buzzfeed Unsolved split (Ryan believes, Shane mocks). They are not: the split here is tone, not belief, and P15 keeps both out of opinions until Segment 7. That is a real difference. The risk is in the verdict block: if the hosts fall into believer vs skeptic during the unscripted run, they become Unsolved. P198's "do not agree to be nice" is fine; the beats should keep it from turning into one host always believing. PROPOSED.

## Counter (the strongest case for originality)

Nobody watching this cold can name one work it copies. The facts are shared by every Walton piece because they are the record, and the script handles them better than most: it gives Walton's own healing account (P113, P114), which most retrospectives skip in favor of the movie's horror; it presents the hoax case piece by piece with a counter to each, which Skeptoid and Unsolved do not do; and it ends on a ledger that admits exactly one thing is unknowable. "Not documented: five days" (P190) is a sharper thesis than anything in the competing coverage I found. The Reality Score with two separate numbers and the facts-first, verdict-last split are format choices I could not find elsewhere under those terms. Fix the Wikipedia echo, the hook and the 2026 exposure, and this is a show, not a recap.
