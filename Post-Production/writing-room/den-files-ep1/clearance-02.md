## 3. Rogers death report, the daughter, relatives, and Pierce (new exposure)

**Exposure, described:**
1. **Statements about Rogers if he is dead.** As general background only, not re-pulled and not a legal opinion: in many US jurisdictions a defamation claim about a person generally ends with that person's death, and survivors generally cannot sue for defamation of the dead unless the statement is about them too. 2. **The lines still about a living man.** P167 (stage the sighting), P177 (talking about creating a hoax), P149, P156 and P206 accuse Rogers and Walton together. Rogers's death, if confirmed, does not reduce exposure toward Walton through the same lines.
3. **The deathbed claim is a new accusation against Walton.** Repeating "his daughter says he confessed on his deathbed" republishes a fraud claim about a living man, sourced to a deleted post that no bench has seen, and contradicted by another account (Stein, James). Attribution ("reportedly", "a family member has claimed") is the shape SC option 1 describes; whether attribution reduces exposure is Q2.
4. **The daughter is a private person.** Naming her, or describing her so she is identifiable, attributes words to her that nobody on the team has read. If the account is wrong, that is a false statement about a living private person. She is also, if the death is real, recently bereaved. Not named in the script today (PROVEN, grep).
5. **Other relatives.** P72 already involves Walton's sister and Rogers's sister (SC MA5 says the relationship in P72 is wrong). Any line about the family dispute (P175 "personal fight", "family falling out" P200) touches identifiable living relatives.
6. **Saying he died.** If the blogs are wrong, an on-air "Rogers died" is a false statement about a living man. Attribute or verify first (PROPOSED).
7. **SC option 2 (date the record to 2021).** Brings in no new names, but P199 "no deathbed confession" said as a present-tense fact may now be inaccurate. That is mostly an accuracy and credibility problem; it favors Walton, so it is not an accusation. Zac's call.
