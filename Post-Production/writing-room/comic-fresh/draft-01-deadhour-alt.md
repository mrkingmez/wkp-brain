## BLUF

- **Pick: DEAD HOUR TOW.** KingZ and Winter Wolf run a two-man night tow outfit in a city with a highway interchange that was never finished. Between 3 and 4 AM the dead-end ramps lead somewhere else, and things come down them and crash. The city pays King and Wolf to haul them away.
- **Oracle is dispatch.** She works from the projection booth of a dead drive-in theater that is now their impound lot, and throws every city camera onto the sixty-foot screen. On the page she is a voice, a violet beam and her hands. Never a face. Reasons in Section 2.
- **Threat: the Line Crew.** Tall figures made of road, a lane line painted down each face, road reflectors for eyes. They are finishing the interchange from the other side. Every stretch they pave makes the dead hour run longer. They can only walk on pavement, and the impound lot is gravel.
- **First arc is nine weeks**, ending in a detour, not a fight. Issue 1 ends with the dead hour running one minute late.

## 1. Three pitches

### Pitch A: DEAD HOUR TOW (PICK)

**Premise.** Calder Bay has an unfinished highway interchange on Route 9. Work stopped in 1987 and the ramps end in midair. Every night from 3:00 to 4:00 AM, the ramps connect to somewhere. Things come down them, mostly too fast, and wreck on the frontage road. The city doesn't admit any of this. It just gives the overnight recovery contract to the lowest bidder: KING & WOLF 24-HR RECOVERY.

**World.** A sodium-orange city asleep. One heavy red wrecker. The impound lot is an old drive-in theater: wrecks parked in the speaker-post rows, all facing the giant screen. Oracle runs dispatch from the projection booth.

**Threat.** The Line Crew, a road crew from the far side. They are finishing the interchange from their end. Everything that comes down the ramps is running from them. Each stretch of road they lay adds minutes to the dead hour. If the road connects, the hour never ends.

**Why it runs for months.** Two engines. Episodic: a new wreck every week, and the wreck can be any genre (a fantasy boat, a sci-fi craft, a horror hearse), which matches a channel that jumps genres weekly. Serial: the dead-hour clock, printed on every issue, that readers watch creep. Production is cheap to keep on model: a truck cab, a ramp, a lot.

**Tone.** Night-shift blue collar. Two guys in a truck cab arguing about donuts, then something impossible slides down a ramp. Creepy at the edges, warm in the cab.

### Pitch B: CROSSTOWN

**Premise.** The city of Marquee is split into districts that run on genre physics. In the Hollow, anyone who splits up from the group vanishes. In the Neon Reach, machines answer back. In the Thornwood, any promise you say aloud binds you. KingZ and Winter Wolf are couriers, the only two who cross borders without picking up the local rules. Oracle reads the borders from a city map nobody else can see.

**Threat.** Someone is moving the borders at night, and a district's rules stick to whatever it swallows.

**Why it runs.** Endless district-of-the-week settings.

**Tone.** Playful, meta, puzzle-box.

**Why not.** Every week costs rule explanation, and three phone-sized pages cannot carry it. It is also a comic about genre instead of a comic in one, and the "characters trapped in genre rules, watched from a control room" shape is close enough to a well-known horror film that a viewer could name it.

### Pitch C: UNCLAIMED

**Premise.** KingZ and Winter Wolf run the overnight lost-and-found desk at a huge transit terminal where some trains arrive from places on no map. Passengers leave things behind. Some of the things are dangerous, some are alive, and some passengers come back for them. Oracle runs the terminal PA and the cameras.

**Threat.** A passenger who has been on layover for forty years and wants something every week that nobody lost.

**Why it runs.** One object per week, a passenger per week.

**Tone.** Cozy dread.

**Why not.** It lives indoors behind a counter, which gives Winter Wolf almost nothing physical to do. Fantasy train stations and "return the cursed item" shows are a crowded shelf.

### Why DEAD HOUR TOW wins

1. It has the strongest single image: a long black boat sliding off the end of a highway ramp at 3 AM. That is a scroll-stopper with no explaining.
2. Oracle's role is native, not bolted on. Dispatch is literally the eyes and ears for a crew in the field, and the drive-in screen gives her a look nobody else owns.
3. The two leads get a built-in two-hander stage (the truck cab) where the KingZ and Wolf contrast can run every week.
4. It needs no outside property to feel nerd-verse. The wrecks carry the genre.
5. The clock caption gives every issue a cliffhanger device for free.

## 2. Series bible: DEAD HOUR TOW

### The world

- **Calder Bay.** Mid-size port city, orange streetlights, harbor fog, nobody awake at 3 AM who doesn't have to be.
- **The Route 9 interchange.** A concrete cloverleaf that was never finished. Four ramps (A, B, C, D) stop in midair, rebar sticking out of the broken ends. Below them, a frontage road and weeds.
- **The dead hour.** 3:00 to 4:00 AM. The broken ends of the ramps lead somewhere else. Things come down. When the hour ends, the ramps go back to ending in air.
- **The Lot.** The Starlite drive-in, closed for years, bought cheap. Gravel rows, rusted speaker posts, the giant screen. Wrecks get parked in the rows facing the screen. The ground was never paved.
- **The Booth.** The drive-in's projection booth, a cinderblock box with a slit window above the lot. Oracle has patched traffic cams, her own cameras on the ramps, the police scanner and the truck's radio into the old projector. The city plays on the big screen.

### The rules (keep fixed, reveal one at a time)

1. The dead hour is 3:00 to 4:00. It runs longer every time the Line Crew lays new road.
2. The Line Crew can only stand on pavement. Gravel, dirt, grass and water stop them cold.
3. Everything that comes down the ramps is running from the Line Crew.
4. The Line Crew obeys road signage. Cones, DETOUR, ROAD CLOSED. They are a crew. They follow the plan. (Hidden until week 8.)

### The leads

**KingZ.** Wants to know what each wreck is and where it was trying to go. Treats every one like a stray dog. Obstacle: the job is to haul and impound, not adopt, and his kindness keeps walking him onto pavement. Role on a call: goes to the thing first, talks to it, notices what it needs.

**Winter Wolf.** Wants the business alive and the two of them home by sunrise. Obstacle: the city pays by the axle, the wrecks keep coming, and the thing that's wrong is a road, which he can't tow. Role on a call: drives, decides, rigs the chain, makes the call nobody else will.

**Oracle.** Wants to see the whole board, all of it, all the time. She is the only one who keeps the minute count. Obstacle: the Line Crew blinds her by paving over her cameras, and she can't leave the Booth during the hour (someone has to watch). Role on a call: sees it first, tells them where, how far, how many, and what's behind them.

**How they work a call.** Oracle spots it on the screen and routes the truck. Wolf decides what gets done. KingZ figures out why the thing is here. Oracle answers *what and where*, Wolf answers *what now*, KingZ asks *why*. When those three disagree, that's the scene.

### Oracle's design decision

**Decision: voice-led and faceless, with three fixed visual signatures.**
1. **The beam.** A violet-white projector beam cutting across the dark lot from the Booth's slit window. When we cut to Oracle, we cut to the beam.
2. **The screen.** The drive-in screen tiled with live feeds. Her point of view.
3. **Her hands.** On the projector controls, a mug, a headset cord. Hands only, never a full figure.

**Why.**
- Gabby is a real person who has not signed off on a likeness. A voice and a beam protect her until she does.
- It keeps the faceless continuity from her earlier show look, but trades a generic glowing circle for an image that is hers and fits a movie channel.
- It stays well away from the familiar "hacker in a dark room facing a wall of monitors" picture. Her monitor is sixty feet wide and outdoors.
- One fewer face to keep on model across weekly AI art.
- If Gabby later wants a face, design it with her. Her design should reveal itself by stepping into the beam, which would make a strong arc-ending moment.

### The threat

**The Line Crew.** Tall, thin, slow, people-shaped but wrong. Bodies the gray of wet asphalt with a fine gravel texture. A yellow lane line painted straight down the center of each face and body. Two road reflector studs where eyes go: dark until headlights hit them, then they blaze white-gold. They don't speak. They measure. They drive orange-ribboned survey stakes wherever the road will go next. The stakes are the creep: on the ramp in week 1, on the frontage road in week 3, at the Lot gate in week 5, inside the Booth in week 7.

**The Foreman.** Bigger, two lane lines (a double yellow, "no passing"), carries a hand paddle sign that reads SLOW on one side and GO on the other. Turns it when he decides.

**The Paver.** The machine behind them. Never fully seen in arc 1. Heat shimmer, the smell of hot tar, a sound like surf made of gravel.

### Arc 1: nine weeks, one line each

1. **RAMP C.** A long black boat slides off the end of Ramp C. King and Wolf tow it. Figures stand at the top of the ramp, and the dead hour ends at 4:01.
2. **GRAVEL.** The boat won't stay parked. It rows in place all day, toward the Lot's back fence. Oracle replays the ramp footage and sees lane lines on faces.
3. **RIGHT OF WAY.** A silver one-seat craft crashes down Ramp A, pilot gone, radio still talking. The Crew gets there first. KingZ trips onto the gravel shoulder, and they stop dead at the edge.
4. **OVERTIME.** The hour runs nine minutes long. The day city notices nothing. KingZ tries to talk to one through the fence, and it answers by driving a stake at his feet.
5. **EASEMENT.** Survey stakes line up at the Lot's front gate. The road is coming straight through the drive-in. Wolf wants to fight, KingZ wants to ask, Oracle wants a plan.
6. **THE FOREMAN.** KingZ meets the Foreman under Ramp C. Gets one answer, sideways: the boat is the last thing on their side that isn't road.
7. **NIGHT WORK.** Heat shimmer at the top of Ramp C. Oracle loses her cameras one by one as asphalt flows over them. The last feed shows a stake inside the Booth.
8. **DETOUR.** Wolf sees a crew member step around a traffic cone. Rule four. All night, the three of them move every cone and DETOUR sign in Calder Bay. Oracle routes them from what's left of the screen.
9. **LAST TOW.** The detour loops the Paver back up its own ramp, and the road stays unfinished. The boat rows back up into the dark on its own. The hour still ends three minutes late, and down on Ramp B, headlights.

**Arc 2 seed.** Ramp B is a different crossing, and what comes down it isn't running from anything.

### Recurring devices

- **The clock box.** Every issue's first panel carries a dashboard-style caption: DEAD HOUR / 3:00 TO 4:00. The end time changes when the story earns it. Readers watch it creep.
- **Unit One.** Oracle's call sign for the truck. She never uses their handles on the radio until something goes wrong.
- **Axles.** The city pays by the axle. Running joke, running stakes: the strangest wrecks pay nothing.

## 3. Issue 1 script: "RAMP C"

Format per page: one 4:5 page, 1080 x 1350, text at least 60 px from every edge. Balloons 12 words or fewer.
Balloon styles: KingZ round, warm white. Winter Wolf round, narrow, heavier border. Oracle a squared radio balloon, violet border, jagged tail into the speaker or radio it comes from. Captions in a dashboard-clock box (amber digits on black) or plain black boxes.

### PAGE 1

**Panel 1 (wide, top third of page).** Calder Bay at 3 AM, seen from below the Route 9 interchange. A concrete cloverleaf ramp curves up and stops in midair, rebar bristling from the broken end, nothing beyond it but fog. The city underneath is asleep under orange streetlights. On the frontage road below the ramp, a heavy red wrecker idles with its hazard lights blinking. Door lettering: KING & WOLF 24-HR RECOVERY.
- SERIES TITLE (small, top left): DEAD HOUR TOW
- CLOCK BOX: DEAD HOUR / 3:00 TO 4:00
- CAPTION: Route 9 interchange. Never finished.

**Panel 2 (medium, inside the cab, two-shot through the windshield).** Winter Wolf behind the wheel, upright, one paw on the wheel, thermos lid of coffee steaming. KingZ in the passenger seat, slumped low, a paper sack of gas-station donuts in his lap, peering up through the glass at the ramp.
- KINGZ: Okay but what if tonight it's nothing?
- WINTER WOLF: Then we don't get paid.
- KINGZ: I'd take nothing. Nothing's nice.

**Panel 3 (close, insert).** The dashboard CB radio. The dial glows violet. The balloon's tail runs into the speaker grille.
- ORACLE (radio): Unit One. Ramp C. Something on the deck.
- KINGZ (off panel): Like a car?
- ORACLE (radio): Moving like it's lost.

**Panel 4 (large, bottom half, low angle from the truck's hood looking up at the broken end of Ramp C).** Out of the fog at the end of the ramp, the prow of a long black wooden boat noses into view. Oars hang off both sides. Water drips off its hull and falls *upward*, beads rising into the air. A single lantern burns at its bow. The boat is tipping over the edge, straight toward the truck.
- WINTER WOLF (from the cab, small): Back up.

*Swipe reason: the boat is coming down on them.*

**Carousel split, if the page reads too small:** Slide 1a is Panels 1 to 3 (ends on "Moving like it's lost"). Slide 1b is Panel 4 alone, full height. Both halves end on a turn.

### PAGE 2

**Panel 1 (wide, top third).** The boat hits the frontage road nose first and slides, wood shrieking on asphalt, a spray of sparks. It stops a foot from the wrecker's grille, which has backed up just far enough. The lantern is still lit. KingZ is half out of the passenger door, one foot on the ground.
- SFX: KRRRRSSHHHK

**Panel 2 (medium).** Winter Wolf out of the cab, circling the boat slowly, blue eyes catching the lantern light. KingZ crouched at the bow, hand drifting toward the lantern.
- WINTER WOLF: Don't touch the lantern.
- KINGZ: I wasn't gonna. Is it warm? It looks warm.

**Panel 3 (close, insert).** One oar, wet, slowly rowing at nothing, its blade scraping a pale line on the asphalt.
- KINGZ (off panel): Wolf. It's still rowing.
- WINTER WOLF (off panel): Then it can row onto the bed.

**Panel 4 (small, tight).** Close on the radio mic clipped to Wolf's jacket collar, by the fur. Violet glow.
- ORACLE (radio): City pays by the axle. Count the axles.
- WINTER WOLF: It's getting towed.

**Panel 5 (wide, bottom third).** Working shot. Wolf on one knee feeding the tow chain under the boat's bow. KingZ standing beside the hull, one hand flat on the black wood like he's calming a horse.
- KINGZ: Hey. Hey, you're fine. We've got a lot. Gravel, even.
- ORACLE (radio): Unit One. Stop looking at the boat. Look up.

*Swipe reason: Oracle sees something they don't.*

**Carousel split:** Slide 2a is Panels 1 to 3 (ends on the rowing oar and Wolf's call). Slide 2b is Panels 4 and 5 (ends on "Look up").

### PAGE 3

**Panel 1 (wide, top third, low angle from beside the truck's light bar, looking up Ramp C).** At the broken end of the ramp where the boat came from, a line of tall thin figures stands at the edge. Wet-asphalt gray. A yellow lane line runs straight down the middle of each face. Where eyes should be, road reflector studs catch the truck's headlights and blaze: pairs of white-gold dots in the fog. They don't move.
- No dialogue.

**Panel 2 (medium, the two leads at the boat, both looking up).** KingZ has stepped half behind Wolf without noticing he did it.
- KINGZ: Are they... with the boat? Are they looking for it?
- WINTER WOLF: No. That's a crew.

**Panel 3 (close).** One gray hand drives an orange-ribboned survey stake into the concrete at the very lip of the ramp.
- SFX: TOK.
- ORACLE (radio): I count six. Seven.

**Panel 4 (medium, from behind the wrecker as it pulls away).** The boat hangs off the tow hook, oars still rowing slowly at the air. In the side mirror, the figures on the ramp, small, still watching.
- CLOCK BOX: 4:00
- KINGZ: Four o'clock. That's it, right? Hour's over?

**Panel 5 (wide, bottom third, the ramp seen from the frontage road, truck taillights small in the distance).** The broken end of Ramp C should be empty fog by now. It isn't. The figures are still standing there. A new orange ribbon flutters on the lip.
- CLOCK BOX: DEAD HOUR / 3:00 TO 4:01
- ORACLE (radio): Unit One. It's 4:01.
- CAPTION (bottom right): NEXT WEEK: GRAVEL

*Hook: the hour has never run late before, and whatever is up there is still standing on the ramp.*

**Carousel split:** Slide 3a is Panels 1 to 3 (ends on the stake and Oracle's count). Slide 3b is Panels 4 and 5 (ends on 4:01).

**Text load.** Page 1 about 45 words, Page 2 about 60, Page 3 about 40. All balloons 12 words or fewer.

### Dialogue self-check (for dialogue-doctor to confirm or overturn)

- KingZ asks, guesses and fills silence: "what if tonight it's nothing," "Is it warm?", "Are they with the boat?", "That's it, right?". He talks to the boat, not about it.
- Winter Wolf hands down verdicts and orders, never asks why: "Back up." "Don't touch the lantern." "That's a crew." Swap test: none of his lines fit KingZ's mouth.
- Oracle talks like dispatch: call sign, location, counts, times. She is the only one who deals in numbers. Her one joke (axles) lands as a jab at the job, not a lesson. She never gives a verdict and never asks a question.
- Nobody explains the dead hour, the city contract or the Crew. The clock box and the art carry it.
- One known soft spot: "Moving like it's lost" says the theme a little early. It's the line I'd test first.

## 4. Visual character bible (one page, description only)

**Palette.** Night base of sodium orange, wet black and fog gray. Character colors: KingZ red, Winter Wolf cold blue, Oracle violet-white, Line Crew asphalt gray with lane-line yellow and reflector white-gold. Truck red. The Lot is the only warm-dark place: amber bulbs and the screen's glow.

**KingZ.** Matches the EP094 page. Masked face, red eyes, crown cap, hoodie with KINGZ in dripping red lettering. For the night shift: a hi-vis vest worn open over the hoodie, never zipped, and work gloves he keeps taking off. Always holding something (donut sack, flashlight, his gloves). Open body: leaning in, crouching to eye level with whatever's wrecked, a hand out. Smaller than Wolf in frame, closer to the thing.

**Winter Wolf.** A wolf. Black hooded jacket with WINTER WOLF across the back, fur collar, blue eyes. For the night shift: a tow chain over one shoulder, a radio mic clipped at the collar, heavy work gloves cut open at the claws. Still body: upright, square to the problem, moves last and moves once. Usually the tallest shape in the panel. His blue eyes are the coldest color on the page.

**Oracle.** Never a face, never a full figure. Seen three ways: the violet-white projector beam crossing the Lot from the Booth's slit window; the drive-in screen tiled with live feeds (her point of view); her hands on the projector controls, a headset cord, a chipped mug. Her radio balloons carry a violet border.

**The wrecker.** Old heavy-duty tow truck, boom and hook on the back, faded red paint, roof light bar, rust at the wheel wells. Door lettering KING & WOLF 24-HR RECOVERY in plain block type. Generic grille and body, no real truck maker's shape or badge.

**The Route 9 interchange.** Four concrete ramps that curve up and stop in midair, rebar bristling from each broken end. Fog past the edge. Graffiti-free, weeds below, a chain-link fence with a faded sign about construction.

**The Lot (old Starlite drive-in).** Gravel rows with rusted speaker posts, wrecks parked facing a towering white screen with patched panels. A cinderblock projection booth on a low rise with a slit window. A hand-painted gate sign with the new business name over the old one. No paving anywhere.

**The Line Crew.** Tall, thin, slow. Bodies the color of wet asphalt with a fine gravel grain. A yellow lane line running straight down the center of the face and body. Two raised road reflector studs for eyes, dark until light hits them, then white-gold. No mouth, no clothing. They carry bundles of thin wooden survey stakes with orange ribbon.

**The Foreman.** Same make, a head taller, a double yellow line down the center. Carries a round hand paddle sign, SLOW on one side, GO on the other.

**The boat (Issue 1 wreck).** Long, narrow, black wooden boat with a high curved prow, two pairs of oars, one iron lantern on a hook at the bow. Water beads drip off it upward. No names, symbols or markings on the hull.

**Never draw.** Any other company's characters, logos or vehicles. A real truck maker's grille or badge. Real highway shields or real city names on signs. Oracle's face or body. A figure with an object for a head.

## 6. Risks I already see (for originality and clearance)

- **Comparisons a viewer might reach for.** Repo Man (a car with something impossible in it), Men in Black (a crew that cleans up the impossible), The Langoliers (time running wrong), the Silent Hill games (roads that end in fog). None is the same story, but originality should check each.
- **The Line Crew look.** People made of road with reflector eyes is the newest thing here and the most important to check. I deliberately avoided object heads (screen, lens, stamp) after the last pass.
- **The title.** "Dead Hour" is a common phrase and likely an existing title somewhere. Search before it becomes a logo.
- **The drive-in.** Keep the Starlite name generic and search it too. Real drive-ins use similar names.
- **Real people speaking invented lines.** Matt and Gabby both. Consent is open.
- **Ownership** of the new characters and world is not in writing (WWD partnership, WKP, or Zac).
