## 1. Premise

Generic logline unchanged: a crew rescues people being written into stock genre parts by a city that runs on genre rules, while a figure circles them. Same list as pass 1 (The Final Girls, Last Action Hero, Pleasantville, Wreck-It Ralph, Westworld, Redshirts, Genrenauts). What makes it not that list is also unchanged and now stronger: the night tow job, the "40" dash card, the car as anchor, and now a villain whose want is a job vacancy. Clears.

**New piece, the replacement rule** (Section 2 "What she wants"). "You can only leave a role by passing it to someone" is an old folklore shape: the ferryman in Grimm's The Devil with the Three Golden Hairs, the Flying Dutchman and Davy Jones in Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Man's Chest, and the suit clause in The Santa Clause. A reader might feel the shape. Nobody names one title, because none of those is a casting call with a shortlist, badges and a callback. Clears. Keep every one of her moves a casting move (Section 2 "What only she has") and it stays hers.

## 4. Texture

- **Dustcover sheets** (Section 2, 1B P4). Dust sheets over furniture are a stock gothic image, best known from The Others (2001), where the shut-up rooms of furniture under sheets read as ghosts. Dustcover moves the image outdoors to a whole street (cars, hydrants, hedges, streetlights), adds knocking from underneath, and forbids any sheet standing up as a figure. A reader may think "haunted house furniture," not "this is The Others." The sheet-ghost of A Ghost Story (2017) is banned in Section 4. Clears.
- **Knocking that moves car to car toward the reader** (2B P5): the approaching sound is common staging. Clears.
- **Oracle in the booth watching the victim framed as horror on a big screen** (2B P4, 3B P5). This is the image pass 1 tied to The Cabin in the Woods. It is now one drive-in screen across a lot, watched by the person trying to save him, showing the shot the ward is about to take rather than a monitor feed. That is far enough. The only close precedent found is Drive-In Massacre (1976), where a killing appears as a silhouette on a drive-in screen, which is too obscure to name. Advisory: never draw more than one screen, never a desk of monitors, never anyone else in the booth, or the Cabin read comes back.
- **Villain on screen walking away with the line trailing her heel** (3B P5): the dragged border is this series' own image. Clears.
- **CALLBACKS shortlist with three sketches** (3B P6): fresh. Clears.
- **Sprocket-hole balloon** (Section 3 "Balloons"): a letterer's choice, not a lift. Clears.

## The counter: strongest case FOR originality

Pass 1 found three borrowed parts and pass 2 replaced each with something only this world has. The horror ward is a street under dust sheets with knocking underneath, which is a shut-up house turned inside out. Oracle sees by running a projector with no film in it and reading where the shot is about to cut, which is a power built from the history of drive-ins (the radio station, the speaker posts, the intermission mic) and from the show's own subject, movies. The villain is an employee who cannot quit until she hires her replacement, and the crew's final move is to do the one thing they always do, tow a car out with a person in it. Every name came back clean. A cold reader will see the nerd-verse vocabulary, because that is what a movie channel's comic should be made of, and will not be able to say "this is" any one thing. Ship it.
