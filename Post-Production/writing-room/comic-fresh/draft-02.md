## 1. Three pitches and the pick

### Pitch A: COLD TOW, revised (PICK)

**Premise.** Marrowgate is a river city whose neighborhoods run on genre: Dustcover is horror, the Glow is science fiction, the Old Wood is fantasy, and the Middle is just a city. The borders are painted white road lines, and every night they crawl. Anyone caught inside a ward for forty minutes starts being written into a stock part. They keep their name and lose their choices. A car is an anchor: tow it with the person inside and they come out clean. Truck Nine, an old wrecker with a 9 on the doors and a card taped to the dash that reads 40, is the only outfit that takes the night calls.

**Threat.** The Callback, a casting woman in a camel coat who drags the border behind her heel and marks cars with a grease-pencil X. An X makes that person's clock run fast. She is not keeping the city in order. She is stuck in her own part, and a casting chair only empties when you cast your replacement. Everyone she has tested so far turned into a stock part. Their badges hang on her lanyard. KingZ, Winter Wolf and Oracle keep walking in and out of wards without changing, which makes them the first real shortlist she has ever had.

**Why it runs.** Episodic engine: a new rescue in a new ward every week, and the ward can be any genre the channel just reviewed. Serial engine: the shortlist, the marks closing in on the drive-in, and the question of which of the three she wants. Cheap to keep on model: a cab, a painted line, a booth.

**Tone.** Blue-collar night shift. Two guys and a bag of fries in a cab, then a street that should not look like that.

### Pitch B: DEAD HOUR TOW (from the archived run)

**Premise.** A two-man night tow outfit hauls away whatever comes down the ramps of an unfinished highway interchange between 3 and 4 AM. Oracle dispatches from a drive-in impound lot. Faceless villains made of road are finishing the interchange from the far side.

**Why it loses.**
1. Same shape as COLD TOW: night tow crew, a clock in every issue, drive-in base, Oracle on the radio. Picking it does not give Zac a second option, only a second skin.
2. Same engine with less in it. The wreck of the week is a thing, not a person, so KingZ has nobody to talk back to life. His whole method goes unused.
3. Oracle's role is the generic one this pass was told to fix: every city camera thrown onto the screen. That is the surveillance-hub job originality flagged on COLD TOW.
4. Its best image (a boat sliding off a highway ramp) is strong, and could be kept as one COLD TOW wreck in the Old Wood later.

### Pitch C: NIGHT FERRY

**Premise.** KingZ and Winter Wolf run the last ferry across a river where the far bank is a different movie genre every crossing. Passengers who stay on the far side too long do not come back. Oracle keeps the lighthouse and talks them across by lamp signal.

**Why it loses.** The ferryman shelf is crowded and readers will name it (the river of the dead, the toll, the crossing). Water in every panel is hard art to keep on model weekly. Winter Wolf spends the issue steering, which is standing still. The lighthouse keeper guiding a boat is a role, not a character.

### Why COLD TOW wins

1. The leads each own a different tool: the talk (KingZ), the hook (Wolf), the sight (Oracle). The rescue needs all three, every week.
2. The forty-minute card and the anchor rule give every issue stakes and a cliffhanger without explaining anything.
3. The villain now wants something specific to her job, and what she wants is one of them. That turns the arc personal.
4. The painted border that crawls is the series' own image. The originality pass called it the strongest in the draft.
5. Name checks from pass 1 came back empty for COLD TOW, THE CALLBACK and Marrowgate (web only, not a trademark search).

## 2. Series bible

### The world

- **Marrowgate.** A river city at night. Bridges, a rail yard, rowhouses, a dead drive-in on a hill above the east bank.
- **The lines.** Fat hand-painted white road lines that mark where one ward ends. After dark they crawl, a few blocks a night, leaving smeared drag marks and paint on curbs. Nobody paints them. Nobody knows who first did.
- **The wards.**
  - **Dustcover (horror).** Everything that stands still too long gets draped in a white dust sheet, like furniture in a shut-up house: parked cars, hydrants, hedges, porch swings, streetlights. One bare bulb on every porch, yellow-green. The sounds come from under the sheets. People here want to lift one and look.
  - **The Glow (science fiction).** Everything lit from inside. Windows show diagrams of the rooms behind them. Machines answer when spoken to. People here want to be the one who says it cannot hold.
  - **The Old Wood (fantasy).** Trees through the asphalt, moss on the lampposts, warm gold light. Anything you promise out loud binds you. People here start making vows.
  - **The Middle.** Ordinary sodium-orange city. The only place the clock does not run.
- **The forty minutes.** Inside a ward the clock runs. Past forty minutes you start becoming the part the ward needs: the one who checks the noise, the one who says it cannot hold, the one who swears the oath. You keep your name and lose your choices. The people this happens to are called **finished**. It is the crew's word; it never appears on the page until someone has to say it.
- **The anchor rule.** A vehicle holds a person to themselves. Get them back in their car, tow the car out with them inside, and they come back clean. Out of the car, the hook cannot help them.
- **The mark.** A grease-pencil X on a car's glass. It makes that person's clock run fast. The X is The Callback testing someone.

### Oracle's sight: how she is the eyes, ears, intel and comms (new for pass 2)

Oracle works from the projection booth of the Tanner Hill drive-in. Everything she knows comes from what a drive-in already had. No city cameras, no scanners, no screens of feeds.

- **Ears: the station.** Drive-ins used to send the movie's sound to car radios on their own small station. The transmitter in the booth still works. Any car radio in Marrowgate tuned to it becomes hers to hear. Truck Nine's radio never leaves that station. Out in the lot, the old speaker posts play whatever those radios pick up.
- **Eyes: the empty reel.** When Oracle runs the projector with nothing loaded, the big screen shows the street around a tuned car, framed the way the ward wants it framed. Inside Dustcover, it is shot like a horror movie: low, from behind, waiting. She does not see a feed. She sees the shot the ward is setting up, which means she sees what it is about to do.
- **Intel: reading the cut.** Her real skill is reading where the shot is going. When the screen cuts to behind a parked car, something is about to happen behind that car. She calls it before it happens.
- **Comms: the intermission mic.** A chrome desk mic with a push bar, once used for "the snack bar closes in ten minutes." Her voice goes out on the station into every tuned radio.
- **The clock: the footage counter.** The projector's counter runs while a tuned car is inside a ward. She reads it as minutes. She is the only one who ever says a number.
- **The limits that make stories.** She sees one shot at a time. A car with its radio off is invisible to her. Daylight washes out the screen. And every time she talks, she is broadcasting from one fixed hill, so anyone listening can find the booth.

**How the three work a call.** Oracle hears it first and tells them where and how long (numbers). Winter Wolf looks at it and decides what happens (verdicts). KingZ goes to the person and asks them small, specific questions until they answer as themselves (names, kids, where they were headed). Oracle answers where and how long, Wolf answers what now, KingZ asks who. When those three disagree, that is the scene.

### The three leads

**KingZ, the talker.**
- Want: get every person out still answering to their own name.
- Obstacle: his method costs minutes, and minutes are the one thing the card on the dash does not have. He is also the one The Callback watches hardest, because sizing up a stranger in seconds is her job too.
- On the page: asks, guesses, stalls, starts sentences twice. One joke an issue at most. When he stops asking questions, something is wrong with him (the arc uses this).

**Winter Wolf, the hook.**
- Want: run it clean and get everybody home, the two of them included.
- Obstacle: the hook only works on someone inside a car, and he cannot talk anyone into one. He has to trust KingZ's slow method against his own read of the clock.
- On the page: short verdicts and orders. Never asks why. Never says a number. Moves last and once.

**Oracle, the sight.**
- Want: see the whole picture. All of it, all night.
- Obstacle: she gets one shot at a time, and every word she says tells The Callback where she sits.
- On the page: call sign, place, minutes. Dry, steady, professional while she is being watched. Never gives a verdict. Her one unbroadcast balloon per issue is the only time she sounds scared.

### The main threat: THE CALLBACK

- **Who she is.** A casting woman, fifties, camel coat, bare head, flat shoes. Clipboard, a grease pencil behind her ear, a lanyard heavy with laminated badges. She walks everywhere, never runs, and the border line follows her heel like a dragged hem.
- **What she wants.** Out. Somebody, long ago, got written into the part of the woman who casts the city, and the only way out of a casting chair is to fill it. She has been holding an open call for decades. Everyone she tests with an X turns into a stock part before the forty minutes are up, and a badge for them appears on her lanyard. She is bored, tired and still looking. The crew are the first people she has seen cross a line and stay themselves. She does not want them gone. She wants one of them holding the clipboard.
- **What only she has.** The open call, the shortlist, the callback (a second look), the badge. Every move she makes is a casting move.
- **What she does not want.** She does not care if the city stays in order. If a ward swallows the Middle, fine. She is not a guardian of roles. She is an employee looking for a replacement.
- **Weakness, hidden until week 10.** She came here in a car. It is still parked in Dustcover, under a sheet, forty years later. The anchor rule works on her too.

### First arc: ten weeks, one line each

1. **The Noise.** Dale, half turned into the man who checks the noise, gets talked back by KingZ and towed out by Wolf, and Oracle watches The Callback circle three sketches on her clipboard.
2. **Drift.** A bus driver in the Glow is becoming the one who says it cannot hold, and Oracle learns that when the screen cuts away early, the ward is about to move its line.
3. **Oath Road.** A teenager on a bicycle in the Old Wood has sworn something out loud, a bike has no radio, and the crew has to find out whether it anchors at all.
4. **Grease Pencil.** Marked cars start turning up in a ring around Tanner Hill, and Oracle counts the ring closing.
5. **Bit Part.** KingZ stays in Dustcover too long and starts becoming the funny friend, and Wolf only notices because KingZ stops asking questions.
6. **Projector.** The Callback follows the station up the hill, sits in a lot space like a customer, and offers KingZ the clipboard.
7. **Wrong Side.** She stands in the projector beam and the screen goes dark, so Oracle has to guide Wolf out of a ward by ear alone, using the speaker posts.
8. **Open Call.** Every marked car in Marrowgate starts its clock at once, three people cannot tow a city, and Oracle works out where the open call is lining everyone up.
9. **Lone Wolf.** Wolf reads for the chair himself so KingZ does not have to, and KingZ pulls him back with a question that has no verdict for an answer.
10. **Final Cut.** Oracle finds the one sheeted car in Dustcover that has always been tuned to her station, the crew tows The Callback home in it, her badges go blank, and the clipboard is left on Truck Nine's dash with nobody's name on it.

**Arc 2 seed.** An empty casting chair does not stay empty. Someone else in the city has started marking cars.

### Recurring devices

- **The dash card.** A card taped to Truck Nine's dash, "40" in marker. It is the only number nobody says out loud.
- **Nine.** Oracle's call sign for the truck. She uses their names only when it matters.
- **The long way home.** Oracle routes them home by a different road whenever she thinks someone is following the truck. Readers learn why in week 6.

## 4. Visual character bible (description only)

**Palette.** Night base of sodium orange, wet black and river blue. Dustcover: bone white sheets, yellow-green porch bulbs, deep black. The Glow: cold cyan. The Old Wood: moss green and lamp gold. Character colors: KingZ red, Winter Wolf cold blue, Oracle violet-white, The Callback camel and grease-pencil red. Truck Nine faded cream with an amber light bar.

**KingZ.** Matches the EP094 page: masked face, red eyes, crown cap, hoodie with KINGZ in dripping red letters. On shift: work gloves he keeps taking off, a radio clipped to his belt. Usually holding something (fries, a glove, someone's arm). Open body: leaning in windows, crouching to eye level, a hand out. Closer to the stranger than anyone else in the panel.

**Winter Wolf.** A wolf. Black hooded jacket with WINTER WOLF across the back, fur collar, blue eyes. On shift: tow chain over one shoulder, heavy gloves cut open at the claws. Still body: square to the problem, upright, the tallest shape in frame. His eyes are the coldest color on the page.

**Oracle.** Faceless until Gabby approves a likeness. Seen as a silhouette in the booth's small square window with the projector glare where her face would be, or from behind, or as hands. White cotton film-handling gloves (her signature). A jeweler's loupe on a cord around her neck, for reading film frames. A knit scarf. A chrome desk microphone with a push bar. No headset, no mask, no lens over an eye, no wheelchair. Do not default her hair to red. Her balloons carry a sprocket-hole strip when she is on air.

**The Callback.** Woman in her fifties. Long camel coat, bare head, hair pinned up, flat shoes. Clipboard always, a red grease pencil behind one ear, a lanyard heavy with laminated badges, each with a small photo and a blank where a name should be. Walks, never runs. The white border line trails from her heel like a dragged hem. Never a black coat, never a hat.

**Dale.** Man in his forties, rumpled plain sweatshirt, tired face. New flashlight with a blank paper price tag on a string. No surname, no logo, no store name anywhere. Not drawn to resemble anyone the team knows.

**Truck Nine.** Old heavy wrecker, faded cream paint, rust at the wheel wells, boom and hook, amber roof light bar, a 9 on each door in plain block numerals. Generic cab and grille. No real maker's grille shape, badge, or boom-unit name. The card taped to the dash reads 40.

**The minivan.** Plain beige minivan, generic body, no badge.

**The Tanner Hill drive-in.** Gravel lot on a hill, rows of rusted speaker posts, a tall patched white screen, a cinderblock projection booth with one small square window. Old transmitter mast on the booth roof. No marquee name visible in Issue 1.

**The lines.** Fat hand-painted white road lines, slightly uneven, with smeared drag trails behind them and paint climbing curbs.

**Dustcover.** A residential block where everything that stands still is under a white dust sheet: cars, hydrants, hedges, swings, mailboxes, streetlights. Sheets are clean, heavy, and hang to the ground. Nothing under a sheet ever stands up as a figure. One bare yellow-green bulb on every porch.

**Never draw.** Any other company's characters, logos or vehicles. A real truck or minivan maker's grille or badge. A bat shape. A sheet-ghost figure. A tall shape in fog. Oracle's face or full figure. A figure with an object for a head. A real store name on any tag.
