## Page 1

**1A P2. ORACLE (radio): "Nine. Calder and Fifth. Minivan, hazards on." / ORACLE: "Nine minutes on him."**
Call sign collision. "Nine" opens her first balloon as the truck's name, then opens her second as a minute count, in the same panel, interrupting a man mid-word. Cover the context and "Nine minutes on him" can read as "Nine, minutes on him." This is the reader's first look at Oracle's two jobs (call sign and clock), and they blur into one word. It was reaching for: her clean dispatch rhythm, plus the first number on the clock.
PROPOSAL: ORACLE (radio, over him): "Eight on him."
(Any number but nine. Change the change-log clock list to match. The shorter balloon also cuts in harder over KingZ.)
Also, dropping "hazards on" here fixes the 2A repeat:
PROPOSAL: ORACLE (radio): "Nine. Calder and Fifth. Minivan."

**1A P3. WINTER WOLF: "Plenty." / KINGZ: "You said plenty last time."**
Keep. This is KingZ's one joke and it is the right size. It ends the slide on a needle, not a punchline.

**1B P4. KINGZ (small, from the cab): "They're covering the cars now?"**
Exposition with a continuity wrinkle. The bible says Dustcover always drapes anything that stands still, so "now" either means the crew has never seen this (they have) or exists to tell the reader "this ward covers things," which the splash is already showing. It is also aimed at the wrong thing. The scary moment is P5, the sheet coming down on a van with a man in it, and the splash line uses up KingZ's reaction a panel early. It was reaching for: KingZ noticing first and asking, which is his voice.
PROPOSAL: P4 no dialogue (let the splash breathe). P5 inset: KINGZ (small, off panel): "Wait. Is that landing on him?"
(Seven words. Gives 1B its swipe line as a question only KingZ would ask. P5 was silent by design, so this is the room's call. If the silence matters more, cut the P4 line and leave both panels silent.)

## Page 2

**2A P1. ORACLE (radio): "Hazards on. Somebody in there still thinks like a driver."**
The line is good. The first two words repeat what she said in 1A, and the hazards are blinking right in the panel. Fix with the 1A proposal above, not here. If 1A keeps "hazards on," then cut the first two words here:
PROPOSAL: ORACLE (radio): "Somebody in there still thinks like a driver."

**2A P2. KINGZ: "Hey. Hi. I'm... what's your name?"**
Keep. Restart, stall, a sentence started twice. This is the voice the bible asked for. Use it as the model for KingZ going forward.

**2A P3. KINGZ: "Dale. You got kids? What're their names?" / DALE: "They're... I..."**
Keep both. The horror beat lands on Dale's broken answer.

**2B P4. ORACLE: "He just jumped to twenty-two. That's too fast." / KINGZ (radio): "Wait. Did somebody mark him?"**
Keep. Nobody answers KingZ; Wolf answers a different question in P5 ("Hook goes on now"). That is real.

**2B P5. WINTER WOLF: "Hook goes on now."**
Slightly stiff. It reads like a stage direction more than a man with a chain on his shoulder. A tow guy says what he is doing, in the fewest words. It was reaching for: the verdict, decided, no discussion.
PROPOSAL: WINTER WOLF: "Hooking it. Now."
(Optional. The current line is not wrong, just a little written.)

**2B P5 SFX: "knok. knok. knok."**
Out of my lane strictly, but at phone size the missing c reads as a typo and a letterer may "fix" it. If the spelling is deliberate, note it in the lettering script.

**2B P6. KINGZ: "Dale? Dale, no no no."**
Keep.

## Page 3

**3A P1. KINGZ: "Wolf! Two minutes! Just give me two!" / ORACLE: "Thirty-one on him. Left of the hydrant."**
Keep both. KingZ bargains, Wolf does not answer, Oracle gives the number and the place. Three voices, three jobs. Note that every hydrant in Dustcover is under a sheet, so "the hydrant" has to be readable in the art (HANDOFF).

**3A P2. WINTER WOLF (radio, from KingZ's belt): "No, you don't."**
Nobody would say that here, because Wolf is not here. In P1 he is on one knee at the van with the chain under the bumper, a block back. Dale's line is a trailing murmur. For Wolf to answer it, KingZ's belt radio has to be keyed open, and nothing in the panel says it is. A sharp reader will ask how Wolf heard. It was reaching for: Wolf's flat verdict cutting off Dale's trance, which is the best two-voice moment in the issue and should stay.
PROPOSAL (staging, not a new line): Wolf has left the chain and walked up behind them. Draw him in P2, in person, no radio tag. P3 can then show him at the van again or walking back, and 3B P4 has him throw the lever.
If Wolf must stay at the van, give the line nobody. Dale reaching and KingZ grabbing his arm with no balloon is also strong.

**3A P3. KINGZ: "Kids, Dale. Their names. Come on, man."**
Keep. Third time he asks about the kids. Repeating is how a real person pushes.

**3B P4. DALE: "Maya. And Theo. ...Why am I holding a flashlight?" / KINGZ: "Don't know, man. Keep it for Theo." / ORACLE: "Counter stopped at thirty-four."**
Dale's line is still the best in the issue. KingZ's reply is warm and no longer a sitcom tag. Two small things.
First, too clean: KingZ gives a neat callback to the kid's name, which is a tidy button. Second, density: this panel has three speakers, and 3B carries about 40 words, the heaviest slide in the issue, while P6 needs the eye for the sketches.
PROPOSAL (option, not required): cut KingZ's balloon. Draw him sagging against the door, breathing. Dale's question goes unanswered, which is truer, and 3B drops to about 33 words. KingZ is still warm on the page: he already got the names out of Dale.
Also "Counter" is a word the reader has not met (the footage counter lives in the bible). It reads fine from context. Keep it unless the room wants "Stopped at thirty-four."

**3B P5. ORACLE (off air): "She's dragging it. She's dragging the line."**
Keep. The repeat is the fear. The plain-balloon swap makes it read.

**3B P6. ORACLE: "Nine. Come home the long way." / KINGZ: "Why the long way?"**
Keep both. No answer. Right ending.
