## Judgment calls

### 1. FTC affiliate disclosure, wording

Where it sits: strong.
- (B1, line 44) and (C1, line 64) use "We get commissions for purchases made through this link." That tracks the plain-language example the FTC itself has published for affiliate links.
- (B2, line 51), (C2, line 71), (D, line 85) say "we get paid." Plain, unambiguous.
- Every version pairs the word "affiliate" with a plain-English explanation. That matters, because "affiliate link" alone is the phrasing regulators have said some viewers do not understand.

Open point: the disclosure covers commissions only. If WWD has received anything else from Opus (a free or discounted account, credits, a partner perk, early access), that is also a material connection, and none of the copy discloses it. Matt reportedly pays for Firefly credits (memory, tools); who pays for the Opus plan and on what terms is not on disk that this agent found. Zac should confirm WWD pays full price, or the disclosure needs to say otherwise.

### 3. YouTube paid-promotion toggle

YouTube's help page says creators must tick the paid promotion box for "paid product placements, endorsements, sponsorships, or other content that requires disclosure to viewers," and that it is the creator's responsibility to comply with the law. The pages retrieved do not address affiliate links by name. An affiliate video with a spoken endorsement and a required FTC disclosure reads as "content that requires disclosure," so the plain-language reading points toward ticking it, but that is not confirmed from YouTube's own wording.

What ticking it does, per YouTube: a disclosure label for about 10 seconds at the start, possible ad replacement where an ad conflicts, and removal from YouTube Kids. None of those look costly for a WWD how-to.

Pass-01 flags this as unverified in (Summary, line 215). It is still unresolved.

### 4. Opus Clip affiliate terms

Read from snippets of the official terms at opus.pro/affiliate-terms and the program pages. Full text was not read. Points found:
- **Paid advertising is prohibited**, including paid social and paid influencer promotion. Organic posting on WWD's own channel looks like the encouraged path. Any boosted post, paid promotion of the how-to video, or a paid Facebook boost carrying the link would hit this. Flag for whoever schedules the companion posts.
- **Claiming endorsement by, or operation by, Opus Clip or its company (Immersively Inc.) is prohibited.** The copy does not do this. Keep it that way in any later edit. A line like "Opus asked us to show you" would cross it.
- **Spam through links or comments is prohibited.** (G, line 199) and any cross-posting of the link into other channels' comments would be the place to watch.
- **Coupon sites are discouraged.** Not relevant to this copy.
- **Commission is on new customers only, and renewals pay nothing** (official terms, snippet). (B2, line 51) says "If you buy a paid plan through it, we get paid a commission." That is broadly accurate but slightly over-broad, since an existing Opus customer buying through the link would not pay WWD anything. Low exposure; it overstates WWD's interest, not understates it, which is the safe direction.
- **No discount, trial, or deal language appears anywhere in (A) to (G).** Matches the brief.
- **Trademark use and brand-asset rules:** not visible in the snippets. Whether the terms restrict use of the Opus logo, or restrict trademark use in titles, is unknown.
- Third-party sites report the commission rate and duration inconsistently. Pass-01 makes no rate claim, so this does not touch the copy.
- The terms say they can change without notice. Read the live page in full before posting.

### 5. Implied claims and statements about WWD's own process (the main exposure)

In an affiliate video, the process description is part of the endorsement. FTC endorsement rules expect it to reflect real use. Each of these conflicts with or is not backed by the repo:

- **(E, 2:50 to 3:30, line 137) "I keep five to seven clips per episode."** TASKS.md, EP120 row: 26 clips, 59 posts, scheduled 2026-11-12. That is not five to seven. Same section, line 139, asks for the real count, which will contradict the spoken line on screen.
- **(E, 5:25, line 164) "every one of them got checked by a person first."** Root CLAUDE.md, zero-question rule locked 2026-11-11: the whole chain, including Opus shorts prep, runs without Zac, and agents fix the cut doc and copy. TASKS.md for EP120 says "Spot-check one in Opus before Thu 6 PM." "Every one checked by a person" is not what the files describe.
- **(E, 5:00, line 159) "I check the first few before they go live."** Same conflict. The file says one spot-check.
- **(E, 0:10, line 107), (F, title 1, line 180), (F, title 3, line 182) "every Short on this channel."** Memory notes review Shorts are built through `shorts-factory.md` (finished clips, burned-in captions), and the Opus pipeline in CLAUDE.md is the FrostCast chain. If any WWD Short was not cut through Opus, "every" is false.
- **(E, 0:00 and 5:25, lines 106 and 164) "Fifty-nine posts. One episode."** 59 is confirmed in TASKS.md as scheduled posts (YouTube 7, Facebook 26, Instagram 26). It is scheduled, not posted, and it counts the same clip across platforms. Spoken over Opus footage, a viewer could take it as "the tool made 59 Shorts from one episode." Title 2 (line 181) and the thumbnail (line 187) say "[N] SHORTS," which would be wrong if 59 is dropped in. YouTube got 7.
- **(E, 1:40, line 133) "Each clip gets a score out of ten, and only the elite ones ship."** Unclear whether the score is Opus's own virality score or WWD's Prompt System v2.1. If it is Opus's, it is a statement about the product. If it is WWD's, say so, or a viewer will credit the tool with it. "Only the elite ones ship" also sits badly next to 26 of an episode's clips scheduled.
- **(E, 0:20, line 116) "without losing every night to editing."** An implied time-savings claim. No number, which helps, but it still says the tool saves the evening. Mild.
- **(E, 3:50, line 147) "it will get these wrong for you too."** A negative statement about the product, framed as a prediction. Generic and fair-sounding as opinion. Low exposure, and it cuts against puffery, which helps credibility.
- **(E, 3:30, line 143) Matt's "It does not find good clips."** Opinion, clearly. Low.

No income promise, growth promise, or earnings claim was found anywhere. (E, line 116) expressly disclaims growth. That is the right shape.

### 6. Free-plan watermark claim

Appears in (B2, line 51), (C1, line 64), (C2, line 72), (D, line 94), (E, lines 155 and 156).

Live check: the official pricing page at opus.pro/pricing did not return readable plan detail. Several third-party sources (toolradar, creatify, costbench, castmagic) agree the free plan adds a watermark, and that paid tiers remove it. Not verified against Opus's own page. Treat as unconfirmed.

Judgment call for Zac: name only what is confirmed on the live page, and consider whether a second limit belongs in the description.

Opus has changed plans before (the "Pro Plan, same price, more features" notice surfaced in search). Re-check on posting day, not today.

### 7. Trademark use of "Opus Clip"

- Use is referential: naming the tool WWD uses, in plain text, as much as needed to identify it. That is the shape of ordinary nominative use.
- The company now styles the product as "OpusClip," one word, on its own pages. Pass-01 uses "Opus Clip," two words, throughout. Not a legal problem on its own; it may matter for search and for any brand-style rule in the full affiliate terms, which were not read.
- Logo: none is called for in the copy. (E, 4:50, line 155) is text only. If the editor adds the Opus logo to the link card or the thumbnail, that moves from referencing the brand toward using its mark as artwork, and the affiliate terms on brand assets were not read. Flag for the edit, and for Matt on the thumbnail (F).
- Titles (F, lines 180 to 182) carry no Opus mark. That keeps the brand out of the title, which avoids any implied partnership read.
- Screen recordings of the Opus interface (E, lines 122, 134, 138) show Opus's UI. Showing a product's interface in a tutorial about using it is common. Low exposure, not zero.
