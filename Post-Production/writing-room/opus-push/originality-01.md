# Originality pass 1: Opus push, "How we cut every Short" (pass-01.md)

Mode: GENERATE draft, so this is a read and report. No replacement lines below, only the problem and the shape of a fix.
Read cold: D:\WKP\Post-Production\writing-room\opus-push\pass-01.md

## VERDICT: SHIPS WITH CHANGES

One real finding, and it sits in the most visible slots. The hook, the loop close, thumbnail text and title 2 all use the "one long video, N clips" construction. That construction is Opus Clip's own homepage tagline ("1 long video, 10 viral clips"). On an affiliate video for Opus, the opening line repeats the vendor's ad. A viewer who has seen Opus's site or any of its affiliates will name it.

Everything else is generic rather than lifted: tutorial boilerplate and engagement questions that could run on any creator's channel. The disclosure lines are boilerplate on purpose, and they should stay that way.

---

## 1. Premise

Generic logline: a creator shows the tool and workflow they use to turn long episodes into Shorts, with an affiliate link.

Works that fit that sentence: Opus's own blog ("How to Turn Long Form Videos into YouTube Shorts at Scale"), and the genre of vendor and creator pieces titled "Turn 1 Long Video Into 10 Viral Clips Using Opus Clip", "How to Repurpose One Long Video into 30 Shorts with AI", "Turn One Long Video Into 12 High-Quality Clips". The list is long. The premise does no work.

What could make it not that list is already in the draft but underplayed: two movie-review hosts, a show where spoilers matter, a human gate on every clip (step 2 rules, the approved copy file, the "checked by a person" close). Right now only the close and step 4 lean on that. The rest of the outline reads like a solo creator-economy tutorial.

## 2. Structure

Hook with a big number, disclosure, "what this is and is not", numbered steps 1 to 5, honest limits, link, loop back to the number. That is the standard affiliate tutorial skeleton. It is a universal shape, not a lift from one known video, so it is not a finding on its own.

The specific problem is the bookend. Section E, line 106 ("Fifty-nine posts. One episode.") and line 164 (the same line repeated as the loop close) put the vendor-tagline shape at both ends, so the structure opens and closes on Opus's marketing.

## 3. Character

- Zac narrator (Section E): reads as the generic "here's my real process" tutorial host. Line 107 ("This is exactly how we cut every Short on this channel") is the "here's exactly how I" opener every creator tutorial uses. Line 116 ("It is not a promise that your channel will grow") is the standard tutorial disclaimer. A type, not yet a person. What makes him a person is the review-show context, which never appears in his spoken lines.
- Matt cameo (Section E, lines 143 to 144): the skeptic co-host is a type, but it is earned, because Matt really is the verdict-first voice. The line itself is the problem: "A clip tool finds clips. It does not find good clips. That part is still on you." is the "a tool is only as good as the person using it" aphorism that closes most honest AI-tool reviews. Zac's reply ("Which is exactly why step two exists") is a setup-and-tag that any two-host channel would write. Shape of fix: give Matt a skeptical line only a movie reviewer would have, about what a clipper gets wrong on film talk specifically.

## 4. Texture (by section and line)

- **E, line 106 and line 108; E, line 164; F, title 2 (line 181); F, thumbnail (line 187).** "Fifty-nine posts. One episode." / "ONE EPISODE. [N] POSTS." / "One Episode, [N] Shorts" / "ONE EPISODE. [N] SHORTS." All the same construction as Opus's tagline "1 long video, 10 viral clips" and the repurposing-blog headlines built on it ("Turn 1 Long Video Into 10 Viral Clips Using Opus Clip"). This is the finding. It reads as the vendor's ad, and on an affiliate video that also costs trust. Shape of fix: keep the real 59 if Zac confirms it, but hang it on something only this show has (the check, the spoiler order, two hosts, a movie) rather than on the long-to-many ratio. Change it in all five places together, including the fallback hook at line 170, or the cutdown slots will keep the old shape.
- **E, line 133.** "Each clip gets a score out of ten, and only the elite ones ship." Opus shows its own virality score on every clip. A viewer watching the screen recording will assume the score is the tool's, which blurs the one thing that sets this workflow apart (your own rules). "Elite" is also stock guru vocabulary. Shape of fix: make it unmistakable on screen and in the line that the score is yours, not Opus's.
- **E, lines 128 to 132.** The four rules (first three words, hook text, mid-clip retention beat, loop ending) are the standard Shorts-advice checklist from every repurposing guide. Fine as content, since it is your real system, but as told it sounds like a course. Shape of fix: one concrete WWD example per rule (from a clip with no spoilers) would turn a checklist into a process.
- **E, line 147.** "Weak hooks. Sentences cut off halfway. Captions that miss a name." These are the stock complaints in every Opus review. Accurate, generic. Lower priority. A name a caption tool would plausibly mangle from a movie world (no past-episode tie-in) would make it yours.
- **E, line 116.** "without losing every night to editing." A creator-economy time-saving phrase. Minor.
- **C1, line 65.** "Tell us what you use and what gives you trouble." **C2, line 73.** "What part of cutting clips costs you the most time?" Both are generic engagement prompts that would sit unchanged under any tool tutorial. Shape of fix: the question should be one only this audience can answer, tied to clips of movie or podcast talk.
- **F, titles 1 and 3 (lines 180, 182).** "How We Cut Every Short on This Channel" is plain and searchable, and generic by design. That is a fair trade for search, not a lift. "The Clip Workflow Behind Every Short We Post" is creator-economy vocabulary ("workflow behind") with nothing of WWD in it. Shape of fix: at least one title option should carry the show's identity (movies, reviews, two hosts) so the list is not three versions of the same anonymous tutorial.
- **A1 and A2 (lines 20 to 30).** Clean. A1's skip condition ("If most of you never edit anything, we will skip it") is a specific, honest choice. A2's "Your vote decides it" is a common poll phrase, and line 33 already flags the honesty issue. Not an originality finding.
- **B1, B2, C disclosure lines, D (lines 44, 50 to 51, 64, 71, 85, 91).** Boilerplate, and it should be. Disclosure language is meant to be plain and recognizable. Not a finding. Do not "freshen" these.
- **G, line 199.** Functional pointer line. Not a finding.

## The counter (strongest case for originality)

This is not a lift of any specific video. The structure is a universal tutorial shape, and disclosure copy is supposed to be plain. The draft has real things in it that the "1 long video" genre lacks: a human-review gate as the point of the video rather than an afterthought, a close that lands on "checked by a person" instead of "go viral", an approved-copy step no vendor tutorial would show (it slows the product down), a skeptical co-host who says the tool does not do the hard part, and a strict no-promises line. The poll copy is honest in a way creator polls rarely are. If the hook stops echoing the vendor tagline, the remaining issues are generic phrasing, not borrowing, and the package can ship.

## Fix list, in order
1. Replace the "one episode, N posts/shorts" construction in E line 106, line 108, line 164, line 170 fallback, F title 2 and F thumbnail. Same fix in every slot.
2. E line 133: make your score visibly yours, not Opus's virality score, and drop "elite".
3. E lines 143 to 144: Matt's line should be a reviewer's skepticism, not the general tool aphorism.
4. C1 and C2 questions, F title 3: tie to this show and audience.
5. Lower priority: one WWD example per rule (E 128 to 132), E 107 "exactly how", E 116 and E 147 phrasing.
