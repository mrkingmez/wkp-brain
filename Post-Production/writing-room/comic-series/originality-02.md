# Originality Pass 2: WWD Comic Series (draft-02, THE LAST WORD)

Agent: originality. Date: 2026-10-30. Read cold against draft-02.md, then checked against pass 1 (originality-01.md).
Mode on the source: GENERATE (room draft, not Zac's writing). Not declared parody, so the standard test applies.

---

## VERDICT: SHIPS WITH CHANGES

The rebuild fixes both pass 1 failures. The motive no longer reads as *Henry Danger*'s Spoiler or *The Simpsons*' Spoiler Boy, and with the screen gone, the head no longer reads as *Skibidi Toilet*'s TV Man. Three smaller things are left. All of them sit in the look and the staging, not the concept, and none of them needs a rebuild.

**ENDING ENCLOSED goes. Use FINAL WORD.** The reasoning is in the Envelope section below.

## BLUF

- **The motive passes.** "Seals shut whatever keeps people arguing" is a new job for a villain. I found no named villain whose goal is ending debate. The Henry Danger and Simpsons reads are gone, as long as the envelope stamp stops saying "ending" (see below).
- **The name passes.** I found no villain called The Last Word in any major work. The nearest names are Marvel's **The Word** (Jack Wordman, an obscure *She-Hulk* cult leader) and a separate DC **Word** from the *DC One Million* era. Both are obscure, and neither is a silencer or a courier. Nobody will reach for them.
- **The stamp head passes.** I found no existing character whose head is a wax-seal signet. The nearest are **Psychonauts' Censors**, suited bureaucrats who stamp out thoughts that do not belong, yelling "No!". They carry stamps as weapons and have normal heads. A gamer might feel the echo, but would not name it from this design. This is a note, not a finding.
- **The look leaks back toward The Question.** A long coat, a brimmed cap, a *blank* face, and a rain-soaked rooftop across the street from the heroes add up to **The Question (DC)**: a blank faceless mask, a fedora, a trench coat. Pass 1 named The Question for the rooftop staging, and draft-02 kept that staging and added a blank face. **Finding.** (Villain section "Look"; Slide 1 Panel 4.)
- **The ragged caption box stacks onto that.** A silent, faceless figure in a coat whose words appear in a rough-edged box is how comics readers know **Rorschach (*Watchmen*)**. Dave Gibbons lettered Rorschach's in-costume balloons with jagged edges. By itself the box would be a watch item. Combined with the coat and the rooftop, it is a second tell pointing the same way. **Finding, minor, tied to the look above.**
- **"Mouths" in the motive is a trap.** Nothing on the slides shows it, but if a later page ever shows a person's mouth sealing over, that is **Neo's mouth closing up in the interrogation scene of *The Matrix***, one of the most recognizable images a movie channel's audience knows. A silent, slow villain who takes the voices out of a town is also the **Gentlemen in *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* "Hush"**. Keep him sealing objects (doors, mics, envelopes), never people. **Watch item, not yet a finding.**

## Envelope: ENDING ENCLOSED goes, FINAL WORD stays

In pass 1 I called the envelope the freshest image in the draft. The envelope still is. **The words on it no longer are.** Draft-02 changed what the villain wants, but the stamp still says ENDING ENCLOSED. On a movie review channel, a cold reader takes "ending" to mean the movie's ending. That is exactly the spoiler read the rebuild exists to kill. KingZ's "Nobody open that" (Slide 1 Panel 3) makes it worse, because the panel plays like there is a spoiler inside. The draft's own note ("ending of the argument") does not reach the reader. Only the stamp does.

So the stamp pulls the reader back to *Henry Danger*'s Spoiler, which pass 1 failed. FINAL WORD fixes this. It matches the motive (one official answer, debate closed), it matches the name (THE LAST WORD), and it matches the draft's own description of what is inside ("a final word nobody ever reads"). I found no work that uses FINAL WORD as a villain's mark. It is a common phrase, not a title.

Decision: **ENDING ENCLOSED goes. FINAL WORD replaces it everywhere** (Slide 1 Panel 3, the villain bible, any future envelope).

## 1. Premise

**In generic terms:** the hosts of a review show, drawn as characters, defend their hangout from a recurring villain who wants to shut them up.
Works that fit: the Gentlemen in *Buffy* "Hush" (a town silenced by voice thieves), the *Nostalgia Critic* story arcs, and the host framing of *Mystery Science Theater 3000*. "A villain who wants silence" is a known idea in the horror genre.

**The specific choice that separates it:** this villain does not want silence for its own sake. He wants the *argument* closed with one official answer. That is the show's own line ("Tell the truth. Have the debate.") turned around into a plot. The Gentlemen take voices to harvest hearts. The Last Word seals things so a debate cannot go on. That difference is real, and it belongs to this channel. Passes.

## 2. Structure

Slide 1 follows the same shape as pass 1: the threat at the door, a closer look, the villain on a far rooftop, a line from the Oracle. That is a universal shape. Slide 3 ends on a threat escalating against the show's live night, which is a stock cliffhanger. No known sequence of reveals and no known twist is being reused. Passes.

The one structural carryover is **staging, not structure**: the villain-on-a-rooftop-across-the-street reveal (Slide 1 Panel 4) is the same shot pass 1 flagged. See Texture.

## 3. Character

- **Winter Wolf, KingZ, Oracle:** unchanged from pass 1. Real people's personas, plus a mentor type the art keeps clear of Zordon. Pass.
- **The Last Word:** half a person, half a type. The motive, the stamp head, the dripping wax, and the tired mail-route walk are his own. The coat-and-brim silhouette with a blank face is borrowed from The Question. The courier details (satchel, oilskin coat, cap) lightly recall **Kevin Costner's *The Postman*** and the porters of ***Death Stranding***. Both are heroes, though, and neither is drawn with a prop head, so no reader will name them. The fix is narrow: change what is *on* the face of the seal, and change where he stands.

## 4. Texture

- **Blank worn face pressed into the seal (Villain "Look"):** the blank face is the problem. A signet seal has a device on it by definition, and a blank one reads as a faceless mask. Give the seal face an engraved mark of his own, one that is ownable and not a real crest or logo, and The Question drops out. **Finding.**
- **Rooftop across the street, head tilted, hard rain (Slide 1 Panel 4):** the same noir rooftop pass 1 flagged (Rorschach, The Question). With the faceless coat it narrows to those two again. Put him somewhere a courier belongs: at street level, on the stoop, in the stairwell, walking away down the block. **Finding.**
- **Wax-white caption box with a ragged edge, "Thud." (Slide 1 Panel 4):** Rorschach's rough balloons. Give the box a shape that comes from wax (a seal, a drip, a pooled blob) rather than a torn or jagged edge. **Minor finding.**
- **Red wax creeping up the mic stand and setting over it (Slide 3 Panels 2 and 3):** no source found. The nearest is ***House of Wax*** (2005), where wax covers people. A sealed mic is far from that. Passes, as long as wax never covers a body.
- **Wax sealing the door's lock and seams (Slide 1 Panel 1):** no source found. Fresh. Passes.
- **Black envelope under the door (Slide 1 Panel 3):** the object passes. The stamp text fails (see Envelope).
- **Velvet rope versus bargain bin (Slide 2, Topic 3):** this is the channel's own "Theater or Straight to DVD" segment. Owned. Passes.
- **Headlines pinned to the wall with knives, tapes with blank spines (Slide 2):** stock props, already noted in pass 1. Pass.
- **"We break them down. Then we put them to the test." (Slide 2 footer):** generic, not lifted. Not an originality finding. It belongs to `no-ai-slop` and marketing.
- **"He's going for the mic, we're talking louder." / "We were always going to." (Slide 3 Panel 3):** I found no source. This is the line that carries the premise, and it belongs to this show. Passes.

## What has to change (shape of the fix, not the fix)

1. Stamp text: ENDING ENCLOSED becomes FINAL WORD, everywhere it appears.
2. Seal face: no longer blank. It gets an engraved mark of its own.
3. Slide 1 Panel 4: move him off the rain-soaked rooftop across the street to a vantage that fits a courier.
4. Caption box: change the ragged edge to a wax-derived shape.
5. Villain bible: strike "mouths" from the list of things he seals. He seals objects, never people. That keeps *The Matrix* and *Buffy* "Hush" off the table on every later page.

With 1 through 3 done, this passes without further review. 4 and 5 are cheap insurance.

---

## The counter: strongest case for originality

The rebuild did what pass 1 asked. It kept the parts the villain actually owned (the envelope, the silence, the rule that he never wins) and threw out the borrowed parts. The new motive is not a genre villain with a gimmick. It is the channel's own mission statement turned around and given a coat. No other villain exists to end a debate, and no other comic is about keeping one specific show's arguments alive. A cast-iron wax-seal signet for a head is a silhouette I could not match anywhere: not in comics, games, or animation. Hot wax that pools wherever he touches is a physical, drawable power that nobody else uses. The Slide 3 beat, where he seals the mic and the hosts just talk louder, is a payoff only this show could have, because the joke is the show itself. The leftover issues are a blank face, a rooftop, and a word on an envelope. Those are art direction and lettering choices, not concept problems. With them fixed, a reader watching cold would have nothing to name.
