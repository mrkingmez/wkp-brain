# Originality Pass 1: WWD Comic Series (draft-01)

Agent: originality. Date: 2026-10-30. Read cold against draft-01.md.
Mode on the source: GENERATE (room draft, not Zac's writing). Not declared parody, so the standard test applies.

---

## VERDICT: SHIPS WITH CHANGES

Concept A (THE DEN AFTER DARK) can move forward. The Leak cannot move forward in its current form. Concepts B and C are clear enough to stand as written.

## BLUF

- **The Leak fails on concept.** A villain whose whole job is revealing how stories end already exists, by name, twice. *Henry Danger* (Nickelodeon) has a villain called **the Spoiler** who finds out how movies and shows end and tells people before they can watch. *The Simpsons* episode "Bart the Bad Guy" (Season 31, Episode 14, 2020) makes Bart into "Spoiler Boy," a supervillain who threatens people with movie spoilers. A reader who has seen either one will name it, and on a movie review channel the audience is the most likely to have seen them. (Section 1, Concept A, "The engine"; Section 4, "The Leak".)
- **The Leak fails on look.** A faceless figure in a long dark coat with a screen where the head should be reads as **TV Man from *Skibidi Toilet*** (black trench coat, black gloves, screen for a head). That series is among the most-watched things on YouTube. The hooded long coat with no face, standing on a rainy rooftop, also reads as **Rorschach (*Watchmen*)** and **The Question (DC)**. The cracked screen points to the **cracked-screen title card of *Black Mirror***. That is four names a reader could reach for. (Page 1 Panel 5; Section 4.)
- **Two pieces of The Leak are original and worth keeping.** One is the black wax-sealed envelopes stamped ENDING ENCLOSED, which no source I can name uses. The other is the rule that The Leak never speaks and never wins. The prop works. The face and the motive are the copied parts.
- **Concept A's story world passes, but only just.** "The hosts of a review show live in a fictional hideout and fight a recurring villain between episodes" describes a known format (the story arcs of *Nostalgia Critic*, framing in the style of *Mystery Science Theater 3000*). That format belongs to the whole genre, though. The tie to WWD's own "teasers, not verdicts" rule is a specific choice. The trouble is that the choice is carried entirely by The Leak, so when The Leak changes, the hook has to survive the change.

## 1. Premise

**Concept A, in generic terms:** hosts of a review show, drawn as characters, guard their hangout from a recurring enemy, with weekly trouble pegged to the episode.
Works that fit that sentence: *Nostalgia Critic* anniversary and story arcs (Channel Awesome), *Mystery Science Theater 3000* (hosts trapped in a setting with recurring antagonists), *The Cryptkeeper* host segments in *Tales from the Crypt*, and the "mascot hosts in a lair" framing used by many channel webcomics. The list is long, so the premise on its own does no work.

**The specific choice that could separate it:** the villain is the channel's no-spoiler rule turned into a character (Section 2, second bullet). That is a real idea. The problem is how it is carried out. "Villain who spoils endings" is the *Henry Danger* Spoiler and the *Simpsons* Spoiler Boy, so the one choice meant to make A stand apart lands on existing work.

**Concept B, in generic terms:** one host builds games for the other and the audience predicts the result. This fits game-show bits in general (*Hot Ones* escalation, stunt segments in the style of *Impractical Jokers*), but no single work. Junk-built machines plus a result paid off live on the show is specific enough. Passes.

**Concept C, in generic terms:** a channel publishes fake pulp covers plus a letters page. Fake covers are a common promo format and the letters page is a standard comics convention. That is genre furniture, so no single title gets named. It passes as homage to a format, which is fine because it is not copying a work.

## 2. Structure

The weekly shape (trouble arrives Monday, show-night promo Wednesday, aftermath gag and cliffhanger on the weekend) is monster-of-the-week plus a stinger. That is a universal skeleton and it is fine.

Page 1 runs threat at the door, closer look at the threat, villain revealed on the far rooftop, cryptic mentor line. Page 3 runs a calm aftermath, the threat foiled, the villain pulls the next weapon from his stack, "Next week, he tries again." That is the standard Saturday-morning cartoon closer (the villain retreating and promising to return). It is too generic to tie to one work. It is a pattern, not a lift.

No known twist or reveal sequence is being reused. Passes.

## 3. Character

- **Winter Wolf (Section 4):** stoic, short lines, gets laughs from being dry. This is the straight man type. He is based on Matt's real on-air persona, which makes him a person and not a borrowed type. Passes.
- **KingZ (Section 4):** warm, chatty, slightly wrong, always holding something. This is the funny half of a double act. Same reasoning as Wolf: he comes from a real host. Passes.
- **Oracle (Section 4, Page 1 Panel 6):** a disembodied voice in a glowing circle that gives sideways advice. This is the oracle or mentor type. The nearest named match is **Zordon** (*Power Rangers*), a disembodied mentor in a glowing frame, but the circle look is far enough from him that a reader is unlikely to name it. A type, not a lift. Watch the "Is that a no or a not yet?" / "Yes." exchange (Page 1 Panel 6). Answering "Yes" to an either/or question is a stock gag found across many works. I cannot pin it to one source, so this is not a finding, just a note that the line is worn.
- **The Leak:** a type wearing two existing costumes. The concept is the *Henry Danger* Spoiler. The silhouette is *Skibidi Toilet*'s TV Man by way of Rorschach. **This is the one character that is a borrowed archetype under a new name.**

## 4. Texture

- **Phone-screen face with cold glow (Page 1 Panel 5, Page 2 corner insert, Page 3 Panel 5, Section 4):** reads as TV Man (*Skibidi Toilet*). The "cracked" detail adds the *Black Mirror* title card. **Finding.**
- **Faceless hooded figure in a long coat on a rain-soaked rooftop, looking across at the heroes (Page 1 Panel 5, Page 3 Panel 5):** noir rooftop staging familiar from *Watchmen* (Rorschach) and *Batman* comics generally. The pose alone would be stock. Combined with the faceless head, it narrows to Rorschach and The Question. **Finding, tied to the look above.**
- **Caption "He's back." (Page 1 Panel 5):** a stock horror-sequel tagline. Not tied to one title. Worn, not lifted.
- **"Next week, he tries again." (Page 3 Panel 5):** stock cartoon closer. Worn, not lifted.
- **Sealed black envelope with wax seal, ENDING ENCLOSED (Page 1 Panels 1 and 3, Page 3 Panel 4):** the only near echo is the Best Picture envelope mix-up at the 2017 Oscars, which is a real event rather than a work, and the read is loose. **Not a finding. This is the freshest image in the draft.**
- **Envelope sitting caged in the glowing ritual circle (Page 2 opener row 2, Page 3 Panel 4):** no source found. Passes.
- **Den Trials handheld screen, PRESS START (Page 2):** generic game UI language. Passes.
- **Newspapers pinned to the wall with knives (Page 2, Topic 4):** stock "obsessed investigator" wall image. Not traceable to one work. Passes.
- **"Nobody spoiled nothing" / grammar correction (Page 3 Panel 4):** a stock bit, not lifted. The room already flagged it as possible filler.

## What has to change (shape of the fix, not the fix)

1. **The Leak's motive** has to be something other than "tells people how it ends." The envelope can still hold the ending. What needs to change is what the villain *wants* with it, so that "a villain who spoils movies" is no longer the one-line description of him. Until that changes, a reader names *Henry Danger*.
2. **The Leak's head** has to stop being a screen. Any flat glowing display on a faceless figure in a long coat lands on TV Man. Dropping the coat and keeping the screen would not fix it. Neither would keeping the coat and swapping in a different screen. The fix is a different silhouette built around the envelope prop, which is the part he actually owns.
3. **The name "The Leak"** is clear on its own (no known villain by that name in a major work that I could find), but it points straight at "spoiler." Re-check it once the motive changes.
4. Run this check again on pass 2 before the look is locked. The draft's own room note (Room notes, last bullet) already asked for this.

## The counter: strongest case for originality

The draft has a real idea in it. Nobody else's comic exists to protect *this* channel's house rule, and the parallel between the plot and the show (the Den guards the ending in the story, the hosts refuse verdicts on air) is structural, not decorative. The Leak never speaks and never wins, and no envelope ever opens on the page. That cuts against the *Henry Danger* and *Simpsons* versions, where the spoiling actually happens and drives the comedy. The sealed envelope caged in a ritual circle is a fresh and ownable image. Concepts B and C borrow formats, not works, which the standard allows. The cast is drawn from real people's personas, not from types. If the villain's motive and silhouette are rebuilt around the envelope, Concept A is distinct enough to ship.

The case does not cover The Leak as written. A reader who knows the *Henry Danger* episode or *Skibidi Toilet* can name what it copies.
