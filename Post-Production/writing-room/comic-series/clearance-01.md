## Judgment calls

### 1. Vision Quest as a story prop (Page 1, Panel 3, line 96; Page 1 caption, line 110; Page 2 Topic 1, lines 137 to 139)
- On Page 2 the title is a plain-text topic mention. That is the normal, low-exposure use: naming a show you are about to discuss.
- Page 1 Panel 3 is different. The title is stamped on the villain's envelope labeled ENDING ENCLOSED. A Marvel/Disney+ property becomes an object in WWD's story, held by WWD's villain. It is not drawn as the property, and that helps. But a plain-text name used as a plot device is a different use from naming a topic, and Zac should know that before the format locks.
- Concept A's engine (line 32) does this every week: "A review week puts that movie's title on the envelope." So this is a standing feature of the series, not a one-off.
- False light read: the envelope suggests a spoiler threat, not anything about the show itself. It does not read as disparaging Marvel or Disney+.

### 2. Netflix attribution (topics line, line 81; Page 2 Topic 3, lines 146 to 149; Page 3 Panel 2, line 187)
- Line 81 says "Netflix's 'Theater or Straight to DVD' list". According to TASKS.md the segment is WWD's own list of Netflix originals. The possessive says Netflix made or endorsed it. Line 81 is a room note, not lettering, but if that phrasing reaches a caption it is a false-attribution and endorsement problem.
- The lettered caption on line 149 ("Netflix originals, sorted.") does not have that problem.
- Topic 3 art puts Netflix originals next to a "dented bargain bin" and a DVD BIN stack (lines 148, 187). That is opinion framed as a question, which is the normal territory for commentary. Netflix is the subject of the panel there, not just a name in it. No Netflix red, no N mark, no Netflix type anywhere near it.

### 3. Real people behind the personas (whole draft)
- **Matt (Winter Wolf).** A living person whose public persona speaks new, invented lines (for example line 193, "Then go listen to yourself lose," and line 198). This is the co-owner of the brand, so the risk is consent and the partnership, not defamation. Nothing should post without Matt signing off on how his persona talks on the page. The draft already routes this to Zac and Matt (line 286). The flag is that the sign-off matters for rights, not only for taste.
- **Oracle.** A real third person. The draft keeps Oracle faceless with no name (lines 228 to 232), which is the low-exposure version. It still gives a real person's persona invented lines (lines 107, 109, 196). That person's consent to being on the page at all is open (line 290, question 2).
- **Zac (KingZ).** He is the client, so not an exposure for him, but he is still a real person being portrayed.

### 4. Character ownership: wolf and crown designs (style bible, lines 214 to 236)
- Winter Wolf (wolf, black hood, fur collar, blue eyes) and KingZ (mask, crown cap, red eyes, dripping KINGZ hoodie) are WWD's hosts. WWD is the one partnership in WKP. The draft is filed under Post-Production\writing-room, which is a solely WKP venture.
- The Leak, Den Trials, and the sealed envelope bit are new characters and properties created in this draft. Nothing in writing says whether they belong to WKP, to Zac, or to the WWD partnership. If the series runs, those are recurring IP, and that question gets harder to fix later.
- If the art is AI generated (line 291), how far character designs can be protected is unsettled. That affects how well WWD could stop someone else copying The Leak or the host designs. Attorney question.
- Text-only resemblance check: nothing in the written descriptions names or describes an existing rights holder's character. A masked figure with a crown and red eyes, and a hooded wolf, are common archetypes. The risk is what the image generator produces, not what the text says.

### 5. The Leak's look (lines 25, 103, 234 to 236; room note line 302)
- A screen or monitor in place of a face is a common trope with several well-known existing uses. The room already flagged it for originality (line 302). From the clearance side: once there is art, it has to be checked against existing screen-headed characters before the look locks, because a recognizable match would move this toward the "named character used as owned" STOP line.
- A "cracked phone screen" face invites a recognizable phone silhouette or brand (camera bump layout, logo). Keep it generic.
- The name "The Leak" has not been checked for existing comic or entertainment uses or trademarks. Not checked in this pass.

### 6. Den Trials handheld console (Page 2, lines 131, 157 to 161; Concept B lines 41 to 43)
- "A battered handheld game console" with PRESS START on a pixel screen. If the art draws a recognizable Nintendo Game Boy or similar shape, that is trade dress used as a story prop. Keep it generic. PRESS START, LEVEL, GAME OVER, and CONTINUES are generic game language. Low exposure.
- Concept B's hubcap prize wheel (line 41). A spinning prize wheel is generic. A specific game show's wheel layout is not.

### 7. Lines that echo existing works
None of these is verbatim from a specific work. Each is a common phrase. They are listed so the originality pass can decide:
- "He's back." (line 104). A stock villain-return line, common in many franchises.
- "IT CAME BACK" with a clawed thing reaching out of a VHS case (lines 142 to 143). It echoes the "It Came From..." horror title pattern. The title is generic. The image is not tied to a specific work as written.
- "Is that a 'no' or a 'not yet'?" / "Yes." (lines 108 to 109). An old joke structure, not traceable to one source.
- "ONE PACK. ONE DEN." (line 177). Echoes common "one X, one Y" slogans. Not traceable to one owner.
- "We don't just talk about the stories. We break them down." / "Then we put them to the test." / "Tell the truth. Have the debate. Welcome to the Den." (lines 130, 132, 175). These seem to come from the EP094 page, which is WWD's own. Not verified against that page in this pass.

### 8. Real commenters on the letters page (Concept C, line 56)
- The draft says "with permission or handle only." Handle-only does not mean permission. Reprinting a commenter's words and handle in a published image, with an in-character reply, uses that person's identity and their text. Platform terms may not cover reuse inside WWD's own artwork. Concept C is not the room's pick, but if any letters-page bit comes back, get permission per comment.

### 9. Headlines panel and real outlets (Page 2 Topic 4, lines 151 to 154)
- The headlines are blurred, which is good. Flag for the art: no recognizable newspaper mastheads.
