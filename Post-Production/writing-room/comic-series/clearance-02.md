## Judgment calls

### 1. The name THE LAST WORD (line 13; Slide 1 Panel 4, line 24)
- "The Last Word" is a common English phrase. It is also the title of many existing works. A Wikipedia disambiguation page lists films, books, and TV shows under that title, including a 2017 film and a Damon Knight short story. It is also the name of a long-running MSNBC news program. That last one matters most for a show that talks about media and news.
- Quick search found no existing comic book villain by that exact name. The nearest is a Marvel She-Hulk villain called "The Word," and a DC Villains Secret Files character also called The Word. That is a different name, but it is a word-themed villain in the two biggest comics catalogs. The originality pass should know.
- Titles of single works generally are not protected by copyright. Exposure on a name is a trademark question, and that depends on whether anyone holds a registration for comics, entertainment, or online content under that name, and on whether WWD's use could cause confusion. **No trademark search was run in this pass.** That is an open item, not a pass.
- The draft keeps the name inside his caption box only (line 13) and never as a series title or logo. That is the lower-exposure version. If THE LAST WORD ever becomes a series name, merch, a logo, or a Reel title, the trademark question gets more serious.

### 2. ENDING ENCLOSED stamp (lines 10, 21) and fallback FINAL WORD (line 10)
- Both are plain descriptive phrases. As text on a prop envelope, exposure is low. Not checked against existing marks or titles in this pass.
- FINAL WORD as a fallback overlaps with THE LAST WORD in meaning. Not a clearance problem, just noted.
- Art flag: a black envelope with a red wax seal is a stock image. If the generator produces a seal with a recognizable crest, monogram, or brand mark (a postal service eagle, a luxury brand monogram), that becomes a trademark problem on a prop. Keep the seal face blank, as written (line 11).

### 3. Weekly topic slots and show names as plain text (Slide 2, lines 30 to 38; Slide 1 header line 17)
- The draft's rule (line 38): show and service names only as plain lettering in a title box, never in logo style, never as drawn characters. That is the low-exposure way to name a title. Plain-text naming of the show you are about to discuss is normal commentary territory.
- What this draft cannot clear by itself: every week brings new titles and new subjects from Matt's Tuesday list. Some weeks a topic is about a living person. EP118's list had "Gunn vs critics" and this week's work includes a Patrick Stewart tribute. A topic question about a real, named, living person on a lettered slide is a defamation and false light exposure separate from copyright, even when phrased as a question. A question can still imply a false fact. **Each week's filled Slide 2 needs its own read before it posts.**
- Topic 3 art (line 35), KingZ between a velvet rope and a bargain bin. Same note as pass 1: if the topic names a specific studio or streamer, that company becomes the subject of a panel framed as "theater or bargain bin." Opinion framed as a question is normal commentary. No brand colors or marks near it.
- Topic 4 art (line 36), headlines pinned with knives, unreadable. Good. Art flag: no recognizable newspaper or site mastheads, fonts, or layouts. Knives through a real outlet's masthead would read as hostility toward that outlet.
- Topic 2 art (line 34), blank-spined tapes. Keep spines blank. No recognizable rental store branding (blue and yellow ticket shapes, for example).
- FROSTCAST 119 header and the date (lines 17, 31, 51) are WWD's own marks. No exposure. Not checked by clearance: whether EP119 is actually Wed 11.04. Fact-research should confirm before lettering.
