## 1. Three series concepts

### Concept A: THE DEN AFTER DARK (story world)

**Premise:** The Den is a hideout above a rain-soaked city, and every week something arrives at the door that the three hosts have to deal with before the show goes live.

**The engine:** A recurring villain called The Leak. Faceless, a cracked phone screen where the face should be, always lit by that cold screen glow, carrying a stack of sealed envelopes. Each envelope holds an ending. The Leak wants to open them in public. The Den's whole job is to keep the story alive until the audience gets to see it for themselves. Some weeks it is The Leak. Some weeks it is a smaller trouble (the power dies before show night, a mic goes missing, the ritual circle won't light, KingZ's new Den Trials machine catches fire).

**A week's 3 pages:**
- **Monday (story page):** The week's trouble shows up, tied to the biggest topic or the week's review. Ends on a question.
- **Wednesday (show-night page):** The EP094 layout. Header, opener rows, numbered hunt list, Den Trials, PLUS, FROSTCAST logo, live tonight, footer. The story beat shows up in one corner (a sealed envelope on the table, a shadow at the window), so story readers get a crumb and new readers get a clean promo.
- **Weekend (story page):** Aftermath. A gag from the episode's topics, no verdicts, plus one closing panel that sets up next week. Points to the episode for the actual takes.

**Tie to the show:** The trouble always comes from that week's slate. A finale week brings an envelope. A sequels week brings something that "comes back." A review week puts that movie's title on the envelope as plain text.

---

### Concept B: DEN TRIALS (game show strip)

**Premise:** Every week KingZ builds Winter Wolf a new game out of junk, the audience plays along in the comments, and the result is only revealed live on FrostCast.

**A week's 3 pages:**
- **Monday:** LEVEL 1. KingZ unveils this week's machine (a cardboard arcade cabinet, a wheel built from a hubcap, a ballot box made of VHS cases). States the rules. Asks the chat to predict whether Wolf survives.
- **Wednesday:** LEVEL 2, show-night layout. Hunt list plus "the final level is played LIVE tonight."
- **Weekend:** GAME OVER screen. Who won is told only as score-card style ("WOLF: SURVIVED LEVEL 3" or "KINGZ: OUT OF CONTINUES"), never what anyone said about a movie. Leaderboard shoutout to commenters who called it.

**Tie to the show:** Every game is built on that week's topics, and the final level is a real live segment on FrostCast. This one needs the hosts to actually play it on air, so it changes the show, not just the posts.

---

### Concept C: CASE FILES FROM THE DEN (one-splash covers plus a letters page)

**Premise:** WWD publishes its own fake pulp comic line. Each week gets three single-image "issue covers," each one a bold splash for one topic, with a cover line that asks a question.

**A week's 3 pages:**
- **Monday:** Cover for the week's review ("ISSUE 119-A"), hosts in a staged pulp pose around a prop tied to the movie, title as plain text only.
- **Wednesday:** Cover for FrostCast ("ISSUE 119"), the hunt list running down the left edge like cover blurbs, LIVE TONIGHT starburst.
- **Weekend:** **THE LETTERS PAGE.** Real chat comments from the week (with permission or handle only) answered in character by the hosts, typewriter style, one panel of the hosts reading mail at the Den table.

**Tie to the show:** The letters page is built entirely from the week's chat, so it rewards people who showed up live.

## 2. My pick and why

**Pick: Concept A, THE DEN AFTER DARK, with Den Trials (Concept B) folded in as its recurring Wednesday bit.**

Why:
- It is the only one that gives people a reason to come back for the next page that isn't the show itself. A promo page gets scrolled past. A cliffhanger gets checked on.
- The Leak is the channel's rule made into a villain. Every week the Den's job in the story is to protect the ending, which is the same thing the hosts do on air. That is a hook nobody else's comic has, because it comes from WWD's own house rule.
- The Wednesday page keeps the EP094 template Zac already built, so the hard part of the format is already proven.
- It gives Oracle a place to exist without a face.

**Weakest point of each:**
- **A (the pick):** Continuity load. Someone has to track the plot week to week, keep three characters looking the same across every page, and keep the story from eating the promo. People who show up mid-arc won't know what the envelope is. Fix: every story page must make sense on its own, with only one plot thread running at a time, and the plot never takes more than one corner of the Wednesday page.
- **B:** Burns out. A new game every week is a big idea tax, and the pages are only as good as the game. It also asks the hosts to change the live show, and that is a format change that needs Matt. If a week's game is weak, all three pages are weak.
- **C:** No reason to come back. Covers are fast to make and look sharp, but each one is a one-off. And a pulp cover built around a movie prop is the format most likely to drift toward drawing the movie's actual characters or key art, which is the one thing that cannot appear.

## 4. Short style bible

### The hosts on the page

**Winter Wolf**
- Look: wolf, black hooded jacket, WINTER WOLF across the back, fur collar, blue eyes. The only cold blue in the frame besides Oracle's glow.
- Body language: still. Arms crossed, hand flat on things, leaning back. Moves last and least.
- Talks: short. Two to six words is home. Questions are blunt. Never jokes on purpose, gets laughs from being dry.
- Page rule: he is the verdict-first host on the show, but on the page his sharpness comes out as impatience and attitude, never as a take on a movie. He never says what he thinks of anything that's on the slate.

**KingZ**
- Look: masked, crown cap, red eyes, KINGZ hoodie with dripping red lettering. Always holding something (a mug, a game, a DVD case).
- Body language: open. Arms out, leaning toward the reader, pointing at things.
- Talks: warm, chatty, uses "we" and "Pack," talks to the reader directly. Longer bubbles than Wolf, but still one thought per bubble. Gets things slightly wrong and keeps going.
- Page rule: KingZ frames the question and hands it to the audience. He never answers it.

**Oracle (PROPOSAL, needs Zac and Matt)**
- Look: no body, no face. Oracle is the ritual circle on the Den table. When Oracle speaks, the carved lines glow.
- Balloons: round, no tail, a different typeface from the other two (a thin, clean serif), pale cold-white text on deep violet.
- Talks: rarely. One or two lines a page, max. Answers sideways. Usually the shortest line on the page.
- Never: Oracle's real name, a face, a silhouette that suggests a real person.

**The Leak (recurring villain)**
- Look: long coat, hood, a cracked phone screen where the face is, cold screen glow on everything near him, stack of sealed envelopes. Never speaks. His captions are cold white boxes with ragged edges.
- Rule: The Leak never wins. No envelope ever opens on the page.

### Recurring bits
- **Den Trials** (Wednesday page): KingZ's game for Wolf, tied to a topic. Screen reads DEN TRIALS / LEVEL # / PRESS START. The page only sets up the game. The result happens live.
- **The sealed envelope**: the week's ending lives in it. It stays sealed. Always.
- **The corner crumb**: one small wordless story panel on the Wednesday page.
- **"It's in the episode. Not here."**: the weekend page always sends people to the show for the takes.

### Lettering rules
- Bubbles: 12 words or fewer is the target, 20 is the hard cap.
- Story pages: 75 words max per page, all text combined.
- Show-night page: topic captions are always a question or a stake, one or two sentences.
- No em dashes. Ever. Use a period or a comma.
- Caps lettering for title boxes, header boxes, and the footer. Sentence case inside bubbles.
- Real show titles and service names: plain lettering in caption or title boxes only. Never in a logo style, never imitating a studio or streamer's own type.

### Color rules
- Base palette: black and blood red, from the EP094 page.
- Accents are owned by characters: cold blue is Wolf's eyes, red glow is KingZ, deep violet and cold white is Oracle, phone-screen white-blue is The Leak.
- Torch amber is the Den's warmth. Use it inside the Den only. The city outside stays red, black, and rain-gray.

### What never appears
- Any other company's characters drawn, posed, or silhouetted in a recognizable way.
- Any other company's logo, title treatment, or key art.
- Oracle's real name.
- Em dashes.
- A host's verdict, rating, or opinion on any movie or show on the slate.
- Quotes from any film or show. Song lyrics of any length.

### Page spec
- Instagram: 4:5, 1080 x 1350 px. Post the week's three pages as separate posts on their days, not one carousel, so each day has a fresh post.
- Facebook: same 4:5 file works. Same posts, same days.
- Safe zone: keep all text at least 60 px from every edge so the app frame doesn't crop it.
- Optional later: a 9:16 version of the Wednesday page for Stories, with the hunt list stacked.

### Post caption copy rules
- Caption under the image is short: the hook plus one question for the comments. No verdicts.
- Every caption goes through marketing-director (copywriting, social-media) and no-ai-slop before Zac posts. Agents draft, Zac posts.
- No hashtag walls. Use the channel's existing set.

### Posting rhythm
- Monday: story page (the trouble arrives).
- Wednesday, show day: show-night page. Earlier in the day so it does its job before 9PM ET.
- Saturday: story page (aftermath, cliffhanger, send people to the episode).
- Times follow the existing stagger convention for the platforms. Final times are marketing's call.

## 5. Open questions for Zac and Matt

Format and brand changes are a joint call.

1. **Is a running story with a villain on brand for WWD?** This is the big one, and it should go to devils-advocate first ("off brand?" and "still a good idea?").
2. **Oracle's look.** Is a glowing circle with no body okay, and does Oracle want to be on the page at all?
3. **The Leak.** Keep the name and look? Does a recurring villain feel like the Den, or like a different show?
4. **Who makes the art, and how?** If the art is AI generated like the EP094 page, keeping Wolf, KingZ, and The Leak looking the same every week is the hardest part of this whole series. Is there a reference sheet, and whose credits pay for it?
5. **Is three pages a week sustainable** next to the review schedule, or should the series start at one page (Wednesday only) and earn the story pages later?
6. **Den Trials on air.** If the page sets up a game, the hosts have to play it live. Is Matt in for that every week?
7. **Show and service names on the page.** Plain-text mentions of shows (Vision Quest) and services (Netflix) are flagged as exposure. Clearance should look at it before anything posts.
8. **Time zone line.** EP094 said EDT. From Nov 1, 2026 the show is EST. Letter it "9PM ET" going forward, or switch to EST each winter?
