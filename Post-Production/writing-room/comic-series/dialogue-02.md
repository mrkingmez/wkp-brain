# Dialogue pass 02: THE DEN AFTER DARK, draft-02

Mode: GENERATE (room draft). Suggested lines below are proposals, marked PROPOSAL.

## BLUF

Mostly holds. The bubbles are short and phone-readable (none over 12 words), and Winter Wolf is right: dry, clipped, verdict-first. The problem is KingZ. He gives orders and calls the play ("Nobody open that." "He's going for the mic, we're talking louder."), which is Wolf's job. He never once wonders out loud or hands a question to the reader, so cover the names and the two blur. No movie verdicts anywhere on the pages.

Worst offender: Slide 1 Panel 4, KINGZ "Tonight? We go live at nine." Both of them know when the show airs. It's there only to sell the time slot, and the footer card on Slide 3 already does that.

## Slide 1 "WAX"

**P2, KINGZ: "Okay, so who locked us out of our own Den?"**
Problem: "our own Den" tells the reader who owns the place. Nobody says "our own" about their front door. It's also a straight, sensible question, with none of KingZ's chat or his habit of getting things slightly wrong.
Reaching for: confusion, plus a first guess at a suspect.
PROPOSAL: KINGZ: "Okay, who's mad at us? Is this the landlord?"

**P3, KINGZ: "Nobody open that." / WINTER WOLF: "Wasn't going to."**
Problem: Wolf's reply works. KingZ giving a flat command is the issue, because that is Wolf's register. KingZ should be the one who is tempted.
Reaching for: the envelope as forbidden.
PROPOSAL: KINGZ: "Don't open it. ...Should we open it?" / WINTER WOLF: "No."

**P4, ORACLE: "He wants this room quiet."**
Problem: this is the villain's logline, said plainly. It's exposition, and it isn't sideways. It also runs longer than Wolf's lines on the same page, which breaks the rule that Oracle gets the shortest line. How would she know his motive in week 1?
Reaching for: a cold read on the stranger across the street.
PROPOSAL: ORACLE: "Mail's early."
(It points at his courier look and leaves the motive for later weeks, where the caption box can carry it.)

**P4, KINGZ: "Tonight? We go live at nine."**
Problem: worst on the page (see BLUF). The hosts are telling each other their own schedule.
Reaching for: stakes, because this is happening on show night.
PROPOSAL: KINGZ: "He picked a Wednesday? Bold."

## Slide 2 "TONIGHT'S HUNT LIST"

**Footer, CAPTION: "We break them down. Then we put them to the test."**
Problem: this is a promo line with a built-in rhythm. It could sit in any podcast's copy unchanged and would fail no-ai-slop. It also doesn't hand anything to the reader.
Reaching for: the turn into the game on Slide 3.
PROPOSAL (as a KingZ caption, so the host is framing it): "Pick one. We'll fight about it at nine."

**Topic captions (placeholders)**
Note for whoever fills these from Matt's list: write each one as a KingZ question to the reader, 12 words or fewer, with no lean either way. "Is X worth the wait?" is fine. "X finally fixes Y, right?" is a verdict dressed up as a question. Kill those.

## Slide 3 "DEN TRIALS"

**P1, KINGZ: "Ten seconds. Name as many as you can. No repeats."**
Problem: three tidy clauses, read like a rulebook. It's fine for a host, but it's too clean for KingZ.
Reaching for: the game setup.
PROPOSAL: KINGZ: "Ten seconds, name as many as you... no repeats!"

**P2, ORACLE: "Pack." / WINTER WOLF: "Mic's going."**
Problem: "Pack." doesn't land. It could mean pack up, wolf pack, or a category answer. Then Wolf narrates exactly what the panel shows, so two lines do one job.
Reaching for: Oracle noticing the wax first.
PROPOSAL: ORACLE: "Look down." / WINTER WOLF: (cut, or) "Huh."

**P3, KINGZ: "He's going for the mic, we're talking louder."**
Problem: a comma splice that nobody performs. He narrates the art, and the wax has already set in this panel, so "going for" comes too late. It's also the theme read out loud, and a decision, which makes it Wolf's beat again.
Reaching for: defiance, with KingZ's warmth.
PROPOSAL: KINGZ: "Okay. So we just... talk louder?"

**P3, WINTER WOLF: "We were always going to."**
Problem: the right sentiment, but it reads as a trailer button. Wolf would say it with fewer words.
Reaching for: the dry closer.
PROPOSAL: WINTER WOLF: "Always do."

## Voice check (names covered)

- Winter Wolf: his lines read as his ("Still warm." "Wasn't going to." "Clock's mine."). He is distinct.
- KingZ: as drafted, he collapses into Wolf. Every line is a statement or an order. The proposals above give him questions, trailing off, and one wrong guess.
- Oracle: should be the shortest voice on the page and never be the one explaining. "Mail's early." and "Look down." fix both problems.

## Reel caption

"What's the one topic you're coming for?" It hands the question to the reader, so it works. No flags.
