# Dialogue notes: draft-01, Den Files "Fire in the Sky" teaser

Bench: dialogue-doctor. Pass 1. Mode: GENERATE (room-written draft, so rewrites below are allowed and every one is marked PROPOSAL). Date: 2026-11-22.
Read: draft-01.md, brief.md, `memory\agents\briefs\script-room-craft-brief.md` (S1 Mazin quotes used: anchor, micro reversals, repeated thoughts), WRITING-ROOM.md, pass bar `SOP\leveling\drills\script-room-drill-1.md`.
Line numbers are draft-01.md file lines.

## BLUF

Not ready. The middle of the teaser (polygraph block, lines 55 to 68) plays like two real co-hosts. The open and the close do not. Three problems hold it back. (1) In beats 2 to 4 the voices merge: Zac's narration is as clipped as Matt's, so with the names covered you cannot tell who is talking. That puts pass bar criterion 3 at risk. (2) Two thoughts get said twice. (3) The turn (beat 7) and the button (lines 95 and 100) sound written, not spoken.

**Worst offender:** line 86, Zac: "You've read all of it. You never make me wait." Both men already know Matt read the file and that Matt always gives his verdict first. Zac is telling Matt about Matt so the audience learns it. That is the "as you know" tell, and it sits at the hinge of the turn. Line 95 is a close second (see beat 7).

## Check 1: anchor on every line (S1, Mazin: "what is the anchor of this thought or piece of dialogue?")

Most lines anchor to the line before them. Lines 30, 41, 44, 52, 62, 65 and 92 each answer the line in front of them, and they are the best lines on the page. Four don't:

- **Line 26, Matt, "Light over the trees. Walton gets out and walks right at it."** It ignores Zac's line 23 ("Let me get them in the truck first"). Matt running over Zac is in character, but nobody reacts to it. On line 30 Zac just follows along ("Of course he does"), so the pace fight the brief promises gets one exchange and then disappears. The line is reaching for Matt bulldozing. On the page it reads as Matt taking over the narration.
  PROPOSAL: have Zac lose the fight out loud for a second, something like "Fine. Skip the truck." before or after Matt's line. That way the reversal registers and line 100 has something to pay off.
- **Line 71, Zac, "Then in ninety-three Hollywood makes the movie, and the movie..."** No anchor. Matt just shut him down. Zac's next move would be to react to that, but instead he says "Then", as if he's reading the next card. The brief also gives the movie to Matt ("Matt keeps skipping ahead to the light, the polygraphs and the movie"). Here Zac is the one skipping ahead, which flips the tracks the brief set up (S1, Mazin, "multi-track narratives").
  PROPOSAL: give the movie to Matt, and let Zac's anchor be a reaction to getting cut off.
- **Line 86, Zac, "Okay."** "Okay" just resets the scene, and everything after it is exposition (see the worst offender above).
- **Line 100, Zac, "Get in the truck."** The anchor is supposed to be line 23. But line 23 was "Let me get *them* in the truck." Line 100 tells the viewer to get in the truck, and that truck is the one that left Walton behind. The callback works on the brief, not on the ear. Nobody watching will hear it as a callback, and the literal meaning is a little off.

## Check 2: repeated thoughts (S1, Mazin: "if you have any sense that thoughts or lines are vaguely repeating, that's a writing problem for sure.")

- **Lines 11 and 20.** Matt: "Six men drove out of that forest without their friend." Then Matt again: "Six coming home." Both say the same thing (seven went in, six came out). The brief calls the second one a micro reversal ("seven guys" to "six coming home"). It isn't, because line 11 already told us. The viewer learns nothing new at line 20.
  PROPOSAL: keep the hook. Have Matt's line 20 interruption jump ahead to something the viewer doesn't know yet (the light, or the sheriff), so the pull-back on line 23 actually costs something.
- **Lines 75 and 79 to 82.** Matt: "Rewrites the part everybody remembers." Zac: "So the thing you picture when somebody says Travis Walton?" Matt: "Probably isn't what he says happened." That's one thought said twice. The draft gives it away itself: the fact tag on line 83 reads "same as above." Three lines cover a single beat in a 90 s piece.
  PROPOSAL: cut it to one exchange. Either keep line 75, or keep lines 79 and 82, not both.
- **"Six" as a word.** It shows up on lines 11, 20 and 48 ("those six guys"), and twice on line 95. The number is the spine, so some of that is fine. But line 95 reads as a fourth and fifth restating rather than a payoff. Cutting line 20 helps.

## Check 3: Matt and Zac with names covered (pass bar criterion 3)

The brief sets the voices up well: Matt short, clipped, events only, no feelings, and Zac longer, second person, names what he feels. The draft follows that in some places and drops it in others.

**Where you can tell them apart:** line 52 (Zac: "Your buddy is missing, and the only story you've got sounds crazy.") is clearly Zac, second person and warm. Lines 62 and 68 (Matt: "Nope." / "You want the results, you watch the episode.") are clearly Matt. Line 65 (Zac: "He does this.") is a real co-host aside and works.

**Where they collapse:**
- **Line 16, Zac:** "November, 1975. Arizona. A tree-thinning crew, end of a long day. One truck, seven guys." Five clipped fragments. That's Matt's rhythm from the brief, or a narrator's slate. It isn't Zac's. Put it next to Matt's line 33 ("Something hits him. They floor it.") with the names covered and nobody could say which man said which. "Tree-thinning crew" is also page vocabulary. Out loud a person says "logging crew" or "they were cutting trees."
  PROPOSAL: give Zac the setup in his own register, one longer line that puts the viewer in the truck (second person, end of the day, tired, one ride home). Leave the date-and-place slate to Matt, or to an on-screen card.
- **Line 44, Zac:** "Not that night. Not the next morning. Five days." Same clipped Matt rhythm, plus the three-beat build (see Check 5).
- **Line 89, Matt:** "Here's what I can't get past." That's Zac's feeling language. It nearly echoes Zac's line 37 ("the part I can't shake"), and the brief says Matt "never states feelings." Covered, it reads as Zac. It's also a repeated thought across the two men: both have a thing they "can't" let go of.

Result on criterion 3 for this draft: beats 2 to 4 fail the cover test, while beats 5, 6 and 8 pass it. Fix lines 16, 44 and 89 and it passes.

## Check 4: exposition both men already know

- **Line 86, Zac:** "You've read all of it. You never make me wait." The worst offender, explained above. Matt knows he read it. Matt knows his own habit. The line is there to brief the audience on Matt's character so the refusal lands. What it's reaching for is the dare: Zac pushing Matt to break his own rule in front of the camera. A real co-host would just push, without describing the rule.
  PROPOSAL: keep "Did it happen or not?" and drop the two setup sentences, or swap them for something with no information in it, like a nudge or Matt's name and a pause. If the audience needs to know Matt usually gives a verdict, the episode or a returning viewer supplies it.
- **Line 16, Zac (to camera):** Date, state and crew size all to camera. It's addressed to the viewer, so it isn't co-host exposition. But stacked in one breath it reads as a slate, not as someone talking (see Check 3).
- **Lines 26 to 34, Matt:** Matt narrates the events to Zac and the viewer. The format note on line 6 covers this ("talking to the viewer and to each other at once"), so I'm not flagging it. It's a fair convention for two hosts telling a story they both know.

## Check 5: sounds written, not spoken

The pattern shows up in the buttons. Several lines end on a crafted contrast or a balanced shape that nobody hits in live talk.

- **Line 95, Matt:** "Either six men saw something in those trees, or six men kept a secret for fifty years. Pick the one that scares you." This is the trailer line: matched clauses, then an aphorism-style instruction. It's also the longest and most polished thing Matt says, from the character the brief makes "short, clipped." And it doesn't hold up when you think about it. A hoax isn't the frightening option, so "Pick the one that scares you" only works for the first half. What it's reaching for is Matt reframing the question onto the crew, which is a strong turn. The idea is good. The delivery is too finished.
  PROPOSAL: let Matt get there in pieces, in his clipped rhythm. One option, then the other, maybe with Zac cutting in between them. End on the question with no instruction attached. The crew framing stays.
- **Lines 92 to 95:** Zac: "That's a no." Matt: "That's a not yet." It's a neat volley and it is a real micro reversal (S1, "mini/micro reversals, reconsiderations"), so I'd keep the idea. But "That's a not yet" is a comeback someone wrote. Out loud Matt would more likely just say "No it's not" or "I didn't say that."
- **Line 44, Zac:** "Not that night. Not the next morning. Five days." This is the rhythmic three-item build, a known slop tell. It also blurs Zac into Matt (Check 3).
- **Line 100, Zac:** "Den Files. Fire in the Sky. Get in the truck." Another three-beat close. The title card needs to be there, and the first two beats are fine as a slate. The weak part is "Get in the truck" (see Check 1).
- **Line 48, Matt:** "With the sheriff looking at those six guys. Not the sky." "Not the sky" is a writer's button, a rhyme-adjacent contrast. The first half is plenty.

**Passed the read-aloud test:** line 37 ("And then, this is the part I can't shake, they turn around. They go back for him.") is a mid-sentence aside a person really does say, and it's easy to perform. Line 79 uses the full name "Travis Walton," but as the name people search for, not as a form of address, so it doesn't count as the full-name tell.

## What's working, keep it

- The polygraph block, lines 55 to 68. Real interruptions, the meta "He does this," and Matt shutting it down. It's the only stretch that sounds like two friends who have done this before.
- Line 41, Matt, "He's gone." Short, anchored, and it lands right after Zac's emotional peak.
- Line 52 is Zac's clearest line in the draft. That's the voice his other lines should match.
