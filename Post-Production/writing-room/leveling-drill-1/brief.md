## Logline
In 1975, six loggers drove out of an Arizona forest without the man they came in with, and the Den's most verdict-first host gets asked straight out whether it happened and won't say.

## Spine (one sentence)
The story worth hearing is not the light over the trees but the six men who came back without Travis Walton, and every beat puts the viewer in that truck with them until the only open question is why the truck turned around.

(Spine stated as a thesis before drafting, per S4. PROPOSED as a house rule, S4 is community tier.)

## Voices: want and obstacle

| Voice | Want (in this teaser) | Obstacle | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matt | To get to the hard part fast, and, true to form, to say what he thinks. | Zac keeps slowing the story down, and Matt himself knows the verdict belongs in the episode, so he has to sit on it in front of the guy who is daring him to talk. | Short, clipped, declarative. Fragments. Cuts Zac off. Mostly states events, never feelings. |
| Zac | To make the viewer feel what it was like to be one of the six, so they care before they get any answers. | Matt keeps skipping ahead to the light, the polygraphs and the movie, the parts that sell themselves. | Longer lines, second person, pulls the viewer in ("Picture the end of a long day...", "you're one of the six who came back without him"), names what he feels ("the bit I'd build the whole episode on"), gets cut off mid-thought ("And they..."). |

The two men run parallel tracks (S1, Mazin, "multi-track narratives"): Matt's thread is the sequence of events and the verdict he is holding; Zac's thread is the men in the truck. The friction between them is about order and pace, not about facts they both already know, so neither one explains the case to the other. Both are telling it to the viewer and competing over how.

## The turn
Up to about 65 s the viewer thinks this is a "was it aliens" teaser and that Matt, the guy who always leads with the verdict, is about to hand it over. Zac asks him straight. Matt refuses, and turns the question around: forget the light, why did the truck turn around? The question the viewer walks away with is about the crew, not the light. No either/or, no hoax option.
