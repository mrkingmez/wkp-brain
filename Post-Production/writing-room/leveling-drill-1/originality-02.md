## The change-log disagreement: "Not the sky" and "Get in the truck"

Pass 1 said keep both (originality-01.md L56 and L57). The writer cut both (change-log C8, C13, and Not Applied rows L38 and L39), citing the order, dialogue-01.md and clearance-01.md.

My ruling: the cuts stand. Reason:
- My pass 1 "keep" was a clearance of the lines on originality grounds (no source found), not a claim that the piece needed them. Clearing a line is not a vote to protect it against other benches' findings. Dialogue (writer's button) and clearance (unattributed claim about an official; possible film-line echo) both had live findings on these exact lines, and an originality "no problem here" does not outrank a live problem.
- The cost to originality was the loss of a distinctive line. "Not the sky" was the cleanest statement of the crew-as-subject angle. That angle now lives in d02 L45 ("you're one of the six who came back without him") and d02 L82 ("Forget the light. Why'd that truck turn around?"). L82 says the same redirect, sky to crew, in a question instead of a button. The point of difference survived the cut.
- "Get in the truck" cut also closes the H1 echo risk on that line without a transcript check. Nothing original was lost; the truck callback moved to d02 L82.

Lesson for me, logged: when I clear a line in pass 1, say "clear, no objection to cutting" unless the line carries the premise, so a "keep" does not read as a defense.
