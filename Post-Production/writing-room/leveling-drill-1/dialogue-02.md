## New problems in draft-02

- **L62, Matt: "Ninety-three, they make the movie."** On the page "they" means Hollywood. To the ear, "they" has meant the crew for four straight Matt and Zac lines: L34 "They floor it," L38 "they turn around," L49 "So they sit for polygraphs," L53 "And they..." So the fifth "they" sounds like the crew made the movie. Nobody listening will stop to fix it, they will just be briefly lost one line before the turn. What the line is reaching for: Matt jumping ahead eighteen years in five words. PROPOSAL: name a new subject in the same clipped rhythm. "Ninety-three, Hollywood makes the movie." brings back the d01 word that originality-01 L52 asked to keep.
- **L38, Zac: "And then, and this is the bit I'd build the whole episode on, they turn around."** This replaced the d01 L37 aside, which I passed on the read-aloud test. Two problems. First, "the bit" is a British register. A Georgia Gen X host says "the part." The originality cut was about "can't shake," not "part." Second, the new aside is signposting: the host announces which beat matters. That does two bad things. It tells the viewer how to feel instead of letting the beat land on its own (S5, what is left unsaid). And it pre-announces Matt's close. When Matt asks at L82 "Why'd that truck turn around?", he is landing on the beat Zac already flagged, so the closing reversal turns into agreement. What the line is reaching for: Zac leaning in, warm, because this is the beat he personally cares about. PROPOSAL: keep the mid-sentence aside shape (it reads aloud well) but fill it with Zac reacting, not producing. For example "And then, and I love this part, they turn around." Or drop the aside and let a pause do it: "And then... they turn around. They go back for him."
- **L27 and L34, back-to-back attributions: "way they tell it" then "Crew says."** Either one alone is natural. "Way they tell it" in particular sounds like a guy telling a story at a table. But two hedges in three Matt lines start to sound like a man protecting himself, which reads as a lean in its own right. The added care does not match Matt, who the brief makes blunt. Clearance asked for this (change-log C15, clearance-01 L47), so I am not overruling it. Flagging the ear cost only. PROPOSAL, for clearance to accept or refuse: keep "way they tell it" on L27 and let it cover the run, so L34 goes back to "Something hits him. They floor it." The attribution carries forward because Matt has not changed sources.
- **L82, the closing question, ear test.** "Why'd that truck turn around?" has an obvious literal answer: to go get their friend. Zac already said exactly that at L38 ("They go back for him."). The change-log's "two open readings" (draft-02 L94) live in the writer's head, not on the page. Nothing in L45 to L69 makes turning around strange. Out loud, a first-time listener can hear a question that was answered fifty seconds ago. This is half structure, so it also goes in HANDOFF. The dialogue-side fix: make the strangeness something a person would actually say, a reason going back is odd (they had just floored it away in fear), set up inside the dialogue. For example Matt's L34 could end on the fear, so L82's question pushes against it. PROPOSAL only, the idea and not the wording. Matt's line itself is the right length and rhythm.
- **Minor, L45 then L49: "So now you're one of..." / "So they sit for polygraphs."** Back-to-back "So" across speakers. On its own it's harmless. Noting it only because it makes Matt sound like he is finishing Zac's thought, when the voice test depends on him cutting across it. Low priority.
