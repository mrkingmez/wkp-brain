## 3. Judgment calls

**J1. Line 95, the hoax option on the crew.** "Either six men saw something in those trees, or six men kept a secret for fifty years." This is framed as an open either/or and the line itself says "pick," which reads as opinion and invitation rather than an accusation. That framing is the protection, and it is real. The exposure is that it puts "these identifiable men lied for half a century" in front of the audience as one of two options, and the brief (line 54) and draft note (line 96) both admit nobody has checked who is living or who still stands by the account. If any crew member has publicly recanted, or publicly been accused and fought it, the line's weight changes in either direction. Describe, not conclude: the line sits on the opinion side of the line as drafted; whether it stays there depends on facts not yet researched, and on how the full episode handles it.
**What would reduce the open question:** fact-research on each crew member's current public position, logged before the episode records.

**J2. Lines 75 and 82, what the film changed versus Walton's account.** "Rewrites the part everybody remembers" and "Probably isn't what he says happened." These are claims about a living person's own account. They are directionally favorable to him (they say the film, not Walton, changed the story), so the false light risk to Walton is low. The risk is accuracy: if fact-research finds the difference is smaller or different than the line implies, the teaser has made a factual claim about him that is wrong. Both lines are tagged FACT-CHECK OWED in the draft. "Probably" in line 82 softens it.

**J3. Line 48, the sheriff.** "With the sheriff looking at those six guys. Not the sky." States as fact that law enforcement suspected the crew. Unattributed, untagged as opinion, about an identifiable official. Tagged FACT-CHECK OWED at line 49. Low exposure if the record supports it, which public accounts are widely said to, but that has not been retrieved this run.

**J4. Lines 26 and 33, events stated as fact.** "Walton gets out and walks right at it." "Something hits him." These are the crew's and Walton's account stated in the narrator's voice as fact, with no "they say." This cuts against the episode's own premise (the open question of whether it happened) more than it creates legal exposure. Flagged for the room, not as a risk to Zac. The tags at lines 27 and 34 already call these crew accounts.

**J5. Title use, line 100 (and line 71).** "Den Files. Fire in the Sky." Titles of single works are generally not protected by copyright. Trademark exposure for using a film title to name a commentary episode about that film is usually low when the use refers to the work and does not suggest the studio made or endorsed the episode. Check scope widened to the whole class: the same three words are, I believe, also the reissue title of Walton's own book (originally "The Walton Experience"), which the search summary confirms the film was adapted from. Reissue title NOT VERIFIED this run. Thumbnail, title card and any poster art are not in this draft; if the studio's poster or logo treatment appears, that is a separate artwork question and moves this up.

**J6. Trademarks.** None in the spoken lines. "Hollywood" (line 71) is generic. No studio named.

**J7. Music cues.** None named. No licensing cost created by this draft.

**J8. Real locations.** "Arizona" and "that forest" only. No real business depicted. Nothing to flag.
