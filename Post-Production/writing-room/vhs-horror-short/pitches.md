# VHS Horror Short — Pitch Batch

Mode: GENERATE, brief stage. Not tied to any existing WKP venture,
franchise, or prior script. Five distinct concepts for Zac to choose
from. Nothing beyond this stage has been written — no beat outline,
no draft, no dialogue pages exist yet for any of these.

Hard constraints locked across all five: 2-8 min runtime, horror,
a VHS tape functioning as a real plot mechanism, and the exact line
"I can explain the blood but the goat is harder" landing somewhere,
unaltered.

---

## 1. RETURN POLICY

**Logline:** A lone video store clerk closing up finds a tape in the
overnight drop box with no case, no label, and a due date from six
years ago, and every minute he watches it, the store's security
footage starts showing him something that hasn't happened yet.

**VHS mechanism:** The tape is a curse-carrier, not a recording of a
fixed event. Each time it plays, it adds runtime the clerk didn't
watch before, and what's added is always one step further into the
store than he's currently standing. The tape is recruiting a viewer —
finishing it means the store's own cameras start recording him
doing what the tape shows next. Rewinding doesn't reset it; the
added footage stays added.

**Line placement:** SAID. Near the midpoint, a police detective who
took the same tape's case six years ago (the store still has the
old rental slip) shows up asking questions about a break-in that
matches what's now on screen. The clerk, holding a mop and standing
in a room that increasingly does not look like the video store,
says it flat, procedural, to a cop who clearly wants a real answer:
"I can explain the blood but the goat is harder." The comedy is
the register — he's answering like a guy caught wrong-drawer at a
cash count, not like a man who just watched a monster.

**Core scare/dread mechanic:** Escalating footage the viewer cannot
stop watching without leaving evidence half-explained, plus the
inevitability of the last shot always being the room you're
currently in. Dread comes from the tape being ahead of the story,
not behind it.

**Rough shape (2-8 min):**
- Cold open: clerk alone, closing routine, drop-box tape with no
  label
- He plays it out of boredom; first minute is dead static then a
  hallway that looks like the store's own stockroom
- Detective knock, exposition-lite (old rental slip, unsolved case)
- The line lands here, deadpan, wrong tone for the room
- He goes to check the stockroom against the tape; footage and
  reality are now one beat apart
- Final shot: the store's own security monitor catches up to him
  in real time, one frame behind

## 2. PROOF OF LIFE

**Logline:** A father who has been mailing himself weekly "proof of
life" tapes from a custody-dispute cabin gets one back postmarked
after his daughter went missing, and it's her, calm, explaining
what happened to the goat pen like she's reading a grocery list.

**VHS mechanism:** The tapes are a real, mundane device before
they're a horror one — a documented, court-admissible practice (his
lawyer told him to record proof he's caring for the property and
the animals weekly, mail the tape to a PO box as a timestamp). The
horror turns on one tape arriving that he never recorded, with a
postmark from after she disappeared, shot in a room that burned
down two tapes ago.

**Line placement:** SAID, straight to camera. His daughter, mid-tape,
addresses the lens the way she always does for these recordings —
same singsong "hi Dad" opening — then without any shift in delivery:
"I can explain the blood but the goat is harder." The horror is
that nothing about her voice changes before or after it. The father
watching (and the audience) gets the tonal whiplash the room needs
without a single line of the film's own dialogue doing anything but
sitting still.

**Core scare/dread mechanic:** Ambiguity horror — is this a
supernatural recording, a hostage tape staged by someone who has
her, or proof she was never in danger and something else in that
house is the danger. The film never resolves which. Dread lives in
the gap between "this is procedure" and "this cannot exist."

**Rough shape (2-8 min):**
- Establish the ritual fast: stack of labeled tapes, dates, a man
  going through the motions of an ugly custody situation
- He gets the new one in the mail, checks the postmark against a
  calendar on the wall (does the math on screen, no dialogue needed)
- Plays it. Daughter, same setup, same chair, same opening line
- The line lands mid-tape, no reaction shot needed
- He goes to the cabin. The chair is there. The goat pen is empty
  in a way that reads as long-empty, not recent
- Final beat: he finds the camera that "shot" the tape, and it's
  been unplugged the entire time he's owned the property

## 3. HAND-ME-DOWN

**Logline:** A woman buys a stack of mislabeled workout tapes at a
swap meet, and the third one starts as aerobics before quietly
becoming a home video of a ritual that knows things about her house.

**VHS mechanism:** The bootleg is not a fixed recording — it's a
tape that reads its viewer and reorders itself, folding details from
the watcher's actual home into what plays back (a hallway that
becomes her hallway, a voice that starts using her dog's name). It
functions as a personalized haunting delivery system: the more of it
she watches, the more of her own life the tape has absorbed and can
now stage.

**Line placement:** SUNG. The ritual footage includes a woman on
screen singing a flat, tuneless little cleaning song while scrubbing
a floor, the kind of nothing-tune people hum doing chores. Partway
through, still in the same melody, same key, no button on it: "I
can explain the blood, but the goat is harder" sung exactly like the
line before it. The black comedy is buried inside domestic normalcy,
which is the scare.

**Core scare/dread mechanic:** Violation-by-familiarity — the dread
isn't the ritual itself, it's watching the tape learn your house in
real time and use it against you before you've finished the tape.

**Rough shape (2-8 min):**
- Swap meet buy, cheap and funny, tapes literally labeled "AEROBICS
  3" in Sharpie
- Home alone, puts it on as background noise while doing chores
  herself
- Tape starts normal, drifts — the on-screen woman's kitchen starts
  matching her kitchen
- The song plays, the line lands inside it, she laughs before she
  registers why she shouldn't have
- She mutes the TV; the humming continues from another room in her
  own house
- Final shot: she finds herself already on the tape, from an angle
  no camera in her house could shoot

## 4. PASS IT ON

**Logline:** In the fall of 1994, a tape arrives in a padded
envelope with no return address and a typed note demanding it be
copied and mailed to three friends within three days, and the kid
who breaks the chain finds out why nobody else did.

**VHS mechanism:** Analog chain-letter horror. The tape's actual
"payload" only fully forms after enough generations of copying —
each successive dub is slightly more contaminated with additional
footage that wasn't on the original, meaning the chain itself is the
delivery mechanism, not the tape. Breaking the chain (not copying it
in time) doesn't stop the threat, it locks whatever's accumulated
onto the last copy made, which is the version the protagonist is
holding.

**Line placement:** WRITTEN. Each copy in the chain has been passed
along with a handwritten note taped to the shell, rules for the next
kid, and successive kids have added their own scrawl underneath the
last one's, like a chain letter's postscript. On the copy the
protagonist receives, one previous kid's handwriting, different pen,
clearly written in a hurry, reads: "I can explain the blood but the
goat is harder. Copy it anyway." It's the darkly funny beat sitting
inside genuine panic, from a kid who is clearly still alive enough to
have written a joke.

**Core scare/dread mechanic:** Countdown dread plus contamination —
static bleed starts appearing in the protagonist's own camcorder
footage of his real life before the three days are up, meaning the
tape is already leaking into his world whether or not he copies it.

**Rough shape (2-8 min):**
- Kid gets the envelope, no return address, VCR click-in, the note
  with the rules
- Skims past older handwriting on the shell without reading closely
  — plant for later
- Watches it with a friend, mostly a lark, ends with something
  wrong in frame
- Rewinds to check the handwriting on the shell, finds the line
- Starts his own camcorder to record a copy and catches the same
  static bleeding into his live shot, not just the dub
- Final beat: three days aren't up yet and it's already here

## 5. LAST TENANT

**Logline:** A couple renting an off-grid cabin for a weekend find
an old VHS tape in the security system's junction box, and the
tape is playing back tomorrow night, one day ahead of schedule.

**VHS mechanism:** The cabin's ancient analog security system
recorded over itself in a loop for years, except one tape got
pulled and left behind, and it shows the interior of the cabin
exactly twenty-four hours in the future. It is a genuine plot
mechanism, not a jump-scare prop: the couple uses it across the
runtime to try to avoid what it shows, and every attempt to avoid it
is what causes it.

**Line placement:** WRITTEN. Stuck to the tape's plastic case with
old masking tape, in handwriting that isn't either of theirs, a
note from whoever the last tenant was: "I can explain the blood but
the goat is harder. Don't watch past the porch." They watch past the
porch anyway.

**Core scare/dread mechanic:** Predestination dread — the horror
isn't a monster, it's the mounting certainty that the tape is not a
warning, it's a record, and every choice made to dodge it is already
in the footage.

**Rough shape (2-8 min):**
- Arrival, cabin small-talk, one of them pokes around the old
  security box out of boredom and finds the tape and the note
- They play it as a joke, expecting nothing, get their own porch,
  timestamped tomorrow
- Line read aloud off the note, played for a laugh before the
  timestamp registers
- They spend the middle stretch trying to not do what the tape
  shows (don't go outside, don't answer a knock)
- The choice to avoid it is the thing that puts them on the porch
  at the exact timestamp
- Final shot: the tape, already rewound, ready for someone else to
  find
