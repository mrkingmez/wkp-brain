### 2c. AI or generated imagery
- Line 114: "Every item can come from free stock or be generated." Generated scenes include a war zone, a medevac ride, a hospital, and a child.
- Ownership: under current US Copyright Office guidance, purely AI-generated footage is not protected by copyright. On a paid job, Ian may assume he owns a trailer that in part nobody owns. That belongs in the client agreement.
- Tool terms: which generator and which model matters. Firefly's own models and Firefly partner models (Veo, Kling, Runway and others) carry different commercial terms and different indemnity. Not checked here because no tool is named in the draft.
- Platform labels: YouTube asks creators to disclose realistic synthetic content, including realistic scenes that did not happen. Meta and TikTok have their own AI labels. A realistic Iraq dust scene and a hospital scene likely land in that zone. Whose account posts it decides who ticks the box.
- Lookalikes: the draft bars faces (lines 30, 31, 35, 114), which lowers the chance of a generated soldier or nurse resembling a real person. It does not remove it.

### 2d. Insignia and military marks
- Helmet "no insignia" (line 30), dog tags blank (lines 33, 130), no unit patches (line 114). Army boots and a US flag are not marks in this sense.
- Exposure that remains: anything generated or stock can arrive with a patch, name tape, rank, or Army star logo baked in. Frame-check every shot. Nothing here implies Army or DoD endorsement; keep it that way (no Army logo, no "Army Strong" or other Army slogans, which are registered marks).
- Ian's own brand kit (CSM chevrons, the red winged crest with the "7") is not on the end card (lines 103 to 108). The crest origin question from earlier passes is still open; if an editor adds his banner, it comes back.
- Real places: BRAGG, GERMANY, IRAQ, LANDSTUHL (lines 29, 32, 52). Real facility named (Landstuhl), shown neutrally. Not a business doing anything criminal. Fine to note, not a flag beyond that. Accuracy (summary line 7 places rehab at BAMC, the card reads as rehab after Landstuhl) is a writing-room question, not clearance.

### 2e. Car marks (Ford / Mustang)
- No make or model is named anywhere in the draft. Car is "silhouette, no badge" (lines 22, 117), and line 114 bars badges, grilles, logos, model names "on screen or in file names." That closes the named-mark issue from earlier passes for this draft.
- Remaining exposure: a classic car's body shape can be recognizable without a badge, and Ford asserts trade dress in the Mustang's shape. If stock or generated footage is a recognizable Mustang silhouette, the brand is there without the name. If it is clearly some other car, that question goes away but the book says Mustang, which is a writing choice, not a clearance one.
- The end card no longer sits on the book cover (line 108: "Nothing else on the card"). The cover carries Ford marks per earlier passes. If anyone adds the cover image to the end card, a thumbnail, or a post, the Ford cover question returns.

### 2f. Stock footage, child footage, music, sound
- "Free stock" (line 114) is not one license. Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit and others each have their own terms, most do not guarantee model or property releases, and some bar use that implies endorsement. This is commercial client work.
- Child: line 22 sneakers, line 23 boy's hand, item 3 and 4. If stock shows a real child, even only legs and hands, check that a model release exists. Generated avoids the release question but raises 2c.
- Music: line 135 "Source and license to be confirmed before use." Open. Needs a license that covers paid client work and the platforms Ian posts to. If Suno or another AI music tool is used, its tier and terms decide commercial use.
- Sound effects: list at line 135, no source named. Zac's Adobe SFX library on P:\ has its own terms; confirm they allow client deliverables.

### 2g. Amazon and the price claim
- End card line 105 to 106: "Available on Amazon / Kindle $4.99 or paperback."
- The word "Amazon" as a plain statement of where to buy is a reference, not use as owned. The Amazon logo or an "Available at Amazon" badge carries Amazon's own brand guidelines; neither is in the draft.
- The price is the exposure. A trailer stays up for years; KDP prices and promos change, and prices differ by country. A stated price that later is wrong is a consumer-protection and platform-policy question, small but real. Line 108 says check it the day it posts, which handles day one only.
- If anyone puts an Amazon Associates link in the description, that brings its own required disclosure.
