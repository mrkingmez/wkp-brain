## BLUF

Big step up from draft-01. The pronoun soup, the brothers line, "was wounded," and the father/dad switch are all gone, and the VO now tells a stranger it's a novel and ends on a question. Most lines pass the kitchen-table test. Two things still need fixing. The 60 is about a dozen words too long for an unhurried read, and the 30 and 15 are tighter than that. And one line is still back-cover copy, not speech.

**Worst offender:** "Heartbreak. Grief. Regrets. Memories that wouldn't leave him alone." Nobody says this across a kitchen table. It's a four-item noun list with the last item stretched out, which is trailer-announcer and jacket-copy rhythm. It's also the one spot where the narrator turns into a different person. It lifts the back-cover words straight across instead of saying them the way a man would.

## 1. VO naturalness, 60 master, in reading order

**"Brandon Turner grew up under a car."** Holds.

**"His dad did the work. Brandon handed him the wrench."** Holds. Pass 1 flagged the second "Brandon." On a second look, keep it. "He handed him the wrench" right after "His dad" doesn't make clear who handed what to whom. Here the name is there for clarity, not habit.

**"He joined the Army."** Holds. Flat, but it's a hinge line and the duffel shot carries it.

**"He came home on leave. His mom cooked his favorite."** Sayable. It's still inventory, though (see Pattern A), and it's already first on the draft's own trim list. Agree with cutting it if the read runs long.

**"His girl had someone else."** Holds. Best short line in the piece.

**"He lost his squad leader. Then he was the squad leader."** Holds. It's the only mirror left, and that's the right one to keep.

**"He got hit. On the flight out, he thought the nurse was an angel."** "Got hit" is right now. The second sentence is the problem. It's 14 words in 5 seconds, so the voice has to hurry. And "On the flight out" leans on a picture a VO-only listener doesn't have.
PROPOSAL: "He got hit. Thought the nurse on the plane was an angel." That's 12 words with a dropped subject, which is how men tell this kind of story. Or cut "On the flight out" outright, as the draft's trim order says.

**"Two years of rehab, and his dad never left that chair."** Holds. Strongest line. "That chair" points at the picture, but a VO-only listener still hears "bedside" without being told.

**"Home turned out to be the hard part."** Holds. It reads one way only now, and "turned out" is how people say it.

**"Heartbreak. Grief. Regrets. Memories that wouldn't leave him alone."** (worst offender) See BLUF. "Regrets" in the plural is also how a book jacket talks, not a man. Heartbreak and grief are already covered by the girl and the squad leader, so the list repeats what the trailer just showed.
Reaching for: what came home with him.
PROPOSAL: "And the memories wouldn't leave him alone." That's 7 words, it uses only back-cover words, it's one thought, and it saves 2 words and three hard stops.

**"So he went out to the garage. To the car he and his dad worked on."** Holds. Pass 1's note still applies: Ian reads "him and his dad" if that's how he talks.

**"He didn't know where to start."** Holds. It's plain, it's a real question, and it isn't a trailer line.

**"The Wrench Turner. A novel by Ian Griffin."** Holds.

## 2. Word count against the slots

Count assumes about 2 words a second, unhurried, which is the draft's own target. VO time means seconds with VO over them; card-only rows are left out.

**60 master**
- Total VO: 118 words. VO time: 53 s (60 minus the three silent card rows, 2 + 3 + 2). Room for about 106 words, with no beats between blocks.
- Over by about 12 words before the block beats the voice notes ask for.
- Rows running hot: 0:04 to 0:08 (10 words, 4 s), 0:12 to 0:16 (10 words, 4 s), 0:27 to 0:32 (14 words, 5 s), 0:41 to 0:45 (9 words, 4 s, but four hard stops read closer to 5 s), 0:45 to 0:51 (15 words, 6 s).
- Fix: cut "His mom cooked his favorite" (minus 5), use the nurse PROPOSAL (minus 2) or cut "On the flight out" (minus 4), and use the memories PROPOSAL (minus 2). That brings it to about 105 to 107 words and it fits with small beats. The closing name line can stay.
- Card note: "even something broken can be fixed" gets 2 s on screen for 6 words under a music swell. That's tight for a first-time reader. If the garage row is trimmed, give the card 3 s.

**30 cut**
- Total VO: 49 words. VO time: 21 s. Room for about 42.
- Over by about 7. Three rows run near 2.75 words a second: squad leader (0:11 to 0:15), chair (0:15 to 0:19), hard part (0:19 to 0:22).
- Fix: take 1 s off the "my wrench turner" card (3 s to 2 s) and 1 s off the "broken can be fixed" card (4 s to 3 s), and give both seconds to the squad leader and chair rows. That gives 23 s of VO, close to 2.1 a second. Don't cut a line. Each one carries a stranger-test job.

**15 cut**
- Total VO: 23 words. VO time: 10 s. Room for about 20.
- The chair line (11 words in 4 s) is the hot one.
- Bigger problem: the 15's VO never says the title. "A novel by Ian Griffin" gives the author, but the book's name only shows on the end card.
- PROPOSAL: the closing VO becomes "The Wrench Turner. A novel." That's the same length, and the title is the one thing a 15-second listener has to walk away with.

## 4. Stranger test, VO only

**60:** passes. The listener hears a mechanic's son, the Army, a girl lost, a squad leader lost, a wound, two years with his dad by the bed, a hard homecoming, the garage, a question, and "a novel." The one gap is that the hope is all on the card. Heard blind, the VO ends bleak. That's fine for a trailer that wants a question, but if this ever runs as audio only (podcast, radio, Ian reading it at a signing), the book's promise is missing. Pass 1 raised this, and it's still Zac and Ian's call whether Ian reads "even something broken can be fixed" himself over the click.

**30:** passes narrowly. It never says he was wounded ("rehab" implies it) and never asks the question. It goes from "home turned out to be the hard part" straight to the title. Acceptable at 30 s.

**15:** fails as written. Heard blind, there's no war (IRAQ is a card), no wound, and no title. With the title PROPOSAL above it passes narrowly: boy under a car, two years of rehab with dad in the chair, The Wrench Turner.

## Patterns (once each)

**Pattern A, the inventory run.** 0:10 to 0:32 is still mostly "He did X. His Y did Z." Seven of the thirteen VO sentences start with "He" or "His." A plain-spoken man does talk like that, so it isn't wrong, but the run from leave to medevac still sounds like a list. Cutting "His mom cooked his favorite" breaks it up the most for the least loss. Examples: "He came home on leave. His mom cooked his favorite." / "He joined the Army."

**Pattern B, back-cover lift.** Wherever the draft used back-cover words verbatim as VO, the voice changes from a man talking to jacket copy. The memories line is the clear case. The "broken can be fixed" card is fine as a card, because a card is allowed to sound like the book.
