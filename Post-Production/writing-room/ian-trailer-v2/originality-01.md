# THE WRENCH TURNER trailer v2, draft-01: originality pass 01

Reviewer: originality bench. Mode checked: GENERATE draft, not parody.
Source read: Post-Production\writing-room\ian-trailer-v2\draft-01.md

## VERDICT: SHIPS WITH CHANGES

**BLUF:** This draft does not read as a version of Gran Torino, of any Chevy holiday ad, or of the usual stock book-trailer voiceover. Its spine is the book's own facts: the boy handing up the wrench, the squad leader succession, the father in the chair for two years, the party. Nobody can name what that is copying. The problem is in the pictures. Four shots come straight from the stock veteran-tribute kit (the backlit nurse, the folded flag, dog tags on a nail, boots lacing up). The 15-second cutdown keeps those shots and drops the father, so it plays like a generic veteran-charity spot. Fix the four shots and the 15, and it ships.

The search ran and found no ad pairing a veteran, a father's old car, and a "can be fixed" tagline (see Sources). The car-ad comparison sits in tone only, with nothing specific to point to.

---

## 1. Premise

Generic logline: a young soldier comes home wounded and heals by going back to the old car he worked on with his father.

Works that fit that sentence, or come close:
- Gran Torino (2008): veteran, classic car in the garage, a car standing for a man's values. Not a match here. Walt is an old Korea vet who mentors a neighbor kid. Brandon is young, the car came from his father, and there is no neighbor kid.
- Chevrolet "Holiday Ride" (2021): grieving relative restores a family member's car in a garage. Not a match. That ad is about grief over a late spouse and a surprise reveal. Nobody dies here, and there is no reveal.
- The wider "veteran comes home and finds himself in a workshop" human-interest genre (see Sources: the Hagerty story of a Marine mechanic back from Iraq, plus many local news restorations). That is a news genre, not one work.

The list is short and not specific. The choice that makes this not that list is the father as wrench man and the son as "wrench turner." The relationship is reversed and passed down, and the trailer opens and closes on the same handoff of the wrench. That choice belongs to the book. It carries the premise.

## 2. Structure

The skeleton is a life in a minute: childhood, enlistment, homefront, deployment, wound, rehab, homecoming, return to the opening image. That is a universal biographical montage, so it is fine.

No known film's reveal order is used here. There is no twist, no ghost ball (the optional ball beat at 0:53 is correctly left out; see the earlier finding that it reads as The Shining 1980 or The Changeling 1980), and no hidden-death reveal. The bookend (wrench into a hand at 0:04, wrench in a grown hand at 0:55) is a standard device that nobody owns.

**Cutdown check (the trap from earlier passes):**
- 30-second cut: holds. It keeps "His father was there the whole time" at 0:16, so the father spine survives.
- 15-second cut: does NOT hold. Its beat order is sneakers, wrench, helmet on sandbag ("Then the Army. Then Iraq."), porch and duffel ("He made it home. That was the hard part."), hand on wrench with "even something broken can be fixed." With the father in the hospital gone, all that's left is soldier, war, home, healing, and a slogan card. That is the shape of a veteran-nonprofit PSA. Nothing book-specific survives past 0:05 except the word "wrench."

## 3. Character

People, not types:
- **Brandon.** Defined by actions that only he took: he handed up the wrench, inherited the squad, found out about his girl in front of a whole party. A person.
- **The father.** He could slip into the steady-dad archetype, but the trailer gives him a hard specific (the chair beside the bed for two years, the Oklahoma drive with the buddy), and that is enough. A person.

Types wearing a book's name:
- **The nurse (0:35 to 0:40).** "He thought she was an angel," over a figure in scrubs, backlit, face lost in white light. The line comes from the book, and that part is fine. The staging is the angel-nurse image from the veteran-tribute kit, and the word "angel" over a white-out frame is the most used version of it. The book line can stay. The picture is the type.
- **The girl (0:18 to 0:23).** The Dear John letter is an old military archetype that nobody owns. The party detail ("The whole party found out when he did") makes it specific. Fine as written.
- **The buddy (0:23 to 0:28).** A survivor's-guilt type, but he is only a passenger in a beat about the father. Fine.

## 4. Texture

Lifted moments and images, by shot:

1. **0:35 to 0:40, backlit nurse, face lost in the light.** Stock. This is the white-out nurse from the veteran-tribute set already logged in this bench's verified matches. Paired with "angel," it is the single most recognizable beat in the cut. Fix shape: keep the line and drop the halo. Pick an angle on the moment that a stretcher-POV angel shot has never used (something procedural, something physical, anything except light swallowing a face).
2. **0:45 to 0:48, folded flag on a shelf plus dog tags hung on a nail.** Stock memorial iconography. It also misleads: in American picture language a triangle-folded flag means a funeral. Paired with the past-tense "He had his dad," a cold viewer will read that the father died. ASK IAN item 6 is already worried about that hint, and this picture says it louder than the line does. Fix shape: one object that belongs to this family, not to every memorial.
3. **0:11 to 0:15, boots lacing plus flag on a porch post.** Stock enlistment shorthand, seen in every recruiting and tribute montage. It is low-risk because it lasts three seconds. One stock beat is tolerable. This is the third in the cut, after the nurse and the flag.
4. **0:31 to 0:35, helmet on a sandbag, wind, distant rotor.** Stock, but carried by the line "He lost his squad leader. Then he was the squad leader," which is specific and strong. It holds in the 60. It does not hold in the 15, where the line is cut and only the helmet and the rotor are left.
5. **0:55 to 0:57, hand turns wrench, music swells on the click, slogan card.** This is the car-ad and tool-ad hero-moment register (a swell on a mechanical sound, then an inspirational line). The search found no specific ad that owns it. It is earned in the 60 because of the 0:04 bookend. Watch it in the 15, where it lands after nothing.
6. **Voice spec (Section D): "warm, plain, soft Texas."** Watch item, not a finding. A slow heartland male voice over working hands is the Ram "So God Made a Farmer" register (Super Bowl 2013). The risk only goes live if the read is slowed and given a drawl over hands and machinery. If Ian reads it in his own voice at his own pace, it's clean. A hired trailer-style read is not.

Things checked and cleared:
- Stock book-trailer VO ("In a world," "One man," "This fall"): absent. The VO is short declarative biography. Clean.
- Gran Torino: no old man, no neighbor kid, no car-as-inheritance-to-a-stranger. Clean.
- Chevy holiday ads: no death, no secret restoration, no reveal to a relative. Clean.
- Opening boots-and-sneakers under the car: a common father-son image, but the wrench handoff makes it specific to this book's title. No single source owns it. Clean.
- Ratchet-to-monitor sound bridge: already searched in an earlier pass, no source. Clean.

## Changes required before it ships

1. Restage the nurse shot (0:35 to 0:40) without the backlit white-out. Keep the book's "angel" line.
2. Replace the folded flag and dog-tag row (0:45 to 0:48) with one object that belongs to this family. As it stands, it also wrongly signals that the father died.
3. Rebuild the 15-second cut so the father survives it. As written, it is a veteran PSA with a book title on the end card.
4. Lock the narrator to Ian, or to a plain, unhurried read. No trailer-voice drawl over hands.

Optional: cut one of the three remaining stock beats (boots lacing or helmet on the sandbag) in the 60 if the restaged nurse runs long.

---

## The counter (strongest case FOR originality)

Every line of VO is a fact from this book, in this book's order, and most of them could not appear in any other trailer: "Brandon handed him the wrench," "Then he was the squad leader," "The whole party found out when he did," "His father was there the whole time." The film this most wants to be compared to, Gran Torino, does not fit on any axis that matters. The protagonist is young, the car is an inheritance from his father rather than a gift to a stranger, nobody dies, and there is no mentor or redemption arc. The draft has already cut the one beat (the ball) that earlier passes tied to a named film. A cold viewer who watches the 60 would come away with "a trailer about a kid and his dad," not "a trailer like X." The stock shots are dressing, not structure. Swap them and nothing about the story has to change.
