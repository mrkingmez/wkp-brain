## 1. Premise

Generic logline: a young soldier comes home wounded and goes back to the old car he worked on with his father.

Works that fit the sentence: Gran Torino (2008), the Chevrolet holiday ads ("Holiday Ride" 2021, "The Sanctuary" 2024), and the news-feature genre of veterans restoring cars. Short list, none specific.

What keeps it off that list: the father is alive and is the one who sat by the bed for two years, and the title turns the son into the father's "wrench turner." Nobody dies, no stranger inherits the car, no reveal. That is the book's choice and it carries the premise. The empty-chair staging (Texture 1) is the one place the draft undercuts it.

## 2. Structure

Life in a minute: childhood, enlistment, leave, deployment, wound, rehab, homecoming, return to the opening image. Universal montage, fine. No borrowed reveal order, no twist, no ghost ball (ball beat still OFF, correctly; the earlier Shining 1980 / Changeling 1980 finding stands if anyone puts it back).

Cutdown check, each on its own beat order:
- **30:** sneakers, wrench card, Army, BRAGG/GERMANY/IRAQ, helmet with the squad leader line, chair line, porch, wrench and slogan. Father survives through the VO. Holds.
- **15:** sneakers, wrench card, IRAQ, chair line, wrench, slogan. The pass 1 failure (veteran PSA shape) is fixed: the father is the middle of it. But the 15 has only two father images, boots at 0:00 and the empty chair at 0:06, so the death misread is strongest here. A cold viewer of the 15 sees: kid with dad, war, dad's empty chair, man alone with dad's wrench, "even something broken can be fixed." That is a grief spot.

## 3. Character

- **Brandon.** A person. Handed up the wrench, inherited the squad, came home to a girl who moved on.
- **The father.** A person on the page (two years in the chair is a hard specific). On screen he is boots, one hand, and a jacket. Not a type, but close to absent, see Texture 1.
- **The mom.** Represented by one plate (0:12). Fine, small.
- **The nurse.** Now sleeve and gloved hands at an IV line (0:27 to 0:32). The "angel" line is the book's and now plays against a procedural picture instead of with a halo. No longer the type. Cleared.
- **The girl.** "His girl had someone else" over a party from behind with the sound cut dead. The Dear John situation is an old archetype nobody owns. Cleared. (Note: draft-02 dropped the pass-1 detail "the whole party found out when he did," which was the specific part. Not an originality failure, just less specific.)

## 4. Texture

1. **0:32 to 0:37, chair pulled to a bed, work jacket over the back, nobody in it.** An empty chair with a man's coat on it is a standard absence image. In US military picture language the empty chair is part of the Missing Man Table, a memorial for the dead and missing. Over "his dad never left that chair," the picture shows him gone. Pass 1 flagged the folded flag for falsely signalling a death; this is the same problem in a quieter object. Fix shape: put the living father in the frame, his back, his hands, his boots, the jacket on him. Anything that shows a body in the chair. Same applies to the 15 at 0:06.
2. **0:45 to 0:51, garage door rolls up, dusty cover dragged off the old car.** The cover pull is the standard car-ad and barn-find reveal shot. It stays clean here because there is no badge, no gleam, and nothing is revealed but dust. Watch item: if the edit or music makes it a reveal (light hits the paint, swell on the drag), it becomes a car commercial. Keep the swell where the draft puts it, on the ratchet click.
3. **0:41 to 0:45, VO "Heartbreak. Grief. Regrets. Memories that wouldn't leave him alone."** Not lifted from a named work. It is generic: the list-of-nouns read is the most common book-trailer VO shape, and it is the only VO block that could sit in any veteran novel's trailer. Back-cover sourcing explains it but does not fix it. "Grief" also feeds the death misread in Texture 1.
4. **0:37 to 0:41, VO "Home turned out to be the hard part."** A commonplace. Searched: no film, doc, or ad owns the phrase, but "coming home is the hardest part" is the stated thesis of a whole run of reintegration documentaries (Searching for Home: Coming Back From War; When I Came Home 2006; the 2010 veterans film project in the Christian Science Monitor). Not copying, but a cold viewer will have heard it. Low risk because it is short and earned by the scenes on either side.
5. **0:19 to 0:22, BRAGG then GERMANY then IRAQ with a line drawing between them.** Clean as written (text on black). Watch item: if motion graphics put this on a map with a travelling line, it becomes the Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981) travel-map device.
6. **0:41 to 0:45, hands flat either side of a cold cup before dawn.** Common insomnia shot, no owner. The window onto the dark garage makes it this story's. Cleared.

Checked and cleared:
- Medevac cabin with gloved hand at the IV (0:27): procedural, no halo, no white-out. The pass 1 stock-nurse finding is resolved.
- Pegboard with the wrench's painted outline, filled at 0:10 and empty at 0:45: shadow boards are ordinary garage furniture, and the filled-then-empty pairing is this trailer's own device. Clean.
- Party sound cutting dead under "someone else": a common sound drop, no source. Clean.
- Voice spec (Section D): explicitly rules out the slowed drawl over hands. The Ram "So God Made a Farmer" (2013) watch item from pass 1 is closed, as long as the read follows the spec.
- Gran Torino: no old man, no neighbor kid, no gift of the car. Clean.
- Stock trailer VO ("In a world," "This fall," "One man"): absent. Clean.

## The counter (strongest case FOR originality)

A cold viewer of the 60 cannot name what this is copying, and the draft has now removed every image that earlier passes tied to a named work or the stock veteran-tribute kit (halo nurse, folded flag, dog tags, ball). What is left is built from this book's facts in this book's order: a boy handing up a wrench, a squad leader replaced by the man under him, a mother's plate, a party that goes quiet, a father in a chair for two years, a wrench outline empty on the wall. The bookend (wrench into a hand at 0:04, wrench in a grown hand at 0:51) belongs to the title, not to any ad. The empty-chair problem is a staging choice, not a story choice, and it is fixed by moving a body into a frame. Nothing in the spine needs to change.
