## A. Pitch

Brandon Turner learned everything that mattered lying on a garage floor next to his dad, handing up the wrench. He joined the Army, came home on leave to his mom's cooking and a girl who had moved on, then went Bragg to Germany to Iraq. He lost his squad leader, took the man's place, and got hit. Two years of rehab, and his dad never left the chair beside him. The trailer walks a stranger through that life in plain words, says out loud what he carried home, and leaves him in the garage with the old car and a wrench in his hand, not sure where to start. It closes on a back-cover fragment, "even something broken can be fixed," and tells the viewer this is a novel they can find on Amazon.

## D. Voiceover, ready to record (60-second master)

Voice: plain, unhurried, one man talking across a kitchen table. If Texas is in it, it is the reader's own voice at his own pace, not a slowed trailer drawl over hands and machinery. Best read is Ian's. Do not put "narrated by" anywhere unless Ian says so. Contract anything the way the reader would say it ("him and his dad" is fine if that is how he talks). Leave a short beat between blocks, not after every sentence.

Brandon Turner grew up under a car.
His dad did the work. Brandon handed him the wrench.

He joined the Army.
He came home on leave. His mom cooked his favorite.
His girl had someone else.

He lost his squad leader. Then he was the squad leader.
He got hit. On the flight out, he thought the nurse was an angel.

Two years of rehab, and his dad never left that chair.

Home turned out to be the hard part.
Heartbreak. Grief. Regrets. Memories that wouldn't leave him alone.

So he went out to the garage. To the car he and his dad worked on.
He didn't know where to start.

The Wrench Turner. A novel by Ian Griffin.

(On screen only, not read: "my wrench turner," BRAGG, GERMANY, IRAQ, and "even something broken can be fixed." If Ian reads the VO himself, the last line drops his name and becomes "The Wrench Turner. My novel." or just "The Wrench Turner. A novel." His call.)
