### 1. Final concept

The book opens and closes on the same small event: a ball rolls under a car where a man is working. The trailer is built on that object. We stay at floor level the whole time, the height of somebody lying on a creeper. The ball is the only thing that moves between chapters of Brandon Turner's life, and each time it rolls out of one place it rolls into the next. It passes his dad's hands, a party where everyone learns his girlfriend has moved on at the same moment he does, a barracks floor, a street in Germany where he is the one leaving, and the sand, where it stops. After that it is a hospital and a house, and the ball sits still under things. His dad's log on the garage wall ends in the middle of a line. Then grown Brandon is back on his back under the old car on a bright ordinary morning, garage door open somewhere off frame, birds outside, a car passing on a far road. The ball rolls in out of the daylight and taps his shoulder. His ratchet stops. He holds still, the way anyone does when something touches them that they did not expect. We cut there, in the living present, and then Ian's own back-cover words. The trailer does not stage a mystery about a person and does not say where the ball came from.
