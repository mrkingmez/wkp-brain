## 3. Research: what works for fiction trailers in 2026

Labels: OBSERVED means I opened the page today. SECONDARY means a search summary only. NOT RETRIEVED means I tried and failed.

**The anchor video (OBSERVED, full transcript read in Chrome on the video page).** "The Secret to a Book Trailer Readers Can't Ignore", The Guerrilla Publisher, Lara Helmling, posted 2026-10-13. The page shows 13:56 runtime, 4.2K views and 16.6K subscribers. What it says, in my words:

- Pick one moment from the book, not the plot. The moment should hold conflict: a discovery, a choice, a confrontation.
- A narrator explaining the plot over good-looking AI images is still telling.
- Her sample storyboard is four shots. For each shot ask: what do we see, what do we hear, what changes for the viewer.
- Do not show the cover, title or author until the very end. Then give one clear step to find the book.
- Vertical frame, close-ups, captions people can read. The action should make sense with the sound off.
- Keep characters consistent from shot to shot. Do not let captions cover the key object.
- Do not give away the best twist. Her line: "A trailer is an invitation, not a spoiler."
- Her four-question test before posting: does the opening stop the scroll, are you showing, does it leave them wanting more, can they find the book.
- One book can have several short videos built on different moments.
- The Blueprint PDF sits behind her community login. Not retrieved, by instruction.

**Length (OBSERVED, three pages, they do not fully agree).**

- BookBub Partners Blog, Nick Bailey of Reedsy, 2026-04-09: stay under 30 seconds on Instagram as a general rule. It cites a TikTok for Business primer at about 21 to 34 seconds. It says many people scroll with sound off, so add large centered text.
- publishing.co.uk, Robert Prime, 2026-04-23: 15 to 60 seconds vertical, calls 15 to 30 the sweet spot for TikTok, and calls the 60 to 90 second cinematic trailer with voiceover outdated. It also says do not summarize the plot, and end with a call to action.
- ebookpbook, Jason Pearce, 2026-05-19: 30 to 60 seconds for a single trailer. It says it could not trace the retention numbers people repeat, so it does not use them. Caption timing rule from subtitling practice: about 0.5 to 0.75 seconds per word, at least 2.5 seconds per card, two lines at most.

My read: 45 seconds is the top of the safe range for the master, and the 15 second cutdown is the one most likely to get watched to the end. None of these pages has hard data for fiction trailers. They are informed opinion.

**Hook (SECONDARY plus one OBSERVED).** Most search results put the hook in the first 3 seconds. The BookBub piece (OBSERVED) mentions TikTok's three-second rule and says creators report faster swipes in 2026. No platform-published number was found.

**Music (OBSERVED, publishing.co.uk).** That page tells authors to use trending TikTok audio and to skip the ominous dramatic soundtrack. I am not following the trending-audio advice here. Platform library songs cannot be baked into a file that goes to four platforms (SECONDARY, from draft 1's clearance file), and a trending sound fights the mood of this book. The useful part is the warning: no stock "epic trailer" music.

**Common mistakes named across the pages (OBSERVED and SECONDARY mixed):** plot summary, too long, cover first, narrator over stock images, no captions, no call to action, generic music.

**NOT RETRIEVED:** Reedsy's own book trailer guide (network error), TikTok's AI label help page (blank), Instagram's AI label help page (blank on desktop), Mixkit's music license text (page shows only a link, twice).
