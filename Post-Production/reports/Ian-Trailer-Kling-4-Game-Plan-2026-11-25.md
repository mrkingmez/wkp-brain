1. Kling 4.0 (partner model in Firefly) can take up to 10 keyframe images in one clip, up to 30 s. Zac's idea: give it our stills as ordered keyframes and write the prompt frame to frame. UNTESTED on our account, so we test it small first.
2. Cut the idea into gates. Each gate is a yes or no from Zac with the exact credit number read at the screen.
3. Gate 0: the three 16:9 stills Kling needs (H1, B1, W1) are made first in Nano Banana, one at a time, per the sitting sheet. Credit cost per still: read at the Generate button, NOT RETRIEVED.
4. Gate 1: ONE 10 s Kling 4.0 test, 600 credits (7.2 percent of 8,295). Three frames: H1, B1, W1, in that order. It tests the scary part (three unrelated scenes, faces, hands, flag, one woman) and the 10 s is real footage if it passes.
5. Gate 2: the 30 s run, 1,800 credits, ONLY if Gate 1 passes. Seven frames. 30 s of story from Kling, 15 s tacked on in Premiere (8 s animated sales page, 7 s author still), total 45 s.
6. The wrench hand-off R3 to R3b is never a Kling frame. It stays a plain 2 s Cross Dissolve of two stills in Premiere, spliced into the middle of the Kling clip (the cut point is built into the frame times, see section 4).
7. The model's jump from one scene to the next is the weak spot, so every frame sits 1 s after the cut zone and Premiere trims the cut zone off. The scene jumps are our editing points, not footage we use.
8. Stop-loss: no more than 3,600 credits on Kling video in total (43 percent of the balance), and three failed tries on one clip means redesign, not a stronger sentence.
9. If Gate 1 fails: Kling 3.0 with Elements, Veo 3.1 first and last frame (400 credits for 8 s, Zac's number), or Seedance 2.5 references. Costs for the others: Zac reads them at the Generate button.
10. 9:16 later: reframe the finished 16:9 in Premiere on a blurred fill (free). A true vertical Kling run is optional, only if credits are left.
