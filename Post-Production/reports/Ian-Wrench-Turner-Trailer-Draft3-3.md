## 6. The wrench hand-off morph: method and what we actually saw

**Recommendation: the free route, a matched crossfade.** If R3b lines up with R3 (same lamp, same wrench, same place), a plain 1.5 s crossfade changes only the hands, and that reads as the hands aging in place. That is the passing-the-torch moment.

What was tested on 2026-11-20 (R3b does not exist yet, so I tested on stand-ins: R3 against a slightly shifted and scaled copy of itself, and R3 against R2, a totally different picture). Frames pulled and looked at, files in `D:\WKP\scratch\ian\morph\`:
- **ffmpeg motion interpolation (minterpolate):** on the unlike pair it did not morph at all. It held picture A and snapped to picture B at the midpoint. Not usable.
- **ffmpeg xfade "dissolve":** a grainy random-pixel dissolve, not a crossfade. Not usable.
- **ffmpeg xfade "fade" (a true crossfade):** a clean double exposure. On the unlike pair it ghosts both pictures, which is exactly why R3b must be composed to match R3. On the aligned pair the change is smooth.
- **Premiere Morph Cut:** built for jump cuts in talking-head footage. Not tested on two different stills, NOT VERIFIED. Not recommended.
- **The real test** happens once R3b exists: line them up with Opacity 50 percent (Appendix G), crossfade, and check the midpoint frame shows one wrench, not two.

**Paid option (only if the crossfade disappoints):** a Firefly video with R3 as the start frame and R3b as the end frame. Whether any model on your menu takes an end frame is NOT VERIFIED; check for an End frame slot in the panel. Credit cost NOT RETRIEVED. Prompt in Appendix C.
