**1. The drop-L1a fallback (line 142) is the likely case, and it breaks the speaker cue.** At my count, any line of Ian's over about 2 s pushes SLOT past 20 s, so the "drop L1a" rule fires almost every time. Then:
- The first voice in the trailer is the dad's line at 0:06.8, read by Ian a touch lower or warmer. The next voice is the narrator at about 0:28.0, the same man, saying "His dad was there for all of it."
- With no narrator ahead of it, nothing sets up the dad's line as a different speaker. The listener has no narrator voice to compare it with. When L2 arrives in nearly the same voice and talks about "his dad", a listener can easily hear the dad narrating his own story.
- Line 142 also never says where Ian's line sits once L1a is gone. Does it stay at 0:06.8, leaving 0:03.0 to 0:06.8 with no voice, or move earlier? It needs a stated time.

If L1a has to go, L2 needs an audible return to the narrator, a gap, a sound change, or a clearly different read. Ian's "touch lower" alone is not enough for a listener to separate two characters played by one man 20 seconds apart.
