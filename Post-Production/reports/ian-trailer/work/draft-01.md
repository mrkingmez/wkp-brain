## BLUF
- 56 s vertical master. One worn rubber ball rolls from a boyhood garage, through a party, a barracks, a street in Germany, the sand, a hospital and a quiet home, and taps a grown man's shoulder under the same car. He goes still. He does not turn. Nobody is shown rolling it.
- The sound spine is the father's ratchet. It clicks under the car in the first frame. In the sand it stops mid-turn. Two seconds of nothing. A hospital monitor comes in at the ratchet's old tempo. In the present day the ratchet starts again in grown Brandon's hand, slower, and stops on the tap. That is how the wound and the father are tied together without rotor noise, a white-out or a flag.
- Narration: four lines from Ian, about 14.5 s (SAFE) or 15.5 s (SLOT) of the 56 s. Nothing in any line, caption or card points at the secret.
- Music: garage room tone only for the first 42 s. One sustained low cello note enters under the present-day beat, drops out half a second before the tap so the tap lands in near silence, and comes back under the cards.
- Biggest dependencies on Ian: his exact "wrench turner" line (SLOT version), OK on the visitor-log image, the text-rights OK for the back-cover cards, and what kind of ball the book describes.

## 1. Concept

The book opens and closes on the same small event: a ball rolls under a car where a man is working. The trailer is built on that object and nothing else. We stay at floor level the whole time, the height of somebody lying on a creeper. The ball is the only thing that moves between chapters of Brandon Turner's life, and each time it rolls out of one place it rolls into the next. It passes his father's hands, a party where everyone learns his girlfriend has moved on at the same moment he does, a barracks floor, a street in Germany where he is the one leaving, and the sand, where it stops. After that it is a hospital and a house, and the ball sits still under things: a visitor's chair, a workbench. Then, years later, grown Brandon is back under the old car in the pose from the cover, and the ball rolls in from off frame and taps his shoulder. He stops. He does not turn. The trailer ends on the question of who sent it, and the back cover's own words. No child, no voice, no footstep and no shadow comes with the ball in the present day. The answer is the book.

### Recording spec for Ian (plain words, phone)

1. **What to read:** the four lines above (and your book line, if we are using it), in order. Then read each line again on its own, three times, with a short pause between.
2. **Room:** the smallest, softest room you have. A walk-in closet full of clothes is perfect. A bedroom with the door shut is fine. Not the kitchen or bathroom (hard walls echo). Turn off the AC, fans, TV and anything humming. Put the dog out.
3. **Phone:** use the voice memo or recorder app. Put the phone in airplane mode so no call or buzz cuts in. Set it on a pillow or a stack of books, not in your hand.
4. **Distance:** about one hand-width, 6 to 8 inches, from your mouth, with the phone a little off to the side so your breath does not hit it straight on.
5. **Pace:** slower than feels natural. If it feels a bit too slow to you, it is probably right.
6. **Takes:** three full read-throughs, plus the three-times-each pass on every line. If you stumble, stop, wait two seconds, and start that line again. Do not stop the recording. We will cut out the stumbles.
7. **Before you start:** record 10 seconds of the room with nobody talking. We use that to clean the noise.
8. **Send it:** email or text the original file. Do not trim it, do not send it through Facebook or WhatsApp (they squash the sound).
