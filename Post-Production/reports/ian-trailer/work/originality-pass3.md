## Pass 4: Texture

**F1. 15 s cutdown stacks the dead-father log directly against the tap. Section 2b, rows 0:06.5 to 0:08.0 and 0:08.0 to 0:12.5 (lines 76 and 77). RESIDUAL RISK, small change.**
- What creates it: the cut has no 8b, so no living buffer survives except the far-road car at about 0:10.0. The viewer sees the father's still ratchet, the broken-off log and the pen, and 3.6 s later a ball touches his son. Pass 2 named this exact structure: the stopped log, the still ratchet and the tap stacked together with nothing from the living present between them.
- Nameable comp: Field of Dreams register, same as pass 2 N1. It is weak at the level of shots or lines, but it is the reading a cold viewer will reach for. The master no longer has this problem. The cutdown brings it back.
- Fix, in the abstract: either the cold-viewer test (QC line 343) also covers the 15 s cut with the same one question and the same pass/fail rule, or the cutdown gets some living-present element between the log and the tap. That is the writer's choice. Note: the 15 s cut is likely the most-seen version on social, so it is not a side case.

**F2. Broken-off log entry with pen and dust. Beat 8a (line 46), rationale line 62. CLEAN on originality. Cold read flagged out of lane.**
- Originality: an unfinished last entry is an indexed trope (TV Tropes "That Was the Last Entry", plus the related "Dead Man Writing"). A pen lying across the break, in flat light, on a garage maintenance log, matches no owned image I can find. The Chevrolet "Holiday Ride" garage has no log (verified in pass 2). Clean.
- Out of lane: the rationale (line 62) says the dust means "nobody came back to it". In this beat order, beat 6 (wound) and beat 7 (two years at the hospital, "His dad was there for all of it") come right before, so the most natural cold read of a pen dropped mid-line may be "he got the call and ran". Two years of dust then fits the absence too. So the image can still read as "interrupted by the news", not "he died". What carries death in the final is L3 ("He came home to his dad's tools.", tools but no dad) and the one plate in 8b. Those are subtle at phone size. The cold-viewer test only asks "Where did the ball come from?" It does not ask whether the viewer understood the dad was dead. That belongs to dialogue and structure, not this verdict.

**F3. Closing card B1 after the tap, with the cello returning. Beats 10 and 11 (lines 48 and 49). WATCH, not a finding, already a gate question.**
- The cello comes back and "even something broken can be fixed" lands 2.5 s after the neutral pause. The acting no longer gives the tap a meaning, but this pairing can hand it the grace/sign meaning anyway. The no-card option (Section 4, gate question 1) is the cleanest for N1/F1. That is the copywriting gate's call. Noted here so the gate knows it bears on originality, not only on teaser rules.

## The counter (strongest case FOR originality)
Three reads in, the master holds no nameable borrowing. Every match raised across October and both passes is gone: the veteran-tribute stock, the Here hold, the Gran Torino porch, the Chevrolet golden hour, the Shining ball and pages, The Changeling, the battlefield cross, and now the Field of Dreams acting note. Each one was removed, not swapped for a cousin. What carries the piece belongs to this book alone: the bookend ball, the creeper-height lens, the ratchet tempo handed to a hospital monitor, one visitor-log page filling with one signature, a mother serving her son like company, and a grown man who simply stops when something touches him. F1 is not a lift. It is an editing order in one cutdown, and the remedy is cheap. A cold viewer watching the master and asked what it reminds them of has nothing to name. For a 56-second veteran-and-car trailer, that is the hard part, and it is done.
