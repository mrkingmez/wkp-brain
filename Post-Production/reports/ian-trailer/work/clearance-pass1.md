## Judgment calls in detail

### J1. The plot secret, by implication (most important finding)
No word leaks. Searched draft-01 for every secret term. Hits for "neighbor", "twins", "child in the present day" and "visitor" exist only in internal notes (L44, L126, L169, L173), never in narration, captions, cards or end card. The implication chain is where the exposure sits:

- **The final event is the book's own doorway to the secret.** In the summary (line 10), the ball rolling up to grown Brandon under the car is the moment that brings in the neighbor kid, and through him the secret. B9 (0:44.0 to 0:45.5) shows exactly that event. The draft's own concept line says the trailer "ends on the question of who sent it" (L16). Who sent it is the secret.
- **The rhyme points at a child.** B1 (0:00 to 0:03) the ball rolls under the car, B2 (0:03 to 0:09) a small hand appears with a wrench. The viewer learns: ball under car means a child is coming. B9 repeats the ball under car with "nobody shown rolling it." The rhyme is intentional (L189). A cold viewer's most likely read of who rolled the present-day ball is "a kid." That is the first half of the secret's shape.
- **The word "secret" lands right after.** Card B (B10, 0:49.0) says "when a surprising secret changes everything" about three seconds after the tap. Juxtaposition invites the viewer to connect the secret to whoever sent the ball. The sentence is Ian's own published back-cover copy, so the word "secret" is already public. What is new is putting it directly after the ball.
- **N-L4 "Same as before" (L112, B9 0:39.0 to 0:42.5)** leans the same way: same as before means same as the boyhood scene, which had a child in it.
- **15 s cut is tighter.** Beat 1, child's hand, sand, log, tap, end card (L54 to L59). In 15 seconds the child-then-ball-then-tap link is the dominant through-line. No "secret" card here, which softens it.
- **6 s cut** (L67 to L69) is ball under bumper, then tap. No child shown, no card. Least implied.
- **What the trailer does not do:** no second person, no shadow, no voice, no footstep near the ball in the present day (L44). No woman arriving. Nothing that points at pregnancy, a daughter, a grandson, an accident, or a death of anyone other than the father. The twins never appear (L173).
- **Where it sits:** the trailer reveals a mystery exists and a likely child-shaped answer to "who sent it." It does not reveal who the child is or how he connects to Brandon. Whether that is too close is Ian's call alone, because the line he drew ("this part I don't want revealed", summary line 10) is his. The draft asks him to reconfirm the fence in writing (L169). That written answer should exist before the master goes anywhere.
- **Not yet written, check at the next gate:** YouTube description, post captions, hashtags, pinned comment, thumbnail, alt text and the .srt. None exist in the draft. At copy stage, watch for any word in these families: neighbor, kid or boy in the present day, grandson or grandfather, legacy, bloodline, family he never knew, second chance at fatherhood, a woman from his past returning, a past night coming back, car crash, loss of anyone besides his father.
