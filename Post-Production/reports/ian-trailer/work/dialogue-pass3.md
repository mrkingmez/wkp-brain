## BLUF

The narration holds up. Every line is something a man reading his own book out loud would say, and nothing hands over the fence: no line touches where the ball came from, and the last spoken line before the tap does not name the dad. Em dash grep found zero em dashes and zero en dashes in the file. "His dad" is used in every narration line and caption; "father" appears only in production notes, never in copy.

The single worst offender is timing, not wording. Three lines have almost no air at 2.3 words per second: L2 SAFE needs about 5.35 s in a 5.4 s window, L2-slot needs about 3.6 s in a 3.6 s window, L1a needs about 3.0 s in a 3.1 s window. A real person counting "one" after "Two years" will run over on the first take. Second worst is B2, which is Ian's wording and not ours to change, but it puts the word "secret" three seconds after the tap and points the viewer at the fence.

## Findings by line

### L1a "Brandon Turner grew up under a car."
- Sayable, plain, and the full name is right for an author opening his own book. 7 words, about 3.0 s against a 3.1 s window (0:03.1 to 0:06.2), with the ratchet stop at 0:06.3. Holds, but only just.
- No change.

### L2 SAFE "Two years. His dad was there for all of it."
- Timing. 10 words plus the 1.0 s count comes to about 5.35 s against a 5.4 s window (0:27.6 to 0:33.0). Effectively zero slack. If Ian's count runs to 1.3 s, as most people's do, the line runs into 8a and steps on L3.
- Wording. "There for all of it" is the one place the script tips toward a tidy, greeting-card formulation. It is the feeling described rather than shown. Not a fail, but it is the softest line in the set.
- Landing. With the count, "His dad" falls about 29.5 on the visitor log and "there" lands on 7b around 30.8. The note that "His dad was there" lands on 7b is roughly true, not exact.
- PROPOSAL (room copy). Use the SLOT wording in SAFE too: **"Two years. His dad was there."** It is already written and already in the recording list, it drops the soft phrase, it buys about 1.7 s of air, and it means Ian records one fewer line. If taken, run it on the L2-slot timing (start 0:28.4) so "His dad was there" lands on 7b at about 0:30.3, and update C3 to the C3-slot caption.

### L2-slot "Two years. His dad was there."
- Wording is the cleanest line in the script.
- Timing. 6 words plus the count is about 3.6 s in a 3.6 s window (0:28.4 to 0:32.0). No slack at all. There is room after it (8a starts 0:33.0, L3 at 0:33.2), so an overrun of up to about 1 s is harmless in practice. The table should say the window is soft at the back end, so nobody squeezes Ian's read to fit it.
- Small read risk. Short as it is, "His dad was there" over a hospital log could, for one beat, read as the dad being the patient. 7b (gray-haired man in the chair beside the bed) settles it, so it holds as long as 7b stays exactly on the line.

### L3 "He came home to his dad's tools. And his mom was polite to him."
- The best line in the trailer. "Polite to him" is the feeling, not a description of it, and it leaves the important thing unsaid. Keep it exactly.
- "He came home to his dad's tools" is compressed, written-prose phrasing, but it is in the register of an author reading his book and it is the death cue in words without saying death. It leans on 8a (pen, dust, broken line) to read as death and not as "dad was out". That is the plan, and the cold-viewer test checks it.
- Timing. 14 words plus a 0.4 s breath is about 6.5 s in a 6.6 s window. "And" starts about 0:36.6 on 8b as written. Holds.
- Pattern note, once. L3's first sentence, "His dad was there" and L4 all say what the picture is showing at that second. Pass 1 flagged this. It is now acceptable because each line reframes the picture (the tools are left behind, the dad is beside the bed, he goes back under) rather than just captioning it. No change.

### L4 "So he went back under the car."
- Sayable, plain, ends hard. "So" is a light summing-up word and edges toward stating the theme, but it reads as a man telling a story, not a moral. "The car" correctly calls back to "a car" in L1a.
- The last people-word spoken before the tap is "he". The dad is not named. Good for the fence.
- Timing. 7 words, about 3.0 s in a 3.2 s window, ends 2.5 s before the tap. Holds.

### L4-cut "Brandon went back under the car."
- 6 words (the room-writer count is right), about 2.6 s, ends 0:10.9, 0.7 s before the tap at 0:11.6. Holds at 2.0 words per second too.
- "Back" has no earlier narration to refer to in the 15 s cut, but the picture just showed sand, a hospital log and a dusty bench, so "back" reads as "after all that". Works. Saying the name correctly kills the "is that the dad under the car" reading.
