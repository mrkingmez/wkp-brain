The list is the same: Gran Torino, the Chevrolet holiday ads ("Holiday Ride" 2021, "A Holiday to Remember" 2023, "The Sanctuary" 2024), Field of Dreams. One note: Draft 02 now hits all three Chevy ingredients on screen, an old car, a dead father, and a mother whose illness reads through "polite to him" (L3, beat 8b). ## Pass 4: Texture

**N1. The tap now reads as a sign from the dead father. Beat 9, 0:45.0 to 0:49.0, with concept line 23. RESIDUAL RISK, small required change.**
- What creates it. The concept now names the beat as "something you forgot comes back to you", and the reaction is directed as recognition (breath out, hand to rest). It sits two beats after 8a, where the father's log stops and his ratchet lies still. Birds carry into the black, then the card says "even something broken can be fixed." Each choice solved pass 1's horror problem. Together they tell the viewer the ball is a message from Dad.
- Nameable comp. Field of Dreams (1989): the dead father comes back through a ball, and the son's reaction is quiet recognition. It's a weaker match than The Shining was. The ghost father is visible, it's a baseball and a catch, and there's no tap. I would not fail the script on it. But it is the nearest work a cold viewer would reach for, and it is already on this premise's list. The general "sign from a lost loved one" grief trope, with birds as the usual messenger, is a trope, not one work, and I don't count it as a finding.
- Why it matters. Same reason as pass 1. The book's secret is a living person, and the trailer would still be promising a dead one, just more gently.
- Fix, in the abstract. Keep every fence. Keep the reaction short of meaning something specific. Pass 1 asked that the stillness not signal alarm. It did not ask that it signal memory of the father. A neutral human pause, stopping because something touched him, lets the birds and road suggest the living world on their own. The breath out with the hand coming to rest on the chest is the piece that most pushes the reading toward relief or grace. Also stop stacking the stopped log (8a), the still ratchet and the tap inside 13 seconds without anything in between that belongs to the living present. That is a structure lever, and the writer can choose it.
- Test. The writer's own note (line 229) asks for a cold-viewer check. Do it, and ask one question: "where did the ball come from?" If the answer is "his dad", N1 is live. If it is "a kid outside" or "I don't know", it's clean. That second answer touches the secret fence, which is a clearance call, not mine.

**N2. Maintenance log entries stopping. Beat 8a, 0:33.0 to 0:36.0, line 44. CLEAN as originality. Cold-read problem flagged out of lane.**
- Searched: Chevrolet "Holiday Ride" (2021) is the nearest garage-grief ad. Its garage beat is an old man finding his late wife's car and photo, with no handwritten log. "A diary that stops" is a trope found across war and found-footage work, not one owned image. Paired with tools in flat light, it's not nameable.
- Out of my lane, but this fix introduced it: entries that stop are explained by absence as easily as by death, and L2 ("His dad was there for all of it") has just given the viewer the absence. The handwriting match between signatures on the visitor log and entries on the maintenance log is also hard to catch in 3 seconds on a phone. Routed to dialogue and structure. It does not affect the originality verdict.

**N3. Faded off-white perforated plastic ball. Line 31. CLEAN.**
- It carries no ghost-film association that I know of, and it reads as backyard and daytime, as intended. That solves T1(a).
- Note for clearance, not originality: the slotted plastic backyard ball is a Wiffle Ball, Inc. product and name. The draft specifies round perforations and no brand, which keeps it generic. Keep "Wiffle" out of every prompt, caption and description.
- Continuity, not originality: "one practical ball, same physical ball in every shot" (line 31) means the boyhood ball is already faded. The writer may or may not want that.

**N4. Birds and daylight carrying into black. Beat 9 into 10, 0:49.0. CLEAN.**
- Birdsong over a cut to black after a war story is generic grammar. I could name no single owner. It does its pass 1 job of reading as life.

**N5. Card B1 alone as the closer. Beat 10, line 46. Watch, not a finding.**
- Pass 1 noted that "even something broken can be fixed" does no originality work, because it's common repair-metaphor copy. It's now the only card and the last line, so the generic phrase carries the trailer's thesis. It's Ian's verbatim back cover, not ours to change. The tap has to carry the trailer's specificity, which is another reason to get N1 right.

**Carried forward, still clean.** The ratchet-to-monitor sound bridge at the same tempo (beat 6, 0:23.0 to 0:25.0) and the slower ratchet in the grown hand (0:41.0) are still the strongest original textures in the package. The floor-level lens height has no source. The party, barracks and Germany beats have no nameable source.

## The counter (strongest case FOR originality)

Draft 02 is the version pass 1 said was possible. Every nameable match from October and from pass 1 is gone: the veteran-tribute stock, the Here hold, Gran Torino, the Chevy golden hour, the Shining ball, the Shining pages, the battlefield cross. None of them was swapped for a near cousin. What carries the trailer belongs only to this book: the ball as the bookend, the camera at creeper height, the ratchet as the father's heartbeat handed to a hospital monitor, one page of a visitor log filling with one signature across the months, and a mother serving her son like company. The Field of Dreams risk in N1 is a reading, not a borrowing. No shot, line or staging is lifted from that film. It comes from one acting note and one ordering choice, both cheap to adjust. If a cold viewer watched this and was asked what it reminds them of, the honest answer for most of the runtime is nothing. That is a strong result for a 56-second book trailer about a veteran and a car.
