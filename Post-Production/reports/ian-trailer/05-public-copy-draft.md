## 1. Answers to the six gate questions

**1. Closing card: B1, B2 or none. PICK: no card.**
- B1 ("even something broken can be fixed") is the last thing read before the title and tells the viewer the story ends in repair. That is a verdict, and review gate 3 says lead with the hook and never hand over the answer.
- B2 puts the word "secret" three seconds after the tap. It teaches the viewer to ask what the secret is and to tie it to the tap. It also works against the fence. Out.
- No card cuts from the held pause straight to the end card. The silence after the tap does the job a card would do, and it tells the viewer nothing.
- Length: trim the master to 53.5 s and run the end card 0:49.0 to 0:53.5 (4.5 s). A 7 s end card with only a URL on it drags. The editor can overrule this, but I would not stretch the end card to fill the time.

**2. B1 as a cut-down quote. PICK: do not use the back-cover sentence at all, whole or cut down.**
The whole sentence ends in "when a surprising secret changes everything," so the whole sentence can never run. The cut-down piece works as a verdict. Same answer for posts, captions and pinned comments: no sentence from the back-cover paragraph appears in any public copy. Every other sentence in that paragraph either lists the plot's obstacles or points toward the reveal ("an unexpected reconnection with his past"), so none of it passes the fence. If Ian wants a pull quote on the book itself, that is his call, from his own pages, after this desk checks it.

**3. End card load. PICK: confirm the URL as the one call to action.**
Final text in Section 2. Where to buy, formats, release status and handles stay on the posting sheet until Ian confirms them. If Ian names one retailer link he wants pushed, the URL line can swap for a short "Find it at [RETAILER FROM IAN]" line, but not both.

**4. Banner line card. PICK: Veterans Day variant yes, 16:9 website cut yes, master no.**
It is Ian's own sentence, it says who he is and nothing about the plot, and Veterans Day is the one date it fits. It never goes in the master. It needs his written OK to run on screen. Text card options are in Section 3.

**5. Caption set C1 to C6. PICK: confirm all six as split.**
All are under 40 characters, none uses a quotation mark, and none touches the fence (they stay on Brandon, the car, the tools and his mom). One note: C2 "Two years." stands alone for 1.8 s, which is right, it needs the beat. The stand-in font still needs its license file saved before anything burns in. Keep the SLOT hold: Ian's book line stays out of captions and every .srt until his written OK.

**6. Secret check on text not yet written.** Done in Section 5 for every line on this sheet. Standing checks for what is still to come: the YouTube description, the thumbnail, file names, export titles, project names and the .srt all get the same read. A hashtag is a caption. Hashtags below avoid every fence word.
