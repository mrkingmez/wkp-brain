### 2b. The rule for a drawl in tight windows

1. **Stretch vowels on stressed words only** (the words in bold in the cues below). Leave the small words alone.
2. **Take the time back from consonant endings and from the gaps between words.** Let small words run together the way they do in real talk.
3. **Never take time from the written pauses.** The 1.0 s count after "Two years." and the 0.4 s breath before "And his mom" stay exactly as written.
4. **If a take reads long, do not rush the line.** Keep the take that sounds like a person. Write down which line ran long and by how much, and tell the editor. The words never change.
5. **L3 running long is an edit-bench call, not yours.** The master is a fixed 56 s. Moving the picture cut later to fit a long L3 pushes L4 and the tap later too. The editor decides; your job is the note.

### 2c. Performance cues, line by line

**L1a: "Brandon Turner grew up under a car."** Window 3.1 s, est. 3.0 s. Tightest line on the sheet (9 syllables).
- Pace: steady, even, warm. Do not slow the front half to "set the scene".
- Stretch: **"car"**, the open "ah" only, then close the r clean and stop. Do not let the r trail. A little on the "a" in **"Bran"**.
- Timing cue: the second syllable of "Brandon" goes light and short (every English speaker does this; it is here because it buys back time, not as an accent feature). Run "up under a" together as one smooth piece.
- Rhythm: two halves, "Brandon Turner" / "grew up under a car". Say "Turner" clearly, it pays off on the title.
- Pauses: none inside the line. **There is no extra tenth anywhere in L1a.** Any stretch on "car" has to be paid for inside "up under a". Stop after "car." and leave the silence for the ratchet stop at 0:06.3.

**L2: "Two years. His dad was there."** Window 3.6 s, est. 3.6 s.
- Pace: plain, almost flat. This line is a fact, not a feeling. The flatness is the feeling.
- Stretch: **"years"** lightly, at most. Nothing else.
- "His dad was there." stays flat: no stretch on "dad", no lean on "there". "His" and "was" go short and light.
- Rhythm: two words, full stop, a count of one, four words.
- Pauses: **the 1.0 s count after "Two years." is fixed. Do not shorten it to make room for the drawl, and do not lengthen it.** No extra tenth inside "His dad was there." If the line runs 0.1 to 0.4 s past 0:32.0 it still lands on the gray-haired man's hands (beat 7b runs to 0:33.0), so a slightly long take is not a lost take. Note it for the editor.

**L3: "He came home to his dad's tools. And his mom was polite to him."** Window 6.6 s, est. 6.5 s.
- Pace: steady. The first sentence is a place, the second one should hurt a little without you pushing.
- Stretch: **"home"**, **"tools"**. Nothing on "mom".
- **"polite": your natural speech only, no added flattening.** It is the most tender word in the script.
- Soften or drop: "to his" runs together. "dad's" stays short here (the stretch belongs on "tools"). "And his" runs together. "to him" goes soft and low, the h in "him" can almost disappear.
- Rhythm: 7 words, breath, 7 words. Land "polite to him" soft, never trailing.
- Pauses: **the 0.4 s breath before "And" is fixed.** There is about 0.1 s of slack in the window and 0.2 s more before the cut at 0:40.0. **That last 0.2 s is the only spare time in L3, and it belongs at the very end, after "him", never inside the breath.** "And his mom" should start about 0:36.6, on the kitchen.

**L4: "So he went back under the car."** Window 3.2 s, est. 3.0 s. The roomiest line (7 syllables).
- Pace: one plain sentence. Stop. Do not trail off or whisper.
- Stretch: **"back"** lightly, **"car"** (the open "ah", then close the r clean and stop).
- Soften: "So he" runs together. "under the" runs together.
- Pauses: none inside the line. **About 0.2 s of spare time here**, best spent on "car". If it runs to 0:44.3, it still ends before the ball at 0:45.0. Note it for the editor.

**L4-cut: "Brandon went back under the car."** Window 2.6 s, est. 2.6 s. Recorded separately. No "So".
- Pace: same plain read as L4, a hair brisker on "Brandon went".
- Stretch: **"back"** lightly, **"car"** (vowel, then a clean r and stop). Not "Brandon": its second syllable stays short.
- Pauses: none inside. **No extra tenth.** Anything past 0:09.4 eats the quiet daylight before the ball. Up to 0:09.8 is accepted in 01. Past that, tell the editor.

## 3. Pronunciation and cadence guide

**General knowledge about East Texas speech. Confirm by ear. Not measured. Not a transcription of any person.** Hedged where we are not sure. These are cues you can try, not a list of features to put on. Every feature is about sound only: the caption spelling never changes.

| Feature | What it is | Script word it attaches to | Use it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long, flat "i" (the "ai" glide flattened toward "ah") | The vowel in words like "polite" and "time" loses its glide and stretches. | **"polite"** is the only candidate ("time" is not in the script). Many Southern speakers keep more of the glide before t, k or p; rural East Texas speakers often flatten it anyway. | **Not added on "polite".** Natural speech only. If your own speech flattens it, fine; do not reach for it. |
| Stretched vowels on stressed words (the drawl) | Stressed vowels get longer. | **"years"** (lightly, at most), **"car"**, **"home"**. | Yes, stressed words only, per Section 2b. |
| "a" breaking (the "a" in "cat" sliding into a second sound) | Short "a" gets a glide after it. | **"back"**, the first syllable of **"Brandon"**, "dad's" in L3. Not "dad" in L2, which stays flat. | Lightly, and spread thin. No single word carries the accent alone. |
| Fronted "oo" | The "oo" vowel moves forward in the mouth. | **"Two"**, **"grew"**. Not **"tools"**: before an l, this vowel usually does not move forward. | Light, if at all. This fronting is now common across American English, so it adds little regional color. |
| The vowel in "home" | "home" can glide. | **"home"**. | By ear only. ("mom" is not a marker: most American speakers already say it with the "ah" vowel. Say it normally.) |
| Rhotic r (the r is said) | Keep the r. | **"car"**, **"under"**, **"Turner"**, **"there"**, **"years"**. | Yes. Older rural East Texas speech was partly r-less, so this is not absolute, but keeping the r is the cue for this read. |
| Pen/pin merger | The "e" and "i" sound the same before n or m. | **No word in the script forms a pen/pin pair**, so nothing can be misheard. A merged speaker would naturally say **"went"** with the vowel of "win". | Do not force it, do not add a word to show it off. If your natural speech does it on "went", that is fine. Caption stays "went". |
| "ain't" | Not in the script, left out. | n/a | Do not add. |
| Softened "-ing" endings | "-ing" said as "-in". | **No -ing word in the script.** | Does not apply. |

**Against caricature.** The test is a real man talking to someone across a table, not a performance of an accent. If you can hear yourself "doing the voice", back off by half. Calm and plain beats thick and colorful every time. The register is still a man reading a book, not a character in it.

- **Listen-back check:** on Take 2 and Take 3, ask "does this sound like a truck ad or a car ad?" (the deep, slow, warm heartland ad read). If yes, use the take with less depth and less slowing.
- **No real voices as reference.** Keep any real person's voice, ad or narrator out of performer notes and out of any voice-tool prompt, if a temp voice is ever made. No "do it like" anyone.

## 4. Recording plan: 3 takes, then stop

**Room checklist (from 01 Section 3):**
- [ ] Smallest, softest room. A closet full of clothes is best. AC, fans, TV off. Dog out.
- [ ] Phone on airplane mode, voice memo app, phone on a pillow or books, not in your hand.
- [ ] One hand-width (6 to 8 inches) from your mouth, phone a little off to the side.
- [ ] Record **10 seconds of room tone first**, nobody talking.
- [ ] Mistake: stop, wait two seconds, start that line again. Keep rolling.
- [ ] Send the original file **untrimmed**. Not through Facebook or WhatsApp.

**Order of lines each take:** L1a, L2, L3, L4, L4-cut.

| Take | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Take 1: your own voice** | Read every line the way you talk, no accent work and no timing worry. This is the baseline. |
| **Take 2: window fit** | Use the cues in Section 2c (try the East Texas cues only where they come easy). Count the 1.0 s and take the 0.4 s breath. Do not time while reading. Afterward, play back the voice memo and time each line against its window (L1a 3.1 s, L2 3.6 s, L3 6.6 s, L4 3.2 s, L4-cut 2.6 s). |
| **Take 3: calm and clean** | One calm, plain read. Then compare all three on playback. |

**Stop rule:**
- Stop after **3 takes per line.** Never a 4th take.
- Stop the whole session at **20 minutes**, or sooner if your voice tires.
- Pick the take that fits its window **and** sounds like a person. If none fits, pick the one that sounds most like a person, write down how long it ran (from playback), and send that note with the file.
- If the accent takes over a take, the plain take wins.
- **Never fix a long take by speeding up.**
