## 3. The cover opener (Zac's idea), designed

### The rules this has to live inside

- Draft 3.2 Section 6: use the cover as is. Do not retouch, recolor, redraw, cut into layers, or run it through any AI tool. Nothing typed over the cover. Optional Scale 96 to 100 and no push past 100.
- Spoiler fence (Section 0): no child in present-day shots, no "father, dad, son, daughter, kid, child" words, no names Logan, Holly, Lauren, no pregnancy, accident or grandson. The cover itself shows a toddler under the car with the man. That is Ian's public cover and stays untouched. We never push in on the toddler.
- AVAILABLE NOW and the URL are typed in Premiere in a band, not on the cover.

### Two ways to make a cover "come to life", with a recommendation

| | A. Alive by dissolve into footage (RECOMMENDED) | B. Alive by cutting the artwork into layers and moving them |
|---|---|---|
| How | The cover holds, then a view-only push toward the grille, then it dissolves into clip V2, the same car from the same angle. The cover file is never changed | Cut the car, the man and the toddler out of the cover, rebuild the background, move the layers apart |
| Cover file altered | No | Yes. This is cutting into the artwork and redrawing hidden areas. Breaks the cover rule |
| Toddler | Never singled out | Would be separated and animated. Against the fence advice "do not push in on the toddler" |
| Cost | Zero. Premiere only | Photoshop and After Effects work, the background fill needs Generative Fill (credits) or hand painting |
| Needs | Zac yes on a view-only push past 100 (Section 6, decision 1) | Ian's OK as cover owner, and a Zac decision. I recommend NO |

I built the plan on A. B is queued as a decision with a NO recommendation.

### Why A works: the match

The cover's car front (grille 750 px wide, horse emblem upper right, bumper and jack-stand-style stance) lines up with V2's first frame (car nose-on, grille about 760 px wide in 1920). A push toward the grille on the cover ends on nearly the same picture as V2's first frame. The dissolve hides the rest. V2's ball does not enter until about 1 s of the clip, so the dissolve (about 0.7 s) lands before the ball shows.

Numbers (starting values, adjust by eye, nothing tested in Premiere):

- 16:9 sequence (1920x1080). Place the cover at 100 percent (1365x2048) so the whole artwork height is not in view. For the opening hold, instead set Scale 52.7 (1080 divided by 2048) so the full cover shows with black at the sides, nothing cut off. Push from Scale 52.7 to about 207 over 1.2 s, anchored on the horse emblem (approx. cover x 1190, y 830 in the cover's own pixels). That lands the car front about the size of V2's.
- 9:16 sequence (1080x1920). Hold at Scale 79.1 (1080 divided by 1365) with 150 px of black above and below (cover 1620 tall). Push to about 158 percent anchored on the emblem. The window then ends near cover y 250 to 1450 and never reaches the toddler (his head starts near y 1450).
- Dissolve: Cross Dissolve about 0.7 s, starting about 0.3 s before the push finishes.
- The push enlarges the cover 2x in 16:9. The jpg is soft. At that speed it is a blur in motion. If Ian sends the original, use it.

### Strict fallback (if Zac says no push past 100)

Hold the full cover, 6 frame dip to black on a ratchet click, then V2. No match, less magic, completely within the rules.

### Sound for the opening

0:00 black, one low held note (music or a recorded pad, license first). 0:01 a single wrench clink on the cover. From 0:03 garage hum fades up and the ratchet starts under V2, off camera. The ball rolls with its own sound (record a baseball on concrete on the phone, the Adobe library had no roll). At 0:08 the ratchet stops dead as V1 shows him lower the wrench.

### 4b. The same 45 s in 9:16

Same order and sound. Differences:

Pan the window per shot, approximately:
  - V2: window center from 35 percent of the frame width to 50 percent as the ball rolls in
  - V1: about 55 percent (his torso, arms and the wrench)

  - 9Sa: about 40 percent (ball and hand)

- If the 4:5 look is too repurposed, the devils-advocate note is to use full-bleed crops where the subject is centered. That costs a 1.78x enlargement. Test one clip on a phone before deciding.
