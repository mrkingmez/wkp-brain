## 1. Premise

Generic logline the words now sell: a man comes home from the war, and the boy who used to hand up the wrench is back under the car.

The bare "soldier comes home and struggles" list from pass 1 (Man Down 2015, Thank You for Your Service 2017, Home of the Brave 2006, Brothers 2009, Coming Home 1978, Landing Home) still fits the hook alone. It no longer fits the cut. Every caption after the hook is about the wrench, the hand or the car.

One premise neighbor found this pass, in the book market rather than film: Tom Fowler, The Mechanic (John Tyler thriller, 2020), sold as an Army veteran with PTSD starting work as a classic car mechanic. Same two nouns, different book: an action thriller where the past "casts a long shadow." A cold trailer viewer would not name it, and nothing in these words echoes its copy. Logged as a watch item for Amazon category and keyword copy, not a finding here.

What makes this not that list: the childhood memory turned present-tense callback (handed up the wrench then, holds it now). No veteran film or ad I have on record runs that hinge.

## 2. Structure

45 s: hook (C1), memory (C2), 28 seconds with no words, final line on black (C4), card. Universal trailer shape. Removing all mid captions takes it further from the veteran-PSA skeleton pass 1 and the 2026-11-10 memory warned about.

15 s: hook, memory, present callback, card. Pass 1 found hook, thesis, card, the veteran-drama skeleton in miniature. That is gone. Then-and-now with a repeated object is a universal device, not a borrowed reveal order.

Checked each cutdown on its own beat order, per the standing lesson that fixes in the master get undone in cutdowns. Both hold.

## 6. The counter (strongest case for originality)

This is now the most specific copy this trailer has had. Every line after the hook is about an object only this story has: the nickname, the wrench, the hand, the car. The hook is deliberately plain, Ian's own framing, and it refuses the punchy "war he never left" tagline the genre runs on. The 15 s now works as a closed loop (then, now) instead of ending on a thesis. The 45 s carries its feeling in pictures for 28 seconds with no words, which is the opposite of the veteran-PSA style. None of the five lines or the four pairs has a findable owner. The honest weak spot is that the hook alone is generic, and the draft already handles that by never letting it stand alone in the video.
