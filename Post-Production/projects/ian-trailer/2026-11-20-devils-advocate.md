# Devils advocate: Ian "Wrench Turner" trailer Draft 3.3 (2026-11-20)

VERDICT: GO WITH CHANGES. Zac's order holds. Keep it.

THE ONE THING: the memory beat can leak the secret by itself. Beat 1 is an adult under a car, then we dissolve to a boy beside an older man under a car. A cold viewer can read the older man as Brandon now, with a kid. That is the twist. The memory has to read as the PAST at a glance, not as a guess.

## Reasons and fixes

1. **Hook (0 to 2 s).** With the sound off, V1 looks like car content, not a book. The viewer gets no question until 0:36. Fix (order kept): burn in a short line over V1 from frame one, built from the back cover, e.g. "He came home from the war." Add the ratchet clicks over the first frame for anyone with sound on. The final line should stop at "a surprising secret changes everything" and drop "even something broken can be fixed". That half hands over the ending, so it breaks teasers-not-verdicts.

2. **Fence check, beat by beat.**
   - Memory (beats 2 to 4): add period markers so it reads as past. Older truck or tools, dated clothes, warm grade, heavy grain, and a slightly narrower frame. The book's memory happens under the dad's TRUCK. The older man must not share Brandon's wardrobe from V1. Caption 1 must not say "son" or "dad".
   - Homecoming (H1): mom and dad only. No children in the background. No lone young woman, which could read as a wife.
   - Sweetheart (W1): one woman, turning away from him. Never two women and never a pair of girls, because that points at the twins. A crowd watching is fine and true to the book.
   - Keep R4, V2 and 9Sa OUT. A ball in the present day means someone rolled it, and the book uses that roll to bring in the fenced child.

3. **PTSD glimpse.** A haunted face in shadow is the stock "broken vet" shot, used for a 2.5 s jolt. Veterans will clock that. The book's real beat is brotherhood: Brandon drives to Oklahoma, and his father goes with him. Fix: two men sitting in silence on a tailgate or porch, a hand on a shoulder. No weapon, no head in hands, no breakdown. Do not shoot it until Ian says yes.

4. **Ending.** Zac is right. End under the car. Ending on the present ball (V2 or 9Sa) all but points at the secret. It also turns the trailer into "who rolled it?", which gets the guessing started.

5. **15 s cut.** It has no war in it. It plays as a quiet car-and-memory piece and drops the selling point. Fix (three beats kept): over beat 3 (V1 lying still), burn in the same opening line or the "real journey begins once he comes home" line, then the card.

6. **The six NEED shots** (cut what you can, credits stay at zero):
   - S6, boy slides in: CUT. R3 already carries the boy, and less screen time for the boy means less fence risk.
   - S3, hand close-up: CUT. Punch into V1 at 0 to 3 s, since 1080 gives room for the crop. Or Zac shoots it on his phone with any wrench.
   - G1, he stands in the garage: CUT the new shot. Use a slow push on R1, and drop "covered" so the cover piece's look is not repeated.
   - H1, homecoming: KEEP. This is the one true need, so write its prompt first.
   - W1, sweetheart: KEEP.
   - B1, battle buddy: KEEP only if Ian says yes. Otherwise cut it and give its 2.5 s to H1.
   That leaves two or three new shots, not six.

7. **Dead tail.** Black text plus the card runs 9 s, a fifth of the runtime. Cut it to 3 s of text and a 4 s card, and give the 2 s back to H1.

8. **Two homecomings.** Beat 6 (Afghanistan) and beat 9 (retirement) will read as one trip home. That is acceptable compression, so do not add a caption to explain it.

## What is working
- The wrench open and the wrench close make a real loop. Keep both.
- V4 coming back is right. Graded as memory, it is the emotional core.
- The garage beat cuts straight under the car, so the cover piece's door rise is not repeated.
- The fence rules in the brief are tight. The leak in point 2 comes from how the shots will read, not from anything the brief says.
