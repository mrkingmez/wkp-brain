originality agent, 2026-11-20. Reviewed: `words-draft-01.md` (45 s captions, 15 s captions, end card, description template, Ask-Ian text). Read cold, then searched the hook, the 15 s line and the final line.

## BLUF

VERDICT: **ships with changes.**

- The 45 s cut ships. "He came home from the war." is plain and nobody owns it. "The car was the easy part." has no owner I could find and is tied to this trailer's last shot.
- The 15 s cut and the description hook do not ship as is. The pair "He came home from the war. / Home was harder." is the logline of the whole veteran-homecoming genre. Each half is fine alone. Together a cold viewer reads it as the stock "the real war starts at home" line that Man Down, Home of the Brave, Thank You for Your Service and Landing Home all sell on.
- Fix shape (no replacement line written here): the second line of the 15 s has to be about this man's hands, the car or the wrench, not about home being hard. The thing that makes this book not that list is the wrench. The 15 s throws it away on its one answer line.

## 1. Premise

Generic logline the words sell: a soldier comes home from war and finds home harder than the fight.

Works that fit that sentence: Man Down (2015), Thank You for Your Service (2017), Home of the Brave (2006), Brothers (2009), The War at Home (1979), Coming Home (1978), Soldier's Home (1977), Landing Home, plus reintegration docs (Searching for Home, When I Came Home 2006). Long list. The premise does no work alone.

What makes this script not that list: the car and the "Wrench Turner" memory. The 45 s cut uses both (C2, then C4 on the return to the car). The 15 s cut uses the Wrench Turner caption, then drops back to the generic thesis on C3.

## 2. Structure

45 s: hook, childhood memory, wordless homecoming and glimpses, line on black, card. Universal trailer shape, no borrowed reveal order. Clean.

15 s: hook, memory, "Home was harder.", card. Hook then thesis then card is the veteran-drama trailer skeleton in its shortest form. Same note memory already holds from 2026-11-10: the short cutdowns keep drifting toward the generic veteran-PSA shape. It happened again here, through words this time instead of pictures.

## 3. Character

Words only, so little to judge. The captions keep "he" unnamed and say nothing about his inner state except C3. C3 is what makes him a type: the returning soldier who can't settle. C1, C2 and C4 make him a person: a man who was a kid handing up a wrench and is back under a car.

Picture plan seen through the Ask-Ian text (not this pass's main job, logged as watch items):
- Uniform hug with parents out front = the viral military-homecoming-reunion video look. Fine as a beat. Do not slow it or score it like those videos.
- Two men on a tailgate, hand on shoulder = the stock buddy-support image. Already captionless, which helps.
- Girl across a bonfire turning away = generic small-town-romance glimpse. No single source.

## 5. Changes required

1. 15 s C3: replace "Home was harder." with a line about the car, the wrench or the hands. Shape only, room-writer writes it.
2. Description template hook: do not ship the C1 plus C3 pair. Use whatever the 15 s lands on, or C1 alone.

Nothing else blocks.

## 6. The counter (strongest case for originality)

The hook is honest and Ian's own framing, and plainness is a real choice: it refuses the punchy veteran-drama taglines instead of copying one. "Wrench Turner" is a private phrase from the book that no other trailer can use. "The car was the easy part" is unsearchable as a tagline, belongs to this story's object, and leaves the ending open. The draft already cut the two weakest lines, both stock (the back-cover "secret" line and "Even something broken can be fixed"), for good reasons. And "Home was harder" as an exact phrase is not on any poster I found. The case against it is pairing and genre, not a lift. A fair reader could call it commonplace, not copied. I still flag it, because the test is whether a viewer can name the shelf, and with C1 in front of it they can.
