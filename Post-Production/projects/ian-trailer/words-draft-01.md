### (a) Hook line, two options

| Option | Line | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (PICK) | He came home from the war. | Built from Ian's own back cover ("his real journey begins once he comes home"). Sound off, it tells a scroller this is a soldier's story, not car content, in six words. Over a man alone under a car it raises the question: and then what? The homecoming hug at 0:16 pays it off. | Plain. That is the point, but it leans on the picture to make the question. |
| B | He survived the war. | Sharper. Plants doubt right away. | Leads straight into the "but not the peace" reading that a lot of veteran stories open with. Ian's book also has a wounding and two years of rehab, so "survived" is true but loaded. |

Pick A. It is Ian's framing, it does not editorialize about veterans, and it sets up the 15 s pair (C1 then "Home was harder.").

### (b) Wrench Turner caption

Approved line: They called him the "Wrench Turner."
Draft pick: **Back then, he was / the "Wrench Turner."**

Reason for the change (a real one, from devils-advocate's ONE THING): the memory beat can read as present-day Brandon with a child, which is the secret. "Back then" puts a date on the picture in two words. It ties the boy to the man we just saw under the car (the boy is him, years ago) and pushes the older hand into the past. The approved line also points at the boy, but nothing in it says "past."
Small accuracy note on the approved line: in Ian's summary only one person calls him that. "They called him" reads as several people. Minor, but the new line avoids it.
If Zac or Ian prefers the approved line, use it unchanged. Same timing either way. Both pass the fence. "Wrench Turner" is Ian's phrase and cleared by Ian.

### (c) Homecoming and glimpses: no captions

Beats 6 to 11 (0:16.0 to 0:38.0) run clean. Reasons:
- The hook already said "he came home." A caption on the hug would repeat it.
- Any caption on W1 (the sweetheart) makes her the story. Paired with a "secret" anywhere in the trailer, a viewer could guess she is part of it. Silence keeps her a glimpse.
- Any caption on B1 tells a veteran how to feel about a brotherhood moment. Two men and a hand on a shoulder say it.
- Devils-advocate point 8: do not caption the two homecomings apart. The compression is fine.

If the cut feels too long without text, the one allowed spare is "Home was harder." over 0:22.5 to 0:27.5 (B1 into W1). Default is off.

### (d) Final line on black, 0:38 to 0:41

| Line | Source | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Even something broken can be fixed. | Ian's back cover | DROPPED. Devils-advocate is right. Over a man fixing a car, it tells the viewer he will be fixed. That is the ending, so it breaks teasers-not-verdicts. |
| **The car was the easy part.** (PICK) | Ours | Plays off the last shot (he is back under the car, working). Says the car will get fixed and leaves the man open. No secret, no ending, no relationship word, and it could not sit on another book's trailer. |
| His real journey begins / once he comes home. (BACK-COVER ALTERNATIVE) | Ian's exact words | Safe, no ending, no secret. Weakness: the hook already says "came home," so "home" lands twice in 41 seconds. Use this one if Ian wants his own words on screen. |
| A surprising secret / changes everything. | Ian's exact words | NOT recommended. It invites guessing, and the only woman on screen is the sweetheart turning away, so "secret" plus her face points viewers toward "she had his baby," which sits close to the real secret. Also reads as stock trailer copy. |
