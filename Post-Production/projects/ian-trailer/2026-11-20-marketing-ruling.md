## no-ai-slop judgment pass (marketing-director, on the final copy above)

Mechanical pass: slop_check.py on this file, result recorded below.

Judgment, line by line:
- "He came home from the war." Plain, short, specific to this book's back-cover framing. Could it sit in another trailer? A veteran-homecoming trailer could use the same four words, which is the one soft spot. It is saved by what comes next: the wrench. The pair "home from the war" plus "the wrench" is the book. The writing room's pass 2 barred pairing the hook with a "home was hard" line, which would have made it stock. Kept as is.
- "The old wrench still fit his hand." Concrete object, concrete body part, no abstraction. Not generic. Slightly soft is "old", but it is doing the work of the antecedent.
- "The car was the easy part." The construction "X was the easy part" is a common spoken pattern. It is not a trailer cliche and it is attached to a specific object. No AI-tell phrase. Passes. If a later lane fails it, the fallback is the writing room's alternate ending, not a new line.
- "The Wrench Turner by Ian Griffin. Available now." Title, name, fact. Not generic, not padded.
- "The scenes in this trailer were made with AI tools." Plain disclosure, intentionally flat. It should be flat.
- Hashtags and YouTube titles: the title and the format only. Nothing that reads as generated growth-speak.
- No em dashes, no AI-tell vocabulary, no throat-clearing opener, no question-then-answer, no triplets, no promo adjectives.
- Fence: no child, no family word, no pregnancy or twin hint, nothing says whose car. Checked every line of the final copy above.

Judgment pass result: PASS.
