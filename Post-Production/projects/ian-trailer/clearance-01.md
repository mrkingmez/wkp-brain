## Judgment calls

### J1. Ford and Mustang (trademark)
- **Words:** clean. No Ford, Mustang, model year or badge text in any caption, card, description or hashtag guidance (lines 101, 140). Ian's summary says "Mustang" (summary line 10), and the draft does not carry it over. That is correct.
- **Pictures:** the brief describes a red Mustang in V1, R1, R2 (brief lines 28 to 29). These are AI images, so they may show the running-horse emblem, grille badge or lettering. The car is the setting, not the product being sold. It is shown being restored, not crashing, failing or doing anything criminal, so I see no false-light angle. But a book trailer is advertising, which carries more exposure than editorial use. The final 45 s line, "The car was the easy part" (line 28), puts the car into the copy as a subject, which pulls the brand a step closer to the center of the piece. If the emblem can be read in any frame, the de-badge question from pass 2 (2026-11-04) comes back. Frame-check V1, R1, R2 for a readable emblem or lettering.
- **End card on the cover (lines 29, 85, 94 to 101):** the earlier passes (2026-11-04 package line 647) found Ford marks on the cover and recommended a type-only end card on black. This draft puts the end card back on the cover. Using Ian's published cover to sell Ian's book matches how the book is already marketed, so the trailer does not create the Ford question. It does repeat it in video ads. Still an attorney question (Q1).

### J2. Cover art (cover maker rights)
- The end card uses the cover in both cuts and both ratios. Ask-Ian item 3 (line 111) asks who made it and whether we may use it. Good. A cover license often covers the printed book and standard retail listings but not always video or ads. The answer has to say whether video and social ads are covered, not just "yes, it's fine."
- Item 4 (line 112) asks whether the two people on the cover are real or stock. Good, and correctly framed as a rights question. If they are real people, putting their faces in a video ad is a right of publicity question separate from the cover license.
- The separate 9:16 cover story piece (cover "comes alive," settled memory line 15) animates the cover. That is a new version of the artwork, a bigger ask than showing it still. Not in this file, but the item 3 answer should cover it too.

### J3. Real people
- **Ian Griffin** is named in the description (line 130) as the author. He consents and he posts. No issue I can see.
- **The battle buddy in Oklahoma (Ask-Ian item 5, line 113).** The book is billed as a "life story" (back cover, summary line 12), and Ian told Zac about this scene himself. If it is drawn from a real event, the battle buddy is a real, living, possibly identifiable veteran whose crisis and grief would show up in an ad. That brings privacy and false-light exposure, separate from copyright. The proposed shot is low-key (no combat, nobody breaking down), which helps. The text should also ask whether that person is real and would be OK being shown, even as a stand-in. It does not ask that now.
- No other named living person in the words.

### J4. AI disclosure (YouTube, TikTok, Facebook)
- Ask-Ian item 8 (line 116) says "Your accounts, your call. I'd turn it on." The description's AI line is marked OPTIONAL (line 135).
- YouTube's published policy (help page "Disclosing use of GenAI content") requires disclosure of realistic altered or synthetic content a viewer could mistake for a real person, place or event. A realistic AI soldier homecoming, a man under a car and a bonfire party all fit that. TikTok's rules similarly require labeling realistic AI images and video. Meta has its own "AI info" labeling. I did not pull the current Meta rule this run, so not verified.
- TASKS.md (Ian trailer table) records that Ian has already said no label is needed. Item 8 should not be read as backing that up.
- The flag: telling Ian it is his call understates it. On at least two platforms it looks required for realistic AI scenes. The poster carries the duty, and YouTube may add a label itself. The search results I got were dated 2023 to 2025. I did not confirm whether either policy changed after that.
- Earlier passes also noted that pure AI footage may get little or no copyright protection. That matters if someone re-uses the trailer. It is not a disclosure issue, just a known gap.

### J5. Music credit slot (line 133, line 139)
- The slot is a placeholder. The track is not picked. TASKS shows the two candidates as not proven: "searching-for-hope" has no license file on disk, and the Alex Morgan FMA track (CC BY) has an unchecked track page and AI label.
- Things to settle before the slot is filled:
  - A book trailer is commercial use. A CC BY-NC track would not fit. Plain CC BY allows commercial use with a credit, so the credit line becomes required, not optional.
  - Many subscription licenses cover only the subscriber's own channels. Ian posts on his accounts, not Zac's. The license has to cover Ian as the poster, or Ian gets Content ID claims.
  - "Delete this line if the license needs no credit" (line 133) is fine as an instruction, but only once someone has read the license.
- This is a licensing cost and paperwork question, not a copyright problem in the words.
