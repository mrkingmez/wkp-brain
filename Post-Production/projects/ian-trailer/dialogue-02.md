## Captions

**C2, "Back then, / he was the Wrench Turner."** (lines 29, 73)
Holds. Read out loud it is one easy breath, the pause sits where the comma is, and the capitals carry the name without the sneer the quotes had. After "He came home from the war," "Back then" reads as before the war, which is what it is for. No dialogue flag. (Who "he" is in the memory picture is a picture and fence call, see HANDOFF.)

**C3, "The wrench still / fit his hand."** (line 74)
- The words hold. Plain, past tense like C1 and C2, six words, no moral attached. It shows instead of tells, which is what the old line failed at.
- Problem, small, line break: line one ends on "still," and on a still frame of a man lying still, the eye can read "The wrench still" as "the wrench is not moving" for half a beat before "fit his hand" turns it into "still fits." It is the same hang I flagged on the old C2 ("he was" left dangling). The draft says "still" does the teasing, and the pun is fine, but the break makes the reader stumble on it rather than catch it.
- Reaching for: the tool from the memory is still his; the man is not.
- Proposal: break it "The wrench / still fit his hand." Same words, the adverb lands with its verb. Check fit at 64 pt on 9:16.
- Note, not a flag: "wrench" lands in C2 and C3 about 1.3 s apart. That echo is the point of the line (draft line 79) and it reads as a tie, not a repeat. Leave it.

## Ask-Ian text (Zac to Ian)

Overall: it sounds like Zac now. The open ("trailer's coming along... whenever you get a sec"), item 5 ("Your site, Amazon, somewhere else?"), item 7 ("hugging his folks out front. Anything I got wrong?"), item 9 and the close ("That's it. Thanks man.") are a guy texting a guy he has known a while. Item 6's tailgate line is still the best voice in the text.

**Pattern: question stacks in items 3, 4 and 6**
- Item 4 (line 109) asks five things: who made the cover, whether the deal covers video and ads, it announces the animated cover clip, whether the two people are real or stock, and asks for a bigger file. Item 6 (line 111) asks how heavy the scene gets, pitches the shot, asks if the man is real, then asks if he would be OK with it. Item 3 is the same shape with three.
- Problem, too clean on paper, too much in a text: each one reads fine alone, but a real reply to a stack like this answers the first question and the last. Ian will text back "Buddy of mine did it, he's cool" and the license question goes unanswered. Answering by number does not save it, because each number holds four answers.
- Reaching for: getting every permission in one message so the trailer is not stuck waiting.
- Proposal: keep nine items, but let each item end on one question mark that matters most, and move the rest into a follow-up text after Ian answers. For item 4 the one that matters is whether the cover deal reaches video and ads; for item 6, how heavy it gets and whether the man is real.

**Item 3 repeats item 1** (lines 106, 108)
- Problem: item 1 quotes "Back then, he was the Wrench Turner" and asks if it fits; item 3 then asks for an OK to put the same words on screen. A friend asks once. Reading both, Ian may think the first was the real ask and the second is formality, or answer "yep" to 1 and skip 3, which is the one that needs to be in writing.
- Reaching for: a fit check and a written permission, which are two different things.
- Proposal: make item 3 the only place Wrench Turner is asked about, plainly as "OK to put Wrench Turner on screen?", and keep item 1 to the hook and the last line. "Wrench Turner is your title and your line" can stay as the reason; it sounds like Zac being careful with a buddy's work.

**Item 2, "Want your own back-cover line at the end instead? It'd read..."** (line 107)
- Problem, small, nobody would say that to this person: the line is trimmed from his back cover, not his exact words (draft line 63). Calling it "your own back-cover line" to the man who wrote it invites "that's not quite what I wrote."
- Reaching for: offering Ian his own words as the ending.
- Proposal: say it is cut down, along the lines of a shortened version of your back-cover line.
