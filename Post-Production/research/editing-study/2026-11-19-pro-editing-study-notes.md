## 1. How Does an Editor Think and Feel? (Every Frame a Painting, Tony Zhou)
- ID 3Q3eITC01Fg, uploaded 2016-05-12, 9:24, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: none, craft only (timeless).
- Editing is about the eyes: cut on a change in the eyes (a decision being made), then cut to what they look at.
- Emotions take time. Hold before and after a line so the viewer can feel it. Luke's failure on Dagobah: about 15 s building with shorter shots, then about twice that coming down with longer holds. Ant-Man's failure: 30 frames, so we do not believe it.
- Every shot has a natural rhythm; watch it repeatedly until you feel the moment it wants to cut. Murch quoted: editing is "70% about rhythm".
- Invisible cutting is the default; a jarring cut is a choice when the emotion is agitation. Schoonmaker: sometimes hold a little too long, then cut.
- You learn it by cutting: one cut at a time.
- WWD use: a host's reaction (eyes, laugh, eye-roll) is the cut point. Hold the reaction a beat after the punchline before cutting to a picture.

## 2. In the Blink of an Eye explained (StudioBinder, on Walter Murch)
- ID PKYeClvvlTw, uploaded 2024-05-27, 15:05, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: none.
- Rule of Six, in priority: emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, planarity (180 line), 3D continuity. Get the top three right and viewers forgive the bottom three, not the other way round.
- The cut is a blink between two thoughts: cut where a thought completes (Murch found Gene Hackman blinked within a few frames of where he wanted to cut).
- "Dragnet" cutting (cut after every line) is punchy but ignores the nuance of conversation.
- 30-degree rule: a cut to a near-identical framing jars (the bee-hive analogy).
- Watch only what is on screen, the way the audience will. Test viewers know where it fails, not why.
- WWD use: cutting from the two-shot to a picture should land after the host finishes the thought, not mid-word. A jump cut inside the same two-shot framing is the "hive moved two yards" problem; cover it with a picture or a solo cam.

## 3. J-cut and L-cut in Premiere Pro (tutvid, Nathaniel Dodson)
- ID DVSZujJDYXg, uploaded 2017-11-15, 7:35, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: Premiere Pro, version not named (2017 era UI). [CHECK current shortcut names]
- J-cut: the next shot's audio starts before its picture. L-cut: the picture changes, the old audio keeps playing.
- Method shown: Rolling Edit tool (shortcut N), hold Alt to roll only the video side of a linked clip; or hold Ctrl (Cmd on Mac) over an edit point to get the rolling edit cursor, add Alt to affect one track. Add Audio Transitions > Crossfade > Constant Power, keep it short.
- WWD use: when the hosts' talk carries on over a picture, that is already an L-cut on the picture track. The useful J-cut for WWD is bringing a sound effect or the clip's (muted) moment in a few frames before the picture lands.

## 4. 6 Ways to Edit Any Scene (StudioBinder, The Shot List ep 10)
- ID FVR8zz8ci2k, uploaded 2022-02-14, 18:48, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: none.
- Three choices drive every edit: what to show, when to cut, what to cut to.
- Eyeline match (cut to what the person looks at), shot-reverse-shot, inserts, cross-cutting, eye trace (keep the subject where the eye already is, Fury Road kept subjects centered for 1.8 s shots), split edits (J and L), intellectual montage (Kuleshov), cut on action (cut mid-movement so the action hides the cut; cut on impact in fights).
- WWD use: cut to a picture on the host's gesture or head turn (cut on action), and keep the subject of the picture near where the speaking host sits in the two-shot when possible (eye trace).

## 5. What is a Jump Cut (StudioBinder)
- ID 03zw4RNv0gE, uploaded 2021-12-27, 13:38, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: none.
- A jump cut removes the middle of one shot, so time jumps. Pros use it on purpose for style, energy, emotion (a few frames removed in L.A. Confidential and The Departed add agitation) and montage (compress time).
- Axial cut (punch-in): cut straight in or out along the same line, like a zoom, to stress a realization or a punchline.
- Unwanted jump cut fix: change the angle 30 degrees or more, or change the shot size a lot (wide to close-up).
- WWD use: StreamYard gives one fixed angle per host, so every trim in the two-shot is a jump cut. The pro fix with one angle is the axial punch-in: on the solo cam, scale 100 to about 115 to 120 percent at the cut so the trim reads as a deliberate push. Or cover the trim with a picture.

## 6. 7 YouTube Editing Tips to Boost Retention (Nate Curtiss)
- ID ODRJkEGTI_U, uploaded 2024-03-01, 7:28, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: CapCut (sponsored, version not named).
- Do not over-edit: match edit density to the audience. Older audiences need fewer gimmicks; editing supports the content, it is not the show.
- Front-load: put the most work into the first 30 s, then keep pacing steady so there is no cliff after minute 3 or 4 ("flatten the graph").
- Save big transitions for big moments (a section change); otherwise plain cuts.
- Sound design on purpose: a sound for things that move on screen, but not a pop on every caption.
- Music: change the music at section changes; he mixes music about 5 to 25 dB under voice. NOTE: WWD rule is no music under talk (Zac 2026-10-17), so this tip applies only to intro, outro and section stings.
- Give viewers a break: short breathing room between sections.

## 7. How to SOUND DESIGN a Video, step by step (Film Editing Pro)
- ID Wcxw3BPSt3A, uploaded 2023-03-23, 17:06, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: not named (NLE-agnostic).
- Three layers: score (temp music), ambience and Foley (nothing is silent; room tone), creative design (sounds for feelings and camera moves: whooshes, hits, reverse cymbal "suck-back" into a cut, bass drop on the cut).
- Do not reuse one sound for every hit; vary takes. Line the peak of the sound up with the frame using visible waveforms and markers.
- Put reverb on a submix (bus) so all Foley sits in the same room.
- Not every moment gets an accent; if every moment is special none is. Pick key moments.
- Rough level guide he gives (meter dBFS, not LUFS): dialogue around -12 dB, effects -10 to -30 dB, music -20 to -30 dB, nothing peaking. [CHECK: these are peak-meter rules of thumb, not a loudness standard]
- WWD use: the Modern transitions each get a paired sound (WWD rule 2026-10-14); a suck-back into the cut and a hit on the picture landing is the pro way to do it. Keep it to the big moments.

## 8. Paddy Galloway on the #1 YouTube editing error (The Editing Podcast)
- ID Q0zRBsYQnA0, uploaded 2022-10-11, 29:02, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: none. Paddy Galloway is a YouTube strategy consultant (claims in the talk: worked with MrBeast and Jesser; studied 3,000 to 5,000 retention curves 2019 to 2021). These are his statements, not checked.
- His most repeated note to clients: "slow down". Fast cutting that leaves no time to process the important line hurts. Give the viewer time on the point that matters.
- The first 30 to 60 s is the most critical stretch. He moved a stronger later section to the front ("open heart surgery") and reports 88 percent retention at 30 s, against a typical 30 to 40 percent drop by 30 s. Reported by him, not verified.
- Watch your own video as a viewer, all the way through, with distractions around you (the "distraction test"); have someone else note the time each time they get bored.
- Write the skeleton: list the 8 or so progressions of the video and ask whether each one is in the right place.
- Transitions between storylines are where videos sag: when one storyline ends, hand the viewer the next one fast, then you can riff.
- Edit density depends on the audience; older audiences tolerate slower pacing. Study traditional film, take parts of it, not all of it.
- Keep telling the stories that dipped, but tell them better; do not just cut every dip.
- WWD use: WWD's audience is adult movie fans; the reviews do not need MrBeast pacing. They need the intro to land fast, a clear next-topic signal between segments, and no long stretch where nothing changes on screen.

## 9. Mastering Multi-Camera Editing in Premiere Pro (Premiere Gal)
- ID KnlPOB34Fng, uploaded 2025-03-04, 13:18, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: Premiere Pro, version not named in captions (2025 era). [CHECK names in 2026 and 2027]
- Select the camera files plus audio, right-click, Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence, Synchronize by Audio, camera names from clip names. Right-click the source sequence, New Sequence From Clip. Turn on Multi-Camera view in the Program monitor (Button Editor + if missing). Press 1, 2, 3 during playback; cuts land on the timeline; Ctrl-drag a cut to roll it.
- Grade each angle once inside the multicam source sequence, and every cut inherits it.
- Long recordings split into several files: nest them into one sequence per camera first.
- WWD use, the biggest workflow win found: StreamYard already gives three synced views (the two-shot main recording, the King-Z cam, the Winter Wolf cam). As a 3-angle multicam Zac can switch cams by tapping 1, 2, 3 while watching, instead of cutting solo cams in by hand.

## 10. The Loudness Secret to Mixing YouTube Videos (Creative Video Tips, Chadwick)
- ID Rp-6F8SWFSY, uploaded 2021-10-12, 14:44, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: DaVinci Resolve 17 (named). Later Resolve versions may rename menus. [CHECK]
- His claims: YouTube turns loud videos down to a -14 LUFS integrated reference and does not turn quiet ones up; Stats for nerds shows "content loudness". Aim near -14 LUFS integrated, true peak no higher than about -1.5 dBTP, ITU BS.1770-4. [CHECK against Google]
- Method: loudness meter (integrated and true peak), compressor on dialogue (ratio 3 or lower normally), limiter on the main bus just under the ceiling, then raise gain into it. Peak normalization is not loudness normalization.
- WWD use: Zac's reviews measure -20.5 to -24.7 LUFS integrated, so by this source YouTube plays them 6.5 to 10.7 dB quieter than a -14 LUFS video and does not lift them. Four of eight peak over 0 dBTP. A master limiter plus a loudness target fixes both.

## 11. The Ultimate Guide to Editing B-Roll for YouTube (Katie Steckly)
- ID pW4WBaIGqqg, uploaded 2025-02-12, 21:19, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: DaVinci Resolve (free), version not named.
- Cut the talking head first (remove pauses by the waveform), then lay B-roll from a script that pairs visuals with lines. Land the cut on the word that names the thing.
- B-roll clips 2 to 5 s. Start a clip mid-motion. Slow keyframed movement on static shots, keyframes on the very first and last frame. Cut to beat markers but break the pattern sometimes.
- WWD use: Zac's 5 s median hold is inside her band. The miss is the gaps between pictures, not the hold length.

## 12. Iconic movie trailers, explained by a trailer editor (Vox, Bill Neil of Buddha Jones)
- ID r01fv4IXZ68, uploaded 2023-02-17, 11:44, pulled 2026-11-19. Software: none.
- YouTube trailers open with a "bumper" of the best moments so people do not click away. Sound is "the soul of the trailer": signature sounds, rises, silence before a hit. Rug pull: start one tone, flip it. Rhythmic cutting on the beat.
- Sell the puzzle by showing four pieces, never the whole picture (matches teasers-not-verdicts).
- WWD and Ian trailer use: the 6 s sting is a bumper; build one silence-then-hit moment; end before the answer.
