## 1. What world-class looks like (tagged)
1. Dialogue has an anchor, and good lines contain small reversals, not a straight statement. SOURCED (S1).
2. Repeating thoughts in dialogue are a defect, not texture. SOURCED (S1).
3. Many-voice scenes are written as parallel tracks, each person following their own thread. SOURCED (S1).
4. Viewers leave at dips, so the compelling content goes early and the first 30 seconds is the measured intro. SOURCED (S2).
5. A documentary piece needs a thesis or guiding principle that drives the structure. COMMUNITY (S4, secondary report of a named editor).
6. What is left unsaid can carry the weight. COMMUNITY (S5).
7. Teasers lead with the hook and withhold the answer. PROVEN as house rule (root CLAUDE.md gate 3, Ian trailer pass logs); not from an outside source.

## 4. What this changes in our work
- Every dialogue pass asks "what is the anchor of this line" and flags a line with no anchor (S1).
- Repetition check: any two lines that restate one thought get cut or merged, even when the repeat is "emphasis" (S1).
- A pass 2 note must name what changed and why, and cut repeated thoughts first (S1, house rule).
- Teasers and Shorts: the compelling item goes in the opening seconds, the 30 second mark is checked as the measured intro (S2). Title and hook words go early (YouTube thumbnails and titles page).
- Documentary and Den Files scripts state the spine as a thesis in the brief before drafting (S4, PROPOSED as a rule for us).
- Memorize-aloud test: read every line aloud as a speaker would have to learn it (S3, PROPOSED, S3 gives only the summary line).
