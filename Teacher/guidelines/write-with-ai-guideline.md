## How to Write with Generative AI "Right"

- Use AI as a collaborative partner, not a ghostwriter. Treat the model as a research assistant, sounding board, or editor for brainstorming, outlining, and structuring. Do not let it replace your core voice or critical thinking.
- Break writing into stages. Engage the AI across distinct phases rather than asking for a single-shot output:
  1. Brainstorming: generate angles, thematic lists, or dialogue ideas.
  2. Outlining: ask the model to map out a structural skeleton based on your premise.
  3. Drafting: write sections yourself, or prompt the AI with explicit constraints, tone guidelines, and mentor texts (examples of the exact style you want to emulate).
  4. Revising: use the AI to check for pacing, expand specific sentences, or flag structural repetition.
- Refine your prompts with explicit context. Vague prompts yield generic, formulaic prose. Always specify:
  - The goal (for example: "rewrite this paragraph to sound punchy and journalistic").
  - The target audience (for example: "technical readers who understand networking").
  - Constraints (for example: "avoid buzzwords like 'delve,' 'tapestry,' or 'testament' and keep sentences under 20 words").
- Manage context windows carefully. For a long project like a book or a multi-part series, do not dump raw manuscripts into a single chat window, because it dilutes the model's focus and misses nuance. Feed it chapter summaries or modular outlines instead.
- Fact-check and edit relentlessly. Generative models predict patterns, so they frequently fabricate statistics, historical details, or citations (hallucinations). Always verify claims independently.
