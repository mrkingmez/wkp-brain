# Write with AI, 3: drafting with context

About 12 minutes to read, plus about 50 to do the practice

## What you will be able to do

- Give the AI a goal, an audience and rules before it touches your draft.
- Use your own writing as the model for your voice, and never another author's.
- Draft your piece yourself and use the AI for the one paragraph that sticks.

## What you will make

A full first draft of your piece, written by you from your outline, with a style note built from your own words and one stuck paragraph that you rewrote after seeing what the AI offered.

## Context is everything

Drafting is where a thin message costs the most. Three things turn a drafting request from generic into useful.

- **The goal.** "Make this opening warmer and shorter," not "improve this."
- **The audience.** "My sister, who was there that summer," not "readers."
- **The rules.** "Under 80 words, no exclamation marks, no phrases I would not say at the kitchen table."

Rules can also ban things. You will start to notice phrases that keep turning up in AI answers and make writing sound manufactured. When you spot one, add it to a "never use" list in your Doc, and paste the list into your rules each time.

## Mentor texts, from your own hand

A mentor text is a sample of the style you want, the same idea as "Show it examples."

Here the mentor text is only ever **your own writing**: an old email, a card, a post, a page from a journal. Not a favorite author's pages, and not something you found online. Pasting another writer's pages and asking for more like them is not fair to that writer, and it pulls your piece toward their phrases. The point is for your piece to sound like you.

Pick something you wrote that you like, with nothing private in it. Paste it, and ask the AI to describe what it sees: sentence length, tone, habits. That description, plus your voice card, becomes the style note you paste into later prompts.

## You write, it assists

Here is the draft rule. Write each section yourself, working down your outline, even if the writing is rough. Rough is fine. You will fix it in the next lesson.

When one paragraph will not come, ask for help with that one: goal, audience, rules, your style note, and your own rough attempt. Ask for two or three versions. Read them for one thing, a word or turn you like. Then close the chat and write the paragraph again yourself.

## Settings

You may read that some AI tools have a setting for how safe or adventurous the answers are. We have not checked whether free Claude or free ChatGPT has one, and tools change. **Check this yourself** on the company's own pages, and note the date you looked.

- Claude help center: [https://support.claude.com](https://support.claude.com)

You do not need any setting for this lesson. Everything here works with clear context alone.

## Keep this in your pocket

1. Say the goal, the audience and the rules every time you ask for help with a paragraph.
2. Use only your own writing as a mentor text, and have the AI describe it as a style note.
3. Write the draft yourself, and use the AI only for the paragraph that sticks.

## Practice

Three jobs, about 15 to 20 minutes each. Use claude.ai and your "My Writing Folder" Doc. Keep private details out of anything you paste.

### Task 1: Same paragraph, two prompts

**Why it matters:** a vague request and a clear one, side by side, show you exactly what context changes.

**Do this:** write your own opening paragraph from your final outline, rough is fine. Save it in your Doc under the heading "Draft." Open a new Claude chat (click New chat at the top left of the page, or the plus sign if you see one) and send the vague prompt. Then open a second new chat and send the clear one with your goal, audience and rules filled in. Compare both to your own opening.

```text
Make this opening better: [your paragraph]
```

```text
Here is the opening of my piece: [your paragraph]. Goal: [what the opening should do, such as sound warmer and be shorter]. Audience: [who reads it]. Rules: under [number] words, no exclamation marks, nothing I would not say out loud. Give me two versions.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I wrote my own opening first.
- [ ] I ran both prompts in separate chats.
- [ ] I can name one difference between the two answers.
- [ ] I kept my opening unless one line in an answer was better.

**What good looks like:** the clear prompt keeps your facts and your tone, and the vague one smooths them away. If neither beats yours, say so in your Doc. Your own draft winning is a fine result.

---

### Task 2: A style note from your own writing

**Why it matters:** a style note built from your words keeps the AI from sliding toward a generic voice.

**Do this:** find something short you wrote, such as an old card or a message to a friend, with nothing private in it. Open a new chat and paste it into the prompt. Save the AI's description under "Style note" in your Doc. Read it and cross out anything that does not sound right. Add the lines from your voice card.

```text
Here is a short piece I wrote. Describe my style in five short bullets: sentence length, tone, words I lean on, and habits. Do not flatter me and do not rewrite anything. [paste your own writing]
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] The text I pasted is entirely my own writing.
- [ ] It has no private details.
- [ ] I crossed out at least one bullet that was off.
- [ ] My style note is saved in my Doc.

**What good looks like:** five bullets and your corrections. If the description is generic, such as "warm and friendly," paste a longer sample or ask "Quote two phrases from my text that show this."

---

### Task 3: Draft it, and rescue one paragraph

**Why it matters:** this is the habit that keeps the piece yours.

**Do this:** write the rest of your draft yourself, section by section, down your outline. Find the paragraph that fights you. Open a new chat, paste it into the prompt with your style note, and read the versions. Pick one word, phrase or turn you like. Close the chat and rewrite the paragraph yourself.

```text
Here is a paragraph I am stuck on: [your rough attempt]. Goal: [what it should do]. Audience: [who]. My style: [paste your style note]. Rules: [length, words to avoid]. Give me three different versions.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I wrote every other section myself.
- [ ] I gave a goal, an audience, a style note and rules.
- [ ] I borrowed an idea, not a paragraph.
- [ ] My full draft is saved in my Doc.

**What good looks like:** a complete draft in your voice, with one paragraph that got unstuck. If you pasted an AI version in whole, read it aloud next to the lines around it. It will stand out. Rewrite it.
