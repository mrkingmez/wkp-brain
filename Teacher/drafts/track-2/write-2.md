# Write with AI, 2: brainstorm and outline

About 10 minutes to read, plus about 45 to do the practice

## What you will be able to do

- Ask for many ideas, then choose a few and throw the rest away.
- Get several different outlines for the same piece, and build your own from the best parts.
- Ask the AI to challenge your outline before you write a word of it.

## What you will make

A tested outline for the piece you chose in "Write with AI, 1": a list of angles you chose from, three possible skeletons, and one final outline in your own words, with the gaps found and filled.

## Why stages beat one big ask

You could type "write my letter" and get 500 words in ten seconds. You would also get one version and nothing to compare it with. You cannot pick the best idea if you only ever see one.

So split the work into stages and use the AI differently in each. Today covers the first two.

1. **Brainstorm.** You want a big, messy pile of angles. Quantity matters here, because the first idea is rarely the best.
2. **Outline.** You want the order: what comes first, what comes last, what the piece builds toward.

At each stage you end by choosing. Ten angles in, two out. Three skeletons in, one out.

## Brainstorming that is not generic

Ask a vague question and the AI gives vague angles: "share a memory, express gratitude." Bring your own material, the way "Your first good prompt" showed. Hand it the reader, the purpose and the true detail from your "My piece" heading.

Then ask for angles, not text. "Give me ten different ways into this piece, one line each." Ask for odd ones too: "Include three angles that are unexpected or funny." The unusual ones are often where your piece lives.

Read the list and mark two. Say why you chose them. That choice is your work, not the AI's.

## Outlining without a straitjacket

An outline is a map. A good one for a short piece has a start that pulls a reader in, a middle with two or three points or moments, and an ending that lands. For a story it holds a turn: the moment something changes.

Ask for three skeletons, not one, and tell it to make them different from each other. One can run in time order, one can open with the ending, one can hang on a single object. You then borrow: the opening from the first, the order from the second. The outline you end up with is yours, and it is often better than any of the three.

## Let it argue with you

Once you have an outline, ask the AI to push on it. Give it a role, the way "Give it a role and context" showed, such as a reader who knows you well or a busy neighbor with two minutes. Ask what is missing, where they would stop reading, and what they would want to ask. You do not have to obey. You are collecting questions to answer in the draft.

## Keep this in your pocket

1. Brainstorm in a pile. Ask for many angles, and mark two.
2. Ask for three different skeletons, then borrow from each to make your own outline.
3. Ask the AI to challenge your outline as a reader would, and decide which pushbacks to take.

## Practice

Three jobs, about 15 minutes each. Work in claude.ai, with your "My Writing Folder" Doc open. Use a new chat for each task so earlier answers do not steer later ones. Keep private details out of your prompts. Nicknames or initials, no addresses, no health details.

### Task 1: A pile of angles

**Why it matters:** the best way into a piece is often the third or fourth idea, not the first.

**Do this:** open a new chat (click New chat at the top left of the page, or the plus sign if you see one). Fill the blanks from your "My piece" heading and send the prompt. Read all ten. In your Doc, type the heading "Angles," paste your top two under it, and write one sentence on why you picked each.

```text
I am writing a piece for [reader]. I want them to [feeling or action]. One true detail is [detail]. Give me ten different ways into the piece, one line each. Make at least three unexpected or funny. Do not write any of the piece.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I read all ten before choosing.
- [ ] I picked two and wrote a reason for each.
- [ ] At least one pick came from the unexpected ones, or I can say why not.
- [ ] The AI did not write the piece.

**What good looks like:** two angles in your Doc, each with a reason in your own words. If all ten feel generic, your prompt was thin. Add one more true detail and ask again.

---

### Task 2: Three skeletons

**Why it matters:** seeing three shapes side by side shows you the choices you did not know you had.

**Do this:** in a new chat, paste the prompt with your chosen angle filled in. Read the three outlines. In your Doc, make a heading "Borrowed parts" and list which piece you are taking from which skeleton, such as "opening from skeleton 1, order from 3." Then write your own outline underneath, in your own words, as five to eight short lines.

```text
My piece: [one sentence]. Reader: [who]. Angle: [your angle]. Length: about 500 words.
Give me three different outlines for it. Make them truly different: one in time order, one that starts with the ending, one built around a single object or moment. Five to eight bullets each. Do not write the piece.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I got three outlines that are different from each other.
- [ ] I listed what I borrowed and from where.
- [ ] My own outline is in my words, not pasted from the AI.
- [ ] I can tell what the piece builds toward.

**What good looks like:** a short outline of five to eight lines that reads like your plan. If the three skeletons came out too alike, tell the AI "These are too similar, make them differ more," and try again.

---

### Task 3: Let a reader push back

**Why it matters:** a hole in an outline costs a minute to fix now and an hour once the draft is done.

**Do this:** open a new chat and paste your own outline into the prompt. Read the questions the AI raises. For each one, write "answer in the draft," "already covered," or "disagree," with a few words of why. Then update your outline wherever a question exposed a real gap. Save the final outline under the heading "Final outline."

```text
Act as [a reader who knows me well / a busy neighbor with two minutes]. Here is my outline: [paste]. Tell me where you would stop reading, what is missing, and three questions you would want answered. Do not rewrite my outline.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I gave each question a decision, not just a read.
- [ ] I changed my outline at least once because of a good question.
- [ ] I ignored at least one suggestion I disagreed with, and wrote why.
- [ ] The outline in my Doc is mine.

**What good looks like:** a final outline with one or two real changes. If the AI agreed with everything, ask "What is the weakest part of this outline?" Praise alone gives you nothing to work on.
