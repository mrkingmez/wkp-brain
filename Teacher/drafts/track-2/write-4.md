# Write with AI, 4: revising with AI as editor

About 10 minutes to read, plus about 45 to do the practice

## What you will be able to do

- Ask the AI to find problems in your draft without rewriting it.
- Use it to catch slow spots, repeated words and loose sentences.
- Keep a record of what you accepted and rejected, so the final say stays with you.

## What you will make

A revised, final version of your piece, with a short log showing what the AI flagged, what you changed and what you refused.

## An editor points, a ghostwriter replaces

A good human editor does not take your pages and return someone else's. They circle things. "This paragraph repeats the one before." "You lose me here." You fix it, in your words.

Ask the AI to behave the same way. The key sentence in every revising prompt is "Do not rewrite my text. List the problems and quote each one." If you skip it, you may get a polished replacement, and your voice is gone before you notice. Quotes let you find each note in your own draft and judge it there.

## Three checks worth running

Run them one at a time. Each is a small step you can verify.

- **Pacing.** Where does it drag, and where does it rush? Ask which paragraphs could lose a sentence, and where a reader would want a little more.
- **Repetition.** Repeated words, repeated openings ("I remember... I remember...") and repeated ideas in different clothes. This is a good job to hand over, because you are too close to your own draft to see it.
- **Tightening.** Sentences with extra words. Ask for the three longest or loosest, and a shorter option for each, to read and choose from. Never "tighten the whole thing."

Paste in your voice card and style note, so it knows what is a habit of yours and what is a flaw. A repeated sound you chose on purpose is style. The AI cannot tell unless you say.

## Watch what it adds

Editing prompts can slip. A "tightened" sentence may lose a detail. A "smoothed" one may gain a fact you never wrote, such as a date, a place or a feeling. Read every changed line against your original. Anything new in the answer that was not in yours gets cut or checked. The last lesson in this track covers checking your piece, and "How to check what AI tells you" in the Stay Safe track covers checking in general.

## The log and the final say

Keep a two-column list as you go: what the AI suggested, and what you did. "Accepted," "changed it my way," or "rejected." The log is how you hold on to the final say, and after a few pieces it shows your patterns: the words you overuse, the places you rush.

Last, read the piece aloud. Task 3 walks you through it.

## Keep this in your pocket

1. Say "Do not rewrite my text. List the problems and quote each one."
2. Run pacing, repetition and tightening separately, with your style note attached.
3. Read changes against your original, keep a log, and read the piece aloud before you finish.

## Practice

Three jobs, about 15 minutes each. Use claude.ai, your draft, voice card and style note from your "My Writing Folder" Doc. Test one task in ChatGPT too if you like. Private details stay out.

### Task 1: Pacing and repetition scan

**Why it matters:** this finds the problems you cannot see because you wrote it.

**Do this:** open a new Claude chat (click New chat at the top left of the page, or the plus sign if you see one). Paste your draft and style note into the prompt. Read the notes. In your Doc, under "Revision log," make a row for each note. Mark it accepted, changed my way, or rejected. Then fix the ones you accept in your draft yourself.

```text
You are an editor. Here is my draft and my style note. Do not rewrite my text. Do two things. First, list up to five places that drag or rush, quoting the line. Second, list repeated words, openings or ideas, quoting each one. Leave alone anything that fits my style note. Style note: [paste]. Draft: [paste]
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] The AI quoted lines, and I found each one in my draft.
- [ ] I made a log row for every note.
- [ ] I rejected at least one note, or I can say why none deserved it.
- [ ] I made the fixes in my own words.

**What good looks like:** a log with a mix of accepted and rejected notes. If the AI rewrote your text anyway, say "You rewrote it. I asked you only to list and quote," and ask again.

---

### Task 2: Tighten three loose sentences

**Why it matters:** choosing between options keeps the final say with you.

**Do this:** open a new chat and send the prompt with your draft. For each of the three sentences, read the options next to your original. Choose one, or write a fourth. Compare each pick to your original to see whether a detail was lost or added. Update your draft and your log.

```text
Here is my draft. Pick the three longest or loosest sentences and quote each one. For each, give two shorter options that keep every fact and add none. Do not touch the rest. Draft: [paste]
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I checked each option for lost or added details.
- [ ] I chose, rather than accepting the first option.
- [ ] Where my original was better, I kept it.
- [ ] My log shows all three decisions.

**What good looks like:** three sentences better and still yours. If an option added something you did not write, such as a color or a name, that is a flag. Cut it, and note it in the log as "added a fact."

---

### Task 3: Read it aloud and finish

**Why it matters:** out loud, you hear the stumbles you skip over when you read silently.

**Do this:** read your whole revised piece out loud, slowly. Mark any spot where you stumble, run out of breath or would never say it. Fix those yourself. Then open a new chat, send the typo prompt below, and compare its list to your version. Save the final under "Final piece" in your Doc.

```text
Check this text for spelling, grammar and punctuation errors only. List each error with the line it is in. Do not change any wording or style. Text: [paste]
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I read the whole piece aloud, not in my head.
- [ ] I fixed at least one spot where I stumbled.
- [ ] I applied only the errors that were real errors.
- [ ] The final version in my Doc is the one I would sign.

**What good looks like:** a clean final piece with your name on it, and a log that tells the story of how it got there. If the typo check proposed word changes, ignore them. You asked for errors only.
