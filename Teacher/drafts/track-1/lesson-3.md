# Your first good prompt

About 10 minutes to read, plus about 35 to do the practice

## What you will be able to do

- Say what a prompt is and why a vague one gets a vague answer.
- Write a clear prompt using four simple parts.
- Steer a reply with follow-up messages instead of starting over.

## What you will make

One vague prompt, rewritten three ways, with the answers side by side. Then your best version, run in both Claude and ChatGPT. You will see for yourself how much a few extra sentences change what you get.

## What a prompt is

A prompt is the message you type to the AI. That is all. The AI cannot see inside your head, your house, or your plans. It only has your words. If your message is thin, it fills the gaps with guesses, and the guesses are usually bland.

Compare these two.

> Help me with dinner.

> I am cooking for four people on a weeknight. One of them does not eat meat. I have chicken thighs, rice, and peppers at home. Give me two dinner ideas that take 30 minutes or less.

The first one could mean a hundred things, so you get a hundred-things answer. The second one gives the AI something real to work with, so you get something you can cook.

## The four parts of a clear prompt

You do not need all four every time. Add the ones that matter for the job.

1. **The job.** What you want done. Write it, plan it, explain it, shorten it. Say the verb.
2. **The details.** Who is involved, what you have, what you already know. This is the part people leave out most.
3. **The reader.** Who the answer is for and what tone fits. A note to a close friend and a note to a landlord are different jobs.
4. **The shape.** How the answer should look. A short list, three sentences, a table, one paragraph, no more than 100 words.

Here is the dinner prompt again, with the parts marked.

> **Job:** Give me two dinner ideas. **Details:** I am cooking for four on a weeknight, one person does not eat meat, and I have chicken thighs, rice, and peppers. **Reader:** I am an average cook, nothing fancy. **Shape:** Keep each idea to two sentences and under 30 minutes.

## You can keep talking

A reply is not the end. If it is close, do not start over. Tell it what to change in the same chat.

- "Shorter, please."
- "Make it warmer, like I am writing to a friend."
- "Keep the second idea and give me three more like it."
- "That is too fancy. Use only what I have at home."

Each follow-up builds on the last reply, so you steer a little at a time. If an answer is far off, it is fine to open a new chat and start fresh with a better prompt.

One caution. Asking the same thing twice can give different wording each time. That is normal. It is another reason to read the answer closely and not take any one version as the final word.

## Keep this in your pocket

1. A prompt is just your message. A thin message gets a bland answer.
2. Four parts help: the job, the details, the reader, and the shape.
3. Steer with follow-ups, and open a new chat if the answer is far off.

## Practice

Three jobs, about 10 to 15 minutes each. Keep claude.ai and chatgpt.com open in two tabs. The free version of each is all you need. Use made-up details. Nothing private.

### Task 1: Rewrite one vague prompt three ways

**Why it matters:** the fastest way to learn prompting is to watch the same request get better step by step.

**Do this:** start with this vague prompt. Run it in Claude and read the answer. Then rewrite it three times, adding more each time. Version A adds the details. Version B adds the details and the reader. Version C adds all four parts. Open a new chat for each version (click New chat at the top left of the page, or the plus sign if you see one), so the earlier answers do not shape the later ones. Save each answer in a Google Doc or on paper. Use your own made-up details, such as the number of guests, the budget, and what the party is for.

```text
Help me plan a birthday party.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I wrote three new versions, each with more than the one before.
- [ ] I kept all four answers where I could compare them.
- [ ] I can point to the exact words in my prompt that changed the answer.
- [ ] I did not use anyone's real name, address, or other private details.

**What good looks like:** four answers in a row. The first is generic. By version C, it should feel like it was written for your party. If version C is no better than A, look at what you added. Details that change the plan help. Fluffy extra words do not.

---

### Task 2: Run your best version in both tools

**Why it matters:** two tools respond differently to the same prompt. Knowing which one fits which job saves you time.

**Do this:** take your version C from Task 1. Paste it, unchanged, into ChatGPT in a new chat. Put the two answers next to each other. Ask yourself which one you would actually use, and why. Mark any fact in either answer, such as a cost or a time, that you would want to check before relying on it.

```text
[paste your version C here]
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I used exactly the same words in both tools.
- [ ] I compared the answers on something specific, such as how usable it is, how long it is, or what it left out.
- [ ] I marked at least one fact I would check.
- [ ] I know which tool I would pick for this job, and I can say why.

**What good looks like:** a short note, such as "Claude gave a tidier plan, ChatGPT gave more ideas". Either result is fine. There is no single winner. You are learning how each one tends to answer, so you can choose on purpose.

---

### Task 3: Steer a reply with follow-ups

**Why it matters:** most good results come from two or three rounds, not one perfect prompt.

**Do this:** open a new Claude chat and paste your version C from Task 1. Once it answers, send these three follow-ups, one at a time, and read each reply before the next. Then write one follow-up of your own that changes something you do not like about the plan.

```text
Make the plan shorter. Give me only the five most important steps.
```

```text
Rewrite it in a warm, friendly tone, like I am telling a friend.
```

```text
Turn it into a checklist I can print.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I sent each follow-up in the same chat and read each answer.
- [ ] I wrote one follow-up of my own and saw what it changed.
- [ ] I checked that the later versions did not drop something important from the plan.
- [ ] I saved the final checklist.

**What good looks like:** a printed or saved checklist that came from three or four short messages. If a follow-up lost something you wanted, tell it, for example, "Put the cake step back in." Rounds of steering are the normal way to work.
