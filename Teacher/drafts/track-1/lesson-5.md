# Show it examples

About 12 minutes to read, plus about 40 to do the practice

## What you will be able to do

- Show the AI one or two samples so it copies your format, length, and style.
- Label examples clearly so the AI knows which part is the sample and which part is the new job.
- Build a fill-in-the-blanks template you can reuse for the same job again and again.

## What you will make

A reusable template that holds two of your own examples. You will use it to turn messy raw notes into a neat, same-every-time format, such as a recipe card, a chore entry, or a thank-you note in your own voice. Then you will test it on two new inputs.

## Show, do not describe

Try describing a handwriting style in words. "Slanted, a little loopy, mostly neat." It is hard. Now put a sample in front of someone. They see it at once.

The AI works the same way. When you want a certain format, a certain length, or a certain voice, showing it a sample often works better than describing it. A sample carries a hundred small choices you would never think to write down: how long the lines are, where the dashes go, whether it says "Hi" or "Hello," whether the ending is short or warm.

Here is a plain description.

> Turn my notes into a nice pantry entry.

The AI has to guess what "nice" means. Now the same job with one sample.

> Turn my notes into an entry in this format.
>
> Example:
> Item: Black beans
> Where: Top shelf, left
> Buy again when: fewer than 2 cans
>
> My notes: oatmeal, bottom shelf, buy when the box is under half

With the sample, the answer comes back with the same three lines, in the same order. You did not have to explain the rules. You showed them.

## How many examples

- **One example** locks a format. Good for lists, cards, and labels.
- **Two or three examples** lock a voice or a judgment call. Good when the result is a sentence or a paragraph, such as a thank-you line or a short product description.
- **More than three** is rarely needed. Past that, you are mostly adding length, and the answer can start to copy your samples word for word.

Make your examples **different from each other** in the small details. If both samples start with "Thank you so much for the lovely gift," the AI will start every answer that way. If they differ, it learns the pattern and not the phrase.

## Label the parts

The AI reads everything as one block of text. Help it tell the parts apart. Put a plain label on each piece.

```text
Here are two examples of what I want.

Example 1:
[your first sample]

Example 2:
[your second sample]

Now do the same for this one:
[the new input]
```

Words like Example 1, Example 2, and "Now do the same for this one" keep it from mixing up a sample with the job. This is the same habit as a good form: label the boxes.

## When it goes wrong

- **It copies your example word for word.** Your samples are too close or too few. Add a second sample that differs, or say "Do not reuse phrases from the examples."
- **It matches the format but the content is wrong.** The sample fixed the shape, not the facts. Check the facts yourself, as in the first lessons.
- **It ignores the format.** Your sample may be buried. Put the examples first, then the new job, last.
- **It adds extra chatter** like "Sure, here is your entry." Say "Give only the entry, nothing before or after."

## A template you keep

The best part of examples is that they are reusable. Once a prompt with two good examples works, you do not rewrite it. You swap in new input and run it again. You are building a small, personal tool.

## Keep this in your pocket

1. A sample often teaches faster than a description. Show the format you want.
2. One example locks a format. Two or three lock a voice. Make them differ.
3. Label the parts: Example 1, Example 2, then the new job last.

## Practice

Three jobs, about 10 to 15 minutes each. Use claude.ai. In Task 3 you will also try ChatGPT. Use made-up details only.

### Task 1: One example locks a format

**Why it matters:** a format you can show once is a format you never have to explain again.

**Do this:** open a new chat in Claude. Paste the prompt below. It has one example and three messy notes. Read what comes back and compare it to the example, line by line. Then try your own version: change the example to a format you would actually like, such as a chore chart line or a freezer inventory line, and run it again with three notes of your own.

```text
Turn each of my notes into an entry in exactly this format. Give only the entries, nothing before or after.

Example:
Item: Black beans
Where: Top shelf, left
Buy again when: fewer than 2 cans

My notes:
- rice, bottom shelf, buy more when the bag looks low
- olive oil, next to the stove, almost empty, buy now
- cinnamon, spice drawer, fine for months
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] The three entries have the same lines in the same order as the example.
- [ ] I checked that each entry says what my note said, with nothing added.
- [ ] I changed the example once and saw the format follow.
- [ ] If a line came out differently, I can see what in the example caused it.

**What good looks like:** three tidy entries that match the example. Notice what the AI did with "fine for months." It had to turn that into a rule such as "Buy again when." If it invented a number you did not give, that is a place the format pushed it to guess. Tell it, "If my note has no number, write 'not stated.'"

### Task 2: Two examples lock a voice

**Why it matters:** voice is hard to describe and easy to show. Two samples let the AI hear how you sound.

**Do this:** below are two made-up thank-you notes. Notice they are short, a little different, and sound like a real person. Paste the prompt into a new Claude chat and run it. Then write two short thank-you notes of your own, in your own voice, about made-up gifts, and swap them in for the two samples. Run it again and compare. Which result sounds more like you?

```text
Here are two examples of thank-you notes in my voice.

Example 1:
Dear Aunt Mae, the blue scarf came on the first cold morning and I wore it out the door. You always know what I will need before I do. Love, Dana

Example 2:
Hi Mr. Ortiz, thank you for covering my shift on Saturday. The kids' game ran long and I would have missed it. I owe you lunch. Dana

Now write a thank-you note in the same voice for this: my neighbor Rosa watered my plants for a week while I was away. Keep it under 50 words. Give only the note.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] My two samples are in my own voice and are different from each other.
- [ ] I read the result aloud and asked whether I would really say it.
- [ ] I checked that it copied the style, not the sentences from my samples.
- [ ] I fixed any detail I did not provide, such as a plant type or a date.

**What good looks like:** a short note that sounds like a cousin of your samples, with one specific detail from the job. If it reads stiff, add a third sample that is more casual. If it copies a phrase like "owe you lunch," add "Do not reuse phrases from the examples."

### Task 3: Build your reusable template

**Why it matters:** the point of examples is to stop starting over. A good template is a small tool you own.

**Do this:** pick one job you do again and again in real life, such as short replies to group texts, product descriptions for a craft, meal notes, or chore reminders. Fill in the template below with two made-up examples of the job done well, and one new input. Run it in Claude. Then run the same template with a second new input. Then paste the identical template with the first new input into ChatGPT and compare. Save the template in a Google Doc. You will use it in the prompt library lesson.

```text
I do this job often: [name the job in a few words].
Here are two examples of it done well.

Example 1:
[sample 1]

Example 2:
[sample 2]

Follow the same format, length, and voice. Do not reuse phrases from the examples. Give only the result.

New input:
[the new thing]
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] My two examples are good ones that differ in small details.
- [ ] I ran the template on two different new inputs and both came out in the same shape.
- [ ] I compared Claude and ChatGPT on the same input.
- [ ] I saved the template where I can find it.

**What good looks like:** one template and a short note on which tool matched your examples better for this job. If either result drifts from the pattern, the usual fix is a better second example, not a longer explanation. A template that works twice is worth keeping. One that works once may have been luck, so give it another test input.
