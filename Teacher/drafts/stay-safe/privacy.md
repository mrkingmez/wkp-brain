# What not to paste into an AI chat

About 10 minutes to read, plus about 30 to do the practice

## What you will be able to do

- Decide, before you paste, whether the AI needs a detail to do the job.
- Strip names, numbers, and addresses out of an email, a photo, or a document.
- Swap the real details back in yourself, so the AI never sees them.

## What you will make

A cleaned-up email with a reply ready to send, and your own never-paste list of five items.

## You have done this job once already

When you used Claude to clean out your email, you picked a job AI is good at. This lesson does that same job with less of your life in the chat.

If you pasted messages in that day and the chat is still in your list on the left side of claude.ai, click it and scroll through to see what went in. Do not copy it anywhere. It is a useful look at what a real paste contains.

## The one test

Before you paste anything, ask one question.

> Does the AI need this to do the job?

Almost always, it needs the situation, not the identity. To write a polite reply about a late order, it needs to know an order is late. It does not need your name, the order number, or your street.

How each company stores and uses your chats is up to that company and can change. This course has not checked those pages, so check them yourself. Open [claude.ai](https://claude.ai), click your name or initials (look in a corner), click Settings, and look for a privacy or data section. Do the same on [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com). Write down the date you read each one. The habit in this lesson works whatever those pages say.

## What to leave out of emails

- Names, addresses, and phone numbers.
- Account and order numbers.
- The signature block.
- The long quoted thread below the newest message.
- Personal links, such as account or unsubscribe links.
- Other people's details. They did not agree to be in the chat.

## Photos, screenshots, and documents

Look at the whole picture, not just the part you care about. Check for other people's faces, license plates, mail, letters, ID cards, and writing in the background.

To hide something, cover it with a solid box in a markup tool, then zoom in and check that nothing reads through. A thin marker line or a light highlight can leave the letters readable. Cropping the detail out is even better.

In documents, watch the headers and footers. They often hold names, addresses, and account numbers. Look for ID numbers, birthdates, and medical details tied to a name.

## Never paste these

- Passwords.
- Full card or bank account numbers.
- Your Social Security number.
- Photos of your ID.
- One-time codes, the short numbers sent by text or email to let you log in.

## How to strip a message fast

Swap real details for placeholders: [Person A] for a name, [My address] for your street. Keep a key on paper that says who Person A really is.

Use Find and replace. In a Google Doc, click Edit, then Find and replace. In Word on a PC, press Ctrl+H. Type the real name in the first box, the placeholder in the second, and click Replace all. Paste the cleaned text into the chat.

When the AI answers, copy its reply into your document and swap the real names back the same way. The AI never needs the key.

## Keep this in your pocket

1. Ask first: does the AI need this? It needs the situation, not the identity.
2. Strip names, numbers, addresses, signatures, and old threads. Keep the key yourself.
3. Never paste passwords, full card or bank numbers, your Social Security number, ID photos, or one-time codes.

## Practice

Three jobs, about 10 minutes each, on free Claude in another tab. Every sample below is made up.

### Task 1: Strip an email, then get a reply

**Why it matters:** this is the email job you already did, with nothing private in the chat.

**Do this:** copy the made-up email below into a Google Doc. Swap every name, address, phone number, and order number for a placeholder. Delete the quoted thread. Write your key on paper. Then compare your version with the cleaned one in the prompt box, open a new Claude chat (click New chat at the top left), and paste the prompt.

> Hi Dana, your order #48213-77 shipped today to 214 Birch Lane, Millbrook. Call me at 555-0142 with any questions.
> Thanks, Rob Kessler, Customer Care, Brightway Home Goods
>
> On Monday, Dana Price wrote: Where is my order? I need it before my sister Carla visits.

```text
Here is an email I got. Write a short, friendly reply saying thanks
and asking when it should arrive. Keep the placeholders as they are.

Hi [Me], your order [Order number] shipped today to [My address].
Call me at [Phone] with any questions.
Thanks, [Person A], [Company]
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I found the name, address, phone, order number, signature, and quoted thread before I looked at the cleaned version.
- [ ] Carla's name went out with the quoted thread.
- [ ] My key is on paper, not in the chat.
- [ ] I used Find and replace to swap the names back into the reply myself.

**What good looks like:** a short reply with placeholders, then the same reply in your document with names filled back in.

### Task 2: Find the leaks in a bill

**Why it matters:** bills hide account numbers, addresses, and bank details in the header and the small print.

**Do this:** say you want help understanding why this made-up bill went up. Before you ask the AI anything, list on paper every detail you would remove, written as a type of detail, such as "account number". Then paste the prompt with your list in it.

> MILLBROOK POWER AND WATER
> Customer: Dana Price. Account 0049-2231-8810
> Service address: 214 Birch Lane, Millbrook. Phone on file: 555-0187
> Autopay from checking ending 4471. Due Oct 24
> Electric: 912 kWh, $118.40. Water: 4,200 gallons, $41.10
> Total due: $159.50. Last month: $96.20
> Manage your account: millbrookpower.example/acct/dprice-x9

```text
I want help with why a utility bill went up. First I listed the personal
details I would remove: [paste your list here]. The bill has a name,
account number, address, phone, due date, autopay bank ending, usage,
charges, and an account link. What did I miss? What do you actually need?
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I made my list before I asked the AI.
- [ ] My list has the name, account number, address, phone, and bank ending.
- [ ] I caught the personal link at the bottom.
- [ ] I can say what the AI really needs: the usage and the totals.

**What good looks like:** usage and totals are the situation, fine to share. Everything tied to Dana is the identity. If the AI calls a detail on your list fine to keep, the stricter choice is safer.

### Task 3: Write your own never-paste list

**Why it matters:** a list in your own words sticks better than a list in a lesson.

**Do this:** on paper, write five things from your own life you will never paste. Write the type of thing, such as "my bank login", never the thing itself. Then open a new chat and paste the prompt.

```text
Quiz me on what is safe to paste into an AI chat. Give me 5 made-up
situations, one at a time. I will answer keep (fine as is), strip (fine
after removing personal details), or never. After each answer, say
whether you agree and why.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] My list has five items, written as kinds of things.
- [ ] I answered each situation before I read the AI's view.
- [ ] Where we disagreed, I can say why.
- [ ] I kept my list where I will see it again.

**What good looks like:** five items on paper and five answers in the chat. If the AI says keep and you said strip, go with strip.
