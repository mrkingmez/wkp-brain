# When to keep the decision yourself

About 10 minutes to read, plus about 35 to do the practice

## What you will be able to do

- Tell a job AI can do apart from a decision that belongs to a person.
- Run a three-question gate before you lean on an AI answer.
- Use AI as a second opinion on a big choice and still make the call yourself.

## What you will make

A sorted list of eight everyday situations, a one-sentence decision on a made-up car repair, and a sympathy card that sounds like you.

## Helping you think is not deciding for you

AI is very good at helping you think and get ready. It can lay out options, explain a word on a form, or draft questions for an appointment.

Deciding is a different job. When it is about your health, a legal matter, money, grief, or anything you cannot check, a person decides. Sometimes that person has a license. Sometimes it is you.

One thing to carry over from [How to check what AI tells you](check-what-ai-tells-you.md): when an AI sounds sure about a health, legal, or money question, that sureness is not information.

## The three-question gate

Before you act on an AI answer, ask these.

1. **Can I check this?** If you can confirm it somewhere else, check it. If you cannot, treat the answer as a starting point only.
2. **What happens if it is wrong?** A bland birthday card costs nothing. A wrong medicine dose, a missed legal deadline, or a bad money move can cost a lot.
3. **Is this mine to decide, or someone else's?** Some calls belong to the person with the license and the responsibility: a doctor, a pharmacist, a lawyer, your bank. Some are yours alone.

If all three answers come easy, let the AI do the job. If any one gives you pause, the AI helps you prepare and a person decides.

## A second opinion that leaves the decision with you

For a big choice, ask the AI for things that make you sharper, not for the answer.

- The questions to ask the real professional
- The options, with the trade-offs of each
- The strongest case against your plan
- What would change the answer
- What to verify, and where

Then step away from the chat. Write your decision in one sentence, in your own words. Under it, write who confirms it: the mechanic, the pharmacist, the bank, someone you trust, or just you. That second line is what keeps the decision yours.

## Grief and hard messages

AI can help you find words for a sympathy card, a hard text, or a note to someone having a rough time. That is a fine use.

It cannot stand in for you. The person reading your card wants to hear from you. So treat the AI's draft as raw material. Change it until it sounds like you, then read it aloud and ask: would I say this to their face? If a sentence makes you wince, cut it.

If you are the one having a hard time, a person you trust or your doctor will do more for you than a chat window. In an emergency, call 911.

## Keep this in your pocket

1. AI helps you think and prepare. A person decides on health, legal matters, money, grief, and anything you cannot check.
2. Run the gate: Can I check this? What happens if it is wrong? Whose call is it?
3. Ask for questions, trade-offs, and the case against. Then write the decision yourself and name who confirms it.

## Practice

Three jobs, about 10 to 15 minutes each, in free Claude or free ChatGPT. Every situation below is made up.

### Task 1: Sort eight situations

**Why it matters:** sorting is how the gate becomes a habit.

**Do this:** on paper, sort these eight into three piles: AI can do it, AI can help me prepare, a person decides. Then open a new Claude chat (click New chat at the top left) and paste the prompt. Compare its piles with yours.

```text
Sort these eight situations into three piles: "AI can do it",
"AI can help me prepare", and "a person decides". Give one sentence
for each choice.
1. Turn a long recipe into a shopping list.
2. Decide whether to stop taking a medicine that makes me dizzy.
3. Write a thank-you note to my child's teacher.
4. Understand the wording on a car insurance renewal letter.
5. Decide whether to sign a lease with an early exit fee.
6. Plan a week of school lunches.
7. Choose between paying down a credit card or saving for a repair.
8. Make sense of new test results before my doctor calls.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I sorted all eight before I saw the AI's answer.
- [ ] I ran the three-question gate on at least two of them.
- [ ] Where the AI and I disagreed, I can say why.
- [ ] The medicine question landed in "a person decides".

**What good looks like:** the recipe, the note, and the lunches are jobs AI can do. The insurance letter and the test results are prep jobs: the AI explains the words, and the insurer or the doctor gives the real answer. The medicine, the lease, and the money choice are a person's call. If the AI put the medicine question anywhere else, it sounded sure and was wrong, and you caught it.

### Task 2: Get a second opinion on two repair quotes

**Why it matters:** two quotes for different work is where a second opinion helps, as long as you make the call.

**Do this:** paste the prompt into a new chat and read the answer. Then close the chat. On paper, write your decision in one sentence and who you would confirm it with. If the AI states a fact about brakes, check it with a mechanic before you lean on it.

```text
I have two quotes for my car's brakes. Do not tell me which to pick.
Shop A: $900. New front pads and rotors, brake fluid flush,
12-month warranty on parts and labor.
Shop B: $600. New front pads only, rotors resurfaced,
90-day warranty on parts.
Give me: the questions I should ask each shop, the trade-offs between
them, the strongest case against picking the cheaper one, what would
change the answer, and what I should verify and where.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] The answer gave me questions, trade-offs, the case against, and what to verify.
- [ ] If it picked a shop anyway, I set that part aside.
- [ ] I wrote my decision in one sentence, in my own words.
- [ ] I named who I would confirm it with.

**What good looks like:** one sentence you could defend out loud, and a name under it. Either shop can be the right pick. A good answer notes that the quotes list different work, so the prices are not a straight comparison.

---

### Task 3: Write a card that sounds like you

**Why it matters:** hard messages are where AI helps most with the blank page, and where your voice matters most.

**Do this:** the neighbor in the prompt is made up. Paste the prompt into a new chat. Copy the draft into a Google Doc or onto paper. Edit until it sounds like you. Read it aloud. Then mark which sentences you changed.

```text
Help me write a short sympathy card to my neighbor Ruth. Her husband,
Walt, died last week. I knew him a little. He waved from his porch every
morning and once fixed my fence gate without being asked. Keep it to
three or four plain sentences. No flowery phrases, no poems.
```

**Self-check:**

- [ ] I changed at least two sentences.
- [ ] I read it aloud, and I would say every line to Ruth in person.
- [ ] I cut anything that sounded like a store-bought card.
- [ ] I can point to the sentences that are mine.

**What good looks like:** a short card, a little plainer than the AI's draft and a lot more like you. If the draft claims something untrue, such as a closeness you did not have, cut it.
