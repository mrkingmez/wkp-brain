## Verdict

**NOT SHOWN, leaning weak. Confidence: medium.**

Demand driver is real (a federal rule requires Level 1 self-assessment). But I found three problems:
1. A free official path exists (Project Spectrum, per DoD page; unverified on the live site) plus free commercial tools and templates. The paid price floor is already $59.
2. No direct buyer evidence found: no sales counts, no reviews on any competitor, no forum posts from small subs asking for a kit. Reddit and Etsy could not be read.
3. The Phase 2 date scout flagged is now confirmed suspended (July 13, 2026). That does not touch Level 1, but it removes the urgency story.

"Not shown" means unproven, not disproven. A cheap free test is in section 5.

## 2. Demand signals

Verified:
- Rule in force: 32 CFR 170.15 requires an annual Level 1 self-assessment submitted in SPRS, and 170.22 an affirmation; must be in place before award for contracts with a Level 1 requirement. Source: https://www.customsmobile.com/regulations/32/170.15 (via search summary); DoD CIO page https://dodcio.defense.gov/cmmc/about says Phase 1 runs Nov 10, 2025 to Nov 9, 2026 and focuses on Level 1 and 2 self-assessments (search summary; page 403 for me).
- Phase 2 suspended July 13, 2026 by the Department of War; Phase I self-assessment requirements "remain firmly in place"; 60-day Reform Task Force. Source: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/department-of-war-immediately-suspends-cmmc-phase-ii-requirements-launches-60-day-reform-review (fetched). Also WilmerHale, Jenner, Holland and Knight per search. Task Force report outcome: not found. Date of report: unverified (one source says mid-September; one says as of October still unpublished).
- Scale (secondary sources, not confirmed in Federal Register text; my attempt to read the PDF failed): about 338,000 covered entities, about 68 percent small; about 63 percent of the base needing Level 1 only; small entities rising from about 1,100 in year one to over 18,000 in year three to nearly 230,000 in year four. Source: https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/government-contracts-procurement-ppp/1677030/dod-finalizes-cmmc-rule-what-defense-contractors-need-to-know. I found no DoD count of Level 1 submissions actually made. **Real actual-demand count: not available.**
- Confusion signals (qualitative only): NCMBC says companies get stuck turning requirements into action; FedBiz Access says many small contractors think they need nothing or jump to the advanced level; a Reddit mirror snippet mentions "a lot of confused takes" on Phase 1. Sources: https://www.ncmbc.us/event/cmmc-level-1-implementation-workshop-hands-on-guidance-through-all-15-controls/, https://fedbizaccess.com/why-cmmc-level-1-matters-small-business-contractors/. No post counts.
- Many publicly funded workshops exist, which shows institutions see the need. Attendance numbers: not found.

## 3. Price tolerance

- Observed market range for Level 1 documents: 59 to 697 dollars; consulting 2,495 plus; vendor-estimated total assessment cost 4,000 to 6,000.
- No sales or reviews anywhere, so willingness to pay at any specific price: **not verified**.
- Inference: a one-person shop weighing a 4,000 dollar quote versus a free Project Spectrum assessment versus a 59 to 97 dollar template will compare to free first. A kit at 39 to 79 dollars would have to beat free with plain-language and time saved. A 20 dollar test of this is better than a model.

## 4. Channels and gatekeepers

- Primes flow requirements down contractually (DFARS 252.204-7021 in subcontract terms) and are responsible for verifying subs; subs receive questionnaires. Source: https://www.rain.tech/cmmc-for-subcontractors, https://consilien.com/cmmc-for-defense-subcontractors (search summaries).
- Level is set by the data the sub handles (FCI means Level 1), not the prime. Same sources.
- Inference: primes' supplier-quality and procurement teams, APEX Accelerators, MEP centers, SBDCs, and state military business centers are the trusted gatekeepers. They give away free help, so they are also competitors, but could also point to a good cheap resource. Whether they would is untested.
- Buyers find things via search and association webinars, not marketplaces browsing (scout's view; I agree, inferred).

## 5. What counts as a real signal, and a free test

Test must not need a product. Suggested, all free, none run by me:
1. Zac manually searches Etsy, Gumroad, Payhip, Amazon for "CMMC Level 1" and records listing counts and any visible review or sold counts. (I could not.) 20 minutes.
2. Zac checks Reddit r/CMMC, r/govcon, LinkedIn for "Level 1" posts in the last 90 days; count threads from actual small subs asking for templates (not vendors).
3. Zac completes the free Project Spectrum Level 1 assessment himself to see how good the free path is. If it is good, the paid case weakens.
4. Free lead magnet: a one-page "Level 1 in 15 questions" checklist posted in veteran business groups. Count downloads and, more important, unprompted "do you have the full version" replies.
5. Pre-sell test: a waitlist page (no product) with a stated price. 

Signal thresholds (my suggestion, arbitrary, set before looking): 25 or more emails from real small defense subs in 30 days, or 3 or more unprompted purchase requests, counts as shown. Under 5 emails counts as not shown.

Zac's own flags from the scout still stand: check his day job's outside-work policy, and add a plain "not legal advice, not a certification" line.

## 6. Bottom line

- Level 1 is a real federal requirement for a large pool of small firms (size per secondary sources).
- But the free tier is substantial, paid templates already sell from 59 dollars with zero visible reviews, and I found no sales evidence for any competitor. Cannot tell whether anybody makes money at the low end.
- Verdict: NOT SHOWN. Do a 1 to 2 week free test (steps 1 to 4) before any build. If step 3 shows the free government tool is strong, drop it.

Confidence: medium on the verdict (many sources agree on the free tier and prices), low on market size (secondary sources only), nothing known on conversion.
