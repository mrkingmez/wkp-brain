The case for paying customers has not been made, the one differentiator is something Zac cannot build himself, and it competes for the same fragmented hours as a main effort that has zero listings.

## IS THE PROPOSED TEST RIGHT?

No, on order and on measure.

- Order. It skips the two gates that can kill this for free: the day-job answer, and a 20-minute look at Gumroad, Payhip, Etsy and Reddit. The deep dive itself says do not start the lead magnet before the competitor check.
- Measure. Emails for a free PDF measure interest in free things. The buyer here already has free things. The question is willingness to pay over free, and the test never asks it.
- Audience. Veteran business groups are mostly not FCI-handling DoD subs. A strong result there proves the wrong audience likes a freebie.
- Unverifiable bar. "Real small defense subs" cannot be confirmed from a signup. Also the two files disagree on the bar (01: 10 replies in 14 days, 02: 25 emails in 30 days). Pick one before running, or the bar will drift to fit the result.
- Hidden cost. The checklist is public copy, so it goes through script-room, marketing-director, no-ai-slop, and clears the day-job gate. "3 to 5 hours" is low.

## THE SMALLEST HONEST TEST

In this order. Stop at the first fail.

1. Day-job gate (Zac, 1 to 2 hr). Answer VENTURE-2026-11-03-01 in writing, specifically the county's outside-employment and ethics policy. No or unclear: stop. This one is free and final.
2. Desk check (Zac or an agent with a browser, about 1 hr). Real listing counts with any review or sold counts on Gumroad, Payhip, Etsy. Count r/CMMC and r/govcon threads from the last 90 days where an actual small sub (not a vendor) asks how to do Level 1 or SPRS. Open the five blocked official pages and confirm Level 1 status. Fail if a free kit already covers scorer plus plumbing, or if there are under 3 real sub threads in 90 days.
3. Try the free path yourself (Zac, 1 to 2 hr). Run Project Spectrum's free Level 1 assessment. If it is good and plain, the paid case is dead. Write down the three places it lost you. Those three are the product, or there is no product.
4. Five conversations, not 25 emails (Zac, 3 to 5 hr over two weeks). Five owners of tiny subs who actually got a flowdown notice (Georgia APEX, Augusta and Fort Eisenhower small business events, an r/CMMC thread). Ask: what did you do for Level 1, what did it cost you in hours and money, what would you have paid to skip the worst part. Pass bar: at least 2 of 5 name a dollar amount at or above $39 for the plumbing part, unprompted. Under 2: stop.
5. Only then, a priced pre-order page for the plumbing guide alone. Paid pre-orders, not emails. Ten sales or refund and stop.

Steps 1 to 3 cost about 4 hours and $0 and can kill the idea without building anything. Nothing public happens until step 4.

Timing: steps 1 and 2 are cheap enough to run now. Steps 3 onward should wait until the Kingdom Planners shop is live, unless Zac decides otherwise, since KP is the main effort and tests the same "can Zac sell a spreadsheet" question with work already done.
