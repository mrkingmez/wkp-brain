## 1. Veterans Day / Christmas photo restore for families (fixed price, flat per photo)

- Pitch: "Send me the cracked, faded photo of your veteran. Restored, returned in 48 hours." Who pays: adult children and grandchildren of veterans, and veterans themselves, buying for Nov 11 and Christmas gifts.
- Demand evidence: thin. Sellers charge about $3 to $50 per photo on Fiverr-style listings, AI-assisted at the low end ($5 to $20), $35 to $350 plus for manual pro work ([fash.com](https://fash.com/costs/photo-restoration-prices), [Fiverr example](https://fiverr.com/umair2020/restore-old-photo-fix-scratch-face-retouch-colorize-image), [Photo restoration cost comparison](https://artimagehub.vercel.app/blog/photo-restoration-cost-comparison)). One provider lists $29.95 flat or $19.95 per photo in a 5 pack ([search result, vendor page](https://memorycherish.com/photo-restoration-prices/)). These are asking prices from vendors. Holiday seasonality and order volume: NOT VERIFIED.
- Saturation: very crowded on Fiverr and online at $3 to $50. Do not compete there. The only angle is local and personal: veteran-made, in-person trust, Veterans Day timing. Local Augusta/Grovetown competitors: not verified.
- Different in one sentence: a disabled Iraq veteran restores your veteran's photo, with a free plain-language note on what was repaired, sold inside the community Zac is already part of.
- Setup time: 2 to 3 hours (one before/after sample, one short offer post, payment method).
- Cost: $0 if he uses tools he already owns. Tool choice and AI-terms: NOT VERIFIED, he must pick the tool and confirm it allows paid client work. Do not sell it as hand restoration if AI did it. Say "AI-assisted, I check every face and detail."
- Realistic 30 days (assumption: he posts to his own Facebook, 2 to 4 veteran/local groups he already belongs to where self-promo is allowed, and messages 15 to 25 people he knows): 3 to 10 orders at $20 to $30 each = $60 to $300. Could be $0 if the posts get ignored. Weekly hours: 2 to 4 once orders come.
- First-dollar path, numbered:
  1. Zac picks one old photo from his own family (WWII relative or his own Army photo) and restores it. This is the before/after sample. About 1 hour.
  2. Zac writes the offer: one photo $20, 3 photos $50, 48 hour return, digital file by email. He sends it to me and I draft copy through the writing room and no-ai-slop. Nothing posts until he posts it.
  3. Zac checks each group's rules on selling. Only posts where allowed.
  4. Zac posts the sample plus offer on his own page and in allowed groups, deadline "order by Nov 8 for Veterans Day".
  5. Customer sends photo by Messenger or email. Zac restores, sends a watermarked preview, takes payment by an account he already has (Venmo, Cash App, PayPal), then sends the clean file. Payment before the clean file.
  6. Ask each happy buyer to share the before/after.
- Biggest risk: price race to the bottom and low reach. Also quality: a bad AI face on someone's grandfather ends the trade. Test on 3 of his own photos first.
- Zac must do himself: pick tool, post, answer messages, take payment, check group rules.

## 3. Podcast and creator Shorts packages, flat price, small creators and veteran-run shows

- Pitch: "10 captioned vertical clips from your episode, delivered in 3 days, flat $X." Who pays: small podcasters and YouTubers who record but do not edit clips, plus veteran business owners with a video or podcast.

Freelance short-form rates in general: $15 to $150 per Short, beginners $15 to $40 on Fiverr, $50 to $200 for experienced editors, and one recurring job at $20 to $50 per video for 30 plus videos a month ([Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-much-to-pay-video-editor-youtube-shorts), [Ad Snipper](https://adsnipper.com/?p=2494), [HireTalent.ph](https://hiretalent.ph/how-much-should-i-pay-a-filipino-video-editor)). These are blog and vendor numbers, directional only.
- Saturation: crowded. AI tools run about $6 to $90 a month (OpusClip, Ssemble), which is the price ceiling for lazy buyers. Number of done-for-you sellers: not verified, clearly dozens. Fiverr reports from sellers say new sellers are nearly invisible, one source says "generic titles like I will do video editing compete with 100,000 other gigs" ([Fiverr guide roundup](https://community.fiverr.com/public/blogs/a-fiverr-guide-for-one-of-the-most-asked-questions-how-to-get-that-first-order-2026-07-14)). Do not start on Fiverr.
- Different in one sentence: the clips come from the same finished pipeline Zac already runs on his own channel (5 finished shorts per episode, burned-in captions, timed hooks, cover frame), and he can show 30 real shipped examples.
- Setup time: 3 to 5 hours (a 1 page offer, 3 sample clips from his own FrostCast or a creator who says yes, a price list).
- Cost: $0, tools already installed (shorts-factory, local Whisper). Zac must not use anything with a paid-credit meter.
- Realistic 30 days (assumption: 20 personal messages or comments to small creators and veteran podcasters he already follows, 1 to 2 replies, 0 to 2 clients): 0 to 2 clients at $100 to $250 for a first pack = $0 to $500. Most likely one small paid pack or none. Weekly hours: 3 to 6 per client. This is the highest ceiling and the lowest odds of a dollar inside a week.
- First-dollar path, numbered:
  1. Zac picks 3 finished Shorts from WWD and lifts the best 15 to 20 seconds into a 1 minute sample reel. No new work.
  2. Zac lists 10 to 15 small creators or veteran-run shows he already follows, 500 to 20,000 followers, who post long video or audio with no clips. He reads their last 3 posts.

  4. First pack price $120 for 10 clips (anchored under the $297 seller; assumption, not tested). Half up front by an account he already has, rest on delivery.
  5. Deliver in 3 days, ask for a testimonial and one referral.
- Biggest risk: overlaps and competes with the Post-Production house for his limited hours, and a clipped public episode can get a copyright strike if the creator did not agree. Ask first, only use content the creator confirms.
