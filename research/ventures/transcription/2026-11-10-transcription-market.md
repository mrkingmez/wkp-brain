# Transcription work market check
Date: 2026-11-10. Read-only desk research. Nothing signed up, posted, or spent.
Labels: OBSERVED = read on the company's own page this session. SECONDARY = blog, review, aggregator, or search summary, not the company. NOT AVAILABLE = looked, did not find. INFERRED = my reasoning, not a source.
Warning: most pay blogs are affiliate or AI-vendor content. Treat SECONDARY numbers as leads.

## Verdict first
1. Marketplace transcriber jobs (Rev, GoTranscript, TranscribeMe, Scribie, Speechpad, Verbit) pay per audio minute, not per hour worked. Headline rates look fine, but beginners reportedly earn low hourly money. Realistic for Zac: about 10 to 25 dollars per working hour IF his Whisper plus Claude workflow is allowed (INFERRED). Whether it is allowed is NOT AVAILABLE on any company page I read. Ask each one in writing before applying.
2. Direct clients (podcasters, small YouTubers, churches, academics) are the better route. Price range SECONDARY 1.00 to 2.50 dollars per audio minute. With an AI-first workflow Zac's hourly could be 30 to 60 dollars before platform fees (INFERRED, depends on audio quality and how many hours of proofing per audio hour). Needs finding buyers, which is the real work.
3. Time to first money: marketplaces slow (test, review, waitlist), direct clients can pay in days if one says yes.
4. Try first: one paid pilot with a real small buyer through a route he already has (his own Facebook groups, podcasters he knows), not a marketplace application. Run the marketplace applications in parallel only after sending the policy question.
5. Honest ceiling: this is a few hundred dollars a month for a part-time evening workload, not rent money, unless he lands a recurring client (a weekly podcast is the model case).

## 2. Direct-client route

What freelancers and shops charge per audio minute for human-verified transcripts (all SECONDARY, vendor blogs and listings, none dated cleanly to 2026 unless noted):
- Marketplace sellers: Fiverr 0.50 to 2.00 per minute, Upwork 20 to 60 per audio hour (WealthVieu guide). Freelancer.com bids seen: 1.00 and 0.85 per minute.
- General direct clients: 1 to 2 per audio minute (2026 guide), podcast-specific 1.50 to 2.50 per minute (roundup).
- Named shops for scale: Atomicscribe 1.50 per audio minute for 1 to 3 speakers, 2.25 for 4 or more. GoTranscript's own customer rate 0.90 per audio minute with 5 to 7 day turnaround. Rev human 1.50 to 1.99 per minute with about 12 hour standard turnaround.
- Machine-only transcripts are cheap (Rev AI 0.25 per minute per a Sonix comparison, SECONDARY), so a small buyer will pay Zac for the human check, not the raw draft.
- Pay for a worker average: ZipRecruiter lists 22.63 dollars per hour for freelance audio transcriber in the US, June 2026 (SECONDARY).
- Turnaround norms: shops quote 12 hours to 7 days. Zac can compete on 24 to 48 hours for short files (INFERRED).
- Niche premiums: legal and medical pay more but come with confidentiality and compliance duties (see risks). Academic interviews and church sermons are lower risk.

Where small buyers look:
- Fiverr and Upwork listings exist for podcast transcription (OBSERVED in search results, no counts retrieved).
- Facebook groups, podcast communities, church staff, local colleges: NOT AVAILABLE as researched data. This is the INFERRED route with no platform fee, and Zac's own reach (he has an audience and Matt's network) matters here.

Platform fee cuts:
- Fiverr takes 20 percent of the seller's earnings, consistent across sources (SECONDARY, a Fiverr terms excerpt via an aggregator; no official page fetched). Buyer fees are separate and reported inconsistently.
- Upwork freelancer fee is variable, 0 to 15 percent per contract since May 2025 (SECONDARY, sources disagree on typical level, one claims flat 10).
- Direct clients: no platform cut, but Zac handles invoicing and payment risk. Use PayPal or Stripe invoices and take payment up front for new clients (INFERRED).

## What to try first
1. Pick a clean sample: one FrostCast clip or any public-domain audio, run the pipeline, time the real hours it takes, note error rate. This sets the real price.
2. Offer a free 10 minute sample plus a paid first file to 5 to 10 people he already knows: podcasters, pastors, small YouTubers, students doing interviews. Price at 1.25 per minute with 48 hour turnaround and one free round of fixes.
3. In parallel, send the policy question to Rev and GoTranscript. Apply only where a written answer allows his tools.
4. Hold off on Fiverr and Upwork until he has two reviews' worth of off-platform proof, or accept the 20 percent cut for reach.

## Risks
- Confidential audio. Uploading a client's audio to any cloud AI, including Claude, is sharing it with a third party. Get the client's OK, say it in the agreement, and keep the Whisper step local (it already is). Proofreading text with Claude still sends their words out. Offer a no-cloud tier if a client insists (INFERRED).
- Medical. Any provider audio is likely protected health information. A transcriptionist generally needs a business associate agreement with the provider, and consumer AI tools without one are risky (SECONDARY, vendor and law firm blogs, not HHS). Skip medical and therapy audio entirely.
- Legal. Court, deposition, attorney client audio carries privilege and certification expectations. Skip.
- Disclosing AI use. I found no authoritative rule that a freelancer must disclose AI assistance (NOT AVAILABLE, needs legal review). Recommendation, INFERRED: disclose plainly ("machine draft, human verified") because the clients who ask for "human transcribed" may object, and a fraud claim costs more than a lost gig. Never claim hand typed from scratch.
- Platform rules. Verbit and likely others control the tools. Violating an AI clause can mean unpaid work and a ban.
- Taxes. Contractor income, nothing withheld by Speechpad (SECONDARY) and others. Zac needs to set aside tax and watch the day-job conflict question he already has open for outside work.
- Accuracy liability. Names, jargon, and speaker labels need the human pass. Voiceprints are not enrolled in his pipeline, so speakers come back as Guest or Unknown and need hand mapping, which adds time.
