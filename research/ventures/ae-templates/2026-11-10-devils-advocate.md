## VERDICT: HOLD

## THE ONE THING

**The part Claude can do is not the part buyers pay for.** A .jsx script can lay out layers, keyframes, easing and expressions. What makes a template sell is design taste (type, timing, texture, sound), plus the buyer-side build: clean controls (Essential Properties or a .mogrt), an organized project, a help file, and a preview video that looks better than the competitors' next to it in search. All of that is the polish step, and the polish step is exactly where Zac's skill is rated "still developing." So the real split is roughly Claude drafts the skeleton, Zac does the product. That is the opposite of what makes an idea fit fragment time.

## HOLES

### 1. Time to first sale is unknown and probably long
- Why it matters: Zac needs cash now. A template earns nothing until it is built, previewed, uploaded, approved by Pond5 review, found in search, and bought. No step of that chain has a time on it yet.
- Missing evidence: Pond5 review turnaround for AE projects, and Zac's OWN Pond5 stock footage sales history. His own account is the only base rate that counts. If his footage has sold little or nothing, that tells you how much search traffic his listings get on that site.
- Fix: before anything is built, Zac checks his Pond5 dashboard and writes down lifetime sales and earnings (10 minutes). If he cannot say, the idea has no base rate.

### 2. Quality bar versus established sellers
- Why it matters: template buyers compare previews side by side. A first template from a developing motion designer, generated from a script, competes against sellers who have shipped many. Untested assumption pending the market report: subscription libraries (Envato Elements, Motion Array) and the free templates inside Premiere's Essential Graphics panel set the price floor near zero for generic titles and lower thirds.
- Fix: do not build generic categories (lower thirds, simple titles, transitions). If anything gets built, it is one narrow, specific piece the market report shows has buyers.

### 3. "Scripted" templates tend to look scripted
- Why it matters: code-built motion defaults to even spacing, linear or stock easing, symmetric layouts, no grit. It reads as tidy, not as designed. The WWD kit proves Claude can build usable rendered pieces in Python (Next Up cards, arcade kit), but those are one-off edit pieces for a show Zac controls, not products that strangers customize. Even there, the Clayface logo melt went to v3 and the notes still say "texture still blotchy."
- Fix: judge by a preview, not by the script. If Zac would not put the result in a WWD episode without fixing it, it does not go on sale.

### 4. Military and sports themes carry clearance traps
- Why it matters: the obvious "military" look leans on branch emblems, unit insignia, seals, and rank marks. Those belong to the services and are protected. "Sports" templates that look like a league or team (colors plus logo layouts, broadcast packages mimicking a network) carry the same problem. A rejected or pulled listing is wasted hours; a complaint is worse.
- Fix: generic only. No insignia, seals, real patches, team names, league looks, or network-style packages. Route the first piece through `clearance` before upload.

### 5. Assets in the template must be redistributable
- Why it matters: the P:\ drive holds Adobe SFX, blood and lava packs, transitions, LUTs and overlays that Zac owns as a user. Owning a pack to use in your own edits is not the same as the right to resell it inside a template. Same for fonts. Firefly output in a sold template also needs Pond5's AI-content rule checked (not retrieved).
- Fix: build from shapes, text and code only, and use fonts with a license that allows bundling, or list fonts as "free download, not included." Nothing from P:\ goes into a sold file without a license read.

### 6. Hours compared with the options already on the table
- Photo restore (GO WITH CHANGES 11-04): first dollar inside 7 days plausible, one photo is a 20 to 40 minute self-contained task.
- Ian Griffin book trailer: a real client, a named person, a real deadline (Veterans Day if wanted), price path already on paper in TASKS.md, waiting only on Zac's choice. It is also the only option that builds a Post-Production portfolio piece someone paid for.
- Kingdom Planners: six finished files on disk. The work is listing, not making.
- AE templates: zero products exist, the skill gap is in the critical step, and the market is unread.
- Ranking for scarce hours: Ian (decide the price today), photo restore, KP test, AE templates last.

### 7. Version, render, and support load
- Why it matters: every template has to say which AE version it needs and keep working for buyers, and preview renders eat evening hours on a PC that only recently stopped freezing. Buyer support questions are ongoing unpaid time.
- Fix: count preview render and upload time inside the test's hour cap, not outside it.
