## B. Transcription: HOLD

### Strongest case against
**The buyer this needs is disappearing, and the buyers who remain are the ones he has to refuse.** Small podcasters and YouTubers already get free or near-free machine transcripts from the tools they record and edit in (YouTube auto captions, and recording or editing apps that build transcripts in; not checked here, ASSUMPTION based on how those products are sold). The research itself says machine-only runs about $0.25 a minute. The people who still pay $1 to $2.50 a minute for human-verified text are mostly legal, medical, and academic. The research says skip medical and legal. Academic interviews usually sit under research ethics rules on participant data, and sending participant words through a cloud AI for proofing is likely to break those protocols (ASSUMPTION, not checked). Churches tend to want it free. What is left is a thin slice: a podcaster who wants a clean, speaker-labeled transcript and will pay for it weekly. That person may exist. Nothing in the repo shows one.

### Failure points, one at a time
- **Marketplaces: KILL.** Rev, GoTranscript, TranscribeMe, Scribie, Speechpad, and Verbit pay on audio minutes, not working time. Verbit and Rev supply their own machine draft, which removes his Whisper edge. Outside AI on their audio is unanswered policy and a ban risk. Newcomer pay is reported at $3 to $10 an hour (SECONDARY). With a full-time job, that is the worst dollar per hour on the whole money list. Do not send the policy questions, do not take the tests. It costs evening hours to learn a no.
- **Fiverr: HOLD.** 20 percent cut. Zero reviews. Competing against sellers who price at AI rates. The research's own net estimate for a new Fiverr seller is $20 to $45 an hour, and that rests on a proofing time that has never been measured.
- **Time per dollar is unmeasured, and the guess is optimistic.** The research assumes 1 to 1.5 human hours per audio hour on clean two-speaker audio. A real accuracy check means listening to the whole file, so the floor is about 1 hour per audio hour before a single fix. His own pipeline returns speakers as Guest or Unknown (voiceprints not enrolled), so every file needs hand speaker mapping on top. Realistic is closer to 1.5 to 2.5 hours per audio hour (ASSUMPTION). At $1.25 a minute that is $30 to $50 an hour on paper, with zero clients.
- **The 3-hour weeknight.** One 60-minute episode is most of an evening. He gets one file a night at most, and Wednesday is gone to FrostCast.
- **The core skill is his hardest one.** ME.md says reading and spelling are still hard for him. Transcription quality is judged on exact words, names, and spelling. Claude can carry the spelling pass, but the human check (does this text match what was said) is listen-and-read at the same time, for an hour or more, on a deadline. That is not a fragment-friendly, ADHD-friendly task. Photo restoration is visual and has a clear finish. This one does not. Said with respect, because it is a real fit problem, not a judgment of him.
- **Confidentiality.** Whisper runs local, good. But proofing with Claude sends the client's words to a cloud service. Every client must OK that in writing before the file is touched. That line will lose some clients, and it has to be there anyway.
- **AI disclosure.** No rule found (NOT AVAILABLE). Disclose anyway: "machine draft, human checked." Never "hand typed." A client who wanted human typing and finds out later is a refund at best.
- **GPU contention.** The same RTX 5060 runs the FrostCast transcription chain and the WWD shorts pipeline. A client deadline that lands on a Wednesday or a review night collides with the shows. Small, but real.
- **Brand splitting.** This is a Post-Production service in everything but name, and Post-Production is ACTIVE-UNSCHEDULED. Running it as an ongoing service is a venture promotion, which only Zac can make. Two new money lines plus WWD, Kingdom Planners (MAIN EFFORT, still no shop), and the free Ian trailer, all in the same evening fragments, is the "keep adding cool-factor ideas" trap ME.md names.
- **Tax and day job.** Same as idea A. Same checks, same answers. No need to do them twice, but they apply.

### What is genuinely in its favor (so it does not get thrown out)
- The pipeline already exists and works every week (FrostCast). Setup cost is near zero.
- A single weekly podcast client is a recurring check, which photo restore is not. The research's model case (4 episodes a month at $90 each, ASSUMPTION) is the only path in either idea to steady money.
