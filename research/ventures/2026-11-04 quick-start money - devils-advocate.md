## Idea 1: Veterans Day / Christmas photo restore

**VERDICT: GO WITH CHANGES**

**Strongest case against:** buyers can restore a photo themselves for free or close to it with consumer AI restore apps (the report does not check this; the $3 to $50 Fiverr range is the paid floor, not the free one). What Zac is really selling is trust and done-for-you service, not restoration. The real failure risk is that AI restore invents a face. If grandpa comes back looking like a stranger, it hurts Zac's name in exactly the community the offer depends on. On top of that, a 48 hour turnaround promise runs into a full-time job, FrostCast on Wednesday, and the WWD review pipeline.

**Evidence missing:**
- Which tool he would use, whether it's free, and whether its terms allow paid client work. The report flags this as NOT VERIFIED and it blocks the whole idea.
- Whether the groups exist and allow selling (see THE ONE THING).
- Whether his restore quality on badly damaged faces is good enough. Untested.
- Whether Zac wants his disability and Iraq service in the sales line ("a disabled Iraq veteran restores..."). That is his call, not a copy decision. Precedent: the POW/MIA post was kept off his own service on purpose.

**Changes:**
1. Do the 3-photo quality test first. Use his own family photos, at least one with real face damage. If any face drifts, the offer says "cleanup and color, faces untouched," or the idea stops.
2. Make the turnaround "within 5 days" and cap it at 5 orders a week. Don't promise 48 hours.
3. Treat Christmas as the real window, not Veterans Day. Nov 11 is 8 days out and the report's "order by Nov 8" leaves him about 2 days to deliver. Use Veterans Day as the hook for the post, and set the deadline at "order by Dec 12 for Christmas."
4. Take payment before sending the clean file, as the report already says. Keep that.

**Cheapest 7-day test:** restore the 3 photos (about 2 hours total). Post one before/after plus the price on his own Facebook only, plus any group that allows selling, by Thu 11/5. Count real "how do I order" messages by Wed 11/11. Kill line: zero inquiries means the network isn't there, so stop. Go line: 2 or more inquiries, or 1 paid order.

## Idea 2: Kingdom Planners on Payhip

**VERDICT: GO WITH CHANGES (as a 1-hour side test, not a launch)**

**Strongest case against:** the one differentiator the report leads with, "real whole-person VA combined rating math," is only unique against Etsy listings. Free online VA combined-rating calculators are common on the open web, so a stranger has little reason to pay $18 for the math alone. The same goes for the Debt Freedom Planner at $12, since free debt snowball sheets are everywhere. There is also a timing trap. The VA Tracker uses "official 2026 figures," and VA pay rates normally change with the cost-of-living adjustment every December 1. Selling it now could mean the file is out of date within 4 weeks, and a wrong payment estimate in a paid file is exactly the "broken formula" risk the report names. Not verified: which rate year the file actually holds, and the Dec 2026 COLA date and amount. Check both before selling.

**Evidence missing:**
- Any sign that strangers buy military planner spreadsheets. The report's own comps show zero reviews and 1 sale, which is evidence against.
- The Google Sheets compatibility pass, which still hasn't been run.
- Payhip's real fee and test-purchase rules. Both come from secondary sources.

**Changes:**
1. Measure demand before opening a store. The free teaser post (a worked combined-rating example) is the test. If nobody asks "where do I get this," Payhip doesn't matter.
2. Only list the VA Tracker if the rate table is current and has a dated "rates as of" line on the Start Here tab, plus a plain "estimate only, not legal or claims advice" line. Stay clear of anything that looks like VA claim help, which an earlier scout already set aside.
3. Hold the Debt Freedom Planner. It has no differentiator.

**Cheapest 7-day test:** 1 hour total. Open the VA Tracker in Google Sheets and check the rate year. Post the worked example (no link) in one group or on his own page, and count replies asking for the file. Kill line: zero asks in 7 days means no Payhip account, and the file stays parked for the Etsy shop. Go line: 3 or more asks means set up Payhip and sell to those people first.

## Idea 3: Flat-price Shorts packages for small creators

**VERDICT: HOLD (one message test allowed, no production)**

**Strongest case against:** the step-by-step plan contradicts itself. Step 3 tells Zac to send each creator "one clip made from their own public episode." The risk line two bullets later warns that clipping a public episode without consent can bring a copyright strike. Downloading someone's YouTube video to cut it also runs against YouTube's terms. That cold-pitch move is the core of the plan, and it can't stay. Next, the proof. The "30 real shipped examples" are WWD Shorts, and the 2026-11-02 subscriber deep dive found WWD Shorts bring in 0.24 subs per 1,000 views, against 2.4 for long-form. Any creator who asks "do your clips grow channels?" gets an honest answer that hurts. Then the offer itself. "10 clips from one episode" is twice what the pipeline makes, since shorts-factory is capped at 5 finished clips per episode with a quality floor. Zac would be selling a product his own process says not to make. Finally, scope: this is a Post-Production service in everything but name, and Post-Production is ACTIVE-UNSCHEDULED. Spending real weekly hours on it is effectively a venture promotion, which is Zac's call. It also competes directly with the WWD review pipeline for the same evening hours.

**Evidence missing:**
- Any confirmed list of creators who post long video with no clips and would answer a cold message.
- Proof the pipeline runs on someone else's footage. It's tuned to WWD (StreamYard layout, speaker mapping, fonts).
- A source for the "$6 to $90 a month" AI tool price ceiling. It isn't cited.

**Changes (if Zac wants the test):**
1. Never cut a creator's content before they say yes. The pitch uses the WWD sample reel only and offers "a free 1-clip sample from your episode if you want one."
2. Make the offer 5 clips per episode, matching the pipeline.
3. Lead the pitch with finish quality (captions, hooks, covers), not growth claims.

**Cheapest 7-day test:** 1 hour. Build the sample reel from already-posted Shorts, then send 10 messages using the corrected pitch. Kill line: zero replies in 7 days. Go line: any creator says yes to a free sample. Only then does it go to Zac as a Post-Production scheduling decision.
