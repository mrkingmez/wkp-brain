# Venture scout, first scan, 2026-10-26

Proposals only. Nothing here is a venture until Zac says so. Nothing was created, listed, posted, bought, or sent.

## Read this first: how solid is the evidence

- This scan used web search only. Etsy, Reddit, and Amazon listing counts were NOT retrievable. Every competitor count marked "not verified" needs a 20 minute manual check by Zac or a later run before money or time goes in.
- The searches mostly returned vendor pages and listicles, not strangers complaining. I found almost no real "I wish this existed" posts. So demand for ideas 2 to 5 is inferred, not proven.
- Only idea 1 has a hard, dated demand driver (a federal rule). That is why it ranks first.
- Search results pointed to a lot of material I could not open. I only report what the search summaries stated. Dates and prices below come from those summaries, not from my own page reads.
- Nothing here is a slam dunk. I rate idea 1 as worth a cheap test, not as a sure thing.

## Ranked ideas

### 1. CMMC Level 1 starter kit for tiny veteran-owned defense subcontractors

- Pitch: A plain-language, low-price workbook pack (Excel self-assessment scorer for the 15 Level 1 practices, a fill-in policy and system plan in Word, and a step-by-step for the yearly SPRS entry and affirmation). Who pays: 1 to 10 person contractors and subs, many veteran-owned, who must report Level 1 status to bid or keep a DoD subcontract.
- The gap:
  - DoD CIO says Phase 1 ran Nov 10, 2025 to Nov 9, 2026, mostly Level 1 and Level 2 self-assessments, with affirmations entered in SPRS. Phase 2 (third-party Level 2 certification where required) is dated Nov 10, 2026, which is two weeks away. One vendor blog says Phase 2 is paused as of July 2026. I could not confirm that officially, so treat it as unknown. Sources: [DoD CIO CMMC](https://dodcio.defense.gov/cmmc/about), [Schwabe summary](https://www.schwabe.com/publication/dod-issues-final-rule-implementing-cmmc-requirements-in-dfars/), [EasyIT Guys](https://easyitguys.com/compliance/november-10-2025-the-day-cmmc-becomes-real-for-dod-contractors-and-their-subcontractors).
  - Level 1 needs a yearly self-assessment and a senior officer affirmation, and a Level 1 plan has no POA&M option. Primes must flow it down to subs that touch FCI. A small shop that gets this wrong can lose a bid or a renewal.
  - One consultancy blog argues the policy folder is only about ten percent of the real work. That is a hint that buyers are confused about the process, not just the paperwork. Same SPRS source as above.
- Saturation read:
  - Seen in search: TalonPoint Security on Payhip, a 6 template starter at $149 and a 12 template pack at $269 ([Payhip](https://payhip.com/b/53XsZ), [Payhip](https://payhip.com/b/CWB5Q)). ComplianceForge Level 1 bundle listed at $5,344 ([ComplianceForge](https://complianceforge.com/bundle/cmmc-bundle-1)). Security Scientist sells 12 CMMC templates, price not shown ([site](https://templates.securityscientist.net/cmmc/)). A Fiverr Level 1 gig exists ([Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/thecyberfriend/help-you-align-with-cmmc-level-1)).
  - So at least 4 visible competitors, none verified as high volume. Count of sellers on Etsy, Gumroad, and Google ads: not verified.
  - Established big players sit at $5,000 plus. Small players sit at $149 to $269. A real Level 1 only product at $39 to $79 with an actual scorer spreadsheet looks open, but I did not verify that nobody is already there.
- Startup cost: $0 to $20 (Payhip or Gumroad free tiers, domain optional). Time to first dollar: honest range 3 to 8 weeks, because B2B buyers find things by search or LinkedIn, not by browsing. Weekly hours: 4 to 6 to build, 2 after.
- Fit: Zac works in cyber and is studying for CySA+. Spreadsheet and template skills are his strongest suit (Kingdom Planners pipeline). Veteran community access gives a free first audience.
- Different, in one sentence: Level 1 only, priced for a one-person shop, with a working Excel scorer written by a practicing cyber person who is also a veteran.
- First test (under a weekend, under $25): Build a free one page "Level 1 in 15 questions" checklist, post it in veteran business groups, and see whether people ask for the full kit. Also search "CMMC Level 1 template" on Etsy and Gumroad and write down the real listing counts.
- Biggest risk: Phase 2 may be paused or shift the rules, and a template can age fast. Also check his day job rules on outside cyber work, and add a plain "not legal advice, not a certification" line. Not a CPA or licensed role, so no licensing filter hit, but verify that. Platform risk is low (Payhip or Gumroad sell digital templates), but Etsy buyers are the wrong crowd.

### 2. Done-for-you sermon and ministry short clips for small churches (and veteran-run ministries)

- Pitch: Weekly, flat-fee sermon-to-Shorts for churches with video but no media person. Who pays: church admin or pastor, $50 to $150 a month.
- Gap: AI clip tools sell at about $15 to $90 a month (quso at $29, OpusClip $15, Pulpit AI $39, Sermon Shots $63, [comparison](https://quso.ai/blog/best-sermon-clip-generators)). Done-for-you shows up at $58 a month for two sermons, up to a $500 to $2,000 estimate, and one job post at $750 a month for 30 reels ([REACHRIGHT](https://reachrightstudios.com/blog/diy-vs-done-for-you-sermon-clips/), [job post](https://www.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/1585293), [Ruah](https://ruahcreativehouse.org/blog/church-video-production-cost/)). The tools exist because pastors do not want to edit, which hints at demand. A caution from the same sources: AI clippers need video, and many small churches record audio only.
- Saturation: tools plus agencies are plentiful. Number of done-for-you providers: not verified. Weak differentiation.
- Cost: $0. First dollar: 2 to 6 weeks, but only via direct outreach (and I may not contact anyone, so that would be Zac's move later). Hours: 3 to 5 a week per 3 churches.
- Fit: strong. Editing, Shorts factory pipeline from WWD (shorts-factory.md already built), faith.
- Different: it is hard to say. A veteran-run, faith-based clip service is a positioning angle, not a moat. By the saturation rule that makes it weak.
- Note: this is close to the Post-Production house, so only count it as separate if it gets its own offer and brand. Also touches recurring customer contact each week, which brushes the "daily customer service" filter (weekly, not daily).
- First test: Make 3 sample clips from one public-domain or permission-given sermon and see if a church page will show them. Needs Zac's call on who to ask.
- Biggest risk: price race to the bottom against $29 a month tools.

### 3. Fort Eisenhower and Augusta newcomer guide for Army cyber and signal families

- Pitch: A local, honest PDF guide: where to live in Grovetown, Evans, Martinez, Augusta; commute and gate times; schools overview; vets and spouse job leads; what to avoid. Who pays: incoming soldiers and spouses, $12 to $24.
- Gap: Searching turned up generic base pages and real-estate and VA-loan marketing, no good independent newcomer guide ([PCS Pay It Forward](https://pcspayitforward.com/buying-a-home-near-fort-gordon/), [Honest MOS compare](https://www.honestmos.com/compare/base/fort-jackson-vs-fort-eisenhower)). The base is home to Army Cyber Center of Excellence and Signal training with constant turnover, per those results. I found no evidence of buyer complaints. No numbers on how many people search for it.
- Saturation: low in what I saw, but I did not check Etsy, Facebook groups, or Amazon. Not verified.
- Cost: $0. First dollar: 3 to 6 weeks. Hours: 6 to 8 to write, 1 a week to keep current. Zac has 15 plus years here, which is the real edge.
- Different: written by a local Army veteran who lives in Grovetown, not a realtor.
- First test: Outline the guide, then check Etsy and Google for "Fort Eisenhower PCS guide" and record the real counts.
- Risk: small market, and housing and schools data goes stale. Real estate affiliate links could bring FTC disclosure duties. Overlaps Kingdom Planners' PCS planner audience, so run it as a companion, not a new brand. Careful with school info: no child data, just public facts.

### 4. WWII family records and story kit

- Pitch: A guided kit for grandkids hunting a relative's WWII service. Includes SF-180 walkthrough, the 1973 fire reconstruction steps, unit and morning report finders, and an optional AI-restored photo plus a short family tribute video as an upsell. Who pays: adult grandchildren, $15 to $35 for the kit.
- Gap: official sources exist but are dense. The 1973 NPRC fire destroyed about 80 percent of Army records for people discharged 1912 to 1960 and about 75 percent of certain Air Force records, and reconstruction can take 6 months or more ([VA](https://www.va.gov/records/get-military-service-records/reconstruct-records/), [NARA](https://www.archives.gov/veterans/faqs/)). That is a real pain point for the buyer. I did not find buyer complaints or sales data, so demand is plausible, not proven.
- Saturation: genealogy is crowded and the core info is free. Etsy and Amazon counts: not verified.
- Cost: $0. First dollar: 4 to 8 weeks. Hours: 5 to 8 to build.
- Fit: WWII interest (RUBY TWO, What If), research skills, AI image and video. The restore and tribute upsell uses existing skills.
- Different: only if the kit bundles the "what to do when the file burned" path with a ready letter and a story-building template. Otherwise it is a free-info rewrite.
- First test: Draft the one page "my file burned" decision guide and see if it holds up against the official pages.
- Risk: the free official info competes. Do not charge for filing on someone's behalf.

### 5. Military retirement and homecoming tribute videos

- Pitch: A fixed-price tribute video from supplied photos and clips for a retirement ceremony or homecoming. Who pays: families and units, $60 to $200.
- Gap: I found only a funeral slideshow price comparison (UK council fees of about 56 to 108 pounds, [Obitus pricing](https://newcastle-staffs.gov.uk/bereavement/obitus-prices/3)) and one hobbyist who said a local funeral home paid about $60 per slideshow and his own ads produced no sales ([DPReview thread](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/post/23340657)). I found no military-specific demand evidence.
- Saturation and demand: not verified. This is the weakest evidence in the list.
- Cost: $0. First dollar: unknown. Hours: 2 to 4 per video, which drifts into custom-work customer contact.
- Different: veteran-made, but that is positioning only.
- Risk: one-off custom jobs do not scale, and copyright on music is a problem. Likely overlaps Post-Production. Listing it only so the idea is not lost.

## Ideas considered and dropped

- VA claim help (lay statements, nexus guides): paid guides already exist, e.g. a $14.99 Payhip guide, and the free official form and law firm guidance cover most of it ([VA form](https://www.va.gov/forms/21-10210/), [Payhip](https://payhip.com/b/j3fst)). Also, charging for help preparing or presenting a VA claim is restricted to accredited people. I believe that is federal law (38 USC 5904) but I did not verify it here. Treat as a regulated area and stay out.
- Podcast show notes, chapters, clip tools: crowded with vendors (Podsqueeze, Descript, Podhome, others), no complaints found ([roundup](https://www.fast.io/resources/best-ai-tools-for-podcasting-2026.md)).
- AI audiobook narration service for indie authors: authors increasingly do it themselves with ElevenLabs, and sources say AI narration of fiction draws negative Audible reviews ([Vappingo](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/audiobook-creation-tools-kdp-authors/), [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/elevenlabs-review/)). Service gap unclear.
- Faceless AI video channel services around YouTube's inauthentic content rules: sources conflict on dates (July 2025 vs a July 2026 clarification) and enforcement, and there is no clear service gap yet ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering/), [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-ai-content-rules-monetization/)). Worth watching, not buildable now.
- Military spouse license transfer: federal law already passed in 2023, no 2026 gap found ([Texas Standard](https://texasstandard.org/stories/a-new-federal-law-lets-military-spouses-with-professional-licenses-transfer-them-across-state-lines/)).
- SDVOSB and VetCert help: certification moved to SBA, but I found only blog posts and no confirmed gap ([DVNF](https://www.disabledveterans.org/vetcert-is-fully-operational-in-2026/)). Also gets near regulated advice.
- General PCS planners: crowded (Gumroad and shop sellers, apps) and already covered by Kingdom Planners ([Gumroad example](https://chicbytestudio.gumroad.com/l/PCS-military-planner)).
- Kindle Scribe write-on books: already researched in TASKS.md, not repeated.

## Recommendation

Test idea 1 first, and idea 3 second.

- Idea 1 is the only one with a dated, official demand driver and a visible pricing gap between $149 to $269 packs and $5,000 plus bundles. It also leans on Zac's actual cyber work and spreadsheet skills, which his other products do not. The weekend test costs nothing. The two things to settle before building anything: whether Phase 2 is really paused (check the DoD CIO site directly), and whether his day job allows outside cyber products.
- Idea 3 costs nothing, uses 15 years of local knowledge nobody else has, and rides the Kingdom Planners PCS audience. Evidence of demand is thin, so the real test is a quick check of Etsy and Facebook groups before writing the guide.
- Hold idea 2 until Post-Production has a rate sheet. It is a service, not a product, and its differentiation is weak.
- Ideas 4 and 5 are low evidence. Park them.

Honest bottom line: this scan found one decent candidate and one maybe. The remaining three are thin. A second pass using Reddit and Etsy counts that Zac pulls by hand would firm this up.
