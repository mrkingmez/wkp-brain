# WWD monetization beyond AdSense: channel evidence

Date of research: 2026-11-05. Analyst: market-research. Web search and fetch only. VidIQ not used. Nothing signed up for, no data entered, nobody contacted.

Labels: OBSERVED = I fetched the page and read it. SECONDARY = from a web search summary or a vendor/blog page, not confirmed on a primary page. NOT AVAILABLE = blocked or not found.

## Headline findings

1. I could not find a single small (under 10k subs) movie or nerd YouTube channel that publicly states its affiliate or sponsor income. NOT AVAILABLE. Do not plan around an invented figure.
2. The only real, named, public earnings data I could retrieve for small film shows is Patreon page snapshots. They are tiny: roughly 7 to 37 dollars a month for shows with 5 to 31 paid members (details below). One YouTube horror channel shows about 102 paid members.
3. YouTube's own thresholds changed the math for WWD. At 500 subscribers plus 3 uploads in 90 days plus 3 million Shorts views in 90 days (or 3,000 long-form watch hours in 12 months), a channel can unlock memberships, Super Thanks and Shopping. WWD at about 744 subs with Shorts as 91% of views is a fit on the subscriber count. Whether it meets the Shorts view bar needs WWD's own YouTube Studio number. Source: YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en (OBSERVED).
4. Starting 2027-02-01, ad entry thresholds rise to 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views, but the fan funding thresholds do not change (SECONDARY, from a search summary of YouTube Help; I did not open the page that states it). Verify at https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851.

## Method by method

### Patreon / memberships (strongest real evidence)

Real public Patreon pages of small film shows, fetched 2026-11-05 (counts are a snapshot of when the page was last cached; date unknown):

- First Time on Set (movie podcast, "talk about whatever movies we want"): 6 paid members, 9 total, 10.15 dollars per month shown. https://www.patreon.com/FirstTimeonSet/about
- License to Watch (movie podcast, formerly Bondcast): 5 paid members, 15 total, 6.83 dollars per month shown. https://www.patreon.com/L2W/about
- Phantom Reviewer (movie reviews): 31 members, 36.72 dollars per month shown (page does not label it earnings), tiers from 4 dollars. https://www.patreon.com/phantomreviews
- Horrible Reviews (YouTube channel, horror, exploitation, cult films): 102 paid members of 316 total on the page I fetched (a search snippet said 105). The page shows "316 dollars per video review", which I read as a per-video pledge setting, not monthly income. Creator says YouTube does not monetize the videos "enough". Subscriber count not shown. https://www.patreon.com/HorribleReviews/about
- FanboyFlicks (comedic movie reviews, links a YouTube channel): 870 total members, paid count not shown, tier starting at 2 dollars. https://www.patreon.com/fanboyflicks
- MovieFusion: page showed no figures. NOT AVAILABLE.
- The Film Flamers (horror film podcast): offers early access and bonus episodes; no member count or price shown. https://www.patreon.com/TheFilmFlamers

Category averages: Rephonic, updated 2024-11-01, average total pledges per active podcast on Patreon: TV & Film 9 dollars, After Shows 23 dollars, Film Reviews 22 dollars, Film Interviews 6 dollars. Sample size not stated. The same article warned that a few big shows skew averages. https://rephonic.com/blog/patreon-and-podcasting

Platform scale: Patreon paid creators of podcasts over 629 million dollars in 2025 across over 47,000 earning podcasters (digitalmusicnews, 2026-04-08). My own division is about 13,400 dollars a year per earner, but that is a mean dominated by huge shows (the article names Joe Budden as earning up to 1 million dollars a month). Do not use it as a small-show estimate. Patreon takes 10% plus taxes per that article. https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/08/patreon-podcast-earnings-2025/

Same article: nearly half of earning podcasters enabled Patreon-only episodes on Spotify, and 15% of Spotify listeners who visit a creator's Patreon page become paid members (Patreon's own claim, promotional source).

One disclosed small podcast: "Build your SaaS" (tech audience, not film) lists 300 dollars per month from Patreon plus 600 dollars per month from sponsors, 900 dollars total, no date, no audience size. https://transistor.fm/faq/how-much-money-do-you-make-from-podcasts/

Fees for Ko-fi (0% on donations, 5% on sales) and Buy Me a Coffee (5%): SECONDARY from comparison blogs, one of which said it could not verify them against official pages. Check the platforms' own fee pages before relying on it.

So what for WWD (inference): a movie show at WWD's size should plan on tens of dollars a month from memberships at best. Horrible Reviews shows a channel can reach about 100 paid members, but I have no subscriber count for it, so I cannot say what size of audience that took.

### YouTube memberships, Super Thanks, Shopping

Unlock at 500 subs plus the 3-uploads and Shorts-views or watch-hours bar (table above). YouTube keeps 30% of membership revenue and the creator 70% (SECONDARY, air.io and vidiq blog summaries). No small movie channel found that publicly states membership income. NOT AVAILABLE.

### Affiliates

- Named channel with a disclosed figure: a TubeTalk (vidIQ) podcast episode dated 2026-03-13 featured "Gabe", who runs a sauna and cold plunge review channel that passed 1,000 subscribers after about two years. The host says it "made over 20,000 dollars", Gabe says 40,000 dollars over two years, so the numbers disagree. About 20,000 dollars of affiliate income tied to a sweat tent brand and about 200,000 dollars in sales. Channel name not given. This is a buyer-intent product niche, not entertainment, so it does not transfer to movie reviews. Gabe got his first affiliate deal by asking a brand for a product and an affiliate link. https://www.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18731724-how-a-1-000-subscriber-channel-earned-20-000-through-smart-affiliates-and-minimalist-content (OBSERVED as a podcast summary; I did not hear the audio.)
- Amazon Associates: no film channel disclosure found. See requirements table.
- Fandango, Bookshop.org: see table. No channel disclosures found.
- Gear, mic, camera, software and VPN affiliates: I did not find channel disclosures for any. NOT AVAILABLE.
- Inference: affiliate money in this niche is driven by buyer-intent content (best gear, what to buy), which is not what a review channel normally makes.

### Sponsors and sponsor marketplaces

- Per-video rates for small YouTube channels: OutlierKit (updated 2026-09-13) lists for 1,000 to 10,000 subscriber channels: integration 50 to 250 dollars, dedicated video 100 to 500 dollars, Short 25 to 150 dollars, monthly retainer 200 to 1,500 dollars. The page says plainly these are "planning estimates", not from a creator survey or private deal sheet. So it is a vendor's formula, not evidence of what channels are paid. https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/
- Other blogs (search summary only, SECONDARY) give similar bands, 50 to 300 dollars per video at 1,000 to 10,000 subs, and say sub-5,000 deals are often free product. I did not open those pages.
- One Reddit-mirror post (NewTubers, lawn care channel, about 2,000 subs) got a 500 dollars per month base plus commission offer from a CRM company. Different niche, one anecdote, mirror site, not opened by me. SECONDARY.
- Podcast CPMs (Acast, 2026-06-12): all podcast ads 15 to 40 dollars per thousand downloads, host-read 25 to 50, general interest 20 to 40, B2B and finance 50 to 100 plus. Worked example in the article: 10,000 downloads, 25 dollar CPM, three ad slots, 750 dollars per episode. At 500 downloads per episode the same math gives about 37.50 dollars per episode (my arithmetic, assumes 3 slots filled, which a small show will not get). https://www.acast.com/blog/how-many-listeners-do-you-need-to-monetize-a-podcast
- Marketplaces: Acast, Libsyn Ads, Podcorn, Gumball named in search results. Libsyn Ads host-read marketplace reportedly needs 20,000 downloads per episode (SECONDARY). Podcorn described as fitting 5,000 to 50,000 downloads (SECONDARY). No hard minimums confirmed from primary pages. Spotify Audience Network thresholds: NOT AVAILABLE.
- Sponsor deals for WWD by name: not in scope of this file. WWD's own Surfshark terms are in TASKS.md.

### Kickstarter and crowdfunding

- Stephen Follows analyzed 47,809 film campaigns from 2009 to 2015: average success 43%, short films 59%, goals under 10,000 dollars about 38%, under 50,000 dollars 18%, a video on the page raised success from 15% to 37%. Old data. https://stephenfollows.com/film-crowdfunding-kickstarter-statistics/ (SECONDARY via search summary; page not opened by me.)
- 2026 success rate figures conflict (95.2% vs 38%) and are unsourced. Do not use.

## What a 500 to 5,000 subscriber channel realistically earns: what I can and cannot say

Can say (retrieved):
- Small film shows on Patreon show 5 to 31 paid or total members and about 7 to 37 dollars a month in the pages above. One YouTube horror review channel shows about 102 paid members.
- Rephonic's 2024 average total Patreon pledges for Film Reviews podcasts is 22 dollars a month, for TV & Film 9 dollars.
- A vendor's planning estimate of 50 to 500 dollars per sponsored video for 1,000 to 10,000 sub channels, not backed by a survey.
- Podcast CPM benchmarks of 15 to 50 dollars per thousand downloads from Acast, with ad networks starting around 1,000 downloads per episode.

Cannot say (not available):
- Any real disclosed monthly income of a movie, TV commentary or nerd culture YouTube channel under 10,000 subs from affiliates, sponsors, merch or memberships.
- Any Super Thanks or YouTube membership payout figure for a small channel.
- Any Amazon, Bookshop.org or Fandango earnings per click for a film channel.
