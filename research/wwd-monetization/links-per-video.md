# Links per video: what comparable channels attach, and where

Prepared by market-research for marketing-director. Viewed 2026-11-05. Scope: how many sponsor, affiliate, product and support links comparable YouTube channels put on each long video and each Short, and where.

## Finding

In this sample, a long video carries at most ONE paid sponsor link, it sits on the first line of the description, and it is repeated in the pinned comment. Everything else is the creator's own links (support page, newsletter, merch, social). Small channels under 10k subscribers mostly carry no commercial link at all. No Short in the sample carried a third-party sponsor or affiliate link.

## Method and limits (read these first)

- Source: YouTube watch pages and channel tabs, opened read-only in Chrome (no login, no clicks on any link). Description text read from the page's own player data. Pinned comment read from YouTube's public comment feed for the same video (a pinned comment always sorts first).
- Counted only what appeared in the text. Subscriber counts are what YouTube displayed on 2026-11-05.
- NOT checked: spoken sponsor reads and on-screen cards (I did not watch or listen to any video), end screens, community posts. Whether any link earns money is not known. I have no click or revenue data for any channel.
- "Not available" below means the comment feed returned no thread (comments off, zero comments, or a feed I could not read). It does not mean there is no pinned comment.
- The Shorts tab on several channels returned old Shorts (dates shown). Those are flagged stale (older than about 90 days). Stale items are listed but not used in the norms.
- One count rule: "links" means distinct commercial or support URLs (sponsor, affiliate, merch, Patreon, membership, newsletter). Own social profiles are listed separately.

- Large channels, long videos in sample: 21. Videos carrying a paid sponsor link: 6 (Stuckmann 1, Jahns 1, Drinker 1, French 3). Each had exactly 1 sponsor link, always on line 1 of the description. Pinned comment repeated the sponsor in 5 of the 6 (not Jahns).

## So what for WWD (recommendation)

My inference from the sample, not a proven earning rule.

1. Per long video: 1 sponsor link (Surfshark), then at most 1 other commercial line (merch, or one product or affiliate), then support and social. The norm among sponsored videos is exactly 1 sponsor link and nothing else commercial competing with it. Do not stack a second sponsor or a second affiliate next to Surfshark.
2. Kinds that sit alongside a sponsor in this sample: the creator's own support page, newsletter or live-show link, merch, and (Stuckmann) one affiliate product link tied to the sponsor's product. Kinds that did NOT appear next to a VPN read: other affiliates.
3. Per Short: 0 third-party links. At most 1 own link, or none and a pointer sentence to the full video. Put the full-video link in the pinned comment.
4. Placement: sponsor first, line 1, before any synopsis. Chapters and the support and social block after. Disclosure line near the sponsor line or the last line.
5. Pinned comment: the sponsor line only, same wording as the description.
6. Small channels (WWD is at 744) mostly carry nothing. WWD already beats most of the small sample by having a sponsor. Adding more links is not what the sample supports; it supports one clean sponsor line, one support link, and a short social block.

Template layout (copy is the writing room's job; no copy here):

Description
- Line 1: sponsor line with link and code (one sentence, disclosure inside it or on the last line)
- Lines 2 to 4: video summary, 2 or 3 sentences
- Line 5: ONE own link (support page, merch, or the next episode)
- Chapters
- Social links block (YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, one line each)
- Last line: sponsorship disclosure ("FTC" style line, as Jaime French does)

Pinned comment
- The sponsor line and code only. Nothing else.

Short description
- One sentence pointing to the full video, plus the full-video link in the pinned comment. No sponsor, no affiliate.

## Confidence

- Counts and positions: high. I read the live text for every video listed.
