## 3. Do the vendors train on free users' chats, and where is it turned off

**Claude (fetched, Anthropic privacy pages).**
- Chats may be used to improve Claude only if the user allows it. The pages I read do not state the default. Third-party news from August 2025 says Anthropic changed to opt-out then, but I did not read that on an Anthropic page, so the default is unverified.
- Setting name on the page dated August 3, 2026 is "Help Improve our AI models." Path: click her name, then Settings, then Privacy, then toggle it off. Direct link claude.ai/settings/data-privacy-controls. Mobile path is the same. A March 16, 2026 sister page calls the area "Model Improvement" in Privacy Settings. Expect the label to differ slightly by date, so tell her to look in Settings, then Privacy.
- Turning it off stops new chats from being used for future training. Data already in a started training run or an already trained model cannot be pulled back. Chats flagged for safety review, and thumbs up or down feedback, can still be used. Incognito chats are not used even when the setting is on.
- Retention (five years with it on, 30 days with it off) appears only in search summaries of Anthropic's August 2025 announcement. Not read on a current page. Treat as unverified.

**ChatGPT (search summary of help.openai.com text, page itself returned 403).**
- Setting named "Improve the model for everyone," in Settings, then Data controls. Turning it off does not delete chat history. Alternative is "Do not train on my content" in OpenAI's Privacy Portal. Feedback she sends can still lead to that whole conversation being used. Temporary Chat is a separate feature.
- Default for free users is not stated in anything I read. Unverified.

**Primary URLs.**
- Fetched: https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/12109829-how-do-i-change-my-model-improvement-privacy-settings and https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023580-is-my-data-used-for-model-training

**Amazon KDP (fetched).**
- "AI-generated" means text, images or translations created by an AI tool, and it stays AI-generated even if she edits it heavily. She must tell KDP when publishing a new book or republishing an edited one. This covers cover and interior images and artwork.
- "AI-assisted" means she made the content and AI helped edit, refine, check or brainstorm. No disclosure needed.
- She must check all AI content against KDP content guidelines including intellectual property, regardless of disclosure.
- Page shows no update date, only a 1996 to 2026 copyright line. URL: https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390. Date retrieved 2026-11-13. Confidence high on content, no page date available.

## 9. Scams, government pages

**FTC, family emergency and voice cloning (fetched).**
- Alert dated March 20, 2023: scammers can clone a loved one's voice from a short audio clip, so do not trust the voice. Hang up and call the person on a number you know is theirs. If you cannot reach them, try another relative or a friend. Be suspicious of requests to wire money, send crypto or buy gift cards and read out the numbers.
- The FTC family emergency page (https://consumer.ftc.gov/all-scams/family-emergency-scams) links alerts dated 2023 to December 2025 and repeats "hang up and call back on a number you know" and slowing down. Report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
- Code word. A search summary of an FTC consumer alert says the agency suggests relatives agree on a code word or phrase and use it at the start of each conversation. I did not read that text on a fetched page. The FBI page below gives the code word advice directly, so use the FBI as the source for it.
- URL: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-enhance-their-family-emergency-schemes

**FTC, impostor scams (fetched).** Never send money, crypto or gold to someone who contacts you unexpectedly. Anyone telling you to move money to "protect" it is a scammer. Talk to someone you trust. Do not use links or numbers in the message, use ones you already know are real. The FTC never threatens you or tells you to transfer money or buy gold. Numbers on the page, citing an FTC alert dated November 12, 2026: more than 1 million impostor scam reports last year, with $3.5 billion in reported losses. The page itself has no publication date. URL: https://consumer.ftc.gov/features/how-avoid-imposter-scams

**FBI IC3, December 3, 2024, alert I-120324-PSA (fetched).** Advice includes "Create a secret word or phrase with your family to verify their identity." Also: look for flaws in images and video (odd hands, eyes, teeth, shadows, delayed movement), listen to tone and word choice on calls, limit photos and voice posted online and set social accounts to private, hang up and call an official number you look up yourself, do not share sensitive information with people met online or by phone, and do not send money, gift cards or crypto to people you know only online or by phone. URL: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203

**FBI IC3, July 20, 2026, alert I-072026-PSA (fetched).** Scammers pose as FBI staff and IC3 to people who already lost money, offer fund recovery, then steal more. Includes AI-made deepfake videos of senior FBI leaders pointing to a fake IC3 site. IC3 does not use social media, does not contact people by phone, email or chat, does not recover funds, and never asks for payment. Type www.ic3.gov yourself and confirm it ends in .gov. Victims aged 60 or over can call the DOJ Elder Justice Hotline at 1-833-FRAUD-11 (833-372-8311). No loss statistic on this page. A $893 million AI-scam-loss figure appeared only in secondary coverage, so do not use it. URL: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2026/PSA260720

**Date retrieved.** 2026-11-13

**Confidence.** High on the FBI and FTC items marked fetched. The FTC code word wording is search summary only.

## 11. Python and a code editor

**Python (fetched, python.org).**
- Downloads page lists Python 3.15.2, released Oct. 25, 2026. Windows: the "Python install manager" (python-manager-26.4.msix) or a standalone 64-bit installer (python-3.15.2-amd64.exe). macOS: installer package python-3.15.2-macos11.pkg.
- Python's own Windows docs: get the Python install manager from the Microsoft Store or python.org, open a new terminal and type `python`, which installs the latest stable runtime if none exists. Check with `py list` and `python -m this`. Adding to PATH is optional. If the Store opens or "command not found," enable the "Python (default)" entries under "Manage app execution aliases." Docs recommend a virtual environment per project.
- URLs: https://www.python.org/downloads/ and https://docs.python.org/3/using/windows.html (last 4,000 characters of the long Windows page not read).

**VS Code (fetched).**
- Download page: Windows 10 or 11 64-bit, offered as User Installer, System Installer, zip or CLI, x64 or Arm64. macOS 12 or later, Universal .dmg, or Intel or Apple Silicon .dmg. Page describes it as "Free, built on open source, and runs everywhere." URL: https://code.visualstudio.com/download
- Beginner installs three things (VS Code tutorial page): VS Code, the Python extension (which does not include Python), and a Python interpreter installed separately. On Windows confirm with `py -3 --version`. On macOS the system Python is not supported and the tutorial suggests installing Python with Homebrew (`brew install python3`). URL: https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/python/python-tutorial

**Date retrieved.** 2026-11-13. Pages show current versions only, no dated notices.

**Confidence.** High. Note the macOS Homebrew advice comes from the VS Code tutorial, while python.org offers a plain .pkg for Mac. Lesson writers should pick one and say which.
