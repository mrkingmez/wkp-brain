## Signals checklist
HARD to fake (weight these):
1. Third-party audit report with named auditor and report number (SGS, TUV, BV, Intertek), downloadable, matching the company name. Kerui shows "Verified by SGS Group"; I did not open the report, so scope and date are NOT VERIFIED.
2. Years on platform AND a transaction history with real order counts and amounts. Lanheng's profile transaction history field is empty.
3. Platform-measured stats, not seller-typed: on-time dispatch, average dispatch time, dispute rate, response time. These are computed by Alibaba. Cross-check them against what the listing says. Kerui lists "ships within 5 to 7 business days" while the platform stat says 29 days average dispatch.
4. Business registration matched to an outside registry (China's national enterprise credit system, GSXT, or a US importer record such as Panjiva or ImportYeti shipping history). NOT checked here, must be done by a person.
5. Physical assets: factory floorspace, staff and QC counts that fit the revenue. Lanheng claims $2.5M to $5M revenue and 51 to 100 staff against $10K to $20K measured online revenue.
6. Selling history of the same item across many buyers with consistent platform ratings over time. Ratings alone are weak.
EASY to fake (ignore):
- Star ratings and review text (user's own concern, agreed). Review counts can be bought, and a 1 year store with 839 ratings is a flag in itself.
- "certified", "Select", "Easy Return", "Guaranteed", "100%" and "High-Value" wording in titles.
- Self-filed Declarations of Conformity, EAA statements, CE or REACH labels.
- Struck-through "was" prices and "50% off ends Dec 31". Both Lanheng and Wujie show the same fake-sale pattern and identical end date.
- Company intro text, "established" year typed by the seller, "operations spanning over 50 countries".
- Revenue bands shown beside a seller's own profile claims.

## Expected value (honest, partly estimated)
Electronics box, $29.50, free shipping on that listing (the page showed shipping $8.73 struck through to FREE).
- Retrieved: price $29.50, 5 items, pool includes cables, razors, flashlights, fans.
- My estimate, NOT retrieved: wholesale cost of such items is roughly $1 to $6 each, so a typical box is worth $8 to $20 at cost, well below $29.50 and below the "reference value above price" claim. A single smartwatch in a $29.50 box is a $5 to $12 class watch. Treat this as an assumption, not data. No unboxing evidence was retrieved.
- Duty and fees: NOT RETRIEVED for these goods. What I did retrieve (search summary, not primary): the duty-free small package exemption for China and Hong Kong ended May 2, 2025 and a FedEx notice says de minimis treatment is no longer available for any country; a 10% reciprocal tariff was held until a suspension date of Nov 10, 2026, and the fentanyl-related tariff was cut from 20% to 10% on Nov 10, 2025. Whether that was extended past Nov 10, 2026 and what Section 301 adds for electronics: NOT RETRIEVED. The earlier report also cites CRS IF12891 on de minimis suspension. Plan on 10% to 40% of declared value plus possible courier brokerage fee, as a range I could not pin down.
- Lithium batteries: smartwatches, earbuds and power banks raise shipping restrictions and fire risk, no safety markings verifiable.
- Net: expect to pay about $30 to $45 landed for roughly $8 to $20 of goods, no returns that cost less than the item. Negative expected value, around minus $15 to minus $30 per box (my estimate).
- Clothing lot (Kerui): MOQ 20 at about $5.58 to $7.58 is $112 to $152 before freight and duty, plus a $25 sample. Retail value could exceed that for resale, but it requires selling 20 baby garments and tolerating a 29 day average dispatch. Not a consumer test.
- Plush blind boxes: MOQ 8 at $5.74 to $7.06 is about $46 to $56. Knockoff-character risk and customs seizure risk not checked.

## Protection terms (secondary, not primary)
Alibaba's pages describe Trade Assurance and money-back protection for missing, defective or wrong items, refunds generally within 7 business days after approval, a coverage cap per supplier (one page cites $2,000 post-delivery), and discretionary outcomes. A mystery box is a "random contents" contract, so "wrong item" is hard to prove. I did not retrieve an official exclusion list, nor whether mystery boxes are excluded. A claim window of 30 days from delivery (60 for some buyers) came from a third-party site and is unconfirmed.

## Rules if Zac tests anyway (a $10 to $30 loss budget, no more)
1. Only a listing with: 5+ years, a named third-party audit report, platform dispatch stat that matches the page, dispute rate under 3%, and a stated item list (not "random"). By that bar only Kerui passes, and its MOQ is 20.
2. Order the sample, not the lot. Kerui offered a sample at $25. That is the only low-risk test seen, and it tests a seller, not a mystery.
3. Pay only inside Alibaba checkout (never wire, never off-platform chat). Photograph the sealed parcel and the unboxing. File any dispute within the claim window.
4. No batteries, no "high-value" promises, no struck-through 50% off anchors, no seller under 3 years.
5. Treat any landed cost as 1.4 times the price to be safe.

- Current US tariff rate and any Section 301 add-on for these goods in Nov 2026; brokerage fees.

- Real unboxing results or buyer outcomes from outside Alibaba (Reddit, YouTube, Trustpilot).
- Contents beyond the "possible inclusions" pools.
- Stated return terms beyond the "Easy Return" and money-back labels.
- Tools, collectibles, toys beyond the single plush listing, general clothing beyond Kerui.
