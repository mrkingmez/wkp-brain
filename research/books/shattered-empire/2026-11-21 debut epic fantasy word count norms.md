# Debut epic fantasy word count norms
Date 2026-11-21. Requested by fiction-director. Book: Shattered Empire Book 1, about 235,000 words, debut adult epic fantasy. Several sources below are older than 2 years and are flagged possibly stale.

## 1. Answer in plain words
Agents who state a number put the safe range for a debut fantasy at about 80,000 to 120,000 words. Epic fantasy gets more room, roughly 120,000 to 150,000, and the longest debut one named agency says it signed was about 160,000. No source I retrieved gives a hard published cap, and none says 235,000 is acceptable for a debut. It is well above every stated norm. A few well known debuts did sell near or above 200,000 words, but the counts are fan or author estimates and they are all from 2005 to 2011.

## 2. Word counts agents and publishers state (debut epic fantasy)
- Mushens Entertainment (UK agency blog, "Word count: How long should my novel be?", posted "Jun 21", year not shown on the page, so date UNVERIFIED). Says epic fantasy can range from 120,000 to 150,000 "or even longer". Longest debut they signed in epic fantasy was "around 160,000 words". https://www.mushens-entertainment.com/blog/how-to-edit-word-count
- Nathan Bransford (former agent, blog post dated April 23, 2018, possibly stale). Fantasy 80,000 to 120,000, science fiction 90,000 to 120,000. Calls 120,000 "kind of the upper limit for the genres that allow some leeway", and says going far over may decrease odds "especially for a debut". https://nathanbransford.com/blog/2018/04/all-about-novel-word-counts
- Janet Reid (agent) reportedly suggests 150,000 as a minimum for sweeping epic fantasy. This came only through a search summary of an agent blog, I did not open the original. NOT VERIFIED.
- Colleen Lindsay (former agent) suggests a cautious 120,000, saying most big fantasy on shelves is 100,000 to 120,000. Search summary only, original not opened, year of post unknown. NOT VERIFIED.
- Jessica Faust, BookEnds (agency blog "Fantasy Word Count"). The search summary says she called 100,000 to 120,000 "pretty non-epic" and preferred 200,000 plus. I opened the page and it did not show that text or a date. NOT VERIFIED. https://bookendsliterary.com/?p=801
- Anecdote, forum only: a writer querying a 225,000 word epic said one agency called the count too high to consider. Forum post, treat as unverified.
- Publisher side: I found no official maximum on any publisher submission page. Angry Robot minimum 70,000 words is from a third party list (Jones Novel Editing, July 2026), not confirmed on Angry Robot's own page. Orbit was listed as agented only in an undated third party listing. Tor Publishing: nothing found. Publisher maximums: NOT VERIFIED.
- Result: the named sources I could confirm directly are Mushens and Bransford. Two confirmed, the rest unverified. The ask was 3 to 6 sources, so the count is short of 3 confirmed. Zac or the director should check the live submission pages of the specific agents on the query list, since each agency sets its own limit.

## 3. Debut epic fantasies at or near 200,000 words
Counts come from one 2014 blog list (E. L. Wagner, "Word Counts and Debut Fantasy Novels", June 8, 2014, possibly stale). Counts there come from author sites, personal communication or an editor's estimating method, not per title. Treat as approximate. https://doggedlywriting.blogspot.com/2014/06/word-counts-and-debut-fantasy-novels.html
- Patrick Rothfuss, The Name of the Wind (2007): list says 259,000. Rothfuss himself wrote in a November 4, 2011 blog post "Take my first book for example. It was over 250,000 words." That is the strongest figure, author stated. https://blog.patrickrothfuss.com/?p=3437 (Another site estimates about 165,500 from page count, an unreliable method, ignore it.)
- Brandon Sanderson, Elantris (2005): list says 199,000.
- Joe Abercrombie, The Blade Itself (2007): list says 192,000.
- Scott Lynch, The Lies of Locke Lamora (2007): list says 190,000.
- Same list shows most debuts lower, for scale: Brent Weeks 156,000, Peter Brett 158,000, N. K. Jemisin 111,000, Mark Lawrence 90,000.
- Caveats. All are 2005 to 2008 releases. Rothfuss's book was reported by him as long, but I did not verify whether it was trimmed before sale. Nothing retrieved covers debuts from 2020 onward. Recent debut over 200,000: NOT VERIFIED, none found.

## 4. Self published (KDP) long books
- KDP's help pages list a black and white paperback maximum of 828 pages on white paper (776 on cream, 812 groundwood), exact limit depends on trim size. Source: KDP trim size help, https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201857950 (figure taken from a search excerpt of that page, not read in full). A 235,000 word book at a common trim would fall well under 828 pages, so the cap is not the blocker.
- A third party guide claims two 400 page volumes cost less per unit to print than one 800 page volume. General claim, no KDP figure. NOT VERIFIED as a KDP fact. Check KDP's print cost calculator for real numbers.
- Nothing retrieved says how KDP epic fantasy authors usually handle length. Splitting practice: NOT VERIFIED.

## 5. What authors usually get wrong
- Treating 100,000 to 120,000 as a hard rule. Most of it is soft guidance, but agents do say debuts over the genre norm are a harder sell.
- Citing Rothfuss or Sanderson as proof that 235,000 will sell. Those sales are 15 to 20 years old and the authors had the books trimmed or were unknown in a different market.

## 6. Open questions for the author
- Is a split into two books an option for the query version? Sources here only show that length is a risk, they do not say splitting works.
- Which specific agents are on the query list? Their own pages are the only reliable word limit.
