# Audit of every logged prompt against the makers' own advice (2026-11-22)

Author: graphic-design. Method: I read every prompt text in `D:\WKP\data\firefly-prompt-log\` (13 garage prompts, 4 joust prompts that have clips, the first joust prompt that is on file only as a file name, 6 later joust prompts with no clips, 4 Ian still prompts that were never run). Then I ran the mechanical lint `prompt_lint.py` over all of them and read each one against the maker quotes in `SOURCES-2026-11-22.md` (ids G1 to G5, A1 to A3, K1 to K3, R1 to R2, L1 to L2, B1, F1 to F4).

Zac was right. The prompts were beginner level. Not one of the 24 video prompts passes the lint (the four Ian still prompts have no hard fail). The lint, the quotes and the clips all agree, and the plain finding is below.

## What the makers say that we broke (the short version)

1. **They all say describe the picture you want, not the one you do not.** Google: do not use "no" or "don't", "Describe what you don't want to see" (G1). Runway: "Describe what you want to see rather than what you don't want" (R2). Kling 4.0: "minimize abstract terms and negative instructions", only "no subtitles and no background music" are needed (K1). Luma: "Negative prompting is counterproductive" (L1). Seedance 2.5: "Use positive descriptions whenever possible", negatives only for subtitles and audio (B1). We wrote negations in all 24 video prompts, up to 12 in one prompt (joust 4), and shouted them in capitals in two (garage 7 and 8, joust runs 5 to 7).
2. **They say do not contradict yourself.** Runway: "Make sure your prompt elements don't contradict each other" (R2). Seedance: "Make sure the prompt does not contain contradictions or unreasonable camera movement" (B1). We asked for "NOTHING IN HIS HAND" and then "turns a wrench" and "holds it out" in the same prompt (garage 7, 8). We asked for a first-person view from the knight and put the knight "on right side of frame" (joust runs 2 to 6), which invites a second knight. We asked for a locked camera and a man who rolls out and sits up (garage 2, 3).
3. **They say once there is a start frame, the prompt drives the motion only.** Runway: image-to-video prompts "focus almost exclusively on motion" (R2). Flux: "your image sets the opening frame and the prompt drives the motion" (F3). Kling: "Use @Image1 as the first frame, then describe what happens next" (K1). We re-described the whole scene, the car direction and the shield position in prompts that also had a start frame (garage 4, 5, 11; joust runs 1 to 6). When the words and the picture differ, the model has two scenes.
4. **They say specificity beats adjectives.** Google: "Specificity helps avoid generic outputs" (G1). Kling: "Describe visual details as explicitly as possible" (K1). Runway: "Instead of abstract concepts, use clear, observable descriptions" (R2). We wrote "a long wooden lance" and "three-pronged coronal tip" (a rare word) and got spears. We wrote "a steel wrench" and got a socket wrench. We wrote "as if he heard something" (a mental state) and "quiet, abandoned mood".
5. **They give a structure and we did not follow it.** Google: [Cinematography] + [Subject] + [Action] + [Context] + [Style and ambiance] (G2). Adobe: "Shot type description + character + action + location + aesthetic" (A1). Garage 13 has no camera sentence at all. The joust prompts mix camera, subject and action in one run-on sentence, and runs 3 to 6 are garbled ("on right side of frame NO ONE else, the camera level", "stircking", "the camera Paint flakes").
6. **They say state the audio, in its own sentences, or leave it off.** Google: "Clearly specify if you want audio" in "separate sentences" with `SFX:` and `Ambient noise:` (G1, G2). Our joust prompts ended "No music, no dialogue", a negation about a track that, by my ffprobe check today, none of the 18 clips has (0 audio streams in all 18). So those words did nothing, and the sting's crack sound was never requested.
7. **They give two tools for geometry and contact that we never used.** First and last frame: Veo 3.1 and Veo 3.1 Fast support it (G3, G4), Luma Ray3.14 (L1, L2), Kling 4.0 (K1), Seedance 2.5 (B1), Flux 3 (F3). Reference images: Veo up to three asset images, 8 s only, Lite not supported (G3, G4). We used neither for the ball and boot (garage 12), the lance and the shield, or the wrench. For contact the makers' answer is give the model both ends: "the frame the shot should open on and the frame it should land on" (F3), "describe the transition and the audio" (G2).
8. **They say beats must be achievable.** BFL: "two or three for a 5-second clip" (F4). Seedance: "If too much content is packed into a given time range, the result may contain excessive cuts or omit parts of the plot" (B1). Runway: "consider how long an action would reasonably take" (R1). Garage 2 and 3 stack six actions in 8 s. Garage 6 to 10 stack a reach, a handoff and a return.
9. **Several of our own words caused the failures Zac blamed on the model** (new finding, I did not see this in the first LESSONS pass): joust 2 and 3 asked for a "slow-motion beat" and "creeping" dolly, and Zac's complaint was a slow strike. Joust 5 and run 1 wrote that the lance tip "settles dead center on the emblem and then strikes", which describes a pause. Garage 12 wrote the ball "gently bumps against his boot", and the clip bounced off the toe. Garage 6 to 8 wrote "as if offering it to someone shorter beside him", and a second person with a second wrench appeared.
