# Devil's Advocate Ruling 2: WWD Weekly Comic (serialized, not promo)
Date: 2026-10-30. Requested by Zac via Jarvis. New-idea gate, WWD off-brand check.
Supersedes the first ruling (Comic-Series-Devils-Advocate.md), which judged a FrostCast promo post. Zac corrected the brief: this is a real weekly comic with its own ongoing story, about 3 full pages a week, Instagram and Facebook, starring KingZ, Winter Wolf and Oracle.

## VERDICT: GO WITH CHANGES
Nothing goes public until 4 finished issues sit in a buffer. That buffer is the test. If the team cannot finish 4 issues in private, it would not have kept up 3 pages a week in public, and nobody watched it fail.

## (1) Off brand?
On brand in identity, a stretch on what the channel promises. The hosts as characters, the Den, the red and black look and "One pack. One Den." are WWD's and nobody else can copy them. But WWD is two friends talking about the nerd-verse. An original serialized story is fiction, which is a new content line, not a new post format.
It stays on brand if:
- The characters act like the hosts do on air. Winter Wolf calls it first and then defends it. KingZ pulls people in. If the comic versions could be any three heroes, it is off brand.
- The story lives in the nerd-verse and keeps the show's humor. All noir with no jokes would make the comic darker than the show people find when they click through.
- Oracle is original. Do not use bat symbols, Gotham, a clock tower or Barbara Gordon's look. "Oracle" as a hacker codename is generic enough, but nothing else may point to DC.
- No other companies' characters or logos, same as ruling 1.

## (2) Still a good idea? The case against
- **THE ONE THING: a serial dies on a missed week, and this one runs on fragment time.** A promo post that skips a week loses nothing. A story that skips a week loses its readers. 3 pages a week comes to about 150 pages a year of sequential art, each word through the writing room, marketing and no-ai-slop, while Kingdom Planners is the main effort. ME.md names this exact pull, "cool factor" ideas instead of finishing what is in front of him.
- **Sequential AI art is the hardest job you can give Firefly.** A promo page needs the hosts to look right once. A story needs the same faces, outfits, props and room from panel to panel, plus real action continuity (who is holding what, where they stand). Firefly drifts and mangles lettering. Expect lettering to be done by hand in Express on every page.
- **Nobody reads a serial in order at 68 followers.** The feed shows part 6 to a stranger with no part 1. Instagram has no reading order. Each installment has to work on its own or it means nothing to a new viewer.
- **The data does not point here.** Our carousels got 2 and 3 likes. The best Reel got 17. Marketing found zero documented cases of a podcast or review channel growing from a recurring comic. This is a brand bet, not a growth lever.
- **It needs three yeses.** Matt (his likeness, his Firefly credits, joint branding) and Gabby (Oracle). One no and it stops.

## The honest case for
- It is the one thing WWD can make that no other review channel can, an IP built on the hosts themselves.
- Webcomics are a real format with real serial audiences (Webtoon, Tapas), so a working run has somewhere to go later.
- Zac is a fiction writer with a finished novel. A story is in his wheelhouse, and the writing room carries the words.
- Carousels lead on engagement where they get seen, and a pan Reel cut from the same panels covers reach at almost no extra cost.
- It is $0 on tools already paid for.

## Conditions for GO
1. **Matt and Gabby say yes first.** No art until they do. graphic-design builds a locked character sheet (KingZ, Winter Wolf, Oracle, the Den) and Matt signs off on the looks.
2. **Pilot one issue and time it.** Log Zac's own hours. Cap is 2 hours of Zac's time per weekly issue. Over the cap, cut to 2 pages or every other week before launch, not after.
3. **Buffer of 4 finished issues before week 1 posts.** If the buffer drops to 1, finish the current arc and pause at an arc break. Never stop mid-arc.
4. **Pages built for a phone.** 3 pages, 4 to 5 panels max per page, 4:5 (1080 x 1350), large hand-set lettering. Post as one carousel: a cover slide with a one-line "previously," 3 page slides, an end slide with the next drop day. Same carousel on Facebook. Cut a 15 to 20 second pan Reel of the best panels each week.
5. **Story built in 4-week arcs, and each issue lands a beat on its own** (a joke, a reveal or a cliffhanger). If it is killed at week 4 or 8, it ends clean. Pin issue 1 or use a "Start Here" highlight.
6. **One fixed day a week, not a review-drop day.** Zac and Matt pick it.
7. **Gates every issue:** script-room writes every word (two passes for the arc script), marketing-director, no-ai-slop, wwd-rights. Caption carries the AI disclosure.
8. **Tracking-director logs per issue:** likes, comments, shares if visible, follows that week, Zac's hours, buffer count. Pull a follower baseline before launch.

## What success looks like
- **Week 4 (arc 1 done):** buffer never fell below 2, Zac's time held under 2 hours, comic posts average 5 likes or more (double the account's 2.3), and at least one comment from someone outside the friend group mentions the story.
- **Week 8 (arc 2 done):** average 10 likes or more, or at least one issue beats the best Reel (17), net follower gain beats the 8 weeks before launch, and the pan Reels are not below the account's median Reel.

## Kill criteria (stop at the next arc break)
- The 4-issue buffer is not done within 6 weeks of starting. Do not launch.
- In the pilot, a reader cannot tell the three characters apart without the captions.
- Zac's time goes over 2 hours in 2 weeks running.
- Week 8 average under 5 likes with no story comments.
- Matt or Gabby wants out.
- It takes time away from a Kingdom Planners milestone. Venture priority is Zac's call, so flag it to him instead of letting the comic quietly win.

## What is working (keep it)
The hosts as characters, Oracle as the team's eyes and ears (this gives the three a real role split), the red and black Den look, and Zac's instinct to make something WWD owns outright. The idea is sound. The cadence is the risk, and the buffer is what handles it.
