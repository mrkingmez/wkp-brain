## 1. Three series concepts

### Concept A: THE DEN AFTER DARK (story world)

**Premise:** The Den is a hideout above a rain-soaked city, and every week something arrives at the door that the three hosts have to deal with before the show goes live.

**The engine:** A recurring menace (drafted as "The Leak", see review in Section 6, needs rebuilding) who carries sealed envelopes. Each envelope holds an ending. The Den's whole job is to keep the story alive until the audience gets to see it for themselves. Some weeks it is the menace. Some weeks it is a smaller trouble (the power dies before show night, a mic goes missing, the ritual circle won't light, KingZ's new Den Trials machine catches fire).

**A week's 3 pages:**
- **Monday (story page):** The week's trouble shows up, tied to the biggest topic or the week's review. Ends on a question.
- **Wednesday (show-night page):** The EP094 layout. Header, opener rows, numbered hunt list, Den Trials, PLUS, FROSTCAST logo, live tonight, footer. The story beat shows up in one corner (a sealed envelope on the table, a shadow at the window), so story readers get a crumb and new readers get a clean promo.
- **Weekend (story page):** Aftermath. A gag from the episode's topics, no verdicts, plus one closing panel that sets up next week. Points to the episode for the actual takes.

**Tie to the show:** The trouble always comes from that week's slate. A finale week brings an envelope. A sequels week brings something that "comes back." A review week puts that movie's title on the envelope as plain text (clearance flags this, see Section 6).

### Concept B: DEN TRIALS (game show strip)

**Premise:** Every week KingZ builds Winter Wolf a new game out of junk, the audience plays along in the comments, and the result is only revealed live on FrostCast.

**A week's 3 pages:**
- **Monday:** LEVEL 1. KingZ unveils this week's machine (a cardboard arcade cabinet, a wheel built from a hubcap, a ballot box made of VHS cases). States the rules. Asks the chat to predict whether Wolf survives.
- **Wednesday:** LEVEL 2, show-night layout. Hunt list plus "the final level is played LIVE tonight."
- **Weekend:** GAME OVER screen. Who won is told only as score-card style ("WOLF: SURVIVED LEVEL 3" or "KINGZ: OUT OF CONTINUES"), never what anyone said about a movie. Leaderboard shoutout to commenters who called it.

**Tie to the show:** Every game is built on that week's topics, and the final level is a real live segment on FrostCast. This needs the hosts to actually play it on air, so it changes the show, not just the posts.

### Concept C: CASE FILES FROM THE DEN (pulp covers plus a letters page)

**Premise:** WWD publishes its own fake pulp comic line. Each week gets three single-image "issue covers," each a bold splash for one topic, with a cover line that asks a question.

**A week's 3 pages:**
- **Monday:** Cover for the week's review ("ISSUE 119-A"), hosts in a staged pulp pose around a prop tied to the movie, title as plain text only.
- **Wednesday:** Cover for FrostCast ("ISSUE 119"), the hunt list running down the left edge like cover blurbs, LIVE TONIGHT starburst.
- **Weekend:** THE LETTERS PAGE. Real chat comments from the week (permission needed for each) answered in character by the hosts, typewriter style, one panel of the hosts reading mail at the Den table.

**Tie to the show:** The letters page is built from the week's chat, so it rewards people who showed up live.

## 2. Pick and why

**Pick: Concept A, THE DEN AFTER DARK, with Den Trials (Concept B) folded in as its recurring Wednesday bit.**

- It is the only one that gives people a reason to come back for the next page that is not the show itself. A promo page gets scrolled past. A cliffhanger gets checked on.
- The menace is the channel's rule made into a plot. The Den's job in the story is to protect the ending, which is what the hosts do on air. Nobody else's comic has that hook because it comes from WWD's own house rule. (It must be carried by a rebuilt villain, not The Leak as drafted.)
- The Wednesday page keeps the EP094 template Zac already built, so the hard part of the format is proven.
- It gives Oracle a place to exist without a face.

**Weakest point of each:**
- **A:** Continuity load. Someone tracks the plot, keeps three characters looking the same on every page, and stops the story from eating the promo. Mid-arc readers will not know what the envelope is. Fix: every story page works on its own, one plot thread at a time, plot never takes more than one corner of the Wednesday page. Second weakness, from the review: the villain has to be rebuilt.
- **B:** Burns out. A new game every week is an idea tax, and the pages are only as good as the game. It also asks the hosts to change the live show, which needs Matt.
- **C:** No reason to come back. Each cover is a one-off. A pulp cover built around a movie prop is the format most likely to drift toward drawing the movie's actual characters or key art.

## 4. Short style bible

### The hosts on the page

**Winter Wolf**
- Look: wolf, black hooded jacket, WINTER WOLF across the back, fur collar, blue eyes. The only cold blue in the frame besides Oracle's glow.
- Body: still. Arms crossed, hand flat on things, leaning back. Moves last and least.
- Talks: short, two to six words is home. Blunt questions. Never jokes on purpose, gets laughs from being dry.
- Page rule: verdict-first on the show, but on the page his sharpness is impatience and attitude, never a take on a movie. He never says what he thinks of anything on the slate.

**KingZ**
- Look: masked, crown cap, red eyes, KINGZ hoodie with dripping red lettering. Always holding something (mug, game, DVD case).
- Body: open. Arms out, leaning toward the reader, pointing.
- Talks: warm, chatty, uses "we" and "Pack," talks to the reader directly. Longer bubbles than Wolf, one thought per bubble. Gets things slightly wrong and keeps going.
- Page rule: KingZ frames the question and hands it to the audience. He never answers it.

**Oracle (PROPOSAL, needs Zac, Matt and Oracle's own say)**
- Look: no body, no face. Oracle is the ritual circle on the Den table. When Oracle speaks the carved lines glow.
- Balloons: round, no tail, a thin clean serif, pale cold-white text on deep violet.
- Talks: rarely. One or two lines a page, max. Answers sideways. Usually the shortest line on the page.
- Never: Oracle's real name, a face, or a silhouette that suggests a real person.

**The menace (recurring villain, REBUILD REQUIRED)**
- Drafted as "The Leak": long coat, hood, cracked phone screen for a face. The screen face and the "spoils endings" motive both fail originality (Section 6). Keep: never speaks, never wins, no envelope ever opens on the page, cold white ragged-edge captions, the stack of sealed ENDING ENCLOSED envelopes. New head, new motive, new name check before the look locks.

### Recurring bits
- **Den Trials** (Wednesday page): KingZ's game for Wolf, tied to a topic. Screen reads DEN TRIALS / LEVEL # / PRESS START. The page only sets up the game. The result happens live.
- **The sealed envelope:** the week's ending lives in it. It stays sealed. Always.
- **The corner crumb:** one small wordless story panel on the Wednesday page.
- **"It's in the episode. Not here.":** the weekend page always sends people to the show for the takes.

### Lettering rules
- Bubbles: 12 words or fewer is the target, 20 is the hard cap.
- Story pages: 75 words max per page, all text combined.
- Show-night page: topic captions are always a question or a stake, one or two sentences.
- No em dashes. Use a period or a comma.
- Caps for title boxes, header boxes and the footer. Sentence case inside bubbles.
- Real show titles and service names: plain lettering in caption or title boxes only. Never in a logo style, never imitating a studio or streamer's type.

### Color rules
- Base palette: black and blood red, from the EP094 page.
- Accents belong to characters: cold blue is Wolf's eyes, red glow is KingZ, deep violet and cold white is Oracle, cold white is the menace.
- Torch amber is the Den's warmth, inside the Den only. The city outside stays red, black and rain-gray.

### What never appears
- Any other company's characters drawn, posed or silhouetted recognizably.
- Any other company's logo, title treatment or key art (check generated art for Marvel, Disney+, Netflix marks).
- Oracle's real name.
- Em dashes.
- A host's verdict, rating or opinion on any movie or show on the slate.
- Quotes from any film or show. Song lyrics of any length.
- Real newspaper mastheads. A recognizable handheld console shape.
- A faceless screen-headed figure (reads as an existing character).

### Page spec
- Instagram: 4:5, 1080 x 1350 px. Post the week's three pages as separate posts on their days, not one carousel.
- Facebook: same file, same days.
- Safe zone: all text at least 60 px from every edge.
- Optional later: a 9:16 version of the Wednesday page for Stories.

### Caption copy rules
- Short: the hook plus one question for the comments. No verdicts.
- Every caption goes through marketing-director (copywriting, social-media) and no-ai-slop before Zac posts. Agents draft, Zac posts.
- No hashtag walls. Use the channel's existing set.

### Posting rhythm
- Monday: story page (the trouble arrives).
- Wednesday, show day: show-night page, earlier in the day.
- Saturday: story page (aftermath, cliffhanger, send people to the episode).
- Times follow the existing stagger convention. Final times are marketing's call.

## 6. Room review, pass 1 of 2

Pass 1 ran: room-writer (draft), originality, clearance. dialogue-doctor not run (concept pitch, sample lines only) and goes on pass 2. The two reviewers do not conflict. Both point at the villain and at the third-party title on the envelope.

### Originality: ships with changes
- The villain concept fails. A villain whose job is telling people how stories end is the Spoiler from Henry Danger and Spoiler Boy from The Simpsons (S31E14). A movie audience is the most likely to name them.
- The look fails. Faceless, long coat, screen head reads as TV Man from Skibidi Toilet, with Rorschach and The Question in the silhouette and the Black Mirror cracked screen in the glass.
- Keep: the sealed black wax-sealed envelope stamped ENDING ENCLOSED, caged in the ritual circle, and the rule that the villain never speaks and never wins.
- Shape of the fix: change what the villain wants so "spoils movies" is not his one-line description. Change the head so it is not a screen. Rebuild him around the envelope. Re-check name and look on pass 2.
- Worn lines, not findings: "He's back.", "Next week, he tries again.", the "no or not yet / Yes" gag.
- Concepts B and C borrow formats, not works. They pass.

### Clearance: flags only, nothing cleared
- No verbatim dialogue, lyrics or poems. Oracle's real name appears nowhere.
- Vision Quest stamped on the envelope turns another company's title into a weekly story prop. Topic-name use on the Wednesday page is low exposure.
- Page 2 Topic 1 TV panel is the most likely to pick up Marvel or Disney+ imagery. Any logo, title style or likeness in generated art is a STOP.
- "Netflix's list" must read as WWD's own list. Keep Netflix red, N mark and typeface out of the art.
- Matt and Oracle are real people given invented lines. Both must sign off.
- Ownership of the new recurring characters is not written down. AI-generated art protection is unsettled. Needs a partner agreement or an attorney.
- Concept C letters page: a visible handle is not permission to reprint.
- Fact check before lettering "The finale drops Tuesday": TASKS.md conflicts on whether eps 7 and 8 both drop 11/3 or the finale is 11/10. Send to fact-research.
