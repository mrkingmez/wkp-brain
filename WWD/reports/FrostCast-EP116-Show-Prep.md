## Topic 2: Netflix Narnia reaction (It's the Magician's Nephew?)

**What happened:** Yes, it is The Magician's Nephew. The first trailer for Greta Gerwig's Narnia: The Magician's Nephew dropped Oct 13, and fans are split. Some call it the best trailer in years and a faithful adaptation with a rock-opera twist. Others say it looks like Marvel-style CGI and "does not feel like Narnia." The trailer has no Aslan voice, only a roar.

**Why it matters:** The Magician's Nephew is the origin story of the whole series (how Narnia gets made), so it is a bigger swing than a straight remake of the first film. It is also the first Narnia film in years that is a theatrical event first and a streaming release second.

**Numbers:** Sneak previews in IMAX Feb 10, 2027. Wide theatrical release Feb 12, 2027. On Netflix Apr 2, 2027. Wikipedia lists the budget at $200 million, but I found no second source, so do not state it as fact. The shoot ran about six months in London across Shepperton, Longcross, and Cardington.

**History:** The earlier Narnia films ran 2005 to 2010 (Lion, Witch and Wardrobe; Prince Caspian; Voyage of the Dawn Treader), then the franchise stalled. Netflix bought the rights. Magician's Nephew is the sixth book by publication order and first by story chronology.

**Colour:** Cast includes Emma Mackey as the White Witch, Daniel Craig as Uncle Andrew, Carey Mulligan, and newcomers David McKenna and Beatrice Campbell in the leads. Meryl Streep is reported as the voice of Aslan (one report says "in talks," another says set, so say "reported"). Gerwig reportedly shot select scenes in IMAX, though that is disputed.

**Quick hits:**
- First trailer Oct 13, fans split: faithful and rock-opera vs "Marvel CGI"
- IMAX Feb 12, 2027, Netflix Apr 2, 2027
- Mackey (White Witch), Craig (Uncle Andrew), Mulligan, Streep reportedly Aslan
- Trailer has no Aslan voice, just a roar
- $200M budget: one source only, do not state as fact

**Why it matters:** The opening is soft next to Supergirl, but the budget is small, so "not encouraging" depends on what you measure it against.

**Numbers:** Tracking: $23M to $33M opening weekend (ComicBookMovie report). Budget about $40 million. One estimate puts break-even near $100 million worldwide, which is an estimate, not a studio number. Supergirl opened to $37.1 million. Release date is reported as Oct 23, 2026, though earlier coverage said Sept 11, so say Oct 23 only after a quick check of a release calendar. Rated R for bloody violence, gore, and nudity.

**History:** Directed by James Watkins from a screenplay by Mike Flanagan and Hossein Amini. Tom Rhys Harries plays Matt Hagen. Naomi Ackie, David Dencik, Max Minghella, and Eddie Marsan also star.

**Colour:** A Greenlight Analytics survey had awareness at 32 percent, below the 38 percent average for four comparison films. Among people who know the film, 21 percent plan to see it opening weekend versus 11 percent for the comparison films, so interest is strong where it has reach. Street Fighter is the competition and Clayface may not open at number one.

**Quick hits:**
- Tracking $23M to $33M opening, budget about $40M
- Supergirl opened $37.1M, Clayface tracking below it
- Awareness low (32 vs 38 average), intent high among the aware (21 vs 11)

## Topic 9: Finales that worked in Fantasy

**What happened:** Open discussion prompt, no news peg. These are the ones that keep coming up in lists.

**Why it matters:** Fantasy finales have to pay off years of lore, which is why so many are remembered for sticking the landing or missing it.

**Numbers:** None, this is a discussion topic.

**History:**
- Avatar: The Last Airbender and The Good Place are the usual "earned ending" examples.
- Angel's "Not Fade Away" is praised for the team spending what might be their last day before a final battle.
- Buffy the Vampire Slayer is the bittersweet one, heavy on loss.
- Game of Thrones is the divisive one: the last half hour hands out happy endings, and people still argue about it.
- Once Upon a Time is often cited as stumbling, with season 6 called a more satisfying place to stop.
- Supernatural's finale does not sit well with everyone.

**Colour:** The counterpoint worth raising live: Lanterns just ended and set up Man of Tomorrow instead of closing, so "finale" and "setup" are not the same thing anymore.

**Quick hits:**
- Landed: Avatar, The Good Place, Angel ("Not Fade Away")
- Bittersweet: Buffy
- Divisive: Game of Thrones
- Stumbled: Once Upon a Time, Supernatural
- Angle: finale vs setup for the next thing
