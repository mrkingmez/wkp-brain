## 4. Diagnosis: where and why viewers leave

**Where (observed, from pulled figures):** the typical long review is watched about 3 to 9 minutes per engaged viewer. The 11 videos above range from 177 to 569 seconds. The best percentages (VQ 3 and 4 at 47.5, Street Fighter at 34.0) belong to two of the three shortest videos in the sample, so length and percent move together.

**Why (inferred, ranked by how strong the evidence is):**

1. **The unseen viewer has nothing to stay for after the spoiler card.** Search traffic is the likeliest source of people who have not seen the movie (15,243 views, 14.9 percent viewed). They would leave at the card. Test: section 5, change 2.
2. **The opening gives no reason to stay.** Godzilla, Street Fighter and VQ7 open with a general question or plain recap, not a concrete tease. Heat is the one with a concrete tease (accountability, a named shootout) and still ran 8.3 percent, so a good first 25 seconds does not rescue a 35 minute video with 1:15 of housekeeping. One data point, low confidence.
3. **The strongest argument is parked at the end.** The pacing complaint (5:39) and the star split (26:20) are what a stranger wants. Only people who reach minute 26 hear the disagreement in full.
4. **No visible structure.** No chapter-style card or text appears at any interval in the plan. A flat voice pace plus no on-screen signal means a viewer never sees where a new part starts, so there is no "stay for the next bit" moment.
5. **Titles over-promise.** MonsterVerse comparison unpaid (observed).

**Confidence:** medium on 1, 3, 5. Low on 2 and 4. No retention curve was available, so every "where" above comes from averages and transcripts. The curve for the last 6 reviews in Studio (Analytics, Content, open the video, Engagement, "Key moments for audience retention") would confirm or kill 1 in about 10 minutes. See `https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415`: YouTube says the Intro figure is the share still watching at 30 seconds, and compares you with your last 10 videos of similar length.

## 6. Re-hook map for a 28 minute review

Use the Godzilla timeline as the working example. Text cards are 2 seconds, white text, plain. Sounds are your own P: library. Keep each re-hook to one move, never two on the same beat.

| Time | What happens | Edit move |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:20 | Cold open: 3 clips from later in the recording (see section 7) | Lay three clips of 5 to 8 seconds on V1, no picture, cut fast |
| 0:20 to 0:23 | Logo sting, 3 seconds maximum | Shorten the ripple-insert sting (planned 6 to 8 seconds) to 3 |
| 0:30 | Title promise paid: each host gives one spoken sentence, no rating | Cut from the opening 60 seconds of the recording |
| 2:00 | First question posed to the viewer ("by minute 6 you will know if the pacing complaint holds") | Text card with the question, 2 seconds. Use a clip of the host saying it |
| 4:00 | First act close: one-line summary of what was settled | Hold the host cam, add a text card "Part 1 done: what it is" |
| 5:00 | Re-hook one. The most surprising line from minutes 5 to 12 gets a pull-forward teaser | Add the teaser clip (4 to 6 seconds) just before 5:00 with a sound hit |
| 8:00 | New picture or clip burst: 3 pictures in 15 seconds on the best reaction beat | Place from the run sheet's gap list, 2 to 4 seconds each |
| 11:00 | Re-hook two: restate the open question, reveal a piece | Text card, plus one picture |
| 16:30 | Act break: mid-video recap in one sentence and a "stay for" line | Text card "Part 3: where it lost a star" |
| 22:00 | Re-hook four: forward teaser to the ratings ("we split by half a star, ratings in 5 minutes") | Spoken line from the recording, or a 15 second pickup recorded after |
| 26:00 | Ratings | Existing sticker, keep it |
| 27:30 | Post-credits or the last open question, then the existing end card | Existing |

Spacing is the point: a visible reset at roughly 0:30, 2:00, 4:00, 5:00, 8:00 and every 5 to 6 minutes after. YouTube's own help says viewership generally tapers over a video (`https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415`), and an editing-practice guide from the AIR media company based on its own review of 100 channels recommends a rehook candidate roughly every 2 minutes, cut changes every 10 to 20 seconds in minutes 0 to 3 and every 25 to 40 seconds in minutes 3 to 7 (`https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/advanced-retention-editing-cutting-patterns-that-keep-viewers-past-minute-8`, its own data, not independently checked). Treat both as starting points. A second creator guide cites no data for its 10 to 20 second interval and 40 to 60 percent dip zone (`https://lenspov.com/articles/audience-retention-strategies`), so I do not lean on those figures.

## 7. The opening 5 minutes, step by step (Premiere)

Do this on the next review. You need the recording and the transcript. All text windows: Window, Text, Transcript.

1. **Find your three cold-open clips.** Search the transcript for the strongest disagreement, the funniest line and the question the title asks. For Godzilla that is "two set pieces missing" (5:42), "My favorite Godzilla series" (26:36 or 27:10) and one more line of your choice. Each clip is 5 to 8 seconds.
2. **Do not include the rating.** The review gate says teasers, not verdicts. A line like "I'm going a little lower than you" works. The words "three and a half" do not.
3. **Place the three clips at 0:00 on V1.** Cut hard between them. Add the 3 second sting after clip three.
4. **Move the existing intro tease (0:00 to 0:12) down.** Cut the generic "can it stand tall" line. Keep "Wardens, let's find out" as the bridge.
5. **Delete the "same day again" banter.** In Godzilla that is the lines around 0:36 to 0:48 (Matt: "Got to see it on the same day again", "within five minutes of each other").
6. **Delete the theater and back injury story** (0:56 to 1:05).
7. **Trim the Minus One recap to 45 seconds.** Keep Winter Wolf's "10 second review" setup at 1:26 and KingZ's "focused on the people" at 1:49. Delete the rest of the recap until 3:35.
8. **Compress cast and runtime** (3:35 to 4:35) to one line each. A text card showing the runtime does it.
9. **Add a 60 to 90 second spoiler-free take after the title promise.** Pull the two hosts' general reaction (no rating) from the recording. Name the chapter "No spoilers" at that point.
10. **Spoiler card goes after that.** Name the next chapter "Spoilers start here" so unseen viewers can leave happily, and so the description shows it.
11. **Check the result.** The first real argument should now start by 3:00, not 4:54.

If the recording does not have a usable spoiler-free take, record a 60 second pickup of both hosts after the review, same cams, same order, and drop it in.

## 11. Shorter cuts, 12 to 18 minutes: do not switch on this evidence

Numbers, from the section 3 table (eleven videos, lengths derived, small sample):

- Videos about 17 to 25 minutes (7 of them): average view duration 230, 248, 269, 367, 424, 498, 549 seconds. Median 367.
- Videos about 36 to 40 minutes (5 of them: Carrie, Crystal Lake, T2, Heat, plus the 42 minute Undertone at 633 seconds): 177, 242, 501, 569, 633. Median 501.
- The longer group watches **more** minutes per engaged viewer. A shorter format does not by itself raise watch hours, because hours are minutes times viewers, not percent.
- Percent viewed is higher on shorter videos, which YouTube may like as a quality signal. A comparison guide notes that watch time per view is length times retention, so a longer video only helps if retention holds (`https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtube-audience-retention-and-average-view-duration`, general guidance, no film-review data). I did not find any film-review channel dataset on cold opens or verdict placement. The comparison claims about other channels here are from general retention guides, not from watching named review channels, because VidIQ outlier and competitor calls were not used.

**What I recommend:** keep review length at 20 to 28 minutes and fix the structure first. If you want a length test, run it as the one change on a single video, such as cutting a 28 minute review to 20 by removing the recap and the weakest 3 minutes. Judge it on minutes watched, not percent.

**FrostCast is the hours engine.** 61 percent of qualifying minutes came from live streams. Nine FrostCast replays ran 7.1 to 35.4 percent viewed, 391 to 1,924 minutes each in the Sep 1 to Nov 1 pull. Two cheap edits to the replay: trim the first 1:23 to 2:21 of small talk (EP 120's first topic starts at 2:21) and keep chapters, which already exist for EP 120. Whether trimming a stream replay in Studio hurts the hours already counted is not stated on YouTube's pages I read, so test on one episode.

**Hours arithmetic (mine, not a forecast):** at 25,666 qualifying minutes per 90 days, a full year is about 1,711 hours. A 30 percent gain on review minutes only is about +3,028 minutes per 90 days, about +50 hours, and takes the 90 day total from 428 to 478 hours. Getting to 4,000 hours inside 12 months needs about 2.3 times today's rate. Subscribers remain the harder number.
