Recording: Wed 2026-10-14. Companion to Topic 9 of the main sheet and to Part 2. Live action only, no anime, no animation. Organized by decade so you can walk it in order. Nothing here repeats Part 1 (Avatar, The Good Place, Angel, Buffy, Game of Thrones, Once Upon a Time, Supernatural) or Part 2 (Korra, Gravity Falls, Dark, Lucifer, Stranger Things, Merlin, Lost, His Dark Materials, Good Omens, Wheel of Time, Shadow and Bone, Warrior Nun). **Read this first (flags):**
- The 1980s are thin. Real fantasy on 80s TV is rare, so that section leans on sci-fi and romantic fantasy. Say so on air rather than stretching it.

## THE 1980s

### Topic 9n: Blake's 7, "Blake" (Dec 21, 1981)

**What happened:** The BBC series ended its fourth and final run on 21 December 1981. Avon shoots Blake, believing he was betrayed, and the rest of the crew are shown being killed by Federation guards.

**Why it matters:** It is the standard "everyone dies" finale. It aired before the internet, so viewers had no warning.

**Numbers:** Four series. Final episode 21 Dec 1981.

**History:** Sources call it one of the most controversial and discussed finales in TV history, and say it left viewers stunned. The fourth series itself has a mixed reputation, though some reviewers praise the writing. The show was not formally renewed.

**Quick hits:**
- Aired Dec 21, 1981
- Avon shoots Blake, crew killed
- Pre-internet, no spoilers
- Fourth series had a mixed reputation

### Topic 9o: Beauty and the Beast (CBS), series end (Aug 4, 1990)

**What happened:** The CBS series ran Sept 25, 1987 to Aug 4, 1990, 56 episodes over three seasons. The final episode ends on a cliffhanger, with Catherine walking down a tunnel toward a chamber where Vincent is suffering a violent madness.

**Why it matters:** It had already been cancelled once on a cliffhanger and brought back by a fan petition, then ended on another one.

**Numbers:** 56 episodes, three seasons.

**History:** The show was cancelled on a Season Two cliffhanger, and a strong fan petition returned it. In 1989 CBS said it would not return that fall, and it came back in 1990 with a planned 12 episodes. Fan sources say Catherine dies early in Season 3 and Linda Hamilton's character exits. Fan campaigns followed in April 1990. I found no contemporary critic reviews or ratings for the final episode.

**Colour:** Confirm the Season 3 details before saying them live. They come from a fan-history source, not a major outlet.

**Quick hits:**
- Sept 25, 1987 to Aug 4, 1990
- 56 episodes
- Fan petition brought it back once
- Ended on a cliffhanger

### Topic 9p: Doctor Who, "Survival" (Nov 22 to Dec 6, 1989)

**What happened:** The last story of the original run aired in three parts. The BBC did not announce a cancellation. It just kept the show off the schedule.

**Why it matters:** It is the quiet end. A 26-year show stopped without a farewell.

**Numbers:** 26-year original run. Three parts. A 1996 TV movie with Sylvester McCoy followed, then nothing until the 2005 revival.

**History:** Sources differ on the framing. One calls it a hiatus that was really a secret cancellation. Another blames the BBC's slow loss of interest in the programme. The closing monologue was recorded on 23 November, the anniversary of the first episode in 1963.

**Quick hits:**
- Aired Nov 22 to Dec 6, 1989
- No announced cancellation
- Closing monologue recorded on the 1963 anniversary
- 1996 movie, then revival in 2005

## THE 1990s

### Topic 9q: Hercules: The Legendary Journeys, "Full Circle" (Nov 1999)

**What happened:** The series ended after six seasons with "Full Circle."

**Why it matters:** A show known for jokes closed on a serious season, and the star had decided to leave.

**Numbers:** Six seasons. Sources disagree on the exact air date, giving November 27, 1999 for the finale and Sept 27 to Nov 22, 1999 for the season.

**History:** One long-running fan review calls the finale close to flawless, saying it brings back Nemesis, the strained bond with Ares, the Zeus and Titans mythology, and the Hercules and Iolaus friendship. Season six was short and more serious than the previous two. Kevin Sorbo's contract ended partway through the season, and he has said the producers wanted three more seasons and he declined because he planned to move on to Andromeda. "Hercules, Tramps & Thieves" was the last episode actually shot.

**Colour:** This is fan writing, not a critic consensus. Say "fans call it" rather than "critics."

**Quick hits:**
- Six seasons, ended Nov 1999
- Star chose to leave
- Fan reviews praise the callbacks
- No critic consensus found

### Topic 9r: Babylon 5, "Sleeping in Light" (Nov 25, 1998)

**What happened:** The final episode skips the big space battle and plays as a quiet farewell.

**Why it matters:** It was nominated for a Hugo and also left some fans cold.

**Numbers:** Aired Nov 25, 1998. Nominated for the 1999 Hugo Award for Best Dramatic Presentation.

**History:** A 1998 column called it a rare case of science fiction becoming a tearjerker, a three-hanky affair, and noted many fans were less than thrilled without the large space battles. Inverse argues it feels out of step with Season 5 because it was filmed during Season 4, when cancellation seemed likely, and Claudia Christian had left by the time it aired, making her return as Ivanova feel jarring.

**Colour:** The finale was shot a season early as insurance, which is a good contrast to shows that never got to finish.

**Quick hits:**
- Aired Nov 25, 1998
- 1999 Hugo nomination
- Praised as a tearjerker
- Shot during Season 4 as insurance

## THE 2000s

### Topic 9s: Xena: Warrior Princess, "A Friend in Need" (2001)

**What happened:** The finale killed Xena and she refused to be brought back, ending with her as a ghost alongside Gabrielle.

**Why it matters:** It is the finale fans still argue over most from this era of fantasy.

**Numbers:** Aired 2001. The episode was screened for several hundred fans and media at the Museum of Television and Radio in Los Angeles before broadcast.

**History:** One retrospective calls it one of the most divisive conclusions in television history. Some appreciated its tragic themes and visual ambition, while many disliked the dark tone, new characters and change of setting. Fans objected to the death and the refusal to be revived, with one fan piece calling it a betrayal of the show. A DVD review called the ghost ending a copout. Fans did credit it for recognizing Gabrielle's role. Fan fiction rewriting the ending followed.

**Colour:** Most sources here are fan or opinion writing. Confirm the exact air date before saying it.

**Quick hits:**
- 2001, Xena dies and refuses revival
- Called one of TV's most divisive endings
- Credit: it honored Gabrielle's role
- Fans wrote their own endings

### Topic 9t: Firefly, cancellation (Dec 2002) and Serenity (2005)

**What happened:** Fox cancelled it in December 2002 after 11 of its 14 produced episodes had aired. The other three premiered in the UK the next summer.

**Why it matters:** The show never got a finale. The film was the finale, and it got made because fans bought DVDs.

**Numbers:** 14 episodes produced, 11 aired on Fox at first. Universal released Serenity in 2005. Sources describe the film as well received by critics and fans, with mediocre box office.

**History:** The cancellation is blamed on low ratings, with out-of-order airing and Fox's expectations as contributing factors. Fans tried letter campaigns to save it at Fox and to get UPN to pick it up. Strong DVD sales are what got the movie made. Fans call themselves Browncoats.

**Colour:** Sources disagree on which episode aired first. I did not find fan reaction to the film's ending, so say nothing about that.

**Quick hits:**
- Cancelled Dec 2002, 11 of 14 aired
- Fan campaigns failed
- DVD sales got Serenity made, 2005
- Film: good reviews, weak box office

### Topic 9u: Battlestar Galactica, "Daybreak" (2009)

**What happened:** The three-part finale ended the series and split viewers immediately.

**Why it matters:** The final season was well reviewed, so the finale split is about the landing, not the run.

**Numbers:** The finale gave the show its strongest ratings since season two, per one source.

**History:** Syfy's retrospective says some fans called it one of the worst finales of all time while others called it the perfect ending to a great story. ComicBook.com lists the complaints as too many plot lines left unresolved, an ending that was far too idealistic, and a lack of action or drama. Season-level critic scores were high.

**Quick hits:**
- Three-part finale, 2009
- Best ratings since season two
- Split: perfect ending vs worst ever
- Complaints: unresolved plots, too idealistic

## THE 2010s

### Topic 9v: Smallville, "Finale" (May 13, 2011)

**What happened:** The two-part finale aired on The CW. The last shot was modeled on iconic scenes from the Christopher Reeve Superman films.

**Why it matters:** After ten seasons the show decided the finale would put Clark at the edge of becoming Superman, not past it.

**Numbers:** About 2.97 million viewers per SpoilerTV and 3.02 million per Looper. Say "about 3 million." It was the series' most watched episode of that season, per SpoilerTV.

**History:** Critics were generally positive and fan reaction stayed divided. Tom Welling defended not showing Clark fully as Superman, saying the show was never going to be about Superman. Co-creator Miles Millar said the planned last moment was Clark putting on the suit and flying into his destiny.

**Colour:** Pairs well with the "finale vs setup" angle from Part 1, since this is a setup ending on purpose.

**Quick hits:**
- May 13, 2011, two parts
- About 3 million viewers
- Ends at the edge of Superman
- Final shot echoes the Reeve films

### Topic 9w: True Blood, "Thank You" (Aug 24, 2014)

**What happened:** HBO's vampire series closed with "Thank You."

**Why it matters:** Fan reaction was harsh enough that headlines used the phrase worst finale ever.

**Numbers:** Aired Aug 24, 2014.

**History:** The Rotten Tomatoes consensus for the season said that despite the promise of a shocking finale, the show is mostly content to limp along on familiar plot points. Individual reviews included a C- from the A.V. Club and 2 out of 4 from Slant, which said the series had exhausted its surprises. Looper quotes Entertainment Weekly finding the blandness almost offensive. I did not get the full piece.

**Colour:** Confirm the EW quote before using it live.

**Quick hits:**
- Aug 24, 2014
- Consensus: limped along on familiar points
- Fan headlines: worst finale ever
- Critics: C- and 2 of 4

### Topic 9x: Penny Dreadful, "The Blessed Dark" (June 19, 2016)

**What happened:** The third-season finale doubled as the series finale. Vanessa chooses to die and asks Ethan to kill her.

**Why it matters:** The cancellation story is disputed, and so is whether the ending was chosen or forced.

**Numbers:** About 578,000 viewers for the original airing, per Looper.

**History:** Slashfilm reports creator John Logan decided to end it because he was reaching the end of the story. Bloody Disgusting disputes that, arguing Showtime told Logan they were cancelling and he was forced to end it. Reaction to the ending was split, with some saying it was never given time to run out of steam and others calling the rushed ending a letdown.

**Quick hits:**
- June 19, 2016
- Vanessa asks Ethan to end her
- Disputed: creator's choice or cancellation
- About 578,000 viewers

### Topic 9y: The Vampire Diaries, "I Was Feeling Epic" (Mar 10, 2017)

**What happened:** The series finale aired March 10, 2017, written by Kevin Williamson and Julie Plec. Plec also directed.

**Why it matters:** Promos teased a big return that turned out to be a trick, which is where the feeling of being cheated came from.

**Numbers:** None verified. I found no ratings or fan polls.

**History:** Den of Geek's review said the episode packed in a lot across decades and that its biggest sin would have been boring. It noted many viewers may have felt cheated by the promos because the Elena scene turned out to be Katherine. Nina Dobrev returned for the finale.

**Colour:** Thin sourcing. Treat this as a light mention only.

**Quick hits:**
- Mar 10, 2017
- Written by Williamson and Plec
- Promo bait and switch: Elena scene was Katherine
- No ratings data found

### Topic 9z: Grimm, "The End" (Mar 31, 2017)

**What happened:** The series finale concluded the story of the villain Zerstoren and ended with a flash-forward 20 years.

**Why it matters:** It is an example of tidy closure that still felt messy to reviewers.

**Numbers:** Six seasons. Aired March 31, 2017.

**History:** Showbuzz Daily called it a messy wrap-up and an example of the show's shakiness at big storylines. A German outlet reported mixed reactions because some storylines were left open.

**Colour:** The title I first searched, "Blood Will Tell," was wrong. Every source calls the finale "The End." Two sources only.

**Quick hits:**
- Mar 31, 2017, six seasons
- Ends with a 20-year flash-forward
- Called a messy wrap-up
- Some storylines left open

## THE 2020s

### Topic 9aa: WandaVision, the finale (March 2021)

**What happened:** Disney+'s first big Marvel series closed with a finale that traded intimacy for MCU spectacle.

**Why it matters:** Fan theories drove the whole run. The finale is where those theories either paid off or did not.

**Numbers:** None settled. Rotten Tomatoes shows different numbers by source, so skip them.

**History:** Critics were more favorable than audiences. The Rotten Tomatoes consensus said the finale gives up the show's intimacy for bigger MCU spectacle while leaving multiverse clues. Discussing Film called it a Marvel third act on autopilot with a strong closing moment between Wanda and Vision. The Hollywood Reporter noted viewers were disappointed that theories, like Evan Peters' Pietro coming from the X-Men films, did not pan out. Showrunner Matt Shakman said the emotional core was grief, not the theories.

**Quick hits:**
- March 2021
- Critics warmer than audiences
- Complaint: spectacle over intimacy
- Theories did not pay off (Pietro)

### Topic 9ab: Loki, Season 2 finale, "Glorious Purpose" (Nov 2023)

**What happened:** The series finale resolved Loki's arc.

**Why it matters:** It is the counterpoint to WandaVision: a finale praised for character while criticized for service to the MCU.

**Numbers:** 11.2 million viewers worldwide in its first three days, slightly above the premiere, per Deadline via SuperHeroHype.

**History:** Rotten Tomatoes' summary said it sticks the landing on Loki's character arc in a surprising and touching way. Pajiba gave it 5 out of 5. MovieWeb gave it 2 out of 5 and called a convoluted second season a limp to a thoughtful end. Film Stories called it odd and said it labored to set up the MCU's future.

**Colour:** Sources disagree on the credited writer. Skip the credit.

**Quick hits:**
- Nov 2023
- 11.2 million viewers in three days
- Praised for Loki's arc
- Dissent: odd, busy with the MCU future

### Topic 9ac: The Boys, "Blood and Bone" (series finale, 2026)

**What happened:** The series finale of The Boys aired in 2026.

**Why it matters:** The showrunner said publicly that fans would retroactively judge the whole show on the finale.

**Numbers:** None found.

**History:** Eric Kripke told The Hollywood Reporter in April 2026 that fans will retroactively judge the show based on how they feel about the finale. The season got generally favorable reviews, but the finale drew much criticism, with critics divided over the ending overall.

**Colour:** This is a superhero satire, not fantasy. Single thin source, so keep it a mention. I did not get the plot of the ending.

**Quick hits:**
- Series finale, 2026
- Kripke: fans will judge the whole show on it
- Season favorable, finale divisive
- Not fantasy, so optional

## THE ANGLES

- **Who got to plan it:** Babylon 5 shot its finale a season early as insurance. Smallville chose to end at the edge of Superman. Firefly and Beauty and the Beast never got to plan anything.
- **Quiet vs big:** Babylon 5 skipped the battle and some fans complained. WandaVision went big and some fans complained. Pick your poison.
- **The ending that killed the lead:** Xena, Blake's 7, Penny Dreadful. Compare to Part 2's Merlin.
- **Fans paid for the finale:** Firefly's movie exists because of DVD sales. Beauty and the Beast came back on a petition.
- **Hype hurts:** The Vampire Diaries promos and WandaVision theories both set up expectations the finales could not meet.
