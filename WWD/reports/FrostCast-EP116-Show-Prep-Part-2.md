## LANDED

### Topic 9b: The Legend of Korra, "Day of the Colossus" and "The Last Stand"

**What happened:** The two-part Book Four finale closed the series in December 2014. It ends with Korra and Asami walking into the Spirit World together.

**Why it matters:** The ending is a quiet character beat instead of a final fight, and it became the most talked-about image of the show.

**Numbers:** Two-part finale. No other figures worth quoting.

**History:** Reviewers were broadly positive, one calling it the best finale the show could have asked for. The creators, Bryan Konietzko and Michael DiMartino, later confirmed the moment as the two becoming a couple. Coverage at the time argued over how explicit it was, since there was no kiss.

**Colour:** It lands in the same conversation as the Avatar: The Last Airbender finale from Part 1, which makes a natural pairing on air.

**Quick hits:**
- Two-part finale, Dec 2014
- Ends on Korra and Asami entering the Spirit World
- Creators confirmed it as a couple later
- Debate: subtle or explicit

### Topic 9c: Gravity Falls, "Take Back the Falls" (Weirdmageddon Part 3)

**What happened:** The one-hour series finale aired Feb 15, 2016. Creator Alex Hirsch had announced in advance that season two would be the end.

**Why it matters:** The show chose its own end date, and critics tied that to why the finale paid off its mysteries.

**Numbers:** One-hour finale, aired Feb 15, 2016.

**History:** The AV Club compared it to the finales of Justice League Unlimited and Avatar: The Last Airbender. Reviewers singled out Stan's sacrifice and his takedown of Bill, plus the payoff of the show's ciphers and symbols. Not everyone agreed: one review called it imperfect and not especially daring, and said Stan's sacrifice got waved away by the happy ending.

**Colour:** A planned ending is a real contrast with shows that were cancelled mid-story, which is the third bucket below.

**Quick hits:**
- Aired Feb 15, 2016, one hour
- Hirsch announced the end ahead of time
- Praised for paying off mysteries and Stan's sacrifice
- Dissent: safe, happy ending undercut the sacrifice

### Topic 9d: Dark, Season 3 (Netflix, June 2020)

**What happened:** The German time-travel series wrapped with its third season in June 2020.

**Why it matters:** It is the clearest example of a show that knew its ending from the start and tied its own loops shut.

**Numbers:** Three seasons.

**History:** Inverse asked whether it might be the best series finale in sci-fi history. Metro Weekly gave it five stars. A Metacritic snapshot showed positive and mixed reviews and no negative ones. The complaints were about difficulty: the time jumps and the added dimensional layer are hard to follow.

**Colour:** Counterpoint to Lost below. Both are puzzle shows, and they ended in opposite reputations.

**Quick hits:**
- June 2020, three seasons
- Praised for closing its own loops
- Complaint: hard to follow, expects homework
- Pairs with Lost as the "puzzle show" contrast

### Topic 9e: Lucifer, Season 6 (Netflix)

**What happened:** The final ten-episode season closed the series, which had moved from Fox to Netflix.

**Why it matters:** A show that was cancelled once and brought back got to plan its ending.

**Numbers:** Ten episodes in the final season.

**History:** Early reviews were mostly positive. One critic said the ten episodes offered real closure by tying threads from every season. The A.V. Club's critic argued it redeemed earlier missteps. The Rotten Tomatoes consensus called it a fitting farewell. Dissent: one review praised the character endings but faulted how some characters were treated, and another said viewers have to accept some leaps for the finale to come together. Most of what I found was first reviews from before release, not long-term fan sentiment.

**Colour:** The "cancelled, then rescued" detail is worth confirming before saying it live; the Fox-to-Netflix move is the part I am sure of.

**Quick hits:**
- Final season, 10 episodes
- Praised for closure across all seasons
- Called a fitting farewell
- Dissent: some characters short-changed

## MISSED OR DIVISIVE

### Topic 9f: Stranger Things, the series finale

**What happened:** The finale aired Dec 31, 2025, ending the Netflix series.

**Why it matters:** It is the freshest big finale argument in this space, and the split is between critics and audiences.

**Numbers:** Aired Dec 31, 2025. Reports say the season's audience score on Rotten Tomatoes fell to a series low after Volume 2 dropped on Christmas Day. Sources give slightly different figures, so no number here.

**History:** Critics split. Empire called it solid but safe. The Wrap called it satisfying with room to nitpick. Nerdist praised the farewell but flagged odd decisions and uneven pacing. Two Hollywood Reporter critics were openly frustrated, especially with the return to the Mind Flayer. Online complaints centered on plot inconsistencies and unanswered questions, with Vecna's backstory and Will's disappearance named. Another criticism: the villain dies and the final battle feels too short.

**Colour:** Several results were low-quality SEO blogs with unverified quotes. I used only the named outlets above.

**Quick hits:**
- Dec 31, 2025
- Critics: solid but safe
- Audiences: harsher than critics
- Gripes: short final fight, unanswered questions

### Topic 9g: Merlin (BBC), "The Diamond of the Day"

**What happened:** The two-part finale ended the BBC series in 2013. Arthur dies of wounds from the fight with Mordred, and Merlin waits for his return in the modern day.

**Why it matters:** It is a faithful-to-legend ending that a lot of the fanbase still rejects.

**Numbers:** Two-part finale, five-year run.

**History:** Reception was mixed. A co-creator publicly acknowledged the finale did not meet fans' expectations and said a more conventional ending, with Arthur surviving, was considered. One reviewer said few shows have moved them as much. Looper reported that some fans felt it retroactively ruined the show. Specific complaints included the magic reveal and how Gwaine's and Morgana's deaths were handled.

**Colour:** Pairs with the Buffy "bittersweet" slot from Part 1. The question: when does bittersweet tip into betrayal?

**Quick hits:**
- 2013, two parts, five seasons
- Arthur dies, Merlin waits
- Co-creator admitted it missed expectations
- Complaints: magic reveal, side-character deaths

### Topic 9h: Lost, "The End" (May 2010)

**What happened:** The series finale aired in May 2010 and set off years of argument.

**Why it matters:** It is the show people reach for first when they say "mystery box," even though it is not strict fantasy.

**Numbers:** Audience reported as 13.5 million in one oral history and nearly 14 million by TVLine. Sources differ slightly, so say "about 13.5 to 14 million."

**History:** Reaction split into two camps. One embraced the symbolic, spiritual ending. The other felt it did not conclude the story, with many angry about not getting more direct answers. A wrong reading, that the castaways had died all along, took hold anyway. ABC later explained the closing shots of the Oceanic 815 wreckage as a visual aid to let viewers decompress before the news. The Associated Press compared the post-finale dispute to The Sopranos in 2007.

**Colour:** Good tie to Part 1's Game of Thrones. Both are "the ending that changed how people feel about the whole run."

**Quick hits:**
- May 2010
- About 13.5 to 14 million viewers
- Split: symbolic ending vs unanswered questions
- Closing wreckage shots explained by ABC as a buffer before the news

### Topic 9i: His Dark Materials, Season 3 (HBO and BBC)

**What happened:** The third season adapted The Amber Spyglass and ended the series.

**Why it matters:** The showdown the books build toward was the thing critics said the finale handled least well.

**Numbers:** Three seasons.

**History:** Reviews were mixed. TechRadar called it a fairly entertaining but disappointing final entry. Paste said it fizzles rather than builds, and that the promised showdown between the angels and Lord Asriel's rebels takes a much different path. Another review called it well-acted and exquisitely designed but plodding and humourless. Cinema Sentries found it a satisfying finale that wove the stories into one series, and News24 said it picks up after the first episode. Critical Popcorn noted the bears of Svalbard are largely absent and the final battle looks limited by budget.

**Quick hits:**
- Adapts The Amber Spyglass
- Praised: faithful, well acted
- Complaint: the climax underwhelms
- Svalbard bears mostly absent

### Topic 9j: Good Omens, Season 3

**What happened:** The last season was cut down to a single 90-minute episode after the sexual assault allegations against co-creator Neil Gaiman came to light.

**Why it matters:** It is a finale shaped by something outside the story, so any judgment of it has to account for the shortened runtime.

**Numbers:** One 90-minute episode. One outlet reported a mediocre Rotten Tomatoes score on 15 reviews. Skip the percentage on air.

**History:** Reception was split. Inverse's critic called it possibly one of the better series finales of recent years. FandomWire said it was worth watching for fans. One commentator called the rushed structure a drawback. The season 2 cliffhanger had also frustrated some fans while others celebrated the angel and demon getting together.

**Colour:** Handle the Gaiman context carefully and factually. This is a show-prep note, not a verdict.

**Quick hits:**
- Cut to one 90-minute episode
- Cause: allegations against co-creator Neil Gaiman
- Split: praised ending vs rushed structure
- S2 cliffhanger had already divided fans

## NEVER GOT TO LAND (cancelled, not finished)

### Topic 9k: The Wheel of Time (Prime Video)

**What happened:** Amazon cancelled it after three seasons, about a month after the Season 3 finale.

**Why it matters:** Season 3 was reportedly its best-reviewed, so the cancellation stung more.

**Numbers:** Three seasons. Reports say viewership slipped in Season 3 and the production was too expensive for a fourth.

**History:** Showrunner Rafe Judkins said ratings were not the problem. The series ended well short of the books.

**Quick hits:**
- Cancelled after three seasons
- About a month after the S3 finale
- Best-reviewed season came last
- Story ended well short of the books

### Topic 9l: Shadow and Bone (Netflix)

**What happened:** Netflix cancelled it before a Season 3.

**Why it matters:** The Season 2 finale diverged from Leigh Bardugo's books, then there was no season to explain it.

**Numbers:** Two seasons.

**History:** The finale had Alina become queen with shadow powers, a change the show never explains. Fans were upset, and Bardugo said she was disappointed.

**Quick hits:**
- Cancelled before S3
- S2 finale departs from the books
- Alina queen with shadow powers, unexplained
- Bardugo disappointed

**Why it matters:** It is the cleanest case of a fantasy show that ended mid-sentence.

**Numbers:** None verified.

## THE ANGLES

- Planned vs forced endings: Gravity Falls, Dark and Lucifer got to plan the end. Good Omens, Wheel of Time and Shadow and Bone did not get a clean one.
- Open-ended vs closed: Korra ends on a door opening. Dark closes its loops. Lost left questions on purpose.
- Critics vs audiences: Stranger Things is the clearest recent split.
- Tie back to Part 1: Lanterns just ended by setting up Man of Tomorrow. Is a setup ending a finale?
