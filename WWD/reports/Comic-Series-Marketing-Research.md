# Comic-style post series for WWD: research (2026-10-30)

Asked by Zac via Jarvis. Plain answer first, then the evidence. Anything not verified is marked NOT AVAILABLE. I did not run the separate market-research department; I searched directly.

## Bottom line

GO SMALL AS A TEST. 4 weeks, one 3-slide carousel per week (not three separate posts), tied to the Wednesday FrostCast. Scrap the other-companies' characters and logos from the panels. Only worth continuing if it beats the current carousel baseline AND sends measurable people to YouTube. Do not build a standing 3-pages-a-week schedule yet.

## 1. Evidence: do carousels and comics perform?

What is solid:
- Buffer, more than 4 million posts, Jan 2022 to Oct 2024: carousels get about 1.12x the engagement of Reels and about 2.14x the engagement of single images. Reels get about 1.36x the reach of carousels and about 2.25x the reach of single images. Not split by follower count. (https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reach-engagement-analysis/) Note: this data ends Oct 2024.
- Reading it: carousels win engagement (likes, saves, comments, shares), Reels win reach. Single images lose on both.

What is weak (secondhand, could not find the original):
- A blog cites a 10,000-post study: carousels 1.38%, Reels 1.23%, single images 0.72%. Original NOT AVAILABLE.
- A summary of Socialinsider 2025 data: carousels 0.55%, Reels 0.52%. Single-image figure NOT AVAILABLE.
- Metricool 2025 vs 2026 single-image engagement down about 46%, cited secondhand only.
- Different sources define "engagement rate" differently, so the percentages do not line up. Only the direction is consistent.

Facebook carousel/comic data: NOT AVAILABLE. I did not find a credible 2025-2026 source.

Comics specifically: I searched for podcasts or nerd/review channels that run a recurring comic on social and found NO documented case study with results. Zero verified examples. Anyone claiming "comics grow podcasts" has no source I could find. That means this test is a bet, not a proven play.

- Our two review carousels got 2 likes (Carrie) and 3 likes (Other Mommy). So our own carousels are not beating our Reels today.
- Views, reach, saves, shares: NOT AVAILABLE (needs 1,000+ followers for per-post insights).
- Facebook numbers: no newer WWD file found.

Important honest read: at 68 followers, format barely matters. Reach to non-followers is what grows an account, and that comes mostly from Reels and shares. A comic carousel mostly gets shown to the 68 people who already follow. Industry engagement averages come from much bigger accounts and do not transfer cleanly.

## 2. Best format

- Aspect ratio: 4:5 vertical, 1080 x 1350 px. Instagram's profile grid now previews in 3:4 (sources disagree on the date: Jan 2025 vs late 2025), so keep text and faces centered so nothing important is cut. The first slide sets the ratio for the whole carousel. Sources are vendor blogs (Buffer, Planoly, others); confirm against Meta's own docs before relying on exact dates. https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-grid/
- The FrostCast 094 example is a single tall page with about 10 panels and small text. On a phone that text is hard to read and it is one dense image. For a feed, split a page like that into slides: slide 1 hook, slide 2 and 3 payoff. One big idea per slide, large lettering.
- Recommended split of "3 pages a week": ONE carousel of 3 slides, posted once, not three separate posts. Reason: carousels are the format with the engagement edge, three separate posts at 68 followers just thin out what little reach there is, and Zac is part time.
  - Slide 1: hook (a question or a claim, no verdict, per the teasers rule).
  - Slide 2: the setup / the panels.
  - Slide 3: call to action (Wednesday 9 PM ET FrostCast, or the new review).
- Timing: post the carousel Tue or Wed daytime to promote the Wednesday 9 PM show. Reviews already have their own posts, so the comic stays the FrostCast promo only in the test. Do not add it on review-drop days.
- Facebook: repost the same slides. No Facebook-specific evidence found, so treat it as free reuse, not a separate plan.

## 3. Does it drive viewers to YouTube and the Wednesday show?

- Instagram captions do not take clickable links. The only tappable options are the bio link and Story link stickers. So a comic cannot send anyone to YouTube directly from the feed. (Multiple vendor blogs agree; Meta doc not checked.)
- So the CTA has to be: "Live Wednesday 9 PM ET, link in bio" plus a Story with a link sticker the same day.
- Whether comics drive YouTube clicks for channels like ours: NOT AVAILABLE, no evidence found. Expect likes and comments, not proven views.
- Hook approach: lead with the topic question the comic asks, not the show name. Example shape: panel asks "Is this a golden era of video game movies?" and the answer is the show. Never give the verdict. Ask for a vote in the comments ("Which topic do you want first?"), because comments and shares are what extend reach.

## 4. Risks

1. Other companies' characters and logos. The EP094 example shows the Daredevil logo, Mario, Sonic, Mortal Kombat, Kratos and Grogu. Those are trademarked and copyrighted. A promo post is commercial use for a channel, and parody or commentary fair use is weaker on promotional art than on review footage. I am not a lawyer and this is risk flagging, not a legal opinion. Recommendation: cut all third-party characters and logos from the panels; show topics with text and original props (wolf den, mics, host characters). Route any panel that still references a franchise through the clearance agent before posting.
2. AI-art labeling on Meta. Meta labels AI-generated images with an "AI info" label, using invisible watermark and metadata signals or creator self-disclosure; the label for edited content moved into the post menu while fully AI-generated content stays prominent. Instagram is also testing a voluntary "AI creator" profile label (Hypebeast, May 2026). Sources are news reports, NOT Meta's policy page, so exact current rules are unverified. Practical stance: assume the post gets labeled and disclose AI in the caption (it matches how we already disclose on YouTube). The label may cost some reach with some viewers; effect size NOT AVAILABLE.
3. Host character consistency. In the example, Winter Wolf and KingZ look consistent within one page, but AI image tools drift week to week. Needs a locked character sheet and fixed reference images for each host, plus Oracle if she appears. This should go to graphic-design to build the sheet before week 1. Zac and Matt need to be fine with the characters (the wolf and masked KingZ look are already their identity in the example).
4. Time cost. Full-page AI comics with this much art are heavy: prompt, generate, fix lettering (AI text errors are common), crop to slides. No verified time number for this; the team has not logged one. Estimate is NOT AVAILABLE. The test should log hours per week. If it takes more than about 1 hour a week of Zac's own time, it fails the part-time test.
5. Zac must approve and post everything. Copy goes through the writing room and no-ai-slop before posting. Any "short idea" gate: devils-advocate should see this idea before it is built, per the org rule.

## 5. Recommendation: go small as a test

Test design:
- Length: 4 weeks (4 carousels), the FrostCast Wednesday promo only.
- Format: one 3-slide, 4:5 carousel per week, same slides on Facebook, one Story with link sticker the same day, CTA to Wednesday 9 PM ET.
- Cost: zero dollars if existing AI tools are used. Do not boost any of it.
- Before week 1: devils-advocate review, graphic-design character sheet, clearance pass on panels, no third-party characters.
- Log per post: likes, comments, saves/shares if visible, new followers that week, YouTube views from Instagram if VidIQ/YouTube analytics shows the traffic source, and hours spent. Tracking-director owns the pulls.

What number says it worked (judge at week 4, my suggested thresholds, not industry standards):
- Comic carousels beat our current carousel baseline of 2 to 3 likes AND beat the account's average of about 2.3 likes per post, ideally land near our best Reel level (17 likes) at least once.
- New followers gained during the 4 weeks beat the weeks before (baseline needs a pull; current follower count 68 is the only data point).
- Any measurable YouTube/FrostCast traffic from Instagram (even a handful). If YouTube shows zero from Instagram and likes stay under about 5 per post, stop.
- Time stays at about 1 hour of Zac's time or less per week.

If it passes: expand to a second slot (a review-drop carousel). If it fails: drop it and spend the time on more Reels, which is where our evidence and reach point.

Honest note: my recommendation for growth at 68 followers is still Reels first. The comic is a brand-identity play with a cheap trial, not a proven growth lever.
