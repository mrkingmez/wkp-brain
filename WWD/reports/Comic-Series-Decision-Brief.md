# WWD Comic Series - Decision Brief

Prepared 2026-10-30 for Zac and Winter Wolf. Branding call, so both of you decide.

## The idea
A comic series on Instagram and Facebook, about 3 pages a week, in the style of the FrostCast EP094 comic.

## What the three reviews said
- **Devil's advocate: GO WITH CHANGES.** It is the most on-brand thing WWD has made. The plan around it is the risk: 3 separate full pages a week is too much for part-time hours, a full tall page is unreadable on a phone, and our Instagram audience mostly responds to Reels.
- **Marketing: GO SMALL AS A TEST.** Carousels get the most engagement and Reels get the most reach (Buffer, 4M+ posts, data through Oct 2024). No podcast or review channel was found running a weekly comic with results, so this is a bet. Our own numbers: 68 followers, old carousels got 2 and 3 likes, best Reel 17.
- **Writing room: pick is THE DEN AFTER DARK.** The Den is a hideout over a rainy city and trouble shows up at the door each week. A silent villain carries sealed envelopes marked ENDING ENCLOSED, and the Den keeps endings safe until you watch the show. Den Trials (KingZ's game for Winter Wolf) is the recurring bit. Sample week and style guide are written. The villain's look and motive must be rebuilt (too close to existing characters).

## Recommendation (Jarvis)
Your 3 pages a week, as ONE 3-slide swipe post every FrostCast Wednesday:
1. Slide 1: story page, trouble arrives at the Den.
2. Slide 2: Tonight's Hunt List, the topics as questions, never answers.
3. Slide 3: Den Trials or a cliffhanger, plus "Live tonight 9PM ET, link in bio."

Same slides on Facebook. Cut a short Reel that pans across the panels, since Reels are what grows us. Run 4 Wednesdays at $0.

## Keep going if, after 4 weeks
- Likes beat the 2 to 3 our old carousels got.
- YouTube shows any traffic from Instagram.
- It takes Zac about an hour a week or less.

## Before week 1
1. Winter Wolf says yes (and Oracle, since all three appear).
2. Character sheet: graphic-design writes the art prompts, Winter Wolf approves the looks. Oracle's look is open (writing room suggests a glowing circle with no face; your call).
3. Writing room pass 2: rebuild the villain, then dialogue-doctor reads the pages.
4. No other companies' characters or logos drawn on any page. wwd-rights checks each page.
5. Who owns the new characters (the villain, the Den) is not written down. Worth a line between Zac and Winter Wolf.
