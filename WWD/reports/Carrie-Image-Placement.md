## Part A: What other channels do, and how we stay us

### What I found

I ran searches on how film-review and horror commentary channels edit. The web gave me general principles and two format details, not frame-by-frame breakdowns of any one channel. Where I go past that, it is my own call and I say so.

- **Dead Meat** (horror commentary): every Kill Count ends with an awards segment, a Golden Chainsaw for the best kill and a Dull Machete for the worst. The takeaway for us is that an awards bit is a proven recurring graphic on a horror channel. We already own one, the Wolfie Awards, and Winter Wolf brings it up in this very review at 02:19.
- **CinemaSins** (not horror, but the best-known running gag in review editing): an on-screen counter registers each sin with a ding, and the counter breaks for a gag. On The Fate of the Furious the counter was disabled and 161 sins showed as "R.I.P." The takeaway is a counter with a sound is a joke engine, and breaking it is funnier than keeping it clean. We should not copy the ding or the look. Part B6 makes our own version for the "who are the good guys" argument at 31:17.
- **General sound-effect guidance** (Storyblocks and other editing guides):
  - Whoosh and punch-in sounds work when the hit lands on the exact frame the camera reaches its target.
  - Match the length of the sound to the length of the transition.
  - Comic sounds only work on a moment that is already funny. A record scratch works best when the music is cut on the same frame.
  - Dialogue stays the priority, music ducks under it, effects stay tasteful.
  - Target loudness about -14 LUFS for YouTube.
  - Pick a distinctive set of sounds instead of the same few every channel uses, and over-editing hurts retention.
- **Not found:** frame-level breakdowns of Nerdstalgic, Critical Drinker and similar channels. The stickers, punch-ins and flash frames below come from that general guidance plus what I know of the form, not from a teardown of one channel. If you tell me two or three channels you like, I can pull their transcripts and descriptions, but I cannot watch their video, so I cannot read their edits.

### How we stay our own

1. **Every cue answers a line one of you actually said.** The fun here is your jokes made visible and audible: "needle," "all four cars and maybe that one truck," "five checkpoints," "I do hereby move." A channel that drops a sound on every cut is noisy. A channel that drops one on your punchline sounds like you.
2. **A fixed sound kit of nine sounds** (Part D) that comes back every review. After a few videos people know your sounds.
3. **One recurring graphic with a name.** The Wolfie Awards sticker is it. Same sticker shape every time, different text.
4. **Horror sounds, not meme sounds.** Church organ, rope creak, heartbeat, siren chirp. No vine boom, no airhorn, no "bruh." Those are on every channel this year.
5. **Match the tone of the movie.** Match the tone of the movie, not just the joke. The fun comes from the hosts' lines, but the sound underneath must fit what is on screen. No comedy music or cartoon sounds over death, abuse, bullying violence or school-shooting talk. Dry and dark beats funny there: a silence, a heartbeat, a low hit, or no cue at all. Zac's example: no Benny Hill chase music while counting dead bodies. Carrie is a story about bullying, abuse and a massacre, so the funny cues are saved for the hosts' own jokes (the Wolfie Awards, the cars, the checkpoints, the donuts) and the sound over the heavy scenes stays horror.
6. **A budget.** About one cue per 70 seconds, none in the first 20 seconds after the poster slam, none on a heavy beat (28:17 spoiler text stays serious, 31:42 the buzzer is for the hosts' argument, not a joke about the death). Dialogue always wins.

### B1. Power Slam: the poster open (#1, 00:01 to 00:19)

The poster does not fade in. It slams into frame like Carrie's power just hit the screen.

1. Poster on V2, 00:01 to 00:19.
2. Effect Controls, Scale: keyframe **150%** at the first frame, **100%** 10 frames later. Right-click both keyframes, Temporal Interpolation, **Ease Out**. Opacity 0 to 100 over the first 3 frames.
3. Shake: on the landing frame (frame 10) add Position keyframes around center (960, 540): +14, -14, +10, -10, +6, 0 on x, one keyframe every 2 frames. That is a 12 frame shake.
4. Flash: Project panel, New Item, Color Matte, white. On V3 for 4 frames starting at the landing frame. Opacity keyframe 80% to 0%.
5. Sound: low thud on the landing frame. Start a reverse-swell or glass-suck sound about 15 frames earlier so it peaks at the landing. Layer the Suno intro sting (Part E, S1) starting at 00:01.
6. Hold the poster until 00:19, then Cross Dissolve 12 frames as in the table.

Extras (not Kit, per-review): siren chirp, truck horn, door slam, gym whistle, church organ stab, scanner beep, boxing bell, buzzer. Keep a tone check on every one: nothing cartoonish over a death or a violence beat.

### Where to get them

- **Pixabay** has a large free sound effects library. Check the license page before you publish.
- **Freesound** uses Creative Commons licenses that differ clip to clip. Read the license on every clip: use only commercial-OK ones, and credit the creator where it is a CC BY license.
- **YouTube Audio Library** (YouTube Studio) has free sound effects. That one is from my knowledge, not from the searches, so confirm it in your Studio.
- Avoid creator-linked "free SFX" Dropbox links from YouTube descriptions. They are slow to verify and can be bait.

### Mix rules

- **Dialogue first.** SFX peaks sit under the voice peaks. If a cue hides a word, turn it down.
- **Music bed:** Essential Sound panel, tag the clip **Music**, turn on **Ducking** against dialogue. Start at roughly 15 dB of duck and set it by ear.
- **Sync to the frame:** hits (thud, scratch, buzzer) land on the exact frame of the word or the visual. Whoosh and swell sounds should end on that frame, not start there.
- **Match length:** trim each sound so it does not run past the visual it belongs to.
- **Silence is a cue.** The record scratch at "needle," the silence at "true test of anarchy," and the silence after the blood slap all work because everything stops.
- **Export target:** about -14 LUFS for YouTube.

## Part H: The transition plan (what changed and why)

### What I got wrong

I limited the list to Adobe's older default transitions, so almost everything was Cross Dissolve, hard cut or Film Dissolve. Adobe's Help Center now has a second set called Modern transitions, about 65 more (page last updated Aug 18, 2026). Those are the ones used below. I did not use the older avoid list on these, because the modern versions look different (Star Wipe Impacts and Burn Alpha Impacts are not the 2000s slideshow versions).

### My honest take on mixing it up

I agree. Fifty-some cuts with three transitions is a slideshow. My one rule is that every designed transition earns its place by matching what is on screen, and it gets its own sound. If every cut has a trick, the tricks stop landing, so about 43% of placements stay plain on purpose (19 of 44) and the 25 designed ones carry the energy. No designed transition appears more than twice, and none repeats back to back.
