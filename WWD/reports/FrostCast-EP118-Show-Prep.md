## 1. James Gunn fires back against criticism / Plan, no plan?

**What happened**
Around 10/26, fans asked what the DCU projects are "building towards." James Gunn answered on Threads that a story does not have to set up a big crossover to matter. He called the DCU a universe you can enter at any point, through any story, and compared it to how Star Wars has worked for 50 years.

**Why it matters**
The question is whether "standalone stories" is a plan or a cover for not having one. Gunn still points to a connected spine (Superman into Man of Tomorrow), so the debate is how much connection a shared universe owes its fans.

**Numbers**
- Superman (2025): about $616M worldwide.
- Supergirl (June 26, 2026): $126.4M worldwide through mid-August on a reported $170M budget (The Numbers). Treated as a disappointment.
- Clayface (Oct 23, 2026): about $24M opening, budget about $40M to $44M.
- Next DCU film: Man of Tomorrow, July 9, 2027. It is the only DCU movie on the 2027 slate so far. The Batman Part II (Oct 1, 2027) is outside the DCU.

**History**
Gunn and Peter Safran took over DC Studios in late 2022 and laid out "Chapter One: Gods and Monsters." So far it's Creature Commandos, Superman, Peacemaker Season 2, Supergirl, Lanterns on HBO (wrapped early October) and now Clayface. The old DCEU was knocked for rushing to a team-up with Justice League, and this is Gunn's answer to that.

**Colour**
- He made his Clayface point directly: watch it as a standalone horror film, not for hints about who shows up later.
- Some outlets read his post as a hint that Matt Hagen comes back. Gunn did not confirm that.
- Star Wars was his comparison of choice, not Marvel.

**Quick hits**
- Threads post, about 10/26: "stories don't have to build to a crossover"
- Star Wars comparison, 50 years
- Supergirl $126M WW vs $170M budget
- Only Man of Tomorrow (7/9/27) on the 2027 DCU slate
- Clayface is the test case

## 2. What's up with Carrie 2?

**What happened**
Prime Video renewed Mike Flanagan's Carrie for Season 2 about two weeks after its October 7 premiere. Flanagan comes back as showrunner, and Stephen King stays on as an executive producer. The renewal says the story goes past prom.

**Why it matters**
Season 1 covered the book. Season 2 means new story with no King text to adapt, so the question is where it goes and whether it should go at all.

**Numbers**
- Season 1: 8 episodes, all released October 7, 2026.
- Reached No. 1 on Prime Video in more than 105 countries.
- Flanagan has talked about a three-season arc, and Deadline reports he is now "two-thirds of the way" through it.
- No Season 2 premiere date or window yet.

**History**
Carrie (1974) was King's first published novel. Brian De Palma's 1976 film earned Oscar nominations for Sissy Spacek and Piper Laurie. A 2002 TV movie and the 2013 remake with Chloe Grace Moretz followed. Flanagan's King track record includes Gerald's Game, Doctor Sleep and The Life of Chuck.

**Colour**
- There already is a "Carrie 2": The Rage: Carrie 2 (1999). Nobody remembers it.
- Flanagan has talked about setting Season 2 in a "larger universe" (Marie Claire's report).
- We reviewed Season 1 on the channel (posted 10/18), so we have a take on record.

**Quick hits**
- Renewed about 2 weeks after the 10/7 premiere
- No. 1 in 105+ countries
- Three-season plan, "two-thirds of the way"
- Story goes past prom, no book left to adapt
- The Rage: Carrie 2 (1999) already exists

## 3. Clayface: hit or not?

**What happened**
Clayface opened at No. 1 for October 23 to 25 with about $24M domestic, slightly under DC's $25M to $30M projection. A24's You Can See Everything was close behind at $19.5M. Critics are mixed, and audiences like it a lot more.

**Why it matters**
On a small budget, a $24M start can be a win even without being a big number. It's also the first test of Gunn's standalone pitch from topic 1.

**Numbers**
- Opening: about $24M domestic (The Numbers lists $24,002,731; Fortune had $24.5M from 3,458 theaters).
- Worldwide opening: about $43M, about $19M of it international.
- Budget: about $44M (Hollywood Reporter, Fortune) or $40M (The Numbers).
- CinemaScore: A-.
- Rotten Tomatoes: critics about 75% to 76%, audience 94% (Forbes says that ties Obsession's record). Forbes also called the critics' score the second-lowest in the DCU.
- First Monday: about $2.3M.

**History**
Directed by James Watkins (Eden Lake, Speak No Evil), written by Mike Flanagan and Hossein Amini, with Tom Rhys Harries as Matt Hagen. Naomi Ackie, Max Minghella and Eddie Marsan are in the cast. It was first dated September 11, 2026, then moved to October 23. It's the DCU's first R-rated horror film.

**Colour**
- Reports say Flanagan distanced himself, saying he turned in his script and has not seen the film.
- The first Rotten Tomatoes review called it "DC's Morbius moment." It was briefly pulled, then restored once the embargo lifted.
- "clayface review" searches jumped about 2,600% on YouTube last week (our VidIQ pull).

**Quick hits**
- $24M opening, No. 1, under the $25M to $30M projection
- About $43M worldwide vs a $40M to $44M budget
- A- CinemaScore, RT 75% critics / 94% audience
- Watkins directs, Flanagan + Amini wrote it
- Second weekend (this coming weekend) decides it

## 4. [First word unclear: "D.find"?] BioWare was already dying after Mass Effect 3

**What happened**
EA announced on October 21 that it is closing BioWare and ending early work on the next Mass Effect. The AP reported about 400 layoffs across EA, about 50 of them at BioWare. It's EA's first big studio cut since its $55 billion buyout.

**Why it matters**
Winter Wolf's line is that the real death came after Mass Effect 3, not this month. The case for: the founders left, then Andromeda and Anthem flopped. The case against: Dragon Age Inquisition (2014) came after ME3 and was a hit.

**Numbers**
- EA bought BioWare in 2007.
- Mass Effect 3: March 2012.
- Mass Effect: Andromeda 2017, Anthem 2019, Dragon Age: The Veilguard October 2024.
- EA buyout: $55 billion, leaving about $18 billion in debt.
- A "Save Mass Effect" petition passed 240,000 signatures.

**History**
BioWare was founded in 1995 by doctors Ray Muzyka and Greg Zeschuk. It built Baldur's Gate, Knights of the Old Republic, Jade Empire, Mass Effect and Dragon Age. Both founders left in 2012, the same year as ME3. ME3's ending caused enough backlash that BioWare released a free Extended Cut. In 2025 EA cut the studio down to a small core team on the next Mass Effect.

**Colour**
- Fans ran a "Save Mass Effect" billboard in Times Square.
- Casey Hudson, the old Mass Effect director, posted that BioWare's "legacy will live on."
- Ex-BioWare staff are now at new studios like Archetype and Arcanaut.
- A Harvard Business School case study, "BioWare: Fallen Angel," dates the shift to the 2007 EA deal, not ME3.

**Quick hits**
- Closed 10/21/26, next Mass Effect cancelled
- Founders out in 2012, the ME3 year
- Andromeda '17, Anthem '19, Veilguard '24
- Counterpoint: Inquisition (2014) was a hit after ME3
- EA $55B buyout, about $18B debt

## 5. Old horror: saving theaters or delaying the inevitable?

**Note:** No report found on classic horror re-releases carrying theaters this month. This is prepped as old horror franchises (sequels, reboots and legacy names) driving the box office. If Winter Wolf means re-releases of old films, the re-release numbers found were tiny (Terminator 2's re-release did about $15K a day on 25 screens in early October).

**What happened**
Horror is having a record year, and a big share of it comes from names that are decades old: Scream 7, Scary Movie, Resident Evil. Original horror is also huge, with Obsession and Backrooms.

**Why it matters**
If theaters lean on horror and horror leans on 30-year-old names, the question is whether that's a foundation or a crutch.

**Numbers**
- Domestic horror: about $1.36B through October 25, about $62M short of the 2025 record.
- Obsession: $522M worldwide (original). Backrooms (A24): $401M (original).
- Resident Evil: $384M worldwide per The Numbers (Wikipedia says $361M), $60.2M opening.
- Scary Movie: $231.4M worldwide, the biggest opening in the series.
- Scream 7: $213.8M worldwide, $76.1M opening.
- Seven horror films opened at No. 1 this year, including Other Mommy (which we reviewed).

**History**
Scream started in 1996, Scary Movie in 2000 and Resident Evil (film) in 2002, so all three are 24 to 30 years old. Horror has carried lean box office years before, because it's cheap to make and draws a young audience.

**Colour**
- The two biggest horror films this year are originals, not legacy titles.
- Scream 7 opened to $76M, then dropped under $100K a weekend by late April.
- Godzilla Minus Zero (11/6) is next up on our calendar.

**Quick hits**
- Horror about $1.36B, near the 2025 record
- Top two are originals: Obsession $522M, Backrooms $401M
- Legacy: Resident Evil $384M, Scary Movie $231M, Scream 7 $214M
- 7 horror No. 1 openers this year
- Old-film re-releases are tiny money
