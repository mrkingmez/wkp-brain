## Movieclips logo bar blacked out

Facts from the clip list: the Fandango Movieclips logo sat inside the letterbox bar under the picture. The bar (y 950 to 1080) is blacked out in the Premiere Ready copies. The picture area is untouched. Source files kept.
- **Does it help or hurt?** It fits rule 13 spirit (no logo on screen) and removes an uploader's brand mark from our video. It does not hide that the clip is from the film, and Content ID matches picture and audio, not the logo, so it does not lower the chance of a claim.
- **Possible issue:** removing a watermark or attribution mark can be raised by a rights holder as removal of copyright management information (US law has a rule on this that turns on intent). Whether covering an uploader's logo in a black bar falls under it is a legal question I will not guess at. QUEUED for an attorney, see ID below. Practical risk looks low because the mark is a channel brand rather than the studio's, and the clip is credited in the description, but that is my read, not a legal conclusion.
- **Safer habit, no cost:** keep the description credit line below so the clip origin is stated openly. Do not describe the blackout as hiding anything.

## Exposure from the longer clips (risk view: MEDIUM, upper end)

Longer clips raise fair use exposure. This is a risk view, not a legal conclusion.
- **More of the work per clip and in total.** Clips went from 3.8 to 3.96 s each (19.14 s total) to 6.38 to 8.01 s each (35.08 s total), about 1.8 times the footage. 35.08 s is about 1.6 percent of the 36:37 video, and each clip is 3 to 6 percent of the Movieclips scene it came from (clips 1 and 5 together take 13.5 s of the 3:32 tournament upload, about 6 percent; clip 2 about 4 percent of 3:13; clip 3 about 4 percent of 2:37; clip 4 about 3 percent of 3:48). I computed these from the clip list durations and the source durations in it.
- **Each clip is now a full, continuous shot** rather than a fragment. That reads more like showing a moment than quoting it. Clips 1 and 2 are last-act payoff scenes.
- **Content ID chance goes up.** Muting removes the audio match, which is the strongest fingerprint, but a visual match on an official studio-licensed clip is still possible, and a 6 to 8 s run gives the matcher more to work with than 3.8 s did. A claim would most likely cost ad revenue on this video or add a restriction, and can be disputed; I cannot promise it will not happen.
- **What keeps it at MEDIUM and not HIGH:** each clip sits under a spoken line about that exact scene (commentary, not decoration), all muted, one shot each, never back to back, 5 spaced across 36 minutes, no cards, dates or logos, and the video is commentary built on the hosts' talk. Nothing here makes it safe, only the usual film-clip level of risk at the upper end.
- **If Zac wants to lower it without losing the point:** use the shorter end (6 to 7 s) where a shot allows, or drop clip 4 first (its fit is looser than the old clip per the clip scout, it shows her leading the dance, not the moment she steps out). Not required.
