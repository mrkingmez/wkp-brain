# Format - Character Expose
 
## The claim
Open with a claim about a character the audience thinks they know,
then spend the runtime showing the received version is wrong,
incomplete, or backwards. No claim, no episode. If the honest answer
is 'he's just a good guy', pick someone else.
 
## Structure
1. COLD OPEN - the received version, stated straight, 30-45 sec
2. THE TURN - one fact or scene that does not fit it
3. THE CASE - 3 to 5 beats of canon evidence, increasing weight
4. THE COUNTER - the strongest argument AGAINST your read, stated
   fairly. NOT OPTIONAL. Skipping it is why most character videos
   read as fan-fiction.
5. THE VERDICT - Matt calls it. Zac connects it to why it changes
   how you watch the thing.
 
## Sourcing
Primary canon only for the case: the films, episodes, books.
Interviews and writer commentary are supporting, never load-bearing.
Fan theory is never evidence. Cite the scene, not 'in the comics'.
 
## Length
12 to 18 minutes. Under 12 and the counter gets cut, which is the
part that makes it credible.
 
## Title
The title is the CLAIM, not the character name.
'Why Boba Fett Was Never The Character You Think' beats
'Boba Fett Explained'.
