## 1. Beware Boiúna Review

**What happened**
Lionsgate's giant-snake creature feature opened Friday, October 2, in about 1,121 theaters and made $550,546 for the weekend, placing around 14th. It follows a medical team in the Amazon whose mission turns into a survival fight when an ancient giant snake named Boiúna wakes up. Jessica Rothe, Kiana Madeira, and Logan Marshall-Green star, with Mike P. Nelson directing.

**Why it matters**
It is a theatrical original that got almost no attention opening weekend, which makes it a live case for the "can anything new break through" conversation on the list later.
**Numbers**
- Opening weekend: $550,546 domestic, 1,121 theaters, about $490 per theater
- Release date: October 2, 2026
- Runtime: 1 hr 44 min, rated R (violent content, grisly imagery, language)
- Rotten Tomatoes: 56% critics, 69% audience (as of 10/6)
- Budget: not found, omitted

**History**
Nelson also directed the 2021 Wrong Turn and the Silent Night, Deadly Night remake, per Fangoria. Jessica Rothe is best known to horror fans for Happy Death Day. Fangoria's headline calls it a creature feature that was "completely ignored" at the box office and expects it on streaming soon.

**Colour**
- Boiúna is a snake figure from Amazon folklore (the name is a Tupi-rooted word, also called Cobra Grande).
- Critics are split. One review line calls it "defanged nothingness" in spots, another praises the mid-film tone shift.
- Audience reactions on Rotten Tomatoes complain about thin characters and not enough monster screen time, while others call it a solid creature thriller.

**Quick hits**
- Opened Fri 10/2, Lionsgate, director Mike P. Nelson
- $550,546 opening, 1,121 theaters, about $490 per theater, around 14th
- Rotten Tomatoes 56% critics, 69% audience
- Giant snake, Amazon medical mission, 1:44, R
- Beware of the audience gripe: not enough snake

## 3. Carrie Episode One Review

**What happened**
Mike Flanagan's 8-episode Carrie series premieres on Prime Video with all episodes releasing at once, at 3 AM ET on Wednesday, October 7. Summer H. Howell plays Carrie White and Samantha Sloyan plays Margaret White. Both hosts need to watch episode one before the show.

**Why it matters**
This is a new Stephen King adaptation from the showrunner behind the Netflix Haunting series, and the fourth screen version of the story if the 2002 TV movie and the 2013 film are counted.

**Numbers**
- Release: October 7, 2026, Prime Video, all 8 episodes, about 45 minutes each
- Rotten Tomatoes: 86%, Certified Fresh (first reviews)
- Metacritic: 73 on 19 reviews, 79% positive and 21% mixed
- Premiere time: 3 AM ET / 12 AM PT

**History**
Stephen King's first published novel, Carrie, came out in 1974. The 1976 film (Brian De Palma) was followed by a 2002 TV movie and a 2013 remake with Chloë Grace Moretz. Flanagan's earlier work includes The Haunting of Hill House, and reports say Stephen King chose him for this adaptation.

**Colour**
- Cast also includes Siena Agudong, Alison Thornton, Joel Oulette, Josie Totah, Arthur Conti, Amber Midthunder, and Matthew Lillard.
- Critics' praise: Howell's performance, Sloyan's Margaret, the modern digital-age update.
- Critics' complaints: extra subplots, light on scares, setup for future seasons weakens the finale, and one review's line "Carrie simply doesn't lend itself to a TV approach."

**Quick hits**
- Prime Video, drops Wed 10/7 at 3 AM ET, all 8 episodes
- Mike Flanagan, Summer H. Howell as Carrie, Samantha Sloyan as Margaret
- Rotten Tomatoes 86%, Metacritic 73
- Complaint to watch for: stretched story, extra subplots
- Matthew Lillard in the cast

## 4. Colbert's Lord of the Rings Movie on Pause

**What happened**
Stephen Colbert's The Lord of the Rings: Shadow of the Past was reported paused after Paramount's takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery. Knight Edge Media broke the pause report. Warner Bros. then told the outlet the film is not canceled and is still in development, and drafts are continuing.

**Why it matters**
It is an early read on how the merged studio treats Warner's franchise projects. Colbert's film was announced in March 2026 with a Tolkien-heavy team behind it.

**Numbers**
- Announced: March 2026
- Merger executives who developed the project: Michael De Luca and Pamela Abdy, both reported as no longer at Warner Bros.
- The Hunt for Gollum (Andy Serkis directing) is still dated December 17, 2027
- Filming: not expected soon

**History**
Colbert co-writes with his son, Peter McGee, and Philippa Boyens (screenwriter on the Lord of the Rings and Hobbit films). Reports on Peter Jackson conflict. Variety's headline says the project came out of a deal that involved Jackson, while Screen Rant says he is not specifically attached. Treat his involvement as unconfirmed. The story is reported as Sam, Merry, and Pippin retracing early adventures 14 years after Frodo's death, with Sam's daughter Elanor uncovering secrets about the War of the Ring. A separate report says it draws on chapters of The Fellowship of the Ring that Jackson left out of the 2001 film. Those two descriptions do not match, so say "reported" if you cite either one.
Reporting also links the pause to the weak box office of The War of the Rohirrim.

**Colour**
- Colbert is a longtime Tolkien fan.
- The studio's "still in development" answer came the same week as the pause report.

**Quick hits**
- Title: The Lord of the Rings: Shadow of the Past
- Reported paused (Knight Edge Media), then Warner says not canceled
- Paramount (David Ellison) took over Warner Bros. Discovery
- Colbert, his son, and Philippa Boyens writing
- Hunt for Gollum still Dec 17, 2027

## 6. Sinister Is the Next Big Bad

**What happened**
Marvel revealed Adam Driver as Mister Sinister in the X-Men reboot at Disney's D23. Jake Schreier directs, the film is dated May 5, 2028, and co-writer Lee Sung Jin said it will lean into the soapier side of the X-Men comics. The "next big bad of the saga" idea is rumor, not an announcement.

**Why it matters**
The official line is that Driver is the X-Men film's villain. The bigger claim is a scooper's report that Sinister will work for the big bad of the next saga. Another scooper says Driver will not appear in Avengers: Secret Wars at all. No one at Marvel has confirmed either.

**Numbers**
- Release: May 5, 2028
- Comics debut: 1987

**History**
Mister Sinister (Nathaniel Essex) was created by Chris Claremont and Marc Silvestri in 1987. In the comics he is a 19th-century English geneticist obsessed with mutant evolution, with cellular regeneration and cloned backup bodies. He fixates on Cyclops and Jean Grey, believing their combined genetics could create the ultimate mutant. Storylines mentioned as possible adaptations are The Mutant Massacre, Inferno, and links to Apocalypse.

**Colour**
- Rest of the announced cast: Christopher Abbott (Professor X), Kit Connor (Cyclops), Sadie Sink (Jean Grey), Samara Weaving (Emma Frost), Maya Boyd (Storm), Inde Navarrette (Rogue).
- Sinister's focus on Scott and Jean points the film at their story rather than a Wolverine-centered one.

**Quick hits**
- Adam Driver, revealed at D23
- X-Men: May 5, 2028, Jake Schreier directing
- Official: villain of the X-Men film
- Rumor: works for the next saga's big bad, possible Secret Wars appearance (scoopers disagree)
- Comics: 1987, Claremont and Silvestri, obsessed with Cyclops and Jean

## 7. Kennedy Leaving: Interview and Article

**What happened**
Kathleen Kennedy told Bloomberg's Screentime event in Los Angeles that leaving Lucasfilm brought "a bit of relief." She stepped down as president in January after 14 years.

**Why it matters**
It is her first big public reflection since she left. She called following George Lucas an "incredible challenge" and named the hardest part: "Finding that balance," respecting devoted fans while letting new work be original.

**Numbers**
- Time as Lucasfilm president: 14 years
- Stepped down: January 2026
- Star Wars: Starfighter (Shawn Levy directing, Ryan Gosling starring): due 2027

**History**
Dave Filoni became president and chief creative officer, and Lynwen Brennan co-president, both announced in January. Kennedy is returning to producing and stays attached to Starfighter. She said she wants to get back to "a kind of eclectic group of movies the way I used to." Asked about regrets, she did not name a specific project, saying no film business gets a perfect hit every time.

**Colour**
- She said she is "so ready to go off and have the chance to make lots of movies."
- She described the post-mortem process of understanding what worked and what did not as valuable.
- Her answer to "bittersweet?" was no.

**Quick hits**
- Bloomberg Screentime, Los Angeles, reported around 10/1
- "A bit of relief", 14 years, left in January
- Toughest part: balancing fans and originality
- Filoni and Brennan run Lucasfilm now
- She is still attached to Starfighter (2027)

## 8. Wasted Potential: Alien or Predator

**What happened**
Both franchises are active right now, which sets up the debate. Alien: Earth season 2 started production in May 2026 at Pinewood in London, with Sydney Chandler returning and Peter Dinklage joining in a mystery role. A sequel to Alien: Romulus is in early development with a new director. Predator: Badlands finished its theatrical run at about $184.5 million worldwide.

**Why it matters**
The two series have crossed over before (the Alien vs. Predator films) and the crossover idea keeps coming back, with neither side producing a hit crossover. The recent Predator box office also reportedly cooled interest in a new crossover.

**Numbers**
- Predator: Badlands: about $184.5 million worldwide ($91.1 million domestic, $93.5 million international) against a reported $105 million production budget
- One outlet (Cosmic Book News) puts the losses near $80 million. That is an outlet's estimate, not a studio number, and other outlets frame Badlands as the franchise's highest-grossing entry.
- Alien: Earth season 2: production began May 2026, one outlet guesses early 2027 for release (no date confirmed)

**History**
Alien vs. Predator (2004) and its sequel (2007) were critical bombs with modest box office, and a third entry was shelved. Alien: Earth creator Noah Hawley said on the SmartLess podcast that a Predator will not appear in season 2, and that Dan Trachtenberg (Prey, Badlands) is handling that franchise without coordination.

**Colour**
- No one at Disney has announced Predator: Badlands 2, and an outlet suggests the next one may go straight to Hulu or Disney+.
- Hawley and Trachtenberg are explicitly not coordinating their franchises.
- Trachtenberg directed both Prey and Badlands.

**Quick hits**
- Alien: Earth season 2 filming since May, Dinklage joins
- Romulus sequel in early development
- Badlands: about $184.5M worldwide vs a $105M budget
- AVP (2004, 2007): critical bombs, third film shelved
- Hawley: no Predator in season 2
