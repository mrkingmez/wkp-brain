# The Perfect Planner: Product Concept Brief

Date: 2026-11-16. Status: concept only. Nothing listed, nothing priced publicly, no public copy written. Next gate is Zac's yes (see Section 10).

Working title is Zac's. Name candidates are in Section 7.

## 1. The one-paragraph pitch

A planner built around how Zac actually works: he captures by voice, he gets his time in fragments, he wants to hand a task off and see it come back done, and he gets buried when there is no clear first move. It is a personal operating system on paper and in a spreadsheet, not a decoration. Zac is customer number one. If it works for him for 30 days, it is a candidate to sell to people who work the same way.

## 2. Who it is for

Primary user (proven need): Zac. ADHD (medicated), US Army veteran, 8 to 5 job plus evenings, WKP time grabbed in fragments, voice-to-text as a main input, chronic pain and some foggy days, glasses for screens, reading and spelling are still hard, brain runs ahead of hands, overwhelmed by not knowing what to do first, pulled toward adding new ideas instead of finishing. All from ME.md.

Possible buyer (unproven, to be tested): adults with ADHD or ADHD-like attention patterns who have a full-time job and a side project, who dictate notes, and who respond to a military-style structure (clear top line, clear map). Veterans are a smaller overlap inside that group. Nobody has been asked yet. This is a hypothesis.

Not for: anyone looking for a medical product. No claim that it treats, manages or improves ADHD or depression. It is an organizing tool.

## 3. Design principles (11)

Each one is drawn from a line in ME.md. These are design choices based on one person's stated needs. They are not clinical findings and not proof that other people will benefit.

| # | Principle | Where it comes from in ME.md | What it means on the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fire and forget | "Give me a task, come back to it done." | A Capture page with no sorting at capture time. Sort later. A Handoff list: what, who or which agent, due, came back yes/no. |
| 2 | Voice first | Voice-to-text is the primary input; brain is ahead of hands; typos and wrong words | Free-text lines, no tiny boxes. A "say it like this" key: start with TASK, IDEA, BUY, CALL, so a dictated line sorts itself later. Spelling does not matter. |
| 3 | Built for fragments | WKP time is "grabbed in fragments, not long blocks" | Every page works in 5 to 10 minutes. A "Got 5 minutes?" lane and a "Got 20 minutes?" lane on the day page. |
| 4 | A recommendation, not a menu | "I want a recommendation, built to flex." | One "DO FIRST" slot with a one-line reason. Only one. |
| 5 | Map plus Semper Gumby | Works better with structure and a clear plan, but stays flexible | Plan on top, a BUMP list underneath for what rolls when life changes. Rolling is normal, not failure. |
| 6 | Bottom line up front | Military background, structure | Top line of every page is one sentence: what wins today or this week. |
| 7 | Not another rabbit hole | "Just don't be another rabbit hole. Keep things tight." | One page per day maximum. No habit-tracker sprawl. No decoration. A stop line at the bottom of each page. |
| 8 | Finish before adding | Pulled to add "cool factor" ideas instead of finishing | A Parking Lot for new ideas (write it down, forget it for now) and a Finish Line page with at most 3 open items. |
| 9 | Easy on the eyes | Glasses needed for screens, sometimes hard to read | Large print option, high contrast, short line lengths, generous space. |
| 10 | A foggy-day page | Pain, fog, feeling "out of it" some days | A minimum page: 3 boxes (must do, nice to do, rest). No guilt. |
| 11 | Honest, no shame | Feeling "not good enough" is real, not background | No streaks that break. A Wins list instead of a miss counter. Evening review is "done / rolls / tomorrow", not a grade. |

Reading load (Zac did not learn to read until 19): short words, icons for the section types, minimal instruction text. This sits under principle 9.

## 4. Page and tab layout

Same content in every format. Names are working labels.

| Section | Pages | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Start Here | 1 | How the planner works in 6 lines. Voice key (TASK, IDEA, BUY, CALL). |
| Year Map | 1 | Big rocks for the year, three at most per area. |
| Mission (quarter) | 1 | One line "commander's intent" for the quarter, three finish-line items. |
| Month | 1 | Calendar fragment, the month's top 3, what rolled in. |
| Week (Monday brief) | 1 | Week top 3, fixed commitments, BUMP list. |
| Day | 1 | BLUF line, DO FIRST plus reason, 5-minute lane, 20-minute lane, capture strip, wins. See mock in Section 9. |
| Foggy Day | 1 | 3 boxes only. |
| Capture | many | Dump anything. One line each. Sort on Saturday. |
| Handoffs | 1 | Fire and forget: what I handed off, who, due, back yes/no. |
| Parking Lot | 1 | New ideas, dated, with a "revisit" date. |
| Finish Line | 1 | Max 3 open projects, next action for each. |
| Saturday Wrap | 1 | Done, rolled, what to drop, next week's top 3. Zac already runs a Saturday wrap in WKP, so the habit exists. |
| Wins | 1 | Running list. Never reset. |

Spreadsheet tabs (Excel and Google Sheets): Capture (a ledger, one row per line, built so it works with phone dictation), Today, Week, Handoffs, Parking Lot, Finish Line, Saturday Wrap, Wins, README. Same build approach as the Budget Tracker 2027 workbook: yellow input cells, locked formulas, data validation, print setup.

## 5. Formats

1. Printable PDF, US Letter and half-letter (A5-style), undated so it never expires. Fillable PDF as a second file. Large-print variant.
2. Google Sheets and Excel workbook with the tabs above. Phone-friendly Capture tab. Also useful for people who dictate.
3. Notebook layout: a stamp sheet or printed headers on dot grid for people who want a bound notebook. A low-content book version (KDP interior) is possible later. KDP is currently on hold per Zac, so this is a later option only.
4. Later, only if the first three sell: GoodNotes or iPad hyperlinked PDF.

Print at home, no physical inventory, no shipping, no supplier. Consistent with how Kingdom Planners already runs.

## 6. How it differs from Kingdom Planners

| | Kingdom Planners (existing) | The Perfect Planner |
|---|---|---|
| What it organizes | Money and life events: debt, budget, PCS move, deployment pay, VA claim, terminal leave, road trip | Attention and time: what to do first, capture, handoffs, finishing |
| Format | Excel spreadsheets with formulas, one topic each | Mixed: printable PDF plus a light workbook |
| Buyer intent | A specific job to be done (plan a move, track a claim) | A daily-use system |
| Rhythm | Used for a project or a season | Used every day and every week |
| Brand | Navy and gold, military family | Same shop family is possible, different line. Not decided. |

The two can feed each other: the Budget Tracker 2027 workbook built today is a candidate "money page" add-on later. No decision.

## 7. Name candidates

None checked for trademark, Etsy name collisions or domain. Do that before choosing.

- The Perfect Planner (working title, generic and hard to rank for)
- Fragment Planner (leans on the fragments-of-time idea)
- Semper Gumby Planner (Zac's own phrase from ME.md, flexible plan, strong voice, check usage first)
- Fire and Forget Planner (matches principle 1, also reads as a military term)
- Bottom Line Planner (BLUF, simple)
- Ten Minute Planner (promise is clear, may collide with existing planners)
- Sit Rep Planner (military shorthand, narrows the audience to people who know it)

Recommendation: shortlist Fragment Planner, Fire and Forget Planner and Semper Gumby Planner, then run a collision check on each.

## 8. Honest market check

Searched 2026-11-16, three web searches plus one follow-up. This is a thin look, not a full study. No Amazon prices were retrieved. Etsy does not publish sales counts on listings, so nothing below proves any listing sells.

What the search showed:

- General ADHD planners on Etsy are crowded. One keyword tool reports about 14,800 average monthly searches for "adhd planner" and 68,451 Etsy listings competing for it. That is one third-party tool's figure and I did not verify it.
- Prices for general ADHD planners on Etsy sit mostly around $5 to $12, with bundles up to roughly $15 to $25. One tool reports a range of $0.49 to $46 for "adhd planner". Many listings show 30 percent or more off an "original" price, so list prices are weak evidence.
- A third-party research page names a shop called UsefulPlanner as the top-selling printable ADHD planner on Etsy. I could not verify that ranking or its date.
- Named shops with ADHD planners include FutureADHD (on Etsy since 2016), GentleMindPlanner, DailyFocusClub and PlanMindco. Some are iPad and GoodNotes, some are printable.
- Paper ADHD planners from established brands (Panda Planner, Simple Elephant) are sold as physical books. One review site lists Panda Planner Classic at $19.97, another guide says $24.97. Conflicting, so treat the range as $20 to $25 and unconfirmed.
- Veteran and military planners found were mostly transition or finance focused. Military Millionaire Planner hardcover about $28 on two retailers. A Veterans' Career Exploration Journal at $9.50. A Veteran guided story journal at $22 to $23. A PCS move planner at $10.99 (Gumroad) and $16.01 (Etsy).
- Voice is showing up as a theme: ADHD apps market voice capture (Saner.AI, Dopa, Tiimo). No paper or spreadsheet planner built around dictation turned up in these searches.
- I found no planner aimed at veterans with ADHD, and none built around fragments-of-time plus voice. Absence in four searches is not proof it does not exist. Search Etsy directly for "veteran ADHD planner" before deciding.

Reading it honestly:

- The general ADHD planner market is saturated and low-priced. Entering as "another ADHD planner" would be a bad bet.
- The only open space seen is the intersection: voice-first, fragments of time, fire-and-forget handoffs, military-style structure. Whether anyone searches for that or pays for it is unknown.
- Price hypothesis to test, not a fact: a clearly differentiated bundle in the $12 to $18 range, which fits the shop's existing price points ($12 to $19 for Kingdom Planners products) and sits above the commodity $5 to $8 listings. A plain printable PDF alone probably will not hold that price.
- The biggest risk is that it only works for Zac. The 30-day test addresses that.
