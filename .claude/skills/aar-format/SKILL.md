# AAR Commander

Full module: D:\WKP\WKP-CHANNEL\AAR-Format-Module.md

## What makes this format work
What If and Hidden Hinge are formats a strong historian could execute.
This one runs on operational experience - the framework applied by
someone who knows what a real AAR sounds like, and what a dishonest
one sounds like. The value is the framework, not the history.

## FORMAT LAW

1 NO INVENTED DIALOGUE. EVER. Participants speak only through the
documented record. Reasoning not preserved? The honest line is "he
never explained this, and we don't know." One fabricated quote costs
more than the episode earns.

2 Improvements must be PERIOD-LEGAL. Tested against technology,
doctrine, communications, training, and institutional culture actually
available. "Should have coordinated better" is worthless if there was
no means to coordinate. If the fix did not exist yet, that is the
limit of the era - and saying so is the more interesting observation.

3 Judgment against CONTEMPORARY standards only. Measured against a
competent peer, in that army, in that decade.

4 Unit of analysis is the COMMAND RELATIONSHIP. Whoever actually owned
the decisions, at whatever echelon. Flexes from a commander and his
host to a commander across a partially controlled mixed force.

5 "They didn't learn" is a legitimate and often BETTER episode. The
lesson was correct, written down, and the institution refused it. Real,
common, almost never covered.

## The four questions

Q1 WHAT WAS SUPPOSED TO HAPPEN - the plan. Objective in the
commander's own words where preserved. Higher intent. Scheme of
maneuver. Assumptions stated and unstated. Intelligence known,
believed, absent. Force and time available versus required.
REVERSE-ENGINEERING INTENT FROM THE OUTCOME IS THE SINGLE MOST COMMON
ERROR IN THIS FORMAT. Where the plan was not written down, say so.

Q2 WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED - phase by phase. Per phase: what occurred,
where reality diverged, WHEN THE COMMANDER BECAME AWARE (usually much
later than the audience assumes), what was decided, what options were
genuinely open.
Track four failure classes: FRICTION (nobody's fault), ENEMY ACTION
(credit them by name), COMMAND FAILURE (wrong on information available
at the time), INSTITUTIONAL FAILURE (the system set them up to fail).
That last class is what most battle content misses entirely.
Close with THE POINT OF NO RETURN and whether he recognized it.

Q3 TAKEAWAYS
Sustain - what was right and should be repeated. There is always
something. SEPARATE GOOD DECISIONS FROM GOOD OUTCOMES. A sound call
that failed still sustains. A reckless call that paid off does not.
Improve - per item: what went wrong, root cause not symptom, the
period-legal fix, and WHETHER HE HAD AUTHORITY TO IMPLEMENT IT. A
lieutenant colonel cannot fix a doctrine problem. Naming the correct
echelon separates analysis from armchair quarterbacking.
Rank the improve items. Which one, fixed alone, changes the most?

Q4 RETRAIN, REFIGHT, VERIFY - three tests in order.
Test 1 Was the lesson available then, or only visible now?
Test 2 Was it identified? Cite the document.
Test 3 Was it institutionalized, or did it die with the individual?
Then verify against the next engagement.
Verdict, plainly: LEARNED / LEARNED - NOT INSTITUTIONALIZED /
PARTIALLY LEARNED / NOT LEARNED / UNTESTED.
LEARNED - NOT INSTITUTIONALIZED is the most common real result and the
most interesting to build an episode around.

## Close
The three claims in your own report you consider weakest, and why.
